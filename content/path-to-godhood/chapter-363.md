<p>Beneath the lands of the Blanks, far below stone streets and quiet homes, there was a place that never slept.</p>

<p>The secret basement stretched deep underground, carved layer by layer with brutal precision. Thick stone walls were reinforced with runes and metal cribs. Torches burned with a dull blue flame, casting long, twisted shadows that crawled along the floor like living things.</p>

<p>This was not a dungeon meant for simple prisoners.</p>

<p>This was a prison for monsters.</p>

<p>Rows upon rows of iron cells lined the vast underground hall. Chains hung from the ceiling, thick as a man’s arm. The air smelled of blood, sweat, rust, and something darker that clung to the lungs. Faint screams echoed constantly, never fully stopping, never fully beginning. They blended into a low, maddening hum.</p>

<p>Inside the cells were the worst of the worst.</p>

<p>Crazed maniacs who laughed while killing. Dark wizards who had stitched people together while they were still alive. Wanted criminals whose names alone could freeze towns into silence.</p>

<p>Some were tied to walls. Some were nailed into place. Some were locked inside cages filled with suppressing runes that crushed mana and will alike.</p>

<p>And all of them were breaking.</p>

<p>A man screamed as hooks dug into his shoulders, slowly lifting him off the ground.</p>

<p>“Please! Please stop! I’ll talk! I’ll tell you everything!”</p>

<p>A hooded interrogator tilted his head slightly.</p>

<p>“You already told us everything,” he said calmly. “Now we’re just making sure you remember it.”</p>

<p>The hooks turned.</p>

<p>The scream turned into a hoarse croak.</p>

<p>In another corner, a woman with burned hands laughed hysterically while strapped to a metal chair. Sparks danced over her skin as a device pulsed against her spine.</p>

<p>“This hurts,” she giggled. “But it’s not enough.”</p>

<p>The interrogator paused and stared at her.</p>

<p>“…What?”</p>

<p>She licked her cracked lips and grinned, eyes wide and unhinged.</p>

<p>“More. More. Pleasure me more.”</p>

<p>The interrogator slowly turned his head.</p>

<p>“What the fuck do you mean by pleasure me more?”</p>

<p>Another guard snorted from behind.</p>

<p>“We’re torturing you, not fucking you, asshole.”</p>

<p>He swung his whip.</p>

<p>The whip was lined with sharpened bone fragments. It cracked through the air and tore into her flesh, peeling skin away in long, wet strips.</p>

<p>She screamed this time but what followed was another crazed laughter.</p>

<p>“Ahhh!The pleasure of pain,I love it.”</p>

<p>All across the prison, similar scenes played out.</p>

<p>Some begged. Some cursed. Some laughed until they choked on their own blood. A few tried to sing. Others muttered prayers to gods that were no longer listening.</p>

<p>At the center of it all stood a raised platform of black stone.</p>

<p>There, surrounded by runes and silent guards, stood Hall.</p>

<p>The Leader of Black Thorns.</p>

<p>He wore simple dark clothes, clean and neat, as if he were attending a meeting instead of overseeing hell. His hands rested behind his back. His eyes were closed.</p>

<p>He listened to the screams. The sobs. The rage. The despair.</p>

<p>He breathed it in slowly, like a man enjoying a strong drink.</p>

<p>This place was not for the weak-willed.</p>

<p>Even trained operatives sometimes broke just by standing here too long. Some fainted and some vomited. A few suffered heart attacks before they even began their work.</p>

<p>Recently, a new recruit had collapsed the moment he stepped inside.</p>

<p>“I warned them,” Hall muttered quietly. “Don’t come here if you can’t handle it.”</p>

<p>He opened his eyes and looked around calmly.</p>

<p>“Everyone wants to be a man in the shadows,” he said. “But no one wants to carry the weight.”</p>

<p>As he spoke, a crystal ball on the table beside him began to glow.</p>

<p>Hall turned slightly.</p>

<p>“Yes,” he said.</p>

<p>A voice echoed from the crystal.</p>

<p>[Sir Hall. There is a troublesome matter.]</p>

<p>“What happened?” Hall asked, his tone steady.</p>

<p>[Frontier is under demon attack.]</p>

<p>Hall’s brows creased slightly.</p>

<p>“Demons? All of a sudden?”</p>

<p>He turned his head toward the map carved into the stone floor.</p>

<p>“What happened? Any clear intel?”</p>

<p>[Not yet. Our forces haven’t infiltrated deep enough.]</p>

<p>[The King has issued multiple orders, but the situation is still unclear.]</p>

<p>“How bad is it?” Hall asked.</p>

<p>A pause.</p>

<p>[One fifth of the kingdom’s border land has fallen.]</p>

<p>[The King has formed a defensive line and is concentrating all power there.]</p>

<p>Hall exhaled slowly.</p>

<p>“I see.”</p>

<p>The light faded as the channel closed.</p>

<p>Hall stood still for a moment, then shook his head faintly.</p>

<p>“I wonder how the empires will respond,” he said quietly. “Though I doubt we’ll learn much.”</p>

<p>Their foundation was still shallow. That was the truth.</p>

<p>They simply did not have enough people embedded deep inside imperial cores. Most of their operatives were strong enough for guards, scouts, and minor officials. Nothing more.</p>

<p>He had often wondered why empires produced so many strong seedlings while smaller lands struggled.</p>

<p>Until his Lord explained it clearly.</p>

<p>Talent was inherited.</p>

<p>Anyone with real potential eventually left for the empire. Centuries of migration had drained smaller lands dry, leaving behind only scraps and nobles clinging to bloodlines.</p>

<p>To change that required insane amounts of money, resources, and patience.</p>

<p>Like Ethan had done.</p>

<p>Hall glanced at his own hands.</p>

<p>He was never meant to reach Master Realm.</p>

<p>Not in this lifetime.</p>

<p>Yet here he stood.</p>

<p>That alone was enough to bind his loyalty forever.</p>

<p>A sudden shout pulled him out of his thoughts.</p>

<p>“Sir!”</p>

<p>A man rushed up the steps, kneeling quickly.</p>

<p>“We have intel. A cavalry unit is approaching our territory. They bear the flag of the Empire.”</p>

<p>Hall’s eyes sharpened instantly.</p>

<p>“Cavalry?” he repeated.</p>

<p>“Yes, sir. Should we deploy countermeasures?”</p>

<p>Hall straightened fully.</p>

<p>“No,” he said firmly. “Not yet.”</p>

<p>He turned sharply.</p>

<p>“Inform Sir Rathlos immediately and ask for his judgment.”</p>

<p>The messenger nodded and ran.</p>

<p>Hall clenched his fist once, then relaxed it.</p>

<p>“Cavarly out of nowhere.This shouldn’t be an attack,” he said quietly. “Not yet.”</p>

<p>He looked back toward the prison, where screams still echoed endlessly.</p>

<p>“Stay on standby,” he ordered. “And keep your eyes open.”</p>
