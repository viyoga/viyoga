-I used a lot of energy. I'm tired.

Kirsi's will transmitted through. Kirsi's clones—the cherry trees—returned to their original forms, and the scattering cherry blossom petals stopped.

"I'm desummoning you. Go back."

-I did well this time, right? You know it. I want to eat a spirit elixir. A thrilling-tasting one, if possible.

"Fine. I'll get you one soon."

Desummon.

Kirsi, who had been behind my back, vanished. Unlike when she was summoned, no spatial distortion occurred.

I tried to move toward Marit, who lay collapsed far off, then flinched. I'd taken only a single step, yet pain surged from my abdomen. Even though I'd passed most of the damage to Kirsi, it was still this bad.

I drank a recovery potion I'd prepared in advance from my inventory. As befitting a top-grade potion, its effect was excellent. The pain vanished quickly.

'Still, some internal injuries remain, it seems. Nothing serious—I'll recover in a day or two.'

No problem using magic. Just a bit tired.

I walked across the snowfield, still crackling with residual electricity.

Marit lay in the snow. The snow around her had turned red. Soaked with blood. Marit herself was drenched head to toe in it.

"Marit. Are you alive?"

Marit slowly opened her eyes. Her lips trembled.

"...I feel like I'm dying."

"So you're alive."

To look at her, she seemed severe enough to be rushed into an ICU immediately, but her pulse was stable. A Rank 7 superhuman wouldn't die from losing a lot of blood. Her heart and brain—the most critical organs—were functioning, so she was fine.

I pulled a potion from my inventory and carefully put it to her lips.

Gulp. Gulp.

As her throat moved with each swallow, color returned to her face.

"...I saw your Thunder Break. It was nothing like the one you gave me—different from the very power level. I thought a small nuclear bomb had gone off."

"A nuclear bomb. You exaggerate too much. Thunder Break's power increased because the environment was favorable. There was more lightning element here than elsewhere, and Kirsi helped."

"Kirsi. So that's the cherry tree's name. She was beautiful."

Marit raised only her hands from where she lay.

"What is it."

"You. Right now I can hardly move from my wounds. I'd like you to carry me on your back."

"A single bottle of the potion you just drank costs 100 million credits."

"I have a constitution that doesn't absorb potions well."

As if.

Marit was faking it right now. I let out a faint sigh and hoisted her onto my back.

"This is the first time I've been carried by anyone other than my father. It's a fresh feeling."

"...Is Haukal finished with this?"

"It's finished. Didn't you handle the remnants before coming here? From the Empire's standpoint, one danger has disappeared."

"Though I can't say the Empire's life has improved just because that one danger is gone."

"Ahaha. The Empire has plenty of enemies besides Haukal, after all."

"The job's done. What will you do now?"

"If I had my way, I'd hunt down and kill every barbarian tribe in the Isbindr Mountains, but... for now, recovering my body comes first. I plan to rest for a while. And you?"

"I'll work as usual."

"Hmm. Are you aware that rumors of you being Rank 7 are spreading through the Imperial Army? Things could get troublesome."

"Even if it gets a bit troublesome, no one will treat me carelessly. That's enough."

At least the riffraff won't come picking fights.

"You. This path doesn't lead out of the mountains—it looks like it's heading deeper in?"

"I smell a spirit elixir nearby. I'll grab it while I'm here."

"Mages can smell spirit elixirs too?"

Marit laughed low.

The spirit elixir smell was a rough excuse I'd made up. I'd simply recalled roughly where this area was through my knowledge of the original work.

'It just came to me. The game's map and the real landscape are a bit different—I nearly walked right past it.'

The Isbindr Mountains are one of the high-level hunting grounds in the original game. I know roughly what's inside.

'I know the monsters inside too. For now they're tough to handle, and there's no need to deal with them.'

Rare magic materials? With enough money, there's no reason for me to take on that risk.

'There. Where three trees rise in a trident shape.'

Beneath them was a boulder about a meter across. I stopped in front of it.

"You. There's nothing here worth calling a spirit elixir."

"It's right in front of you. You have no eye for this."

"...This rock is a spirit elixir? There's no mana reaction at all. Did your head get scrambled by the aftermath of the battle earlier? Mmm. They say mages are all scrambled in the head, so maybe it doesn't matter...?"

"That's a baseless rumor. Slander from non-mages jealous of mages. And most natural spirit elixirs like this aren't detected by mana. If they were detectable by mana, someone else would have dug them up long ago."

"Then how do you tell them apart?"

"Normally you can't. If you're lucky, you find them."

"And you just found one now."

"I'm lucky."

Squeeze.

Marit gripped my shoulder hard. It didn't hurt, so I ignored it.

I cast magic at the boulder.

[Wind Cutter]

Rank 2 wind magic.

A blade of wind conjured from thin air sliced the boulder cleanly in half.

A sweet scent burst forth. From inside the rock, golden honey oozed down in streams.

Marit, startled, craned her head sharply over my shoulder.

"That's?!"

"Millennium Stone Honey. It's not honey made by bees—it's honey naturally formed from condensed energy."

How can honey be born naturally? In this world, you just accept it and move on.

Marit couldn't resist the sweet fragrance and leaped down from my back. She dipped her index finger deep into the Millennium Stone Honey and put it in her mouth.

"It's sweet! Tastier than any honey I've ever eaten! And suddenly I feel bursting with energy!"

"If you eat about a cup, you'll feel your body strengthen. Any more than that won't have additional effect, though."

I took two cups from my inventory. One for her, one for me. Thick honey sloshed inside the cups. I swallowed the honey. With ordinary honey, swallowing a full cup in one go would be impossible, but... Millennium Stone Honey was so easy on the body that I could drink not just a cup but a full liter at once.

The effect was immediate.

The physical enhancement was negligible, but my mana rods increased by twenty. Combined with the twenty newly appeared mana rods, that made a total of 290 mana rods.

"Good. Marit. We'll split the Millennium Stone Honey half and half."

"No need. Weren't you the one who found this Millennium Stone Honey? You take all of it. Ah, just pass me a little for snacking."

"Are you sure? Selling it would fetch quite a bit of money."

"You. Aren't you misunderstanding something? I'm not some bitch obsessed with money. I'm on the poor side for a noble, sure, but—well, I get by fine without it."

"I didn't mean to insult you. I just have a lot of money."

Marit shot me a look. I laughed low and put the Millennium Stone Honey into my inventory. Later I'd set aside a proper share for Marit.

I wasn't in urgent need of money, so I had no intention of selling the Millennium Stone Honey.

'A spirit elixir like this makes a good gift.'

Only for important people, of course. The first person who came to mind was, naturally, Yuria. And the other women connected to me as well.

'I hear Yuria is working at the royal palace for 100 days to take the final Prime-rank exam this time. Just in case, I should gift some to the Queen and the Royal Head Maid too.'

I can't say there's no bribery involved.

'But the Queen and that Head Maid wouldn't be swayed by something as simple as Millennium Stone Honey. The royal vaults must be overflowing with things far more precious than this.'

Still, receiving a rare gift would make them pay some attention. That's enough.

"Can't exactly refuse when you put it like that."

I walked toward the bed.

Neo London District A, at the entrance of Buckingham Palace, a carriage bearing the LK Group's name came to a stop.

The carriage door opened, and a silver-haired maid stepped down with light footsteps.

Yuria gazed at Buckingham Palace with calm eyes.

Knights guarding the entrance.

A magnificent garden, perfectly maintained.

A palace building rebuilt thirty years ago, yet breathing with living tradition.

Any citizen of Neo London would have been overcome with emotion at the sight of this palace, eyes growing moist—but Yuria's blue eyes held no emotion at all, only transparency.

In her steps toward Buckingham Palace, there was neither excitement nor nervousness.
