I checked the reports.

Things were going well. The LK Group was growing by leaps and bounds, and Lumen Prison had stabilized. There was nothing I needed to do right away, so time was suddenly on my hands.

'The nobles of Neo London keep sending me social party invitations and investment proposals, but there's no need to deal with any of it right now.'

The duties of a noble?

There was no such thing in Neo London. Nobles were a dime a dozen here to begin with. And because they were so common, their power and authority amounted to nothing much. Holding a count's title didn't grant you the kind of political clout that could shake Neo London.

'Being a noble in Neo London is basically an entry ticket to high society. It's good for rich folks who want to look important.'

Noble society ran on connections. That's why nobles hosted social gatherings, hunting parties, and meetups practically every other day. New nobles often spent fortunes just to get invited to these events.

'At my level, I don't need to bother with networking. The nobles already walk on eggshells around me.'

Anyway, the important thing was that I'd been given some free time for the first time in a while.

Normally, times like this meant locking myself in the workshop to research magic, but…

'I've hit a wall.'

I was feeling the limits of my magic research. Not a limit of talent, though.

'Lately it's been hard to focus on magic research. My mind keeps wandering, I suppose.'

The truth was, I knew the cause.

Now that I had enough power and force to be reckoned with even in Neo London, my desire for revenge against Alpha Tier Pharmaceuticals was rearing its head.

'I want to find everyone connected to Alpha Tier and kill them right now.'

I didn't care if they were clueless low-level employees.

I just wanted to kill them for the sole reason of being connected to Alpha Tier.

'…Not yet. I can't openly oppose Alpha Tier. Alpha Tier Pharmaceuticals is one of the world's leading megacorps. If the current LK Group goes head-to-head with Alpha Tier… LK Group will be crushed. One hundred percent.'

Even if LK Group's future was bright—no, radiant—it was still just a fledgling company. Alpha Tier Pharmaceuticals, on the other hand, was a colossal enterprise that had grown over centuries. Even a child could tell who'd win in a fight.

'Revenge is premature. I know that. I know it, but…'

My hands were itching.

'Should I hide my identity and raid a few Alpha Tier branches? I happen to have the vampire dark mage Moegen identity too…'

The Moegen identity also carried the nickname "Soul Collector."

'…No. I should drop it. On second thought, it's too risky.'

I'd already destroyed one of Alpha Tier's secret experiment sites under the Moegen identity. Alpha Tier wasn't stupid—they'd be watching Moegen. As the saying goes, a long tail gets stepped on. If I got careless with the Moegen identity, my true identity could be exposed.

'Lying low is the best move. Maybe I'll go see Rose for the first time in a while. I still haven't given her the Millennium Stone Honey.'

Rose was one of the few highly trustworthy brokers in Neo London. Assassinations, information, commissions—there was plenty to be gained through Rose. I wanted to maintain a steady connection with her.

I quietly headed to District X. I entered a second-floor manpower office tucked in a back alley of District X.

"What brings the chairman of a group to a shabby place like this?"

Rose spoke bluntly. Black hair, wine-colored blouse. Her right arm was mechanical. A black rose tattoo adorned her neck. Her chest was estimated at an E-cup.

"I recently got something good, so I came to give you a gift."

"I never turn down a gift. Surely a man of your standing wouldn't bring something strange?"

I pulled a glass bottle containing the Millennium Stone Honey from my inventory and handed it over.

"…Honey?"

"It's Millennium Stone Honey."

Rose's eyes went wide.

"Millennium Stone Honey?! The elixir that permanently improves your physical abilities just by drinking it?!"

"So you know about it. Most people don't."

"You do remember I'm a broker, right? I get tons of requests to procure this stuff. Where did you get it?"

"I obtained it by chance. Rest assured, it wasn't acquired illegally. Ah, were you thinking of selling it?"

"I'm not so money-crazed that I'd sell a gift the moment I receive it. I'm going to drink it myself. There aren't any side effects, right?"

"Seems you don't know everything about Millennium Stone Honey."

"Most artificial elixirs have side effects."

"If you're worried, get it tested."

"I'll trust you, Chairman."

Rose opened the bottle's lid. Before the sweet scent could even spread, her lips met the glass. Glug. Glug. She boldly downed the Millennium Stone Honey and set the empty bottle down. Her eyes looked slightly glazed.

"…The effect is incredible. I can already feel vitality surging through my body. I can literally feel my physical abilities getting stronger without having done anything."

"It's not just your bones and muscles that improved—your internal organs and eyesight would have too. It's only natural you'd feel it."

"…You're really giving something like this away for free? Be honest with me. What's your angle?"

"Can't you just take goodwill as goodwill?"

"If goodwill is too heavy, it's nothing but debt."

"Then that works out. Consider it me repaying the debts I owe you, without you even knowing."

The fact that I'd been able to establish myself relatively easily in Neo London was partly thanks to her.

"So. You didn't really come just to hand over a gift, did you?"

"While I'm at it, I'd like to see if there are any commissions worth my time."

"That's unusual. Most mages who've established themselves like you tend to avoid fieldwork."

"Sitting still makes my hands itch. The commissions?"

"There's an overflow of commissions a 7th-rank mage could take. A 7th-rank itself is rare, but a mage on top of that? The power dynamic flips. Normally the client holds all the cards. Wait a moment. I'll look through the public commissions."

Public commissions. Requests that aren't sent to just one broker but distributed to multiple brokers simultaneously.

Public commissions fell into two categories. Either extremely easy, or extremely difficult.

Commissions seeking a 7th-rank mage belonged to the latter.

A short while later, Rose brought over the commission documents.

I quickly scanned through the papers, one by one.

"No illegal work here."

"They're public commissions, after all. You can't openly post corporate terrorism or contract killings. Most are legal. That said, it's not all light work. There are commissions asking you to stay and defend a fortress in the middle of a war."

"So join a war as a mercenary. It's common enough, but it doesn't appeal to me. Takes too long. And if possible, I'd prefer my identity not be revealed."

"Unless it's a commission where you're openly standing in front of crowds, clients won't go around blabbing your identity. Nobody's fool enough to want a 7th-rank combat mage as an enemy."

My hand, which had been flipping through the documents without pause, stopped mid-motion. I'd found a commission that caught my interest.

The commission was seeking a 7th-rank mage to explore a dungeon together on the day the Dark Moon rises.

The client was Nora Hanger.

In this world, she was counted among the most famous explorers alive.

'And the character I played in the game.'

Rose checked the commission I was staring at and made a peculiar expression.

"That's the Ruin Wrecker's commission."

"Ruin Wrecker?"

"It's Nora Hanger's nickname. More than half the ruins she's explored have been destroyed. That's why the owners of ruins dread Nora Hanger. There's no point in a ruin being discovered if it gets destroyed. That aside, Nora Hanger's skill is top-tier in the industry. I don't recommend this commission."

"Why?"

"Are you seriously asking? She's saying she wants to explore a dungeon on the day the Dark Moon rises. A normal person can't do something like that."

She was right.

On the day the Dark Moon rises, the world turns into a madhouse. Dungeons are also affected by the Dark Moon and change.

'In the game, dungeon difficulty increased on the day the Dark Moon rose. Or they'd transform into special dungeons.'

I'd deliberately sought out such dungeons before. Higher dungeon difficulty meant better rewards.

"It says here the commission reward is negotiable upon meeting. Is that acceptable?"

"Huh? Nora Hanger's commissions are always like that. From what I hear, she gives one of her treasures as payment. Weapons, artifacts, spellbooks… They're things you can't get on the market, so Nora Hanger's commissions are popular."

"A world-class treasure hunter's treasure—it won't be anything ordinary. I'm intrigued. I'll take this commission."

"…Haah. Right. You're a lunatic too. Fine. I'll get in contact for now. You negotiate the reward face-to-face. If you don't like it, come back."

• • •

I met Nora Hanger at a sparsely populated café on the outskirts of Neo London.

Nora was a beauty with sun-bronzed, copper-toned skin. Her hair, tied in a ponytail, cascaded down to her waist. Her eyes gleamed amber. She wore a white tank top and black leggings that clung tightly to her legs. Leather straps formed a harness binding her shoulders, waist, and thighs. Pistols and daggers were mounted on the harness.

"Wow. You really are Eugene Meyer. I never thought a corporate chairman would accept a dungeon exploration. Never thought I'd see the day."

Nora straightened her back in admiration and extended her hand. Her chest heaved in the process. Perhaps because she wasn't wearing a bra, the bounce was tremendous. The inner cleavage, untouched by sunlight, was stark white.

'…Aside from the tanned skin, her appearance is exactly as I designed it. Unlike the game, I suppose sun-darkened skin is unavoidable.'

I put on a blank expression and took Nora's offered hand for a handshake.

"Nora Hanger. I've long heard of your reputation. The industry's finest treasure hunter."

"I never particularly wanted to become the finest treasure hunter. At some point, people just started calling me that."

"Shall we get to the point? I'll accept the commission as long as the reward is sufficient."

"I like how straightforward you are. Still, that's too hasty a decision. There's information not written in the commission document. I've explored this dungeon twice. The first time was with a 6th-rank dark mage, the second with a 7th-rank mercenary mage. Both attempts failed. The mages died in the dungeon."

"…Why reveal information you could have hidden until the end?"

"We're going to be comrades, even if temporarily. We'll be crossing the line of life and death in that dungeon—we need at least a minimum of trust. If you refuse, that's just how it goes."

"The reward? If it's such a dangerous dungeon, you wouldn't offer something ordinary as payment, would you?"

"First, the rewards obtained from this dungeon will be split exactly fifty-fifty. As for the commission reward… Have you by any chance heard of Merlin's Numbers?"
