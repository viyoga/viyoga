Kukukukukukukukung.

The moment I entered the [Eternal Eden] world, what greeted me was an earthquake.

I feel the vibration of the ground from the tips of my toes. The walls and ceiling shake visibly, and objects I'd placed on the table topple off.

'A strong quake rarely seen in Korea.'

By this world's standards, strong earthquakes of magnitude 5 or higher—rare in Korea—occur two to three times a week.

Korea's major buildings have been reinforced against earthquakes, so there's no major problem. The citizens will grow accustomed to them, and when a magnitude 7 or higher quake hits, they'll handle it on their own. We trained them that way.

'The cause of the earthquakes is the Japanese archipelago. The Japanese archipelago is approaching the Korean peninsula.'

Earthquakes caused by massive tectonic shifts.

That the Japanese archipelago's movement only produces magnitude 5 quakes was a blessing.

'War will come soon.'

Peace with Japan?

Impossible. Korea and Japan have been preparing for war since this world went to shit. The only way to maintain peace now is for one of the two nations to bow its head. And that's something neither country can ever choose.

I summoned Lee Yeonhee, the Director of the National Intelligence Agency. Her title is NIA Director, but her work and power exceed that of the Presidential Chief of Staff.

"You called for me, Your Excellency."

Lee Yeonhee stands before me. Unlike the modified hanbok suit she wore in the [Martial Tower] world, she's dressed in a simple black suit and coat, holding a posture of attention.

Her military discipline was far sharper than in the [Martial Tower] world. Yet the faint smile on her lips was the same. The external assessment of her: a capable woman whose inner thoughts are unreadable.

"Is the start of the Korea-Japan war as reported? Any changes?"

"Yes. It's proceeding as calculated. In approximately six months, the Japanese mainland will become visible to the naked eye. The air defense system for that has been perfectly prepared. Dr. Na's assistance was invaluable."

"As I've said before, bombing via missiles... civilian casualties must be minimized."

It's not because I'm worried about international scrutiny over civilian massacres. International organizations like the UN have effectively lost all meaning. Even the United States is mired in internal chaos. It has no capacity to concern itself with foreign affairs.

"The Japanese must become Korean."

"Wise words. The reeducation programs for that purpose are fully prepared. We referenced the history of the Japanese colonial period."

"Japan's war plans?"

"My apologies. We could not obtain accurate information. We attempted to plant spies within the current Japanese government... all failed. It seems the Japanese government has some method of identifying information."

"So they possess that kind of psychic ability."

"In cooperation with the strong AI Zena, we attempted to infer Japan's war plans. Japan will with high probability use biochemical weapons, and their top priority is Your Excellency's capture. The probability of a special operation targeting Your Excellency being executed before the war begins is over 90%."

Japan wants to capture me alive.

It wasn't even surprising.

My ability, Recovery, is somewhat known externally to be capable of reversing aging. Rumors that I can resurrect the dead are quietly spreading, but they're so absurd that no one believes them unless they're a devotee of the Seongjin Church.

Youth. That alone was enough for Japan's power brokers to covet.

"Director."

"Yes, Your Excellency."

"Begin internal purges. Spies, traitors, rebels, criminals... no exceptions. Arrest, interrogate, and execute those who are of no use to the nation. I authorize the use of all armed forces, including the Holy Knight Order."

Lee Yeonhee's eyes gleamed.

What I was ordering was, in effect, a purge. There was no way Lee Yeonhee wouldn't recognize that.

In this internal crackdown, all those who stood as obstacles to Korea would die.

"Yes. I will not disappoint you."

• • •

Lee Yeonhee began the crackdown on Seong Yujin's orders.

It started with the military.

Officers who had committed even the slightest corruption were to be swept away entirely with this opportunity.

She had been waiting for the chance for a long time and had already compiled lists, so the crackdown proceeded swiftly. Generals and officers dragged into the NIA vomited every kind of information under brutal torture. Those with relatively light offenses ended with labor sentences; those with filthier crimes were imprisoned or executed.

"Director Lee Yeonhee! You're committing madness with His Excellency's power! What you're doing is an abuse of authority!! If you keep stirring up the military like this, who will defend Korea, who will fight the war! I'm not saying don't do it at all! Communicate with us! Why can't you understand that some degree of graft is unavoidable?!"

"Korea's military must be perfect. It must be without blemish. It will become the world's finest military, after all. Naturally, it must be so."

"You insane—! I demand to see His Excellency! You, woman, do nothing and wait until I return!"

"It was His Excellency who gave me this order."

Tang.

Lee Yeonhee put a bullet in the lieutenant general's head without hesitation. The body of the lieutenant general, who had been taking kickbacks from a small-to-medium enterprise, was cremated—all except one finger. The reason for leaving the finger was simple. It was in case something became necessary from him later, in case additional punishment was needed.

In a single day, five generals and seventy-two officers were executed.

Generals and officers who felt threatened by the unprecedented speed attempted to commit treason.

"Why should we?"

The soldiers didn't follow. Most of the soldiers were devotees of the Seongjin Church. To the soldiers, Seong Yujin was a savior.

Instead, they seized the traitors and handed them over to the NIA. The traitors were executed along with their families.

Lee Yeonhee did not hesitate to surveil civilians either.

It was a national state of emergency. Martial law remained in effect. Thanks to the strong AI Zena's outstanding performance, every individual's social media, video comments, and even messages exchanged with family and friends were all monitored.

Citizens with anti-state ideologies were thrown into correctional facilities. When they left those facilities, they would become faithful devotees of the Seongjin Church. Those who couldn't would be quietly eliminated.

Everything was going smoothly.

That was precisely why Lee Yeonhee felt a bitterness lingering on the tip of her tongue.

"I wished for Korea to become great, but I never wished for a nation that eliminates and controls individual freedoms... but given the times, it can't be helped."

Korea's survival. And beyond that, seizing hegemony.

"Director. A peculiar organism was discovered in the brain of the protest leader arrested yesterday. Experts believe it to be a parasite."

Lee Yeonhee's brow furrowed.

• • •

I held a banquet at the Blue House.

Those invited to the banquet were the chairmen running Korea's major corporations.

Thirty major corporate chairmen in total.

Originally, over a hundred major corporate chairmen would have qualified for invitation to this banquet. But as the world went to shit, companies went bankrupt one after another. Being a major corporation was no exception.

Major corporations that couldn't hold on and went bankrupt were bought up cheaply by other companies, or simply collapsed.

As the world fell apart, the market shrank, and in the already narrow South Korean market, the thirty major corporations gathered here held over 90% of it. Looking at it this way, it seemed like it wasn't the government ruling Korea, but the corporations.

That was why the corporate chairmen could wear expressions of tension mixed with confidence before me.

Some sent me looks mixed with expectation and greed. They were imagining freely that I would commend them and bestow the treasure of youth upon them.

Seated at the head of the table, I raised my wine glass. What was in the glass was cheap wine sold at convenience stores, but even that was a thing of the past. In this era, with sea routes blocked, the value of a single bottle of this cheap wine had soared beyond what ordinary citizens could even dream of touching.

"For Korea's future."

I recited a clichéd toast and raised my glass.

At that, the chairmen wore fake smiles and each raised their glasses. A few seemed to have relaxed.

"For Korea's future!"

The chairmen downed their wine in one gulp. Cheap as it was, it was wine hard to find in Korea now. Not a single chairman left any behind.

I too took a sip of wine.

The sharp, stinging sourness unique to cheap wine hit me. I immediately hurled the glass.

Clang!

Shards of transparent glass scattered in all directions.

The warm atmosphere was smashed into the gutter. The eyes of the chairmen looking at me trembled.

They seemed to finally recall old memories.

Sejong.

What had happened in that place that became a city of corporations.

Korea's major corporations held 90% of the Korean market? Did the government not know and simply leave them alone?

"Korea enters a wartime footing as of today. Everything in the nation will be controlled. Corporations are no exception."

"Y-Your Excellency. A w-war, you say. Th-that's still premature. I'm aware that the Japanese archipelago, Korea's greatest enemy, is approaching the peninsula, but there's still plenty of time! A wartime footing this early will bankrupt the national treasury!"

Shin Wooseop, chairman of the Guryong Group, the number one in the business world, spoke.

I looked at him. Though Shin Wooseop was the chairman of Guryong Group, he surprisingly held no real power. The true power lay with his father, Honorary Chairman Shin Godeok. Shin Godeok was beside him. A man in his twenties who at a glance looked like his son. Having received youth from me, he maintained silence with a rigid expression.

"No. Even this is late. Japan has already restructured its entire national system to invade Korea."

“…….”

Silence.

Through the uncomfortable air, the chairmen glanced at each other. They seemed to want someone to step in and dissuade me on their behalf.

"During wartime, a special wartime tax will be applied. All taxes rise without exception. For corporations, it's 70%."

One chairman shouted as if screaming.

"Y-Your Excellency! 70% is too much! The company can't survive!"

I crooked my finger at him.

A knight in white power armor with a lightning mark on the chest and a red cape on the back—the knight serving as my guard—pulled the trigger of his rifle.

The head of the chairman who had risen from his seat burst.

Blood and brain matter splattered across the silent banquet hall.

"All corporations shall prioritize the production of military materiel and deliver it to the military. Corporations that faithfully carry out the nation's orders will receive tax reductions. Conversely, corporations that do not follow the nation's orders will be treated as traitors."

Shin Godeok, honorary chairman of Guryong Group, quietly raised his hand.

"Shin Godeok. Speak."

"Your Excellency. Our Guryong Group will gladly comply with government control. However, producing military materiel inevitably requires money. By any chance, will the government issue war bonds?"

"War bonds? Not in the plans. If the nation falls, everyone falls. You will cooperate with all your heart and soul."

“…….”

"Instead, after the war ends, I will give you the opportunity to devour the archipelago's market, the opportunity to absorb the archipelago's corporations."

"Your Excellency. Will the people of the archipelago become the same people as the Korean peninsula?"

"Ultimately they will, but I am aware that for now, they will not. They will be distinguished until the peoples of both nations are fully assimilated."

A faint smile appeared at the corner of Shin Godeok's mouth.

"I do not doubt Korea's victory, nor Your Excellency's. I await the day of victory."

Having said all I needed, I rose from my seat. I had no desire to spend time with old men.
