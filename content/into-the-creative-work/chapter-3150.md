"Kraaaaaaah! This can't be happening! Just kill this sovereign instead! For this sovereign to become the slave of Ppangz-Ppangz! The dignity of True China is being ground into the dirt!"

"It's already too late, Chwesik."

"This sovereign despairs!"

Chwesik's head, pinned beneath my foot, vanishes. To be precise, the grimoire swallowed his life vessel and dragged him inside along with it. This happened because a lich's essence is its life vessel. The magic nunchaku Chwesik had been using disappeared as well.

'In the original game, killing Chwesik drops a Life Vessel Fragment, a top-tier magical material.'

Tk.

The Necromantic Grimoire that had swallowed the life vessel dropped a key onto the ground.

As I furrowed my brow at the key's sudden appearance, Nora snatched it up without hesitation.

"Oh, this is that thing."

"That thing?"

"The treasure vault key. You know, the one up on the cliff."

"...Ah. I see. A dungeon reward, then."

The key had been reduced to garbage.

Nora and I had already gone into the treasure vault and cleaned it out completely. Nora examined the key just in case, then tossed it aside carelessly.

"Nora. The exit?"

"It's open. With the boss at the dungeon's core gone, this place will collapse in a few hours. We're safe for now, so don't worry. Take your time. How's your body?"

"Who are you worrying about? On the surface, my body's in better shape than yours."

The fight with Chwesik had been tough, but I hadn't taken any seriously effective critical hits.

'If I'd been properly hit, I'd be dead before I could even worry about wounds. Without Avalonia, the battle would've been even harder.'

I won't be able to use Avalonia for a while. Last Illusion in particular will take at least a few months before I can use it again.

'Being able to use illusion magic of that caliber without any other penalty is already generous. I can't complain about a long cooldown.'

Without Avalonia's illusions, I would have died.

'...No, that's not right either. I wouldn't have driven the puppet needle into my own head in the first place.'

Regardless, I'd earned rewards commensurate with the hardship.

I'd gained the barrier technique known as the Eight Gate Boundary Formation, and captured a rank-7 lich to use as a familiar. Plus No.2 Avalonia, obtained as the commission fee.

'All things worth risking my life for.'

But the work wasn't entirely finished.

Nora and I looked at the steamed-bun-faced mercenary crumpled off to one side.

When Wolfgang felt our gazes converge on him, he waved his hands vigorously.

"I-I get embarrassed when you look at me like that, y'know."

Nora's eyes narrowed sharply.

"Cut the bullshit, Wolfgang. Your mercenary band chased after me. You planned to steal my treasures and kill me if it came to it. It's obvious."

Wolfgang's eyes darted between me and Nora.

Even if he looked like a fool, he was a man who'd led a mercenary band. It couldn't be said he had no instincts at all. He was probably weighing possibilities in his head right now.

The battle was over. All three of us were exhausted. But on the surface, I looked fine, and Nora had taken minor wounds. Of the three of us, the one who'd be spending the longest time in a hospital bed was clearly Wolfgang.

'His face is a steamed bun and he's still talking and moving fine—it's almost impressive. Is his pain tolerance really above a sandbag's?'

Come to think of it, among the papers on innate abilities, some claim that innate abilities are related to the user's disposition and talent. Wolfgang's innate ability was Reflection.

'In other words, it's possible he got his ass beaten so fucking much that he awakened Reflection as his innate ability because he was so desperate to stop getting hit.'

I can't be 100% certain of this theory.

There are too few rank-7s to study innate abilities properly, and most rank-7s wouldn't cooperate with such pointless research anyway.

"P-please spare me, Count. We're comrades in arms, even if it doesn't look like it. If I hadn't stalled for time back then, we'd all have died to that damned China undead!"

"...Mercenary. Isn't there something between us that needs settling? I killed your subordinate outside the dungeon. Better to resolve it cleanly here than have you come at me later for revenge."

At my will, the Necromantic Grimoire floated into the air. A perfect opportunity to test the performance of my new familiar, Chwesik.

"Revenge?! Don't be absurd! I'd never even think of it!"

"A subordinate dies and you don't seek revenge? A mercenary band is an organization too. When an organization's prestige falls, its value falls with it. Do you expect me to believe that?"

Especially in this world—once you're seen as weak, it's over.

"Fuck! I'm telling the truth! I'll swear an oath if you want. The Wolfgang Mercenary Band is finished anyway! There's no prestige left to protect."

"...Don't tell me they're all dead?"

"Yeah. Fuck. Weak-ass bastards. Dying to a bunch of jiangshi."

"You feel nothing for your subordinates?"

"Feelings? Whoa. Our Count is a softie, huh. Most of a mercenary band is just people who gathered for profit. You think anyone founds a mercenary band out of some grand cause? You band together so you can get more jobs, and once you've got underlings, you make a mercenary band. The guys who join are mostly either talentless or criminals with too much heat on them. I figured I'd run a band and skim some easy money too... Fuck. Those bastards were so incompetent I was practically supporting them. Good riddance, honestly. I'm going back to being Wolfgang the freelance mercenary!"

Wolfgang spat out excuses that sounded like lamentations.

They didn't seem like lies. He didn't appear to have the intelligence for that.

"Haa. Whatever. I don't want to keep grappling with this idiot trash. Let's go back and rest. I'm tired."

I agreed with Nora.

I only looked fine on the surface—combat fatigue had piled up considerably. Last Illusion didn't restore combat fatigue or spent mana, after all.

"Hey, mercenary. Keep quiet about today. If you spread what you saw here... I'll put a 10 billion credit bounty on your head."

"Kek. Count really seems like the type to do it, and that's terrifying. The ones who die first in this world are the ones with loose lips. I'm a man whose mouth is so heavy that if I fell into the sea, my mouth would sink first, so don't worry. More than anything, if word of this gets out, it's bad for me too. My reputation would be destroyed."

In the mercenary industry, reputation is everything—you could say it's most of the job.

Only with a good reputation do high-paying commissions come your way.

I lowered the grimoire. I'd originally intended to return it to my inventory, but the grimoire wouldn't go in.

'My inventory was never able to hold living things or souls. Is this because Chwesik is bound to the Necromantic Grimoire?'

It was slightly inconvenient, but not a major problem.

We exited the dungeon.

The Dark Moon still hung in the night sky. Neither of us particularly minded. We were rank-7—why would we fear a Dark Moon?

"It feels good to breathe outside air. I don't know about anything else, but during a Dark Moon the air quality changes and it's great."

Nora said, stretching. Her moderately full chest and firm abs drew my gaze.

"...It's not air quality. The mana density is higher than usual. Those who can sense mana just perceive it as feeling better than normal."

"Look at you, Mister Wizard. What a buzzkill."

"...On a day the Dark Moon rises, I should be out making a score. Damn it."

Wolfgang let out a heavy sigh and continued.

"We're not the type to go to a restaurant together and chat amicably, so let's part ways here. Ah, Count. Here's my card. Take it. I'll give you a 10% discount, so contact me if you need anything."

"Here's mine. Don't contact me for trivial matters."

"I even get the Chairman's card. I figured it'd be gold-leafed or something... It's pretty plain."

We exchanged cards without fuss.

Wolfgang might be a bit of an idiot, but as a rank-7 warrior his durability is the real deal. A natural-born meat shield—he'd be useful when needed. And building connections with rank-7s can't hurt.

Wolfgang waved and disappeared.

Only Nora and I remained.

From the moment we safely exited the dungeon, Nora's commission was complete. The treasures from the dungeon had been split exactly in half, and the commission fee, Avalonia, had been received inside the dungeon.

"This is where we part too. Thanks to you, I made it out safely. If it weren't for you, I might've died alongside that unlucky mercenary."

"Do you plan to keep working as a treasure hunter? If you want, I can offer you a position at LK Group. Talent like yours is always welcome."

"I appreciate the offer. Even if I don't look it, I've got a bad case of wanderlust. Staying put in one place doesn't suit me. If I ever settle down somewhere... it'll be after I retire as a treasure hunter."

"If you need my help, say the word anytime. As long as it's not too difficult a problem, I'll help."

"Haha. The Chairman's help, huh. That's reassuring. Oh, if a good dungeon or ruin shows up later, can I ask for your help? Finding a capable mage like you is nearly impossible in this world."

"That's what I want. Of course, I'll charge a fee."

"Don't worry about the fee. I'm not the stingy type."

Nora carved something into a rock near the dungeon entrance with a chisel.

"What are you doing?"

"Leaving a mark that I came and went here. It's not just me—every treasure hunter does it. It's to mess with the latecomers."

What she carved into the rock was a four-leaf clover symbol.

🍀.

"Good luck to those who wasted their time getting here."

Nora giggled.

Nora and I descended the mountain and parted ways. She waved as she walked off.

"See you again sometime."
