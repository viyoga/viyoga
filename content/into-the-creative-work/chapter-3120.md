Marcel, the President of the European Union, nearly let out a sigh but swallowed it back.

"What insolence is this? Are you truly saying Great Korea intends to make an enemy of our European Union? There's a limit to how much rudeness I can tolerate."

"The rude ones here would be you lot. Technology sharing? Compared to the Empire's technology, is there a single field where yours comes out ahead? Same goes for youth. Did I ever ask you to entrust your youth to me?"

"...Huu. Fine. I'll acknowledge that I was rude. However, diplomacy varies depending on circumstances. Europe is in a stable state, but your Empire—Great Korea—is not, is it?"

"What nonsense. The Empire is currently stable and enjoying peace. We won the Korea-Japan War, and our losses, including casualties, were astonishingly minimal. It should already be public on the internet."

"Only a fool would believe that information one hundred percent, no? Great Korea may have won the war against Japan, but what about China and Russia? Especially China—I hear their robot technology has reached an incredible level."

Marcel narrows his eyes as if probing my reaction. No—it's a ploy to extract information through my actual response.

"It's true that the homeland is in a standoff along the border with China. However, there has never been a direct armed clash."

It's like the border region between South and North Korea before reunification. Troops are stationed, but no actual combat has ever broken out. China is currently in a state of incomplete internal consolidation.

"So you're in a standoff, at any rate. Aren't you anxious? Your relations with Russia can't be amicable either. Do you feel the need for an alliance now?"

"Nonsense. Russia has no interest in the homeland. The homeland likewise has no interest in Russia. China? It's true they're dangerous, but China's interests lie to the west. From China's standpoint, expanding westward costs less and yields more than dealing with the Empire. Your European Union fears China and Russia—and among them, Russia most of all. Am I wrong?"

Since ancient times, Europe had guarded against and feared Russia. Whatever else might be said, Russia's sheer size was absurd, and its proximity to Europe was undeniable.

"...You're right. I'm relieved that you're not entirely devoid of diplomatic insight. Our European Union is wary of Russia above all else. Russia is currently on the verge of occupying Ukraine. They're openly demonstrating this to Europe."

"Russia on the verge of occupying Ukraine? Are you joking? There's no way Russia is that strong."

"What nonsense are you spouting. That Russia. Even if the Empire won its war against Japan, haven't you underestimated Russia far too much?"

The Russia-Ukraine war grinding into a quagmire... that's a story from another world.

'I got confused for a moment.'

Russia in this world seemed stronger than Russia in the other world.

It's a minor error that arises because each entertainment world has slightly different economic and military levels by country.

"You want our Empire to keep Russia in check."

"That's right. I won't ask for much. A military standoff. That alone will suffice. In return, we will also keep China in check."

"That's quite the unappealing proposal."

Europe doesn't share a border with China. Keep China in check? It's containment in name only. They have no intention of actually conducting military operations against China. China wouldn't pay Europe any mind. On the other hand, Korea? Korea actually shares a border with Russia. Though it's small compared to the border with China.

"Young Emperor. Allow me to offer one piece of advice. Diplomacy is about making friends well."

"Old President. Let me give you one piece of advice. There is no such thing as an eternal friend."

"You'll make enemies of every nation? That arrogance will bring about the worst possible outcome."

Marcel glares at me.

"I believe I already told you. The Empire regards every nation in the world as a potential enemy state. There are no exceptions."

Just because you make enemies of every nation doesn't mean every nation except the Empire will join hands. Even now, the European Union and Russia are snarling at each other.

"Ha. Further conversation seems pointless. How about we end things here for today?"

Honestly, I'd expected the European Union to offer up at least a few technologies. But judging by their reaction, they don't seem to be in a hurry. Perhaps the European Union already has other plans in place.

"Let's do that."

There's no need to go fully hostile with the European Union right this moment.

Even if we're not allies, we can still share intelligence.

The communication cut off.

'If Europe is at this level... nations will start finishing their internal consolidation and turning their eyes outward before long.'

I called for Lee Yeonhee, who had been watching from the corner.

"Lee Yeonhee."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"We lack intelligence on foreign nations. China, Russia, Europe, the Americas and Africa... I need information on what the situation is like."

"...It will take time, Your Majesty. The number of agents currently dispatched overseas is extremely small, and even those are frequently discovered and eliminated. With Your Majesty's ability, we could still obtain information even if they die, but..."

"The efficiency is poor."

"That's correct. They're discovered far too often, and foreign nations are conducting intensive internal surveillance just like the homeland. China in particular has made considerable advances in robots and AI. Above all, they execute people at the slightest suspicion."

"They don't even have regeneration abilities like mine, yet they slaughter humans left and right. I suppose it's because of their large population."

"That's not the only reason. According to recently obtained intelligence, there have been sightings of humans attending to machines."

"Smart machines take on managerial roles, and stupid humans become their attendants and keep the wheels turning. It's not surprising—I'd anticipated this."

Honestly, if you're going by pure efficiency, wouldn't that be the correct approach? Even I, the Emperor, think that way sometimes.

'But I didn't take this seat to pursue efficiency alone.'

What matters is women.

I want human women.

"Lee Yeonhee. Can we drop a nuclear missile on the European Union?"

"It's possible using intercontinental ballistic missiles. However, even if we fire a hundred, there's a chance they could all be intercepted. The distance is simply too great. Conversely, the Empire can intercept all nuclear missiles flying in from the European Union."

"We can't get out to sea, so... in effect, we should consider a nuclear war with the European Union to be impossible."

"That's correct. If missiles won't work, aerial warfare and close-quarters combat would become the mainstay, but... that won't be easy either. We're simply too far from the Empire."

In the end, distance was the problem.

Of course, there was a countermeasure for this.

Air battleships.

Even if it's impossible right now, with superconductors, strong AI, and Na Chaeyoung's ability, the construction of air battleships will be possible within a few years.

Once the air battleships are complete, we'll be able to project military force to the opposite side of the globe.

'Though I'll have to make the superconductors myself.'

For the time being, I'll focus on internal affairs.

Her mission was to maintain public order in the Japanese territory of the Holy Korean Empire. However, it was rare for her to personally take on assignments. The same was true for other military units dispatched to Japan.

There was no reason for a holy knight to personally mobilize for mere city policing. That would be a waste of manpower and a waste of resources.

The matters that required a knight's intervention were those beyond what military units could handle. For example, striking hostile organizations of company size or larger. Or when mutants appeared.

Biological research—and among that, the Japanese archipelago had been a hub for intensive mutant research, and it had more mutants than even the Korean Peninsula.

"Ah, fuck. I want to watch baseball!"

So free, in fact, that there were occasional incidents—usually knights fighting among themselves until one of them ended up dead.

Wiiiiiiiiiiing.

Choi Hyejin frowned and pulled out her smartphone. The voice of the AI, Jenna, rang out from her phone.

Combat was an enjoyable exercise for her. A Class 3 mutant would let her work up a decent sweat.

Having been trained by Seong Yujin, Choi Hyejin had ascended to the ranks of humanity's strongest.

So she must never show weakness.

She must never rest.
