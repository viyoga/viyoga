Clank. Clank.

Michael walked down the corridor, the chains of his cuffs and shackles rattling with each step. I walked alongside him, watching. He was docile. For now, at least, he seemed to have no intention of causing a scene.

"I heard this was a prison out in the frontier, so I assumed the facilities would be run-down. But it's surprisingly clean, and the equipment's new."

"Just because it's the frontier, does the building have to be old? That's nothing but prejudice."

"Try telling that to the inspector when he eventually shows up."

"A pointless worry. I've used alchemy to fix up this building myself. Even if an inspector comes, there won't be any problems."

It was true that I'd used alchemy on the building for my own training.

"You'll bribe that inspector too, I'm sure. Is the Rainbow Show set located further inside?"

“…….”

The Rainbow Show.

A death survival show run with prisoners. It was the largest source of income at Lemen Prison.

*Michael was originally a noble. It's no surprise he'd know about the Rainbow Show being held at Lemen Prison.*

The upper echelons of the Haist Empire knew as well. They were viewers too, and they used brokers to spread bribes around appropriately. And behind me stood Mari Müller—Corps Commander of the 6th Corps and an 8th-rank spirit mage—so for the time being, everything was running without a hitch.

*The problem is, I don't know how long Mari Müller's goodwill will last.*

Even now, I had no idea what Mari Müller, who knew my true identity, wanted from me.

I entered the consultation room with Michael.

It was called a consultation room, but the interior held nothing but a single desk and two chairs. On the surface, it was closer to an interrogation room.

"A bleak consultation room. I think the atmosphere would improve considerably if you put a potted plant in the corner."

"Enough with the pointless chatter. What did you want to say?"

"Leon Schneider. I'll make you an offer. Release me in three days. Whether you report it to your superiors as an escape, an execution, or an accidental death—do as you like. All you have to do is let me go in three days."

"…I think I see where this is going. The reason you deliberately came to Lemen Prison out on the frontier—it's to cross the border and defect, isn't it?"

"Your head seems to work well enough. That saves me time on persuasion. The price is 3 billion credits. I'll provide it in cash in three days. Clean money, no strings attached."

About 90 billion won in Korean currency. A considerable sum.

"So a comrade of yours is bringing the money in three days."

"That's right. I'm not alone."

"The Resistance?"

"Of course. Did you think an imperial would help me?"

I looked at Michael. He had been confident and self-assured throughout. He was certain I would accept this offer.

The calculator in my head started running.

3 billion credits. Obtaining a lump sum like that in one go wasn't easy. And it was even laundered money? A list of magical tools and materials I could buy with 3 billion credits flashed through my mind.

"I don't understand."

"Hm? Was my explanation insufficient?"

"You're an imperial noble—why are you helping the Resistance's revolution? The Goltvolf County is a family with tradition and prestige, isn't it? If you were born somewhere good, couldn't you just live content with that life?"

"Ha. Are you really asking because you don't know? The empire is overflowing with corruption. From bottom to top. Did you think noble society would be any different? No, it's not different at all. At the center of the rotting empire sits the imperial family. As long as the imperial family stands, the entire empire will rot. I simply made my decision before it was too late—before everything rotted and crumbled to dust."

"I don't understand. You helped the Resistance for a reason like that? Even throwing away everything you had?"

"Hmph. A greedy commoner like you could never understand nobility of purpose. I didn't even expect you to."

"I refuse your offer."

"What? You're not tempted by 3 billion credits?"

"After you defect overseas, you'll continue your Resistance activities. The imperial army will find out. They'll investigate me, and the moment they learn I was involved in your defection, I become a traitor. I have no intention of becoming a traitor for a mere 3 billion credits."

Compared to the return, the risk was absurdly high. No—there was nothing but risk.

Throw away the Leon Schneider identity, you say? Discarding the Leon Schneider identity for a mere 3 billion credits would be a devastating loss. For reference, running Lemen Prison stably for a few months would earn well over 3 billion credits.

"I thought you were smart, but you're a fool. I told you my comrade is coming in three days, didn't I? My comrade isn't just carrying a bag with 3 billion credits in it. He's carrying guns and blades too. I'll give you one last chance."

"Now the prisoner is threatening the warden. I'm scared enough that I'd better throw the prisoner in front of me into a cell."

"The imperial army doesn't listen to words. In that sense, you're imperial army down to the bone."

Clang!

The shackles and cuffs binding Michael were torn off simultaneously. He had reinforced his strength with body enhancement magic and smashed the restraints apart.

I immediately placed my palm on the iron desk and used alchemy.

[Form Transmutation]

[Telekinesis]

The desk transformed into sharp stakes, and I launched them at Michael. My target wasn't his head but his flank. Mages were precious magical materials in and of themselves, so I planned to capture him alive rather than kill him.

Kaaang.

The stakes were blocked by a rock shield that appeared in front of Michael.

The rock shield hadn't been summoned—it had been created. Mana had been processed through a formula to make it, the same way one would make fire or lightning, but with rock.

*The Goltvolf family has produced rock mages for generations.*

Crack!

The rock shield cracked, then shattered.

*A rock shield that blocks perfectly and then breaks? There's no way a 6th-rank mage would use such a clumsy one-use rock shield. He converted the shield into an offensive weapon.*

I rose from my seat and thrust the iron chair forward.

[Form Transmutation]

The chair became a shield, blocking the flying rock fragments.

Kwa-kwa-kwa-kwa!

[Detection]

My vision was blocked by the shield, but detection magic told me what Michael was doing. Around him, sharp-pointed rocks were being created.

*Five rocks. And the ones he's made are even rotating. Not telekinesis—he's controlling the rocks themselves. Even if it's a low-rank spell, the efficiency is excellent.*

He was different from mages who did nothing but research. He had real combat instincts.

I swallowed my breath.

Without using a chanter's delay, the formula was processed and manifested in an instant.

[Frost Horn]

The room froze in an instant. The five rotating rocks were no exception. They froze, their rotation halted, and dropped to the floor.

Michael was covered in white frost. But mana rippled from his body, and the frost clinging to him fell away. The breath he exhaled froze solid in an instant and shattered.

"Frost Horn…?! Manifesting a 6th-rank ice spell in an instant! Is it a high-grade artifact?! If it's not, this makes no sense! Unless you're 7th-rank… ugh…."

Michael's eyes went wide. Unlike before, I no longer concealed my rank. The astral of a 7th-rank mage dominated the space.

The 6th-rank mage, who had failed to achieve his first transcendence, ground his teeth.

"You—what are you really? Are you even Leon Schneider?"

"You don't need to know."

As I focused my mind, Michael's astral became distinctly perceptible.

It was an astral as hard and heavy as rock. But its size was small. There was no sense of transcendence. His astral was desperately squeezing out magical formulas.

"Even a 7th-rank mage isn't invincible…! There are plenty of cases of 7th-rank mages being defeated by 6th-rank mages! There's no reason I can't do it too!!"

"That would only be possible if the 6th-rank mage had finished all his preparations before facing the 7th-rank. You have no preparations—you don't even have a magic staff or artifacts."

"You should have killed me instead of wasting time gloating! I'll make you regret giving me time! This is the magic passed down through generations of the Goltvolf family!"

Small stone fragments appeared around him. Dozens of them clung to his body, taking the shape of armor. The mana-infused armor glowed faintly gold. Its form resembled a wolf.

*Goltvolf meant golden wolf, didn't it. So that's what it refers to. The body enhancement magic he used earlier too—does he like fighting with his body directly despite being a mage?*

He was still a man I couldn't understand.

"Gave you time? I had already finished my preparations and was simply watching."

Before Michael could cast his spell, I chanted a spell that was already prepared.

[Freezing]

7th-rank ice magic.

The cold that had filled the consultation room converged on Michael in an instant. Rapid cooling. Michael froze solid in his armored state, unable to move.

"I deliberately didn't kill you."

I had adjusted the output to a certain degree so Michael wouldn't die.

"Capturing a high-ranking mage alive requires a great deal of luck. A 6th-rank mage, no less? This is a stroke of fortune rolled right to my feet. If I harvest your organs and sell them on the black market, I could easily earn billions of credits. But don't worry. I'll put you to good use."

My magical advancement mattered more than money.

"When people think of alchemy, they usually think of turning stone into gold first. That's not wrong. Alchemy's beginnings certainly started from that kind of greed. But I believe the essence of alchemy lies in change. And everything in this world changes. Humans are no exception."

I extended my hand toward him.

"Your voluntary bodily donation is welcome. Thanks to you, my magic will advance, even if only a little. By the way—do you like dogs or cats?"
