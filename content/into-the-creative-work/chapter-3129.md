Marit's mouth fell open in surprise. But only for a moment. She quickly composed her expression and smiled faintly.

"To think that the identity of the Residual Extremity Alchemist—who has neither blood nor tears—would be the most famous superconductor mage on the continent right now. I never predicted that. At most, I figured you were a hidden imperial."

If anything, I'm the one who's surprised.

"Residual Extremity Alchemist? Superconductor Mage? What are those ridiculous nicknames?"

"What do you mean, what? They're all nicknames referring to you. In the superhuman communities on the internet, everyone calls you that. The Residual Extremity Alchemist—because your cruelty in violating criminals' human rights reached its extreme. The Superconductor Mage—because you're the chairman of LK Group who invented and sells superconductors. Nicknames usually attach themselves to a target's most memorable traits, don't they?"

That's true enough.

Just as a mage missing an arm gets called a one-armed mage. Though any mage worth their salt would just regrow the arm themselves.

What surprised me was something else entirely.

"...Superhuman communities? Those exist?"

"It's a community on the deep web. One that only those at Grade 5 or above can access. Though in reality, it seems the majority are Grade 4 or below. It's useful because there's quite a bit of information you can't find on censored official internet sites."

"More useful than brokers?"

"No. Of course not. Those people deal with information professionally, don't they? The superhuman communities are mostly information that's nice to know but not essential. There's not much truly valuable intel. They know how precious that information is, so they don't spread it around."

"I get the gist. So low-grade freelancers use it. Doesn't interest me much. If I need information, I can just trade with professionals. That's more reliable and saves time."

"Normally, that kind of information costs an astronomical amount. Only the ultra-wealthy like you can afford it."

"Says the imperial noble. I don't think you're in any position to talk."

"I may hold the title of Archduke, but it's merely a rank bestowed upon talented imperials. The benefits amount to nothing more than a monthly dignity allowance from the imperial household and receiving deference from nobles. My personal wealth isn't particularly substantial."

"A Grade 7 doesn't have money?"

I asked back, unable to comprehend.

Once you reach Grade 7, there are countless ways to make money. Just lending your name to a decent corporation earns you a considerable sum every month.

Having no money doesn't make sense. The military status? At Grade 7, even the military can't treat you like an ordinary soldier. If you take on side work, they'll look the other way. Even if you engage in corruption, unless it's egregiously serious, they'll turn a blind eye.

"I tend to spend quite a lot. The problem is there aren't many channels for money to come in. I'm doing this and that, but it's not enough to accumulate wealth."

"...Are you asking me to slip you some money?"

"What? Ahahaha! I came to find you because I was curious about your identity. I didn't come to get money. Above all, I cannot accept money without compensation or money obtained through unjust means. That's the contract I made."

Contract.

The power used in that shitty truth game was also Marit's ability—Contract.

I felt my curiosity surge.

"Is that Contract ability similar to the curse magic, Geas? If you don't want to talk about it, you don't have to."

"Geas? I've heard of it. A vision magic that grants you something in exchange for imposing a restriction on yourself. Curious about my ability? If you give me 100 million credits, I could give you a rough idea."

"Should I give you 100 million credits right now?"

"That's a joke. But if you help me eliminate Haukal, I can tell you. This isn't a joke."

"That won't work as a trade. I'm going to kill Haukal too. As Leon Schneider, I cannot abandon my family's revenge."

Even if they aren't my real family, those bastards laid hands on me.

I can't just sit back and do nothing. The moment word spreads that Leon Schneider gave up on his family's revenge, my reputation will be shredded and every two-bit bastard will come crawling to take a shot at me.

More than anything, even if I gave up, Haukal would eventually come to kill me.

"That's exactly why I'm proposing this. So that you don't kill all of Haukal by yourself. Good things should be shared, shouldn't they?"

Marit smiles.

A thick stench of blood seemed to waft from her mouth.

Hatred born from parents being murdered by savages? No. I don't sense anything like that. What's in those eyes is clearly greed and pleasure.

"...You look remarkably young. How old are you?"

"My age? Twenty-five. In my prime."

Grade 7 at twenty-five.

Not that I'm one to talk, but she was a genius. Of course, you couldn't say she reached Grade 7 so quickly purely through talent alone. There's something else.

I had a feeling. Even if I refused Marit's proposal, she wouldn't stop. There's no telling what kind of variable she might introduce. So it would be better to move together.

"I accept your proposal."

"Ahaha. Good. To think I'd become colleagues with the Superconductor Mage. Even if it's temporary, it's quite reassuring."

"Your ability, Contract?"

"Right. Like the Geas magic you mentioned, I can make contracts with myself. I receive the price of that contract. What I said earlier about not accepting money without compensation or unjust money is also part of my self-contracts."

She used her ability, Contract, like [Geas].

'She gained power in exchange for restricting herself?'

Grade 7 at the age of twenty-five.

Marit's body seemed to be bound tightly by chains called contracts.

'It doesn't seem like she can only make one self-contract. At minimum dozens. Perhaps she's restricting herself with hundreds of contracts.'

I clicked my tongue softly.

She really wasn't an ordinary woman. You'd have to be twisted somewhere to do something that insane.

"In some ways, it's not just similar to Geas but also to the savages' oaths."

Savages make oaths when they come of age, and gain power by keeping those oaths for life. It's information you can easily find on the internet.

Marit's brows furrowed immediately. For the first time, she glared at me.

"How dare you compare my self-contracts to the savages' bullshit? Their oaths are worthless. Starting from meaningless vows like not eating dog meat for life, to laughable oaths like never forgetting their parents' names. Comparing my contracts to oaths is an insult to me."

"You're right. My apologies. I didn't know any better."

"I'll let it slide once. There won't be a second time."

"By the way, do you eat meat? Among the restrictions that come to mind, I'd say diet is one of the more useful ones."

"Of course I don't. I eat eggs and dairy. I gained physical abilities in exchange for a lifetime dietary restriction."

Marit said proudly, clenching her fist. Kkwaaaang. A powerful wave of force emanates from her clenched fist.

"...What happens if you break a self-contract? If you end up eating meat without knowing it?"

"You die."

A resolute expression and voice.

'Even so, you die just from eating a bit of meat? Isn't the penalty too excessive?'

Marit soon smirks.

"That's a joke. You don't die. The contract is broken and I lose the power. As recoil from the contract's destruction, I'd need at least dozens of days of recovery. What you probably want to ask is what happens if I'm forced to eat meat, or eat it without realizing. Ah, for reference, the fact that I don't eat meat and still maintain this excellent figure is also the power of the contract. Whether I eat a lot or a little, this body stays fixed, and I don't age either. Aren't you jealous?"

I ignored Marit's added remark.

Though my gaze did briefly drift toward her ample chest and rear.

Gaining immortality through contracts is interesting, though.

"...If my question is burdensome, you don't have to answer."

"What matters in self-contracts is the person's will. Unless it's my mistake or intention, the self-contract doesn't take effect. Same for contract violations. If I'm forced to eat—if I clearly recognize it and am forced to eat—the contract is forcibly destroyed. Conversely, if I don't recognize it and someone secretly feeds me meat, it's not grounds for violation. The contract isn't broken."

"Are you sure you should be telling me the weakness of your ability so easily?"

Marit smiles meaningfully.

"It's fine. I'm only telling you right now."

An invisible force coils around my existence.

I knew it was a contract.

The contract's terms: I will not reveal Marit's ability to anyone by any means. If I fail to keep the contract, I will pay the price.

"...I never said I'd make a contract."

"Didn't you say you'd accept my proposal?"

"I never heard the price."

"You should read contracts carefully. It's your fault for not asking."

My mind races.

At a glance, Marit's ability, Contract, is a complex power. But complex as it is, it has rules. Unilaterally forcing something on the other party would be impossible.

'If the ability is the contract itself, then the target's consent is required.'

Marit proposed to tell me about her ability. Though there were hidden clauses, she presented the contract. And I accepted the proposal without even asking about hidden clauses.

'That's illegal. No, by that logic, self-contracts are illegal too. Bringing up the law against an ability is laughable anyway.'

Thankfully, the contract terms weren't serious.

"What a headache-inducing ability. And... your energy has gotten slightly stronger than before. Did you grow stronger by revealing your ability to others?"

"Oh. Remarkable senses. Just as you said. There's a self-contract for that."

"If you spread your ability through broadcast, you'd become absurdly strong."

"Unfortunately, it's not that convenient. The effect varies greatly depending on the level of the person who learns my ability. Grade 4 and below are practically meaningless; only Grade 5 and above matter. Even if everyone in the world learned my ability, I'd lose more than I'd gain."

"I see. I understand that your ability is unusual. It's about time we started planning how to deal with Haukal."

"Hm? I haven't told you everything about my ability yet."

"You don't need to detail every self-contract. I roughly understand how you use contracts."

"That's not it—I haven't finished. My contracts fall into three major categories. One is self-contract. Another is free contract. The truth game I played with you falls under that. And the last one is compensated contract."

Compensated contract.

It might sound complicated, but it's roughly a contract where both parties pay a price. You could call it a transaction where both sides buy and sell something of each other's.

"Since it's an ability, you're not buying and selling ordinary things, I assume."

"Your abilities and power. In your case, it could be magic. Grade 7 magic is quite tempting... Would you consider selling to me? Ah, your magical ability won't disappear. You'll just receive a slight penalty."

Marit opened her palm. A single contract appeared in the air above her palm. The contract created by her ability slowly burned away. In its place, a fist-sized sphere of light appeared.

Light. A Grade 1 spell, cast not by a mage but by Marit.

"This is Light, a Grade 1 spell I purchased for 300,000 credits. I bought it ten years ago as a test... I've never used it until now. If I need light, I can just use a flashlight. Before that, my eyesight was good enough that I rarely needed light itself."

"So you purchase magic with money as payment, and the mage sells magic by receiving a penalty. Is there no limit? Can you trade Grade 7 magic without issue?"

"Hmm. Up to Grade 6 magic was no problem. I've never traded Grade 7 magic, so I'm not sure. But trading other Grade 7 abilities was fine. Ah, the penalties vary somewhat. You can't use the sold magic for a certain period, its efficiency drops, or your mana rod becomes deactivated."

"...It's surprising that abilities other than magic are possible. Surely not innate abilities too?"

"It's possible."

Marit grinned, as if showing off her ability.

"Though the price or penalty is rather steep."

"...It seems the other party can't set the price arbitrarily?"

"A minimum is set. If you propose a price that doesn't match the value to some degree, the contract itself won't form. The minimum for a Grade 7 innate ability was about 5 billion credits."

"What was the other party's penalty?"

"That person couldn't use their innate ability for half a year."

"What a lunatic. Selling an innate ability for a mere 5 billion credits. I can't even imagine how desperate for money he was."

Half a year, so what, you might ask? Absolutely not. A Grade 7 who can't use their innate ability is nothing more than half a person. Moreover, an innate ability is the source of one's pride and beyond. It expresses everything you are. Letting someone else use it? Just imagining it brings a horrifying sense of revulsion.

"It wasn't just about money. That person had other debts. Other Grade 7 superhumans wouldn't sell their innate abilities even for 10 billion."

"That price. Does it have to be paid in money?"

"What matters is value. Money is the most intuitive representation of value. Labor, or my body... Ah, could it be you want my body?"

"I was just asking. Why would you think that?"

"Don't you remember when we played the truth game earlier? Contrary to appearances, you seem quite lustful. If you sell me your innate ability, I could give you my chastity."

"Is that a joke too?"

"I'm serious, though?"

Marit didn't avoid my eyes. Her red eyes held not a single tremor.

"You'd sell chastity you've kept for twenty-five years just like that? Like a whore?"

"If I could use someone else's innate ability even once, it'd be worth more than expected. You're surprisingly romantic. Valuing chastity, I mean."

"It's surprising you've kept your chastity this long with those values."

"This body isn't a bitch. If someone had offered a proper price, I'd have sold it already. From the start, none of the people I've met have coveted me. And you're the strange one, obsessing over sexual desire."

The higher one's level, the more physical desires fade. Even at Grade 5, managing sexual desire is no task at all. Sexual desire is, from the start, the easiest of physical urges to control. Sleep and hunger are far harder to master. Because you die if you don't eat, and you die if you don't sleep.

"You have sexual desire too, don't you."

"Unlike you, I raised my level through trickery using my ability. It's hard to perfectly control my urges. No, I'm at the level of an ordinary person, so there's no need to. Anyway, if you sell your innate ability, I'll give you my chastity. A body that's ripened to its peak, no?"

Marit crossed her arms and twisted her body slightly.

She seems to think she's being seductive. It's hopelessly clumsy. What's irritating is that it works. Her beauty is exceptional, so even a little coquettishness is attractive.

'Even if I can't use my innate ability for half a year, I have magic. On the other hand, the woman before me might never be met again if I miss this chance. ...Isn't this worth trading for?'

After calculating seriously, I shook my head.

"...I'd be at a loss."

"Hmm. Do you know that innate abilities differ from person to person? If you tell me what your innate ability is, I'll try to match a price for it."

"An innate ability is valuable information in itself. If I go to an information broker and sell it, I could earn at least 300 million credits."

"I'll contract not to reveal it to anyone else. And I'll tell you my innate ability too. The penalty will be my life. How about it?"

"Not bad. You go first."
