The next morning.

"I can't head up to Seoul right now. I've got a mountain of things to do as a Hunter. There are over five dungeons I need to clear."

Jin Seyoung said, emerging from the shower. She dried her soaked hair using her wind-manipulation ability.

"That many?"

"Gyeongsang Province is bigger than Gyeonggi. Naturally, more dungeons spawn there."

Dungeon clearing is a Hunter's livelihood. Knowing how much an A-rank dungeon pays out, I couldn't argue against her. Not that I wanted to. She takes pride in her work.

Stepping out onto the street, I gazed at Mount Gara where the meteor had fallen. The lockdown had been lifted as if yesterday's events had been a lie.

'It wasn't a meteor—it was something like a spacecraft with alien life lying dormant inside.'

Where those bastards came from was obvious.

The sky.

They must have come from a dungeon that opened in orbit above that azure sky.

'They've already fallen to Earth once. They'll come a second, third time too.'

There's a reason the World Hunter Association designated these things as an invasive species.

'They're weaker than the Phiron. But they're on par with A-rank monsters. I killed them easily because it's me—but for an ordinary A-rank Hunter... they'd struggle. And among the Hunters gathered yesterday, A-ranks were few.'

A-rank Hunters aren't a dime a dozen.

'Thinking there are disgusting things above my head puts me in a foul mood. Like cockroaches nesting on the ceiling, I suppose.'

The thought was horrifying.

Not that I could abandon my home because of cockroaches.

I pulled out my smartphone and contacted Yura.

"It's me."

-I know. You handled the Xenorages that came out of Mount Gara yesterday. The Association is grateful. We're discussing the reward money too. Probably around 300 million?

"Forget about that. Is the World Hunter Association's request still valid?"

-Did you change your mind? Let me check first. It hasn't been many days, so it should still be valid.

Yura hung up.

She called me back fifteen minutes later.

-Confirmed. None of the S-rank Hunters stepped forward, so the World Hunter Association was preparing to handle it themselves. They're thrilled you're taking the job.

"Good thing I'm not working for free. But how are we getting up to orbit? Are they launching a spaceship?"

-Just come up to Seoul first. The World Hunter Association seems urgent—they've already sent someone.

I traveled to Seoul on a cloud. Since there was a chance eyes were on me, I deliberately avoided using a spatial teleportation scroll. It was only a ten-minute trip anyway.

• • •

Yura and I moved to somewhere in America via a teleporter belonging to the World Hunter Association.

It was a sprawling wasteland, and already dozens of Association personnel had gathered. At their center stood the Association President, James Craft.

A middle-aged gentleman with salt-and-pepper hair and a beard approached me with a hearty chuckle.

"It's been a while, Hunter Sung Yujin. Thanks to you taking this job, I can finally breathe easy."

"Didn't expect the Association President to be here. Were you planning to go yourself? Did I steal your job?"

"Haha. Please, steal it all you like. For certain reasons, I avoid fieldwork. There are too many things I must protect. I was going to step in myself this time if unavoidable... but thanks to you, that's no longer necessary. I'm always grateful for your dedication to humanity."

"How are we getting to orbit?"

"We'll use a ship. Do you see that vessel over there?"

He pointed. There was a ship sitting on the ground. A sailing vessel with masts.

The fact that a ship was here at all was absurd, but the state of it was even more ridiculous. The sails were torn to shreds, and the hull was battered and broken in places.

"Don't tell me we're riding that thing to orbit."

"Correct. It may look like this, but it's a ghost ship layered with all manner of magic. Because it exists yet doesn't exist, it can partially ignore the laws of physics. It will deliver you safely to orbit. And back to Earth as well, of course. While aboard that ghost ship, you can breathe and feel gravity even in space."

"So it's a badass piece of equipment. What intel do we have on the dungeon in orbit?"

James Craft let out a small sigh.

"What opened in orbit is an erosion-type dungeon. We call it a dimensional erosion. Originally, that area is a space where cosmic debris drifts... but due to the dungeon's influence, the debris has clumped together and become fixed. In a way, you could say that's fortunate. Better than things that keep drifting."

"What happens if the worst-case scenario occurs?"

"Are you asking what happens if you fail to clear the erosion-type dungeon?"

"If that ghost ship blows up."

"I can't say the possibility is... entirely nonexistent. It's an erosion-type dungeon, not a normal one. In that case, you'll use the emergency supplies we've prepared to survive in space. We'll come retrieve you using another ghost ship. At that point, I'll act personally."

"I have an inventory, so surviving isn't a problem. As for not being able to breathe... well, I'll just bring an air tank."

A spacesuit?

Didn't even cross my mind to pack one.

I'm an S-rank Hunter—did they really think I couldn't adapt to outer space? Even if I couldn't live there forever, I was confident I could survive at least three hours in a vacuum.

"Can I board that ghost ship right now?"

"Specialists are inspecting it. They say it'll take about three hours... You can rest comfortably in the meantime, Hunter Sung. If you'd like, I can have a teleporter take you into the city."

"Three hours is nothing. I'll wait."

I entered the prepared tent and picked up my smartphone. With a smartphone, killing time is a piece of cake.

Three hours later.

I boarded the ghost ship.

Drrrrrrrk.

The wheel-shaped helm turned on its own, and the ghost ship rose into the air. It would sail along a predetermined route without any need for me to steer.

The ghost ship began flying toward the sky. At first it was slow, but it gradually accelerated, ascending faster than an airplane.

Aboard the vessel, I felt a cool breeze. The ghost ship's protective functions activated properly, giving the sensation of sailing across the sea.

In less than thirty minutes, the ghost ship reached low orbit. Far in the distance, a massive object floated in orbit.

It hung in the void. A dark purple membrane held cosmic debris together, and atop it, things resembling the meteor I'd seen from the ground jutted out everywhere.

Alien creatures over five meters tall moved across its surface, ceaselessly laying eggs roughly thirty centimeters in size. Just the visible eggs numbered over three thousand.

'An erosion-type dungeon. They've set up a hatchery in orbit.'

It felt disgusting—like aliens had set up a multiplayer spawn point in Earth's front yard.

I grabbed the ghost ship's helm and locked it in place. The ship stopped in front of the hatchery as if dropping an invisible anchor.

Woooooong.

Reinforcing my body with mana and protecting myself with a defensive aura, I stepped down onto the hatchery.

Tak.

'There's gravity. No—more importantly, I heard a sound just now?'

Even if low orbit has some atmosphere, hearing sound this clearly makes no sense.

I carefully released the defensive aura protecting my body. I could breathe the air just like on Earth. Gravity was similar to Earth's too.

"Ha. The erosion-type dungeon even altered the environment of outer space."

From my perspective, it was convenient—easier to move around.

I moved my feet to smash the erosion-type dungeon core.

"KIIIIIIIIIIIIIIEK!"

Xenorage.

Somehow the aliens detected the intruder and came swarming out from everywhere.

'Were they hiding under the floor or wedged into gaps in the cosmic debris?'

I knew from yesterday's meteor—these things had a talent for shrinking their bodies and squeezing into tight spaces.

Watching the enemies swarm, I pulled Hwaryeon Bido and Stormbreaker from my inventory.

Sword in right hand.

Spear in left hand.

I swung both simultaneously.

Thunder Heaven Style—Thunder Flash.

Two lightning-wreathed slashes extended simultaneously, cutting down enemies. But not all were swept away. Some of them, displaying exceptional reflexes, dodged the slashes and charged in.

I had no intention of retreating.

Wrapping lightning qi around my entire body and my spear, I charged forward.

Thunder Heaven Style—Spear Form—Mighty Quake.

I collided head-on with the charging Xenorages, piercing through and bursting them apart.

But their numbers didn't decrease. They increased. Not just runners—even flying ones swooped toward me.

'Each one is on par with an A-rank monster, and dozens are charging at once.'

The one saving grace was that each individual had similar physical capabilities. And that they lacked the special abilities most A-rank monsters possess.

'Feels like fighting an entire army.'

Thunder Heaven Style—Spear Form—Wave Source.

I swung Stormbreaker, wreathed in lightning, in a wide arc. A shockwave spread out, grinding down the charging enemies. Their blue blood and flesh splattered across the ground.

Unlike the speed-focused Thunder Heaven swordsmanship, the Spear Form was relatively slow but delivered heavy, powerful blows—a type of area attack. Thanks to that, it was effective against large numbers.

My footsteps, which had been advancing while killing enemies, stopped.

Some distance away, there was a being draped in black cloth.
