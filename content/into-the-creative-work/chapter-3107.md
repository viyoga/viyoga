I looked at the tablet.

A camera installed high up in Busan was showing the sea in real time.

It wasn't as if I'd suddenly wanted to see the ocean, of course.

I zoomed in on part of the screen with my index and middle fingers.

When I magnified a tiny dot-like section, the Japanese archipelago came into view. Korea and Japan had drawn close enough that you could see the Japanese islands from Busan. Even accounting for the fact that this camera was the latest model installed at a high elevation, it was still absurd.

'An impossible tectonic shift. At least they said landmasses won't collide. How far apart did they say they'd stop?'

The distance between Korea and Japan would become as close as the Dover Strait between France and England.

Knock knock.

A knock sounded at the door.

"Your Excellency. It's Lee Yeonhee, Director of the National Intelligence Agency."

Normally, when someone came to see me, the secretariat would give advance notice. The NIA Director was no exception. In other words, this was an unofficial visit—Lee Yeonhee had come on urgent business. She'd probably used her spatial teleportation ability to come directly.

"Come in."

Lee Yeonhee entered quietly and got straight to the point.

"Your Excellency. Japan's naval fleet has begun assembling. Their destination is Fukuoka."

The seas had gone to hell, but Japan's navy was still intact. Korea, on the other hand, had steadily reduced its navy. At this point, Korea barely had one. The warships they'd had were dismantled and used as resources. Having given up on naval power, investment was concentrated entirely on the air force.

"No matter how big a ship is now, the moment it gets far from land, it's just a target for sea monsters and other mutants. The Japanese government knew that and still maintained their navy... There's only one use for it. They're planning to gather their warships and bombard Busan."

It was inefficient. Dismantling the warships and building long-range missiles would be far more effective.

"Japan's mechanical engineering technology can be considered stagnant at this point. They likely lacked the technology and time to repurpose the warships. And Japan has a strong sense of pride regarding its navy. Even without knowing the details, there may have been internal pushback against dismantling it."

Of course, Japan wasn't foolish either—they wouldn't have been investing in their navy.

After this war ended, Japan's warships would all be decommissioned and their navy dissolved. They were probably already calculating the acquisition of Korea's mechanical engineering technology into their plans.

'Using nuclear missiles to precisely target only their warships... it's possible, but the problem is that it would trigger a nuclear war.'

I was confident I could win even if a nuclear war broke out.

The problem was the aftermath. Land, people—everything would be fucked. What good was winning a war if the land you'd rule was contaminated and there were no people left to rule?

'And those Japanese bastards will 100% use nukes if the war drags on and they're cornered.'

Japan and Korea had possessed the technology to build nuclear weapons for a long time. Now, just as Korea did, Japan would have nukes in its arsenal.

'There's the missile interception system Dr. Na built, but this world has espers as a variable.'

If a spatial teleporter threw a nuke and vanished, there was no answer to that.

'A short decisive war is the answer. No matter how long it takes, I need to end this within a month. Before they launch their nukes.'

I looked at the map on my office desk.

A map showing only the unified Korean peninsula and the Japanese archipelago. There were locations marked in red on both the peninsula and Japan. Military bases. Of course, Japan's military bases couldn't be 100% accurate due to variables in intelligence gathering.

"Did you say Korea's current missile stockpile is about a week's worth?"

"That's correct, Your Excellency. Precisely 35,128 rounds."

Originally, it hadn't even been 10,000. This was the result of shifting to a wartime footing six months ago and pushing missile production to the extreme.

Of course, that was missiles only—separate from other munitions.

"Deploy 10,000 immediately. 3,000 for Japan's warships. The remaining 7,000 target Japan's military bases. Ah, and send about 1,000 at Japan's major cities as a bonus. Pay a courtesy call to the Japanese cabinet with bunker busters while we're at it."

"We'll be firing missiles all day long, then. However, unless Japan's air defense system is neutralized, there's a chance 90% of missiles targeting military bases will be intercepted."

"Doesn't matter. They'll concentrate their defenses on military bases, so the warships' defenses will thin out. I just need to neutralize the warships. It's not that I particularly fear Japan's warships, but if that's their pride, then we'll smash it to pieces."

"...When should we begin? I'll draft the declaration of war and deliver it to the Japanese cabinet myself."

"Japan loves analog. Write it by hand on paper and deliver it by drone. As for the timing... just before we fire the missiles would be good."

Lee Yeonhee's eyes widened, her mouth falling slightly open in surprise.

"As expected of Your Excellency. That lack of hesitation, that bold decisiveness—I can only admire it. I'll carry out your orders. When exactly should we launch the missiles?"

"When most of Japan's warships have gathered."

"That would be around midnight, then. Understood."

Lee Yeonhee bowed and withdrew.

All war preparations were complete. We could begin right now if needed, and the detailed operational plans had already been drawn up within the military. The moment war began, every unit would move simultaneously according to plan. Defensive units would hold their positions, and offensive units would strike Japan.

'Japan has probably been preparing for a Korea-Japan war from the start. They'll have several other means besides the clones.'

It would be a difficult war.

But if we won, occupying and governing Japan would be easy. The current Japanese populace felt discontent and anxiety toward their own country, which was constantly battered by natural disasters. Even if they resisted to the end, it would be meaningless.

• • •

Close to midnight.

Japan's warships had gathered in the waters near Fukuoka. The ships didn't stray far from land. The monsters in the deep sea didn't come near the coast.

Todoki, the Maritime Self-Defense Force's Chief of Staff, put a cigarette in his mouth. He and the rest of the MSDF personnel knew that after this operation, the Maritime Self-Defense Force would be dissolved. They couldn't argue against the Japanese cabinet's decision. They knew better than anyone that a navy unable to venture into open waters was useless.

"So this is the flow of the times... If this is the last, I'll go out in a blaze of glory."

Once all warships had assembled, they would depart for Busan, Korea. By the time they reached Busan, the Prime Minister would be reading the declaration of war, and by then the warships would have Busan blockaded. After that, they just needed to ferry the army to Busan.

Normally, it was unthinkable for the Chief of Staff to personally come to the field and command warships. But this time, he'd received special permission from the Prime Minister. After all, it would be the Maritime Self-Defense Force's final operation.

"Chief Todoki! Radar is picking up missile signatures! Korean missiles are incoming!"

Todoki's eyes gleamed.

It seemed Korea had made the first move.

He wasn't flustered. He already knew that missiles were Korea's primary method of warfare. An interception system had been prepared for this. Not only the ships—the interception systems installed along Japan's coastline were activating.

"Intercept them."

The air defense system activated.

Todoki looked at the night sky and fell silent for a moment. Hundreds of missiles were cutting through the sky, propellant blazing. Interceptor missiles flew out and detonated the incoming missiles in the air. He'd hoped the explosions would trigger a chain reaction, but Korea wasn't ignorant of that either. There was no chain reaction.

'Splendid and majestic, like fireworks.'

Some of the hundreds of missiles flew toward the Japanese mainland, while others plunged into warships and exploded.

'Those damn bastards at the Defense Equipment Agency. They said they could intercept 95% of missiles... It's 70% at best!'

The interception wasn't going well.

The reason was simple. A technological gap. Korea had a Technologist, and Japan didn't—that was all it came down to.

"To all soldiers. All forces, administer Oboro X!"

Oboro X was a combat drug modified by Japan. It was safer than Vister and lasted longer.

Todoki also administered Oboro X. His heart began pounding vigorously, and his vision sharpened. The faint fear and guilt lurking in a corner of his mind vanished.

"To all fleets. We set sail! Even if your ship burns, do not stop! We must open the path to Korea!"

At his command, the fleet began to move.

The missiles didn't miss the moving warships. Missiles that hadn't been intercepted collided with warships and exploded. No warship sank instantly, but with missiles raining down continuously, they slowly went under.

Todoki let out a hollow laugh.

'Can't even put up a proper fight. How futile.'

There was no fear.

Only the regret of being unable to do a single thing.

Soon, the warship he was aboard also sank.

Todoki was lucky. He didn't die on the sinking ship and managed to swim to shore. It was partly thanks to the combat drug that enhanced his physique.

However, what greeted him on the shore were the corpses of his subordinates and a 3-meter-tall robot. It walked on eight legs like a spider, and its upper body was equipped with eight mechanical arms. Six arms were mounted with weapons, and two arms had blades attached.

On one side of its metal body was a taegeuk symbol, and seaweed hung from various parts, with barnacles clinging to it.

"...Don't tell me. It infiltrated by walking along the seafloor?"

A multi-legged combat robot manufactured in Korea. The Land Spider's sensors glowed red.

To conserve ammunition, the Land Spider charged at enemies and swung its blades. Soldiers too far away were shot with guns. The accuracy was astonishingly high.

The Land Spider also spotted MSDF Chief Todoki. The AI knew Todoki's identity, but it didn't stop its slaughter. Kill the enemy. An absolute command the AI must obey.

The Land Spider's eight legs moved.

Far faster than a human sprinting at full speed.

Todoki drew his pistol and fired at the Land Spider. It was useless. Only minor scratches appeared on the Land Spider's metal body.

That was when it happened.

A massive sphere of flesh flew in from the mainland and collided with the Land Spider. The Land Spider rolled across the ground before quickly righting itself.

The sphere of flesh uncurled its coiled body. It was a mutant resembling a gorilla, wrapped in tough hide and muscle.

"...So this is the combat artificial mutant the Defense Ministry was secretly researching."

The combat machine and the combat organism began to fight.

Metal legs were torn off, bullets embedded in abdomens, fingers severed—yet neither stopped.

Weapon of war and weapon of war tangled together.

Todoki's voice trembled as he watched.

"...This is what they call war?"

Bang!

A single round fired from the Land Spider's gun barrel as it fought the mutant pierced clean through the forehead of the dazed Todoki.

The Land Spider's AI, having killed the enemy it could eliminate with maximum efficiency, continued its battle with the mutant.
