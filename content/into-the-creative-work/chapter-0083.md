I stood at the front entrance of the Plune Knights training grounds.

"I've come to see Sir Embis. Guide me."

I gave him the exact same line as before.

The squire's back bent even further.

'He's almost on my side. I didn't expect it to be this effective so quickly, though.'

What I had overlooked was that entertainment didn't exist in this world. TVs obviously didn't exist, and even literature was limited.

Commoners were too busy trying to make a living to be interested in literature. Even if they were interested, they would quickly get bored. This was because most of the books in this world were for knowledge, not for fun.

The most common forms of entertainment in this world were plays and circuses.

'Should I sell novels at this opportunity...? No, that's too much of a hassle.'

Books didn't just pop into existence, and how many commoners would actually buy books with expensive paper? More than anything, I would have to translate the text into this world's language. The profit wouldn't seem to be that great compared to the amount of work involved.

Moreover, most commoners were illiterate.

"Um... Young Master Eugene-nim."

The squire, who was walking ahead, spoke to me.

"Yeah?"

"Could I possibly buy some cigarettes now?"

I chuckled. The entire Plune Knights were smoking cigarettes, so the squire must have gotten a taste for them too.

"Of course. You know the price, right?"

There was no reason not to sell them if he was going to buy them himself.

"Hehe. Of course."

The squire took out one black silver coin from his pocket. It was worth 100,000 Neru.

"You're buying two packs at once?"

"Is that not allowed?"

"No, it is. Don't you need a lighter?"

"N-no, I'm okay."

I nodded. Well, it wasn't hard to get a fire.

"Here. Take it."

"Thank you!"

"But what's your monthly salary?"

"1 million Neru!"

I nodded.

It was definitely a small income compared to the average commoner. However, considering that he was a squire, it wasn't that small. Squires lived in the training ground dormitory as attendants of the knights, so they didn't need to spend money on living expenses.

'If you become a formal knight from a squire, you can receive treatment that can't even be compared to what you get now.'

I looked at the squire, who was putting the cigarettes in his pocket and looking happy. This guy would also become one of my main customers in the future.
