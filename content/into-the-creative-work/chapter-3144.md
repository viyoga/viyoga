Wolfgang, the leader of the Wolfgang Mercenary Corps, climbed the mountain with his subordinates.

With a longsword at his hip and black leather armor hugging his frame, his stride was unhurried. His platinum-blond hair was slicked back, sunglasses perched on his nose, and he mentally reviewed the operation.

'Nora Hanger. To think I'd run into a top-tier Treasure Hunter. One good shake-down and I'm set for life.'

When he'd stumbled across traces of Nora Hanger's movements, he'd felt good enough to scream with joy.

'Nora Hanger is Grade 7, but she's not a combat specialist. Me and my crew cornering her is child's play. I don't plan to kill her. I'll offer a deal. There's a dungeon here too, so we clear it while we're at it. With an expert like Nora Hanger helping, clearing the dungeon is nothing.'

The reason for not killing Nora Hanger was simple. Their goal was Nora's wealth, not Nora's life itself. Killing her outright would yield almost nothing.

Wolfgang arrived at the mountainside with twenty subordinates in tow.

What he saw there was a scene he hadn't anticipated in the slightest.

A blood-soaked clearing in the mountains.

Standing atop the torn corpses of his team members was the team leader, Grak. Grak's body was drenched in blood. As if he were the one who'd caused this carnage.

Electricity crackled and sparked across Grak's body. Even a cyborg wouldn't have current jumping from every inch of their body like that. His eyes were stranger still. Blue luminescence poured out in streams, like flames. Most striking of all was the long needle embedded in his temple. Brain-lightning sparked ceaselessly from that elongated needle.

"Captain! Grak's condition is abnormal!"

"You little shit. I can see that too. It's probably that needle thing causing it. I don't know what it is, but Nora Hanger, that bitch, must've pulled some trick. I'll subdue Grak. You lot will just get in the way, so watch."

Perhaps he'd heard the conversation. Grak, who'd been standing still, snapped his head around.

Kkiririk! Kkrik!

Every time Grak's neck and limbs moved unnaturally, a horrifying friction sound rang out. Like an old puppet being forced to move without any oiling.

Grak aimed his gun and pulled the trigger.

Tutatatatatatatata!

Wolfgang drew his longsword from his hip and deflected the incoming bullets. Through the hail of gunfire, he could see Grak grabbing a dagger with his other hand.

'No reason left, but his combat habits are intact.'

Wiiiiing.

The sound of Grak's implant drawing on mana for power rings out. Even with electricity sparking across his entire body, the implant remains functional.

The bullets ran out. Grak threw the gun to the ground and charged. Wolfgang charged at Grak as well. The distance between them closed in one second. Longsword and dagger crossed.

Kwaaaaaang!

The one pushed back in the shockwave was Wolfgang.

'Even if that wasn't full power, I'm the one getting pushed back? I'm Grade 7?'

Pshuuuuuk!

Compressed smoke bursts from Grak's implant. A classic overclock symptom. A technique that surpasses the implant's performance limits at the cost of drastically devouring its lifespan.

Wolfgang focused harder on the fight, elevating his technique. But subduing Grak wasn't easy. Grak's combat technique was at a higher level than usual.

'Did his combat power rise in exchange for losing his mind?'

Suddenly, the implant installed in Grak's flank clicks open. He'd fired a hidden miniature missile.

Unique skill: Reflector.

Wolfgang activated his unique skill. A skill that reflects any attack that enters within 10cm of him, once recognized.

The miniature missile's trajectory reversed as if that had been its original direction, flying back at Grak.

Peong!

Grak, caught in the explosion, was sent flying backward.

"This bastard is actually trying to kill me? Unacceptable. Grak, you're expelled from the Wolfgang Mercenary Corps!"

Wolfgang unleashed his full power for an instant. He closed the distance to Grak in a flash, thrust his longsword through his chest, then slashed across his waist. Even as his upper body was about to fall away, Grak swung his dagger at Wolfgang.

Once again, Wolfgang's unique skill activated. The dagger's direction bent backward. Wolfgang drove his longsword into Grak's forehead and split his head open. More than half the brain was sliced through. Yet Grak's upper body, collapsed on the ground, kept writhing without stopping.

"Fuck. Even zombies die when their brains are sliced. Why won't you die?"

The long needle embedded in his temple catches his eye.

'That needle is suspicious. Should I pull it out?'

Just as Wolfgang reached out his hand. The long needle, which had been faintly glowing blue, vanished. Grak's movements stopped. Melted brain matter streamed down from Grak's head.

"...The overclock earlier too, it seems like it uses things to their limit then discards them. It wasn't simply a mental ability dominating the body. Fuck. What the hell is it. Was there an ability like this? Necromancy, curses? No, it didn't feel like that..."

An incomprehensible power.

It left a thoroughly unpleasant aftertaste.

As Wolfgang stood there brooding, his subordinates approached.

"Captain. Isn't this Grak's head going haywire from the Dark Moon's influence? Even machines go bad in the Dark Moon."

"Even if Grak is a cyborg, his head was fine. Even under the Dark Moon's influence, this is beyond reason. If you don't know shit, shut up. Don't advertise your ignorance."

"No... I was just saying something, that's all... So are we going to just sit here?"

"No. We're going into the dungeon. Some dungeons have exits in different places. If we wait here and Nora Hanger comes out somewhere else? Grak's team just died for nothing. We go in and catch Nora Hanger. We'll squeeze that fucking bitch for Grak's share on top of everything else."

Wolfgang entered the dungeon with his subordinates.

Having entered the dungeon with Nora, I reflexively scanned my surroundings.

A large red moon hung in the dark sky. Far larger than a normal moon. As if the moon, which should have been distant, had stepped right up close.

Below it, rows of traditional Chinese buildings with curved roof tiles lined the street. Wind lanterns hung throughout the buildings, illuminating the surroundings. Countless buildings, yet not a single sign of life could be felt.

Looking closely, some of the wind lanterns were machine-made street lamps. Under the eaves, atop the railings, camera-like devices were mounted.

'Traditional on the surface only. This kind of scenery... I've seen it in the original game. This setting is one of the cities of True China. The rewards from this dungeon should be at least decent.'

I turned my head to look at Nora. She'd been quiet for a while, and sure enough, she was crouched at the dungeon entrance, hands moving busily.

"What are you doing?"

"Setting traps. The moment they enter the dungeon—boom! We can kill one or two."

"...You'd go that far?"

"I have to go this far. You haven't explored many dungeons, so you wouldn't know. What's more annoying than monsters in a dungeon is people blinded by greed. Especially the Wolfgang Mercenary Corps—they won't give up over just that much. We need to feed them a little bait."

Coming from an expert, it seemed reasonable.

I thought about going over to prepare a magic trap or something, then decided against it. We're still at the dungeon's entrance. I need to conserve mana as much as possible.

"Done."

Nora stood up. She turned not toward the wide street visible ahead, but toward the small alleys between buildings.

"Setting aside the wide street, aren't you going to check inside the buildings? There might be treasure. Even if not treasure, there could be useful items."

"There are. But usually those things don't sell for much. Only people with weird tastes buy them. Dungeon exploration is my specialty, so you just follow me."

Confidence overflowed from Nora's words and actions.

I shut my mouth and followed behind her. Because I knew the source of her overflowing confidence.

Nora's unique skill: Pathfinding.

The ability to find the path leading to a desired destination. In the original game, it's an ability that marks various things on the minimap. No real combat effect, but in terms of convenience alone, it could be called the best unique skill.

"You said this was the third time too. Were the dungeon interiors the same for the first and second?"

"It's a dungeon influenced by the Dark Moon. The theme is similar, but the details differ somewhat."

"Why do you need a mage? So far it doesn't seem like you particularly need me."

"Wait a moment. You'll need a mage's help soon enough."

Nora stopped in front of a building. It was a butcher shop where, instead of pork, human limbs and entrails hung from the ceiling and walls.

"We need to go inside here, but we can't get in. Most tricks I can handle myself, you know? But this one's hard with just my power."

Nora reached her hand toward the building's door. It was blocked by an invisible force.

"A barrier."

"That's right."

"At this level, couldn't you destroy it with force?"

"I call these things puzzles, you see? If you break them with force instead of clearing the puzzle, there's more than a fifty percent chance you won't get the reward. You can dispel a barrier of this level, right?"

"Barriers aren't my specialty, but... this isn't a particularly difficult one."

I reached my hand toward the barrier.

The moment I touched the barrier, information about it was formulated into a formula and flowed into my mind.

'A barrier with some immortal arts mixed in. Is this what they call a formation? Fortunately, the fundamentals of the barrier are similar. Even if it weren't me, a mage of Grade 5 or above could dispel this barrier without much difficulty. It would take some time, though.'

Through my mana rod, I processed the formula matching the barrier.

Wooong.

The barrier shook once, then disappeared. The smell of blood that had been blocked by the barrier rushed out in a gust.

"...Five seconds flat. The Grade 7 mage I hired before took about two minutes. This is embarrassing to even compare."

"That mercenary mage must have been poor at barriers."

"That's true. He was a mercenary, after all. He said himself that combat magic was his specialty. He ended up dying, though."

Nora descended underground via the stairs. In the center of the wide underground space lay a black coffin. Talismans were plastered all over its surface.

Without hesitation, Nora approached the coffin and drove a silver stake she'd pulled from her subspace into it.

Deolkeong!

The coffin shifted once, but that was all. Soon the coffin went still.

"...I didn't sense any living presence from the coffin. An undead?"

"A jiangshi. The jiangshi that come out of this dungeon are at least Grade 5, so fighting them is a hassle. It's easier to take them out before they rise like this. But didn't I tell you about the dungeon?"

"I couldn't hear the details for security reasons."

"Ah. Right."

She picked up one of the jars placed in the corner of the wall and showed it to me.

"Look at this. Jackpot."

"That jar?"

"There's a fetish inside. A straw doll. This should be Grade 5 level. Curse practitioners would drool over it. It'll probably go for 30 million credits."

"...I haven't done anything and I've earned 30 million credits?"

"That's the charm of dungeon exploration."

Maybe I should try being a Treasure Hunter with an alt character too. I seriously considered it.
