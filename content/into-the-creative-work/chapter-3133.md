Before the photon cannon could touch Reger's body, his hair turned white. Lekreu—the innate ability of the bastard Marit had spoken of. Its effect was complete evasion.

'He used his innate ability. Even if it's a simple effect, an innate ability is still an innate ability. There must be a usage limit. Or perhaps he can't use it consecutively.'

The photon cannon touched Reger's body. No matter how sturdy the flesh of a Rank 7 warrior might be, taking a photon cannon head-on should have inflicted a fatal wound. Yet there was no explosion. The photon cannon suddenly appeared behind his back and fell to the floor. Reger's hair returned to black.

'He didn't dodge by moving himself. There's no trace of time being accelerated or rewound. And if space had been warped, I would have noticed.'

By process of elimination, what remained was causality.

The highest-order concept—one that intervenes in cause and effect.

'Is he manipulating the result out of cause and effect, selecting only the outcome where he inevitably evades?'

It wasn't an entirely absurd innate ability. He wasn't manipulating causality at will—it could only influence evasion.

'Innate abilities are inherently absurd techniques tied to one's existence. Which is exactly why the individual variation between them is so extreme.'

While he was briefly lost in thought, Reger closed the distance.

He fired another photon cannon. This one could be fired in rapid succession. Though it devoured mana at a monstrous rate.

Faced with the follow-up photon cannon, Reger's expression twisted. Unlike before, he didn't use his innate ability—he twisted his body midair. His waist bent to its absolute limit as he desperately tried to dodge the photon cannon.

'So he can't use his innate ability consecutively.'

The photon cannon grazed his upper body. That alone charred his right side black. But it wasn't fatal. At best, it only made him stagger.

Seizing that small opening, he threw a button.

[Matter Transmutation: Basilisk Venom]

The button transformed into basilisk venom. Black-green smoke billowed up like an explosion.

"This body is not one to wither from mere poison!"

Reger openly swallowed the basilisk venom. Blood vessels writhed across his skin, but they quickly settled. Yet the poison wasn't entirely ineffective. Reger's movements grew slightly duller.

He aimed his right arm at him again. Reger flinched and dodged sideways, then furrowed his brow. The photon cannon hadn't fired.

"You bastard…!"

"The photon cannon consumes too much mana, you see."

[Biological Transmutation]

His right arm changed.

Clack! Clack!

Not a photon cannon, but a laser barrel. Less power, but greater efficiency.

The laser rounds fired without much noise. Reger raised both arms to shield his body and tried to break through.

"If I can just close the distance! If I can just close the distance, it's my victory!!"

He charged in, screaming.

He was right.

What a mage or alchemist can do in close combat is limited. The same goes for combat mages and combat alchemists. Before you can aim the gun called magic and pull the trigger, a blade comes in and stabs you—you're done for.

"Come at me if you can."

He threw dozens of buttons from his coat pocket at Reger.

Reger flinched when he saw the buttons scattering through the air. He knew the buttons could become bombs, so he instinctively shrank back.

But the buttons didn't transform—they clattered to the floor.

"Logically speaking, no matter how outstanding an alchemist I am, there's no way I could turn dozens of buttons into bombs all at once."

"You dare…!!"

Boom.

Reger kicked off the ground and closed in with explosive propulsion.

[Matter Transmutation]

He activated alchemy.

The dozens of plastic buttons scattered on the floor became mithril.

Was it animal instinct? Even though he couldn't have fully grasped the situation, Reger activated his innate ability. His hair turned pure white.

[Form Transmutation]

The dozens of buttons elongated like spears, trying to pierce Reger's body. Even without any spell formula imbued, the material was mithril. Even a Rank 7 powerhouse couldn't ignore it.

Reger stopped in front of me. He was in a kicking stance. The blade on his foot halted 3 centimeters from my body. His hair returned to black. The dozens of mithril spears hadn't pierced his body in the slightest, but he was now in a state where he couldn't move.

"So he treated dozens of attacks as a single attack and evaded. Quite a convenient innate ability."

"God damn it…!"

Reger put strength into his entire body. His poisoned body tried to bulk up, muscles swelling—just for an instant.

[Form Transmutation]

The dozens of mithril spears became chains, coiling around his body. He struggled, but there was no escaping the tautly drawn chains.

Reger gritted his teeth.

"How is this possible? As far as I know, you spellcasters shouldn't be able to cast spells so recklessly like this!"

"Right. Logically speaking, that's true. But there's a famous saying. Don't fight a mage in a mage's workshop."

"You're saying this place is your workshop!"

"When people hear 'workshop,' they tend to picture a small, musty workroom. To a mage, a workshop is an environment tailored to the mage and a combat fortress. How many years do you think I've spent in this prison? Did you really think I'd just leave this prison alone all that time?"

He exhaled deeply.

His astral sense expanded, and he could feel the countless magic circles carved throughout the prison.

From the ceiling, walls, all the way to the underground. There wasn't a single one that wasn't connected to him. The burden he bore during combat—over seventy percent was handled by the workshop. When he calculated a spell formula, the workshop assisted. When he used mana, the workshop replenished it.

"You scheming bastard…! If I'd known you were Rank 7…!"

Reger extended his foot toward me as if he hadn't given up to the very end. But it never reached my body. The dozens of mithril chains binding Reger were connected to the workshop, perfectly suppressing his strength.

I reached out and placed my hand on his head.

[Biological Transmutation]

Blood vessels burst through and protruded from his body. His blood vessels were sharp as metal.

"Kuaaaaaaagh!"

"Charging head-on into the prison was your arrogance. If you'd known the warden was an alchemist, you should have brought a bomb to blow up the building."

Reger's limbs were torn apart. A massive amount of blood poured out. Dark green blood contaminated by basilisk venom.

A fatal wound that would have killed any ordinary human—but a body that had reached the realm of a transcendent clung stubbornly to life.

"Aaaagh! Hn, hnhnhn. A memory from not long ago comes to mind. Your parents and your wife… They begged for their lives to the very end. Your wife, while being violated by me, said she'd do anything. When I asked if she could kill you, she said she would without hesitation."

"Ah, is that so. So it was you. Should I say you saved me the trouble?"

"Acting calm won't help. The fact that I killed your family doesn't change. And you'll die the same way soon enough. Haukal does not let enemies and their families live!"

"I understand. I have no intention of doing so either. You killed my family, so I'll find and kill yours. I may not look it, but I'm called the Alchemist of Cruelty. I won't let them die peacefully. Ah, you barbarian tribes consider comrades as family, don't you? How fortunate—you have so many families."

"You bastard…!"

"We'll talk later."

He flicked his finger.

The mithril chains moved, wrapping Reger's body without a single gap.

Having temporarily sealed Reger, I looked around.

The front gate was in shambles from the battle. Some troops were laid out, but the majority had minor wounds. No dead or critically injured. The corpses strewn about belonged to the barbarians, and quite a few barbarians had been taken alive.

In the center, I spotted Marit dusting off her fists. The blood smeared on her knuckles didn't come off easily.

When our eyes met, she trotted over with quick steps.

"It's been a while since I fought bare-handed, so quite a few got injured."

"…They didn't die, so it's fine. Can you interrogate the barbarians with your ability?"

"You mean Truth Game? Unfortunately, it's not a lie detector that unilaterally extracts information. To get information, I have to give information too. As I said before, my contract depends on will—if the other party has no will to contract, no will to live, then even forcing a contract yields almost nothing."

"How do you normally get information from barbarians, then?"

"Hm? Torture, obviously. That's more reliable than using an uncertain lie detector. Their love for family? That's just pretty words. Even barbarians, when pushed to extreme circumstances, forget about family and everything else. Barbarians who genuinely sacrifice themselves for family are extremely rare. And that applies to non-barbarians too, for that matter."

"Good. There are plenty of torture specialists here. These bastards will spit out every piece of information they know."

• • •

A week passed.

The barbarians, excluding Reger, couldn't last a day before spilling information. But there was nothing meaningful.

Reger endured torture for a week. Ordinary torture couldn't open his mouth. Reaching Rank 7 meant not just the body but the mind becoming that of a transcendent. His mental fortitude itself was on another level from ordinary people.

So they brought his direct family and tortured them in front of him. His siblings, his wife, his children.

How did they find them? Another barbarian couldn't endure the torture and talked.

The most important piece of information obtained that way:

"Haukal's leader is preparing to reach Rank 8."
