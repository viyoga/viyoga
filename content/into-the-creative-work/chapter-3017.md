The next day.

I habitually checked my phone and emails.

Perhaps because of the trouble I'd gone through summoning the top ten guild masters yesterday to set them straight, the usual flood of messages had dropped by more than half.

'Word must have gotten around about what I did yesterday. Any Association employee with half a brain would've caught on by now.'

That had been the intention, honestly. It made my life easier.

'As long as it doesn't reach the general public, I'm fine.'

I checked the internet. There was a fair amount of news about me. Expected, given the emergence of the youngest S-Rank Hunter. But there was nothing beyond that. As if someone had intervened.

'The government, or the World Hunter Association. Excessive attention just draws flies anyway, so it's not bad.'

What if my personality became public knowledge?

'Then there's nothing to be done about it. I'll just crush every bastard that comes at me.'

I'd figured it out from Yura's reaction yesterday. The World Hunter Association would tolerate or even assist me no matter how much chaos I caused.

I was killing time as usual, eating a late lunch, when the doorbell rang. I checked—it was Yura.

She let herself in like she owned the place and pulled a 500ml plastic bottle from the fridge.

It was the Water of Heaven. A bottle of perfectly ordinary water that cost 1 point. Its only distinguishing feature was that it tasted remarkably good for water.

"This water really is strange. It tastes like normal water. Why is it so good?"

I couldn't answer even if she asked. I didn't know either. Just in case, I'd commissioned a lab to analyze its composition. It was ordinary water. Nothing special about its mineral content.

"Where'd you get this water, anyway?"

"Mm. A dungeon?"

Yura stared at me like I'd said something absurd, but soon lost interest and savored the water, drinking it slowly.

"If you boil ramen with this water, or make coffee with it, it's incredibly good. Green tea's not bad either."

"I bet. Still, my preference is this clean water."

"Did you come because of yesterday?"

"Yesterday. You're not wrong. Because of yesterday, Hwang Cheonseong contacted the World Hunter Association saying he wants to meet you."

"Hwang Cheonseong."

"You know him, right? The S-Rank Hunter, Hwang Cheonseong—'Mountain Breaker.'"

"How could I not? Especially because of that nickname—he's been a laughingstock on the internet."

Hwang Cheonseong, the bankrupt! Hwang Cheonseong, the man who should never touch stocks! Childish, sure, but that's what the internet is.

But that was ancient history. In present-day Korea, his existence was barely a whisper. A figure you'd only encounter in textbooks, really.

'Nearly twenty years without a single official activity.'

His trajectory was completely different from Ha Heonsin, the 'Monster God' and chairman of Sejin Group. Ha Heonsin still made appearances now and then to remind the world he existed.

"The great-great-great-grandmaster of the Goryeo Guild wants to meet me. Hwang Chiseung. That little shit must've run crying to him after getting his ass handed to him yesterday."

"That's really the only way to read the situation."

"An S-Rank Hunter who supposedly shattered a mountain in his prime. Let's see if he's still got it."

I clenched my fist.

My condition? Why even ask. It's peak.

"I already told you, I have no intention of being hostile toward you. And Hwang Cheonseong is in his nineties. Are you really going to fight a ninety-year-old?"

"I'm the kind of man who can swing at an old man without hesitation."

“…….”

Yura stared at me in disbelief, then took a drink of water.

After that, I headed to where Hwang Cheonseong was, together with Yura.

Having some retired old bastard summon me like a dog pissed me off, but on reflection, it beat having the old coot come to my house.

Hwang Cheonseong's location was a nameless mountain far from Seoul.

"Did he build a villa on a mountain or something?"

I grumbled, hands on the wheel. Yura, in the passenger seat, had reclined it all the way back and was half-lying down, fiddling with her smartphone.

"It's not publicly known, but about thirty years ago, Hwang Cheonseong suddenly bought a mountain and holed himself up there."

"He became a hermit? The man who shattered a mountain, feeling guilty toward mountains or something?"

"That's a plausible theory."

I got bored of driving.

I parked the car in a suitable spot and got out.

"Let's fly."

"...I can't fly."

I wrapped one arm around Yura's waist and pulled her in. She let out a sigh and gave up resisting. I lifted her and rose into the sky. 'Leaping' would be more accurate than 'flying.'

"Which direction?"

"That way."

I ran in the direction Yura pointed. I didn't use the Lightning God technique. Yura wouldn't be able to handle it.

At some point, I was running without Yura's directions. Hwang Cheonseong's presence had registered on my senses.

Hwang Cheonseong was living halfway up a mountain. An old house with a poorly maintained yard.

The kind of dwelling people typically imagine when they think of a hermit.

For the residence of a retired S-Rank Hunter, it was remarkably shabby.

'Did his family abandon him here to die, or what?'

Even a retired S-Rank Hunter abandoned by his family wouldn't live like this. He might not be at his prime, but he'd still have enough power that even a decent A-Rank Hunter wouldn't stand a chance against him.

"Hohoho. I didn't expect you to come running across the sky. Welcome. I am Hwang Cheonseong."

Hwang Cheonseong looked like a man in his sixties with a white beard. He wore comfortable hiking clothes. Aside from the long white beard reaching down to his chest, he looked like any old man you'd pass on the street. If he'd dressed properly, you'd believe he was a Taoist sage.

"Sung Yujin. Why did you want to meet me?"

"An impatient one, aren't you. I wanted to apologize. A true apology should be delivered in person to the offended party, shouldn't it?"

"Shouldn't an apology be made by coming to me?"

"There are quite a few who'd be wary of me leaving this mountain. Among them, the Tiger would be the first to come looking."

The Tiger?

Were there wild tigers in this day and age? I understood he wasn't talking about an actual tiger.

Hell Tiger Lee Yeonsuk. A woman who retired from being an S-Rank Hunter and now works for the government.

"You don't get along with the government?"

"Let's talk inside. Ah, and the woman beside you—is she a ninja? Her aura is distinctly ninja-like."

Yura, who hadn't flinched even in front of the Association Chairman while smoking, had gone rigid. She was clearly on guard against Hwang Cheonseong.

"...I'm Yura. I'm affiliated with the World Hunter Association and serve as an aide to Hunter Sung Yujin."

"Don't be so wary. This isn't the old days. The era of killing ninjas on sight ended long ago."

“…….”

What era was he talking about? The Japanese colonial period? Given his age, that would make sense, but... something felt off.

'Was there some historical event I don't know about?'

Curiosity flared for a moment, but I snuffed it out immediately.

'Who'd want to hear an old man's stories about the past?'

I followed Hwang Cheonseong into the old house. Despite its crumbling exterior, the interior was clean. Not only that, it was filled with the latest appliances.

"What the. So you're not a hermit?"

"A modern man should enjoy modern conveniences, shouldn't he?"

"For someone who says that, the outside of your house is on the verge of collapse."

"That complements the yard better. In other words, it's aesthetic."

I sat at the table. He poured tea from an electric pot. Looked like it was brewed from herbs he'd gathered himself. It tasted healthy. After one sip, I decided not to bring it to my lips a second time.

"Seems it doesn't suit your palate."

"Old man. I didn't come here to leisurely drink tea with you. If you're going to attack, attack."

"Hohoho. As I said, my purpose is apology. If you and I fought, one of us would almost certainly meet the Grim Reaper. And it would be me."

"Right. So I've received your apology—can I leave now?"

"I'm well aware that words alone are the least trustworthy form of apology. My nephew's grandson, Chiseung, will step down from his position as Goryeo Guild Master as of today."

"Hwang Chiseung is resigning? Over just this?"

"It's not 'just this.' The Master of the Goryeo Guild earned the displeasure of an S-Rank Hunter. Normally, even crippling him wouldn't be enough, but despite appearances, he is my blood. I'll bow my head like this, so please, for my sake, let it go."

The retired S-Rank Hunter rose from his chair and bowed to me. The white crown of his head was visible. I stared at him for a long moment, then nodded.

"I accept the apology. I wasn't planning to kill him anyway."

"Thank you."

"From my perspective, I don't understand bowing your head like that."

"Young man. Do you know what S-Rank Hunters were called fifty years ago?"

"Never learned it in university either. What were they called?"

"Gods among men. There was a time when no one could hold an S-Rank Hunter accountable for any crime. Even for murder, everyone stayed silent. The silence itself was too busy watching the S-Rank Hunter's mood. And my nephew's grandson committed a discourtesy against such an S-Rank Hunter. Fifty years ago, it wouldn't have been strange for our entire family to be wiped out."

S-Rank Hunters were also called calamities.

Why are calamities called calamities? Because humans cannot oppose them.

The military? Unless it's the army of a great power, stopping an S-Rank Hunter is difficult. Not that an S-Rank Hunter would be foolish enough to fight a military head-on in the first place.

The only somewhat efficient way to deal with an S-Rank Hunter is to throw hundreds, thousands of A-Rank Hunters at them.

Using nuclear weapons is mutual suicide.

"This isn't fifty years ago."

"No, it isn't. And you are stronger than I was fifty years ago."

“…….”

"My physical abilities have declined with age, but my senses have grown sharper. Sharp enough to feel a measure of your power. I can tell because I was an S-Rank Hunter like you. Everyone around you must seem worthless. Things you could sweep aside whenever you felt like it. Weak creatures, yet so full of fear they try to tie a bell around the cat's neck—rats, all of them..."

I felt killing intent in his voice. He had a lot pent up. Seeing Yura flinch beside me, I released my own aura.

My presence devoured and suppressed Hwang Cheonseong's killing intent in an instant.

"That's enough."

"My... I've made a mistake. When I dwell on old memories, my emotions sometimes get the better of me."

"Now I understand why the government keeps you under surveillance. This place is a kind of exile, isn't it."

"You could say that."

"Business is done. Yura. Let's go back."

Yura and I rose from our seats.

"Wait. I have a gift prepared. I can't send a distinguished guest away empty-handed. It's something that doesn't even compare to Moonlight Grass, so please accept it."

He held out a small, luxurious box. Inside was a single golden pill.

"What is this?"

"The Golden Sun Pill. A pill made from the secret recipe of the Heavenly Lord Hwang family. Immense yang energy sleeps within it. For S-Ranks like you and me, it's meaningless, but... for Hunters of A-Rank or below, it's a treasure beyond compare. If an ordinary person takes it, they'll live long and free of illness."

"Now that I focus, I can feel the yang energy."

As he said, for an S-Rank Hunter it was nothing more than a health tonic.

I picked up the Golden Sun Pill and forced it into Yura's mouth. Her eyes went wide.

"Mmph?!"

"Eat it. You suffer because of the yin energy from the things sealed inside your body. This will help."

"Young lady. You're fortunate. To swallow the Golden Sun Pill. The moment it touched your lips, spitting it out would've turned it to garbage."

Yura, who'd been flustered, quickly regained her composure and swallowed the Golden Sun Pill.
