<p>Luis had built his life on being invisible and honest.</p>

<p>For 6 month and 4 days, his world consisted of spreadsheets that never balanced on the first try, petty cash envelopes that always came up short, and the particular hell of chasing department heads for receipts they’d &quot;definitely left on your desk yesterday.&quot;</p>

<p>The other junior accountants had a nickname for their workspace: &quot;the Dungeon.&quot; No windows, bad air, and stacks of paperwork that never seemed to shrink.</p>

<p>Luis had developed a careful routine - at 5:58 PM, he’d save his files twice (just in case), straighten his desk, and watch the clock hands crawl toward 6:00.</p>

<p>Home wasn’t entirely an escape either. It was just a little better than this office.</p>

<p>&quot;Luis.&quot;</p>

<p>The sudden voice made him jerk, elbow bumping the little bamboo plant his wife had bought him for good luck. Director Liam stood over his desk, his sharp suit casting a shadow across his keyboard.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re... reliable.&quot; Liam said it like it wasn’t quite a compliment.</p>

<p>He tapped the cubicle wall with well-groomed nails. &quot;I have something to do outside. You’re covering Finance today.&quot;</p>

<p>Hearing this Luis mouth went dry. The Finance desk. Real money. Real responsibility. The stamp that could approve six-figure payments with one thump of authority.</p>

<p>&quot;Just me?&quot; he asked, trying to sound calm.</p>

<p>Liam smirked, &quot;Yes, Don’t screw it up.&quot;</p>

<p>He walked off before Luis could reply.</p>

<p>Two hours later</p>

<p>Without even looking at him, she slapped a piece of paper down on his desk.</p>

<p>Luis picked it up. His eyes widened at the amount - enough money to pay his salary for years. It even had the &quot;pre-approved&quot; stamp.</p>
