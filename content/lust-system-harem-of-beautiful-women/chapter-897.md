<p>Mira rolled her eyes. &quot;Sharing one man? Yeah, right. Isn’t Nancy married?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I know that Hans Bar is actually run by her husband, Daniel.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And I know Deewa and Nancy’s personalities—if they accepted that, it’d be scarier than a ghost story.&quot;</p>

<p>Chloe laughed. &quot;You’ve done your research. Yes, Deewa runs the company now, but Nancy is my man’s sister-in-law. He invested because of her.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Ah...&quot;</p>

<p>Mira was stunned, staring at Luis. &quot;He’s married?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Exactly!&quot;</p>

<p>Chloe said casually. &quot;She’s a great person. I actually get along better with her than with Nancy. She’s Nancy’s younger sister. I’ll introduce you sometime.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Anyway, we’re here to talk business—why are we gossiping?&quot;</p>

<p>Mira was truly shocked. Her mind was buzzing, wondering if she’d misheard.</p>

<p>If it were Deewa, who wasn’t well-off, or Nancy, who came from an average background, becoming mistresses for money wouldn’t have surprised her.</p>

<p>Even if the guy was handsome, Chloe’s family had always been well-off—not filthy rich, but comfortably above average.</p>

<p>Nancy and Deewa were proud because of their own attributes, but Chloe actually had real backing.</p>

<p>Her own family, her parents, her siblings—all of them held official positions.</p>

<p>Her career as a flight attendant was practically a family disgrace. Under those circumstances, what did money matter? If she wanted to ruin someone, she could.</p>

<p>Chloe as a mistress—Mira could barely believe it. If anyone had seen the real world, it would be Chloe.</p>

<p>Chloe was also tactful. Some things should stay hidden. They weren’t close enough to share everything, so she kept certain secrets to herself.</p>

<p>Seeing Mira’s shock, Luis cleared his throat. &quot;Ms. Mira, shall we get down to business?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Of course!&quot;</p>

<p>Mira snapped back to reality. &quot;I’d like to cooperate with Blossom Media. My entire studio would merge in.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Revenue would follow the company’s bylaws, with the same split as Nancy’s...&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;My main and side accounts would all be folded in, in exchange for a reasonable stake. That’s what we should be negotiating.&quot;</p>

<p>That wasn’t unreasonable—as long as her equity demands weren’t excessive.</p>

<p>But even with no business experience, Luis spotted the issue. &quot;We can discuss that later. But right now, the ’Top Creator’ competition doesn’t seem to involve you anymore.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Whether it’s Iron Ox or your rivalry with Nancy, it hasn’t escalated to that point.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Even without his cooperation, you could run your own team and do fine. So why come to us now?&quot;</p>

<p>Luis looked at her calmly. &quot;Your feud with Iron Ox is your business. If you were sincere, you’d talk to Deewa directly. Coming through me suggests a lack of sincerity.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Or maybe you chose Chloe not because she’s easy to talk to or your best friend, but because she’s your most likely way in.&quot;</p>

<p>Chloe realized he was right and her expression darkened with a hint of anger.</p>

<p>Mira’s expression didn’t change. She said calmly, &quot;I came to Chloe because we’re close and can communicate properly. It’s not as complicated as you think.&quot;</p>

<p>Luis smiled. &quot;Fair enough. We’ll discuss it and get back to you.&quot;</p>

<p>After the meal, Luis dropped Chloe and her baby off at home, then called his sister-in-law Nancy.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m at Daniel’s hometown!&quot;</p>

<p>Nancy said sweetly. &quot;Do you want to come over?&quot;</p>

<p>Luis took a taxi there. He had to admit, the estate the Zester family had built in the countryside—using collective and buying extra land—was quite impressive.</p>

<p>Four neat villas, spacious and bright courtyards, quiet and surrounded by hills and water—just as nice as any city villa.</p>

<p>The door was opened by Brook, wearing a house dress, glasses, and her hair pinned up—a vision of wifely seduction.</p>

<p>&quot;Is your husband home?&quot;</p>

<p>Luis noticed the security cameras and behaved himself, though a lecherous grin still crept onto his face.</p>

<p>&quot;He’s out playing cards. I came with Nancy.&quot;</p>

<p>Brook’s eyes also held a hint of spring. &quot;But my father-in-law and the others are home, so don’t try anything.&quot;</p>

<p>Next to her villa was a prefab structure—a used RV shell that actually looked quite stylish in the yard.</p>

<p>&quot;Brother-in-law Daniel, come have some tea.&quot;</p>

<p>At the prefab’s entrance, Brook’s father-in-law was chatting with Nancy. On an intricately carved stone root table sat elegant tea sets, exuding a refined aesthetic.</p>

<p>Luis sat down without ceremony and said warmly, &quot;Uncle, what were you two discussing?&quot;</p>

<p>Brook sat down too, sipping tea and smiling. &quot;A while ago, Nancy said she wanted to order a batch of ordinary jade pieces for a few hundred dollars each.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It just so happens my father-in-law recently took on two carving apprentices. She’s here to pick up the finished pieces.&quot;</p>

<p>Luis walked into the prefab with interest. It was filled with jade-carving machines, lined up in rows.</p>

<p>Two middle-aged women in dust masks were working at a table, the machines emitting a harsh screech and the acrid smell of stone dust.</p>

<p>Piles of jade fragments—of all colors and sizes—were stacked like mountains. Before polishing, they looked like construction waste.</p>

<p>Both apprentices were in wheelchairs—disabled. This kind of work suited them well.</p>

<p>When they noticed someone enter, they looked up briefly then resumed working, wearing protective eyewear.</p>

<p>Luis stepped back outside. Brook’s father-in-law brought out a table and laid out the finished jade pieces.</p>

<p>Jewelry is fascinating—even if you’re not an expert, you can instinctively tell when something is expensive.</p>

<p>Cheap pieces are just as obvious. It seems to be a natural human instinct.</p>

<p>The pieces on the table were varied in style—safety buckles, plain plaques, all with simple carvings and mixed colors. Cheap stuff, about twenty pieces in total.</p>

<p>&quot;Uncle, I really appreciate this!&quot;</p>

<p>Nancy said cheerfully as she admired them.</p>

<p>She took out a red envelope with ten thousand dollars and said apologetically, &quot;You have to take this, or I’ll feel too embarrassed to come back.&quot;</p>

<p>Brook’s father-in-law didn’t refuse. He casually set the envelope aside and said with a smile, &quot;We’re family. You’re being too polite. Fine, I won’t argue with you.&quot;</p>

<p>That worked out to less than five hundred dollars per piece—very cheap indeed.</p>

<p>Luis added, &quot;Uncle, even between brothers, accounts should be clear. You should at least cover your costs—this is too cheap.&quot;</p>

<p>Her father-in-law poured tea and chuckled. &quot;I’m still making a profit.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;These are leftover scraps from years ago. Carving them myself isn’t worth the effort, and throwing them away felt wasteful, so they’ve just been collecting dust.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Since Nancy needed them, and an old buddy of mine heads the local association, I took on these two apprentices to give them practice.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It’s enough to cover their wages and a small red envelope.&quot;</p>

<p>Brook brought over a bag of jewelry boxes.</p>

<p>She sat beside them and packed the pieces with practiced ease, tying them with fine gold thread—instant class. No one would believe these were under five hundred dollars each.</p>

<p>&quot;The carving is still a bit rough.&quot;</p>

<p>Her father-in-law was still critical. He called out to the prefab, &quot;Take your time and do it right. In the old days, with work like this, you’d be too embarrassed to ask for payment.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Got it!&quot;</p>

<p>The two apprentices said in unison.</p>

<p>Nancy still felt bad and brought two bottles of Remy Martin from her car. &quot;Uncle, keep these for yourself.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Deal!&quot;</p>

<p>Her father-in-law didn’t refuse this time. He laughed. &quot;The red envelope will be their wages, and the liquor is your filial gift.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’ll bring you some tea next time.&quot;</p>

<p>Nancy said sweetly.</p>

<p>Riding back to the city in Nancy’s G-Wagon with Brook, Luis learned that Nancy had practically begged the old man to take the ten thousand. And that was for fifty pieces, not twenty.</p>

<p>Luis was surprised. &quot;Wouldn’t that mean your father-in-law is taking a huge loss?&quot;</p>

<p>Brook chuckled and said, &quot;No loss at all. He made his fortune long ago.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;People lose money on things like gambling on rough stones. But my father-in-law buys pre-cut stones and bids in bulk—he’s been at it so long he’s practically a wizard at it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;My mother-in-law said that back then, just the bracelets he cut from the stones already covered the costs and then some.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;The cores of the bracelets were pure profit. Even the scraps with decent color and quality could be turned into simple pendants and plaques—still profit.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;As for this batch, they’re just leftovers that aren’t worth selling but are a shame to throw away.&quot;</p>

<p>Luis said, &quot;Even so, these scraps look pretty nice to me. Places like this would sell them for thousands.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Not that many suckers out there.&quot;</p>
