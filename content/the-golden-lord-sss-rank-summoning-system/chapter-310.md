<p>Ron wasn’t happy with the idea of Goldenveil opening its ports to other merchants, but his unhappiness didn’t matter.</p>
<p>Even Amelia shouldn’t be able to help Ethan create something that big, so Ethan must have an even stronger supporter!</p>
<p>Ron couldn’t afford to lose Ethan as an ally, and he knew full well that the young lord’s backer was even more terrifying than his mother.</p>
<p>If there was one thing that governed merchants as much as gold itself, it was the fear of ending up dead—and without that very gold—for being too curious and greedy.</p>
<p>Being entitled to fifty percent of all surplus production was already a marvelous thing in itself, and he had already won Ethan’s trust anyway.</p>
<p>With a stifled sigh, Ron raised his fist, meeting Ethan’s halfway. Their spatial rings touched with a soft metallic clink, a faint pulse of energy threading between the two artifacts.</p>
<p>He transferred the entire amount at once, drawing a broad smile from the young lord standing beside him.</p>
<p>From that moment on, Goldenveil had a source of income capable of generating millions of gold coins every month.</p>
<p>This, of course, excited Ethan, even though he knew full well that the plans he was drawing up for the future would demand much, much more.</p>
<p>For now, Goldenveil could support itself and still have a fortune left over at the end of each month. But what about when he founded a duchy? Or an entire kingdom?</p>
<p>A large-scale national development plan could cost as much as tens of billions of gold coins, and the Darkwall family itself spent several tens of millions a year just to develop its territory—even though they were merely a family of counts.</p>
<p>For Goldenveil, a centralized state whose power was entirely concentrated in Ethan’s hands, such a sum would be merely a drop in the bucket compared to what he would need to spend in the future to build a functional state from scratch.</p>
<p>"I’ll have my men deliver all the goods directly to your chamber of commerce. Let’s go," Ethan said, giving Ron a gentle pat on the back.</p>
<p>Ron nodded silently.</p>
<p>It pained him to spend such a large portion of the massive investment he’d received from the banks of Solene and the Kingdom of Burning Ice, but he would make every coin count.</p>
<p>Alongside Ethan, he returned to his chamber of commerce, where his staff were already bustling about, settling in throughout the building.</p>
<p>In total, he had hired two hundred people just to work in the chamber’s main building, divided among a private security force, cleaning staff, administrative personnel, array masters, and strategy masters.</p>
<p>At first, there wouldn’t be enough work to keep everyone busy, so Ron instructed them to assist Ethan and Goldenveil itself with whatever was needed in the meantime.</p>
<p>The young lord, naturally, didn’t refuse the offer. A broad smile spread across his face as he said his goodbyes, leaving Ron to attend to his own affairs.</p>
<p>Officially establishing a chamber of commerce was no easy task, and he would need a long meeting with Tom, the current mayor of Goldenveil, to discuss all the legal issues and, of course, tax matters.</p>
<p>Goldenveil operated under a flat tax rate based on income, ranging from 1% to 15% for ordinary citizens and self-employed business owners.</p>
<p>For large companies, the tax bracket was also based on income, ranging from 5% to 17%.</p>
<p>There was also a third level of taxation, created specifically to prevent the kind of corporatism Ethan had seen back on Earth.</p>
<p>In principle, every corporation had the right to make as many billions in profit as it wanted, as long as its top priority was maintaining the well-being of Goldenveil’s population.</p>
<p>Ethan was the only one with the authority to make this classification and, with the stroke of a pen, he could transform any corporation willing to sacrifice the common good for short-term gains into an entity required to pay—also based on income—between 50% and 95% in profit tax.</p>
<p>The only way to escape this tax bracket was to pay a fine equivalent to five times the damage caused to the people of Goldenveil, and to cede an entire year’s worth of the company’s profits to the government.</p>
<p>The other option was, of course, to hand everything over to the Goldenveil government.</p>
<p>Ethan would pay an amount equivalent to one-tenth of the company’s market value and transfer it to more competent leadership, keeping the company private but ensuring its loyalty to the people of Goldenveil.</p>
<p>Ethan simply wanted to make sure that all these companies understood, without a shadow of a doubt, that he was Goldenveil’s protector.</p>
<p>Attacking the people of Goldenveil was, after all, attacking himself.</p>
<p>Choosing to destroy a market in pursuit of quick profits, while ignoring long-term stability and development, was the same as declaring that their own company was no longer welcome in Goldenveil.</p>
<p>The free market was the foundation of Goldenveil, and Ethan would defend it with an iron fist and blood, if necessary.</p>
<p>"Ah... being an authoritarian leader is just too good..." Ethan thought, a satisfied smile curving his lips.</p>
<p>Well, compared to the average leader in that world, he gave his people a great deal of freedom.</p>
<p>In the Kingdom of Burning Ice, for example, even the slightest criticism of the royal family would result in the punishment of having one’s tongue cut out.</p>
<p>In Goldenveil, there was no censorship whatsoever of the media, and even publicly cursing him was permitted without any punishment.</p>
<p>Insults directed at his wives and future children, however, were punishable by fines and even imprisonment, depending on the severity of the case.</p>
<p>Furthermore, experts above the fifth stage, in the rest of the continent, could kill peasants with virtual impunity.</p>
<p>In Goldenveil, that mattered little.</p>
<p>Even for a fifth-stage expert, killing even the most insignificant commoner without valid justification was still a crime punishable by death.</p>
<p>"I don’t want to create a thousand-year dynasty," he declared softly, coming to a stop before the gates of his own palace.</p>
<p>His eyes fixed on the sun already setting on the horizon, tinging the sky with shades of orange and red.</p>
<p>He wanted to create a truly eternal dynasty.</p>
<p>He wanted to be more than just a simple Lord. He wanted to be a...</p>
<p>Ethan’s gaze rested on his palace, bathed in the amber light of late afternoon.</p>
<p>A God-Emperor.</p>
<p>He wanted to be worthy of the title of God-Emperor.</p>
<p>He wanted every citizen of Goldenveil to bow their heads before him, not out of fear, but out of reverence.</p>
<p>That every child would have plenty of food on the table, good teachers, and safe places to play.</p>
<p>That every father and mother would have the peace of mind of knowing that, simply by being born in Goldenveil, their children’s bright future was already guaranteed.</p>
<p>That was why he invested so much in the well-being of the people of Goldenveil.</p>
<p>He wanted to build a paradise capable of making every other empire in history green with envy.</p>
<p>That was his ambition.</p>
<p>"Ah... now, I need to decide what to do with all this gold," Ethan whispered, his lips curving into a thoughtful smile.</p>
<p>Tomorrow a new month would begin, and it would finally be time to build a divine forge and a magical tower for Goldenveil.</p>
