<p>"Ah... fuck this damn forest!"</p>
<p>An irritated voice echoed as a sharp blade carved a path through the low bushes of the eternal forest.</p>
<p>He was one of the twenty guards protecting a merchant caravan.</p>
<p>Behind him, ten enormous beasts advanced through the trees, their heavy footsteps sending tremors through the ground.</p>
<p>They resembled bulls, but far larger, reaching the size of elephants.</p>
<p>Giant black water buffalo. One of the few creatures capable of hauling cargo through the eternal forest.</p>
<p>On their broad, muscular backs, each carried roughly three tons of merchandise. Thirty tons in total.</p>
<p>"You complain too much! It doesn’t even seem like you’re about to receive a payment that would keep you from working for years once we reach the Kingdom of the Burning Ice, hahaha."</p>
<p>A relaxed laugh rolled out from somewhere behind the guard.</p>
<p>It was the caravan leader. A wealthy merchant from the Kingdom of the Burning Ice.</p>
<p>With short black hair and a powerful build, he looked far more like a warrior than a man who made his living behind a pen.</p>
<p>But when it came to anything other than money, he was a coward through and through.</p>
<p>This love for money was precisely what had made him so successful.</p>
<p>He ran crossings from the wealthy city-states of the western plains, threading through the eternal forest to the kingdoms of the continent, where he sold imported delicacies to local elites for small fortunes.</p>
<p>The ideal route would have been by sea, and he was just two crossings away from affording his own boat.</p>
<p>"Sir, there seems to be a settlement here."</p>
<p>The lead guard stopped, stepping out of the treeline into a clearing that stretched nearly two kilometers toward Lake Azur.</p>
<p>"Darkwall or something like that. A miserable place, not worth stopping at. Just keep going." The merchant waved a hand without opening his eyes.</p>
<p>"Well... this isn’t the Darkwall settlement, sir. You should see this for yourself."</p>
<p>The merchant opened his eyes with an irritated expression, already forming a retort, but his lips closed the moment he took in the sight before him.</p>
<p>On one side, a long road led straight to Lake Azur, shimmering faintly in the distance.</p>
<p>On the other, a palisade with fresh repairs stood with its gate wide open, the wood still pale and raw, clearly installed not long ago.</p>
<p>He still wanted to just continue his way. But curiosity had already won.</p>
<p>Patting the thick neck of the giant black water buffalo beneath him, he steered the beast toward the Goldenveil settlement.</p>
<p>The others followed close behind, their massive frames barely clearing the gate.</p>
<p>The people of Goldenveil were not frightened by the enormous creatures. Quite the opposite.</p>
<p>Clutching silver, bronze, and the occasional gold coin, they surged toward the merchant’s employees with eyes burning with excitement.</p>
<p>Others, like Roderik, even seized the moment to sell their own creations.</p>
<p>Merchants in Goldenveil were rare. Very rare. They had to make the most of it.</p>
<p>The merchant dropped from the bull’s head with ease and gestured for his four employees to handle the negotiations.</p>
<p>He had no interest in haggling over trinkets. He wanted the lord of this place, and it didn’t take long to find him.</p>
<p>From the entrance of the lord’s mansion, a young man stepped forward. Golden hair, blue eyes, and a composed bearing.</p>
<p>"Greetings, and thank you for welcoming my humble caravan into your settlement, Lord..." The merchant tilted his head, waiting.</p>
<p>"Ethan Goldenveil." The young lord smiled warmly. "Come inside. I’ll pour you something to drink, and then we can talk business."</p>
<p>"Business! Now that’s what I like to hear, hahahaha." The merchant matched the smile, his excitement already sharpening.</p>
<p>"This humble one’s name is Ron, by the way. I passed through this area a few months ago and the place was in ruins. What did you do to bring it back like this?"</p>
<p>He followed Ethan through the mansion with a curious look, turning thoughts over in his mind.</p>
<p>Perhaps the young lord had unearthed a great treasure. And if he had, would he be willing to part with it?</p>
<p>"I just got a little lucky." Ethan smiled, leading him into a well-kept office.</p>
<p>From a shelf, he retrieved a bottle filled with a strange green liquid, wisps of mist curling from it in the same pale color.</p>
<p>The moment the cork came free, the room filled with a powerful, medicinal aroma, dense and alcoholic, rich enough to make Ron’s aura reserves stir on their own.</p>
<p>He swallowed hard, watching Ethan pour two glasses.</p>
<p>Whatever that drink was, the mana and aura radiating from it were very dense.</p>
<p>"May I, my lord?" Ron asked, reining in the urge to grab the glass and drain it in a single motion.</p>
<p>"Go ahead." Ethan watched with quiet amusement as the merchant brought the glass to his lips and drank it down in greedy gulps.</p>
<p>It was divine.</p>
<p>Clearly brewed with healing spiritual herbs and second-stage mana beast blood, yet the density of mana it carried felt closer to something third-stage.</p>
<p>"What’s it called? And how many bottles do you have?" Ron set the glass down, wiping his mouth with his sleeve.</p>
<p>"The name is Green Fairy Wine. I currently have fifty bottles available, with another fifty already in production."</p>
<p>"Hm..." Ron leaned back slightly, turning the numbers over in silence.</p>
<p>The quantity didn’t disappoint. Wines of that caliber were always sold in small batches, offered exclusively to wealthy nobles at a fortune per bottle.</p>
<p>Fifty of these, placed with the right buyers, and he would have everything he needed for his boat.</p>
<p>"Five gold coins per bottle!" he declared, full of confidence.</p>
<p>Ethan laughed.</p>
<p>"Twenty-five gold coins per bottle. Plus a commitment: in one month, you return here and open a shop in Goldenveil.</p>
<p>Do that, and I’ll supply this wine exclusively to you and no one else, with at least a thousand bottles a year beginning next year."</p>
<p>Ron fell silent after hearing those words, scratching his chin thoughtfully.</p>
