<p>Ross woke up to an empty bed, but he didn’t care. He stretched lazily, enjoying the quiet before getting up.</p>

<p>Rachel was next door, no doubt locked in another heated argument with her husband.</p>

<p>He wasn’t interested in their drama. She’d come back soon enough—she needed something from him, and he knew it. That was enough for now.</p>

<p>He dressed without hurry, buttoning his shirt and adjusting his cuffs before slipping out of the condo.</p>

<p>As the door clicked shut behind him, Rachel came rushing in, breathless, eyes darting around the room.</p>

<p>Her heart sank. &quot;He’s gone,&quot; she muttered, as if saying it aloud would change something.</p>

<p>Jimmy stormed in after her, his earlier fury replaced by cold determination. &quot;Do we go after him?&quot; he asked, his jaw tight.</p>

<p>Rachel turned on him with a glare. &quot;This is all your fault! If you hadn’t wasted time fighting me, arguing, and throwing a tantrum over spilled milk, we wouldn’t be in this mess!&quot;</p>

<p>She checked her watch and groaned. &quot;We lost over thirty minutes because of you.&quot;</p>

<p>Jimmy let out a sharp laugh. &quot;My fault? You’re the one who let the target screw you, and now you’re blaming me? That’s rich, Rachel! Maybe if you actually did your damn job instead of—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Instead of what?&quot; Rachel shot back, stepping closer, her tone daring him to finish.</p>

<p>Jimmy clenched his fists. &quot;You know exactly what. You got yourself fucked and you enjoyed it!&quot;</p>

<p>The tension crackled between them, their argument ready to spiral out of control again.</p>

<p>But the clock was ticking, and Ross was getting farther away.</p>

<p>Rachel would have run after Ross if she thought it would change anything, but there was no need.</p>

<p>She already had him. Instead, she stayed behind, choosing to deal with her husband’s anger for now.</p>

<p>Their argument dragged on, accusations flying back and forth, but Rachel remained unfazed. She had bigger things to worry about.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>A day passed. She spent it waiting, but not idly. She texted Ross a few times, but he ignored her—for now.</p>

<p>That was fine. Rachel knew better than to push too hard. She had her own motives for staying close to him, and patience was key.</p>

<p>Like a leech clinging to its host, she would hold on until she got what she wanted. It was only a matter of time.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, Faith occupied Ross’s attention. She was his latest prize, fresh and untainted by the complications of his world.</p>

<p>Unlike Rachel, Faith had no ulterior motives—at least, not yet. She was still new to him, still learning his ways, still basking in the thrill of his attention.</p>

<p>Ross hadn’t introduced her to his wives yet, and for good reason. Faith had insisted it was too soon, and he agreed. It would be awkward, even for him.</p>

<p>After all, blending one new lover into a household with over a dozen wives wasn’t something that could be done overnight.</p>

<p>That evening, Ross took Faith out on a date. He spoiled her, treating her to an extravagant dinner, whispering sweet nothings that made her blush.</p>

<p>She looked at him like he was the only man in the world, and he enjoyed that.</p>

<p>Later, when they returned to his place, they made love—slow and sweet, like they had all the time in the world.</p>

<p>Days passed, then a week. Ross kept Faith close, indulging her whims, letting her adjust at her own pace. Finally, she took a step forward in their relationship—she introduced him to her family.</p>

<p>It was a significant moment, one that told him she was serious about him.</p>

<p>Ross smiled to himself. Things were falling into place.</p>

<p>They arrived at the Richards’ ancestral home just as the sun was setting, casting long shadows over the sprawling estate.</p>

<p>Ross could tell at a glance that this was an old-money family—everything from the well-manicured gardens to the towering columns of the house spoke of generations of wealth and tradition.</p>

<p>Faith held his hand a little tighter as they stepped inside.</p>

<p>The entire family was waiting in the grand living room. Her parents sat on a plush, antique sofa, her father’s sharp gaze locked on Ross the moment he entered. Her mother, though more composed, regarded him with quiet scrutiny.</p>

<p>Faith’s two older brothers stood by the fireplace, arms crossed, their expressions unreadable. The tension in the room was palpable.</p>

<p>Faith took a deep breath before speaking. &quot;Everyone, this is Ross—my new boyfriend.&quot;</p>

<p>Silence.</p>

<p>Then, chaos.</p>

<p>&quot;What?! What happened to Tristan?&quot; her mother gasped, eyes wide with shock.</p>

<p>&quot;Are you serious right now?&quot; one of her brothers scoffed.</p>

<p>&quot;You do know this guy is already married, right, Faith?&quot; her father said, his tone sharp with disapproval.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re out of your damn mind if you think this is a good idea,&quot; her other brother added, shaking his head.</p>

<p>The barrage of protests continued, voices overlapping, emotions running high. But Faith remained firm. She met each of their gazes with unwavering determination.</p>

<p>&quot;I know what I’m doing,&quot; she said. &quot;I love Ross. My decision is final.&quot;</p>

<p>Her father let out a long, frustrated sigh, rubbing his temples.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re twenty-four now, I get it. You can make your own decisions… but that doesn’t mean we have to like them.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross said nothing, simply watching as Faith fought her battle. He understood this was her moment, her family, and she had to stand her ground.</p>

<p>Despite the tension, dinner was served. The dining hall was just as grand as the rest of the house, with a massive chandelier hanging overhead, casting a warm glow over the long mahogany table.</p>

<p>Plates were passed, wine was poured, and slowly, the mood shifted. The initial hostility cooled into wary curiosity.</p>

<p>Faith’s father drank heavily, his gaze flicking between Ross and his daughter.</p>

<p>Halfway through the meal, he slammed his glass down and leaned forward, his face flushed from alcohol but his tone deadly serious.</p>

<p>&quot;Listen here, boy. If you ever make my daughter cry, I’ll kill you. Do you understand me?&quot;</p>
