<p>Beeep!</p>

<p>The first whistle pierced through the deafening cheers, and just like that, Game One of the finals was underway.</p>

<p>The atmosphere inside the arena was electric—fans packed every seat, waving banners, chanting, and holding their breath in anticipation.</p>

<p>It wasn&#x27;t just any finals match. This was the moment everyone had been waiting for: the beginning of Ross&#x27;s run for his tenth consecutive championship.</p>

<p>The hype was unreal. People weren&#x27;t here just to watch a game—they were here to witness a legend at work.</p>

<p>And with Ross on the court, anything felt possible. His fanboys were out in full force, decked in his jersey, screaming his name like it was a war cry.</p>

<p>The whole arena seemed to throb with expectation.</p>

<p>Ross&#x27;s team won the tip-off, and within seconds, the ball was in his hands.</p>

<p>There was no hesitation, no warm-up. He pulled up from deep and sank a flawless three-pointer on his first touch of the ball.</p>

<p>The crowd exploded. That single shot was like a signal flare—the beginning of the end for the opposing team.</p>

<p>From there, things spiraled fast. Ross was everywhere—driving, dishing, dunking, draining shots from half-court like it was a training drill.</p>

<p>His teammates played well, but Ross played like he was from another planet.</p>

<p>By halftime, the scoreboard read a soul-crushing 80 to 30.</p>

<p>The other team looked shell-shocked, like they&#x27;d wandered into a nightmare they couldn&#x27;t wake from.</p>

<p>It didn&#x27;t feel like a competitive match. It felt like a demolition. An exhibition. A warning.</p>

<p>That&#x27;s what every Ross-led game eventually turned into—a public reminder of how far above everyone else he was.</p>

<p>And through it all, he hadn&#x27;t missed. Not once. Every shot, every move, was flawless.</p>

<p>No one dared challenge him physically, either. Not anymore.</p>

<p>The league had learned the hard way: messing with Ross didn&#x27;t just get you embarrassed—it got you injured.</p>

<p>Seriously injured. He wasn&#x27;t just the best player on the court—he was a walking career-ender.</p>

<p>Elbows, ankles, knees—it didn&#x27;t matter.</p>

<p>If someone came at Ross the wrong way, they ended up on the floor, in the hospital, or worse: out of the league entirely.</p>

<p>The list of players forced into early retirement by crossing paths with him was as long as it was brutal. Some called him a demon.</p>

<p>Others said he was karma in sneakers. But everyone agreed on one thing—when Ross played, you either watched in awe… or prayed you didn&#x27;t end up his next victim.</p>

<p>And this was just Game One.</p>

<p>&quot;This can&#x27;t go on,&quot; Colton muttered under his breath, staggering back to the bench during a timeout.</p>

<p>His vision was blurred with sweat, his breathing ragged, his arms trembling from sheer fatigue.</p>

<p>Every muscle in his body screamed for relief, but the game wasn&#x27;t even close to being over.</p>

<p>Worse yet—Ross didn&#x27;t look like he&#x27;d even started sweating.</p>

<p>Colton had tried everything. Man-to-man. Zone help. Double teams. Triple teams, even.</p>

<p>But it was like trying to cage a shadow. Ross danced around every trap with insulting ease, slipping past defenders with that same casual arrogance he&#x27;d worn since the tip-off.</p>

<p>It wasn&#x27;t just skill—Ross made it look personal, as if humiliating his defenders was part of the show.</p>

<p>No matter what Colton threw at him, Ross had an answer. A spin. A step-back. A no-look pass.</p>

<p>A fadeaway three that barely touched the net.</p>

<p>The guy played like gravity didn&#x27;t apply to him—and it was breaking Colton&#x27;s spirit.</p>

<p>And what could he do about it?</p>

<p>Get physical?</p>

<p>He&#x27;d thought about it. God knows he&#x27;d thought about it.</p>

<p>A well-timed elbow, a hard shove on the drive—anything to rattle Ross and knock him off his rhythm.</p>

<p>But then he remembered what happened to the others. The list was long.</p>

<p>Veterans, enforcers, even rising stars who thought they could play tough guy.</p>

<p>They all ended up the same: broken bodies, shattered careers.</p>

<p>Ross didn&#x27;t just retaliate—he finished you.</p>

<p>One guy tore his ACL trying to take a charge.</p>

<p>Another caught an elbow to the temple and never played again.</p>

<p>Word around the league was clear: you don&#x27;t mess with Ross unless you&#x27;ve already updated your will.</p>

<p>So Colton kept his distance. Physically, anyway.</p>

<p>But mentally?</p>

<p>He still had one card left to play.</p>

<p>If he couldn&#x27;t beat Ross with defense, and he couldn&#x27;t bully him with contact, maybe he could get inside his head. Rattle the cool, cocky superstar.</p>

<p>Push the right buttons. Force a mistake. Maybe—just maybe—make him snap.</p>

<p>The halftime ended. The buzzer sounded.</p>

<p>Ross jogged back onto the court, barely looking winded.</p>

<p>Tap. Tap. Tap.</p>

<p>The ball was back in play. Ross had it again.</p>

<p>Colton squared up, forcing his exhausted legs into a defensive stance, but this time, he didn&#x27;t watch the ball.</p>

<p>He watched Ross&#x27;s eyes. Waiting for his moment.</p>

<p>Then he leaned in.</p>

<p>&quot;Hey, Ross,&quot; Colton said, his voice low but sharp, like a knife pressed against skin.</p>

<p>&quot;I hear some of your wives are stepping out on you.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross didn&#x27;t blink.</p>

<p>&quot;You&#x27;ve got what, thirty? Forty wives now? I guess when you collect women like trading cards, it&#x27;s hard to keep track. Harder still to satisfy all of them.&quot; He gave a theatrical sigh.</p>

<p>&quot;But don&#x27;t worry. I&#x27;m sure those young studs they&#x27;re seeing can pick up the slack.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross dribbled once. Twice. His eyes remained fixed forward.</p>

<p>Colton smirked, encouraged.</p>

<p>&quot;Your knees aren&#x27;t what they used to be, old man. They&#x27;re out there chasing real athletes now. Fresh meat. Strong backs. Guys who don&#x27;t need to ice their joints after one quarter of play.&quot;</p>

<p>He chuckled, trying to sound casual, but underneath, he was bracing himself. He half-expected a blow. A shove. Anything. But Ross just kept dribbling.</p>

<p>&quot;I mean, think about it,&quot; Colton went on, desperate to twist the knife. &quot;Why else would they be so bored? They&#x27;ve seen the legend. They want someone who actually moves in the bedroom.&quot;</p>

<p>Still nothing.</p>

<p>Colton leaned in closer, pushing his luck.</p>

<p>&quot;I bet even now, while you&#x27;re out here showing off, one of them is getting handled by some fresh-out-of-college rookie with a six-pack and no bedtime.&quot;</p>
