<p>But Brandon wasn’t finished. Holding Derek steady, he began to tie him to the chair with thick ropes, each knot pulled tight with meticulous precision.</p>

<p>Derek squirmed weakly, his breath hitching as the ropes bit into his battered skin, but it was no use.</p>

<p>Brandon’s hands worked quickly and expertly, binding Derek so securely that even the faintest attempt at resistance was futile.</p>

<p>&quot;Good work,&quot; Ross said with a faint smile. His eyes flicked to the bed, where crimson stains from Derek’s earlier torment had seeped into the sheets.</p>

<p>&quot;Now, clean this up. I want everything spotless.&quot;</p>

<p>Brandon nodded and immediately got to work. He stripped the blood-soaked linens with practiced ease, bundling them into a pile before wiping down the mattress.</p>

<p>The room itself carried no hint of the earlier chaos, save for Derek’s broken form tied to the chair.</p>

<p>Iris Davies stood at the bathroom sink, the faint hum of the overhead light casting a soft glow across her reflection.</p>

<p>She gently dabbed at her face with a towel, removing the last traces of makeup from yet another event-filled evening.</p>

<p>Tonight, it had been the birthday party of a family friend, one of many celebrations she and her husband Karl attended as part of their highly public lives.</p>

<p>Her hands stilled as she stared into the mirror, her blue eyes shadowed with exhaustion. How many of these parties had she endured over the years?</p>

<p>Hundreds, perhaps thousands.</p>

<p>Once, she had thrived in the glitz and glamour of it all. The thrill of wearing designer gowns, the sparkle of chandeliers, the whispers of admiration as she walked into a room—it had been intoxicating.</p>

<p>But now, at 45, it all felt hollow. The endless small talk, the shallow laughter, the polite smiles—all of it blurred together in an exhausting cycle.</p>

<p>The life she had once dreamed of now felt more like a gilded cage.</p>

<p>&quot;I wanted this,&quot; Iris murmured, her voice barely audible over the soft drip of the faucet. She sighed, running her fingers through her carefully styled hair, loosening it into soft waves.</p>

<p>And it was true—she had chosen this life.</p>

<p>In her youth, she had been the most beautiful girl in town, and men had flocked to her like moths to a flame. But Karl Davies had stood out among them all.</p>

<p>At just 20 years old, he was already the mayor of their city, a rising star in the political world. He wasn’t just promising; he was a dream fulfilled.</p>

<p>Marrying him had been an easy choice. He was charming, ambitious, and driven, offering a life of security and success.</p>

<p>They had built a family together—three beautiful children: one boy and two girls. Their life was perfect, or so it seemed.</p>

<p>And yet, as she stood there now, wiping her hands with a plush towel, she couldn’t ignore the restlessness stirring inside her.</p>
