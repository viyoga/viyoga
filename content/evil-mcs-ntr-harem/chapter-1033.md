<p>&quot;You bend time again,&quot; Althea murmured softly when he passed near her, her lips curving into a knowing smile.</p>

<p>Ross only smiled back.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s the least I can do,&quot; he replied.</p>

<p>&quot;After all, no amount of power means anything if I can’t spend it on those I care for.&quot;</p>

<p>Her heart warmed at his words, and for a brief moment, she reached out to touch his hand.</p>

<p>&quot;You always say the right things,&quot; she whispered.</p>

<p>&quot;Only because they’re true,&quot; he said simply.</p>

<p>And so the night continued, a night that stretched far beyond the limits of ordinary time.</p>

<p>Within the sanctuary, the hours moved like a gentle current—slow, calm, and eternal.</p>

<p>Outside, the stars still shone over a quiet world, unaware that deep below, a man who had conquered heaven and earth was choosing, once again, to conquer something even greater—love, devotion, and the fragile beauty of human connection.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley, master of time, savior of his people, and ruler of realms and the multiverse, was at peace.</p>

<p>And for that one long, endless night, the world stood still just for him.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>One week had passed, and the world was unrecognizable.</p>

<p>Cities that had once thrived with life now lay in ruin, streets littered with abandoned cars, overturned debris, and the unmistakable signs of panic.</p>

<p>Millions of people had already fallen to the plague, their deaths only marking the beginning of the nightmare, for they rose again as mindless, ravenous zombies.</p>

<p>There was no real defense against them.</p>

<p>Ordinary citizens did not go about their daily lives armed with guns or combat training.</p>

<p>Even if they tried to fight, one scratch or bite was enough to seal their fate, spreading the infection almost instantaneously.</p>

<p>Entire neighborhoods could fall in hours, and the few who survived lived in constant terror, always moving, always hiding.</p>

<p>The heart stones had granted some people extraordinary abilities, creating what could be called superhumans.</p>

<p>But these gifts were far from a universal solution. Not every power was suited to the chaos of combat.</p>

<p>Some could see through walls or sense danger from miles away, while others could emit light from their hands like a walking lantern.</p>

<p>None of this helped when a horde of zombies swarmed through a narrow alley, teeth bared and claws ready.</p>

<p>Many abilities were clever but impractical in direct confrontation, leaving their wielders exposed to the relentless threat.</p>

<p>Even the military, the police forces, and elite squads of soldiers found themselves stretched impossibly thin.</p>

<p>A single battalion could hold one street, one city block, at most—but there were countless others under siege at the same time.</p>

<p>Commanders were forced to make impossible choices, prioritizing areas that could be defended while abandoning zones that were already lost.</p>

<p>Rescue missions became near-constant operations, teams running themselves ragged to pull civilians from doomed zones before they fell prey to the horde.</p>

<p>Many of these rescue teams had consumed heart stones themselves, gaining enhanced abilities to increase their chances of survival.</p>

<p>They could leap over burning cars, track enemies by heat signatures, or even manipulate small amounts of energy.</p>

<p>Yet even with such powers, the hordes seemed infinite.</p>

<p>For every zombie they killed, dozens more emerged from shadows, from buildings, from underground tunnels.</p>

<p>The ground itself seemed to pulse with death, and every victory was temporary, fleeting, and far too small against the scale of the apocalypse.</p>

<p>The death toll continued to rise, day after day, the numbers climbing into the millions with terrifying speed.</p>

<p>Families were torn apart, entire cities emptied, and despair settled over the living like a thick, suffocating fog.</p>

<p>The world had become a battlefield unlike any other, a test not of strength or skill but of endurance, wit, and sheer willpower.</p>

<p>Humanity was no longer the dominant force on Earth—it was prey, struggling against an enemy that seemed to multiply faster than it could be contained.</p>

<p>Even those who survived the first week knew the days ahead would only grow more desperate.</p>

<p>Food and clean water were becoming scarce. Shelters were falling to relentless attacks.</p>

<p>Communications were breaking down, and the once-cohesive response of governments was now fractured and chaotic.</p>

<p>In the midst of this, rumors of pockets of resistance and secret safe zones circulated, but they were few and far between.</p>

<p>The world had changed, and the survivors were forced to confront a harsh truth: civilization as they had known it was gone, replaced by a new, brutal reality where survival alone was the only law that mattered.</p>

<p>Still, that was not Ross’s concern.</p>

<p>He always took care of his own, and the fate of the rest of humanity was a problem for them, not him.</p>

<p>He had no desire to involve himself in every struggle, every disaster that befell the masses.</p>

<p>Humans were resilient creatures, he reminded himself.</p>

<p>No matter how dire the situation, they always found ways to survive, to push forward through storms that would crush lesser beings.</p>

<p>Of course, there were always a few who would give up too quickly, surrendering to despair, letting fear or exhaustion swallow them whole.</p>

<p>But most—most clung stubbornly to life, even when every circumstance seemed stacked against them.</p>

<p>It was a trait etched deep into their DNA, a mark left by countless generations of ancestors who had survived famine, war, disease, and every other calamity.</p>

<p>Humans survived—or they died trying.</p>

<p>Ross observed quietly from his vantage point, detached yet calculating.</p>

<p>He didn’t need to worry about the general survival of humanity; he had other priorities, matters far closer to him and far more pressing.</p>

<p>His gaze settled on a figure that had caught his attention—a woman of extraordinary beauty.</p>

<p>Her features were soft yet striking, framed by dark hair that fell loosely over her shoulders.</p>

<p>Despite her composure, there was a flicker of distress in her eyes, a weight of worry she could not entirely mask.</p>

<p>And yet, she continued typing on her phone, absorbed in some private world of messages and distractions, as though the excitement surrounding her could never touch her.</p>
