<p>Thomas met with his man in a dimly lit parking garage on the outskirts of the city.</p>

<p>It was quick and efficient—just the way he liked things.</p>

<p>They reviewed the details one last time, going over every step with precision.</p>

<p>The plan was deliberately simple. No theatrics, no unnecessary risks.</p>

<p>The kind of classic, straightforward kidnapping that wouldn&#x27;t draw attention until it was too late.</p>

<p>Just a clean grab and go.</p>

<p>&quot;It won&#x27;t be hard,&quot; his man assured him. &quot;We&#x27;ve kept it old school. No tech, no loose ends.&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas nodded. That was exactly what he wanted to hear.</p>

<p>Once everything was in place, he gave the signal to wait.</p>

<p>Timing was crucial, and he wasn&#x27;t about to rush it.</p>

<p>Patience was something he&#x27;d mastered over the years.</p>

<p>For now, the best move was to act natural, to maintain appearances.</p>

<p>And that meant continuing his usual visits to Jade.</p>

<p>He showed up at her office just like he always did—confident, charming, pretending as if nothing else in the world mattered.</p>

<p>Jade was in the middle of reviewing some contracts, but when her assistant told her Thomas was here, she set them aside without complaint.</p>

<p>It was rare for her to break routine for anyone, but Thomas was different.</p>

<p>He wasn&#x27;t just another businessman.</p>

<p>He was her biggest investor, the man whose money had taken her company to the next level.</p>

<p>That kind of influence came with privileges—even the privilege of wasting her time.</p>

<p>They sat in her office, a private corner suite on the top floor with a view that overlooked the city skyline.</p>

<p>The table between them held a tray of delicate pastries, fruit, and tea—afternoon snacks she usually reserved for high-profile clients or personal indulgence.</p>

<p>&quot;How does he even manage to keep all of you satisfied?&quot; Thomas asked, reaching for a slice of kiwi, his tone laced with disbelief.</p>

<p>He shook his head slowly, trying to wrap his mind around what he&#x27;d heard.</p>

<p>&quot;Ross? More than two dozen women, right? Every single day? That&#x27;s not just insane, it&#x27;s physically impossible.&quot;</p>

<p>He wasn&#x27;t being subtle—he didn&#x27;t care to be.</p>

<p>The jealousy in his voice was thinly veiled beneath forced laughter, and Jade could see right through it.</p>

<p>Jade smiled, leaning back in her chair with an amused look in her eyes.</p>

<p>&quot;He&#x27;s more of a man than you can even begin to understand, Thomas. There&#x27;s simply no comparison at all.&quot; Her voice was calm, but there was an edge to it—one that made it clear she wasn&#x27;t exaggerating. Not in the slightest.</p>

<p>Thomas gave a low whistle, then laughed again, this time more bitterly.</p>

<p>&quot;That&#x27;s crazy,&quot; he muttered, almost to himself. His ego didn&#x27;t take hits easily, but Ross—Ross was something else.</p>

<p>The way Jade talked about him made Thomas feel small, and he didn&#x27;t like that feeling one bit.</p>

<p>Despite the tension in the air, Jade seemed perfectly at ease.</p>

<p>She sipped her tea, legs crossed, eyes glinting with quiet superiority.</p>

<p>She didn&#x27;t have to explain anything to Thomas, and she knew it.</p>

<p>He might hold the money, but Ross held something far more valuable: her admiration, her attention, her loyalty. And perhaps more.</p>

<p>Thomas watched her closely, feeling that quiet burn in his chest. He had everything—wealth, influence, power.</p>

<p>But no matter what he did, Jade never looked at him the way she talked about Ross. And that made this whole game much more personal.</p>

<p>Still, he smiled back. Polite. Controlled. After all, everything was already in motion.</p>

<p>And soon, Ross would be out of the picture.</p>

<p>Thomas didn&#x27;t make any overt moves on Jade that week. There was no point anymore.</p>

<p>Not with what he had planned. The decision had been made days ago, and the wheels were already in motion.</p>

<p>He kept up appearances—friendly, flirtatious even—but he was already looking beyond the surface, thinking about what would come next.</p>

<p>And then the weekend arrived.</p>

<p>It was a cool, gray Saturday afternoon.</p>

<p>The sky hung low, threatening rain, and the streets were soaked in that dull, metallic light that comes just before a storm.</p>

<p>The perfect weather for what was about to happen.</p>

<p>Jade was returning home from the office.</p>

<p>She had stayed later than usual, catching up on reports and attending a few last-minute virtual meetings.</p>

<p>Her driver was not Brandon today—Ross&#x27;s number one, the undead bodyguard who could pass for human better than most people actually were.</p>

<p>Brandon was precise, lethal, and calm under pressure.</p>

<p>But Jade didn&#x27;t request him unless Thomas was involved.</p>

<p>She didn&#x27;t think she needed him today. After all, it was just a regular ride home.</p>

<p>Her current driver, another of Ross&#x27;s reanimated soldiers, was less personable but just as loyal.</p>

<p>These men didn&#x27;t breathe. They didn&#x27;t tire. And they obeyed orders without question.</p>

<p>Jade of course did not know that.</p>

<p>In her mind, she was perfectly safe.</p>

<p>The time was just past five o&#x27;clock when they turned onto a quieter stretch of road—one of those back routes she liked for its privacy and lack of traffic.</p>

<p>That was when it happened.</p>

<p>Without warning, a black SUV veered into the street ahead of them and stopped, blocking their path.</p>

<p>A split second later, another vehicle slammed into position behind them. The trap closed instantly. There was no time to react.</p>

<p>The driver&#x27;s hands tightened on the wheel, and he glanced at the rearview mirror, catching Jade&#x27;s eye.</p>

<p>&quot;What the hell…?&quot; she murmured, leaning forward.</p>

<p>Before she could say more, the SUV doors flew open. Masked men in tactical gear poured out, rifles raised and aimed directly at the car.</p>

<p>Their movements were quick, practiced—military, maybe mercenary. Professional.</p>

<p>&quot;TURN OFF THE CAR OR DIE!&quot; one of them barked, voice muffled by a mask but laced with deadly seriousness.</p>

<p>Jade&#x27;s heart pounded, but her face remained composed. She looked at the driver again.</p>

<p>He waited for her signal.</p>

<p>She gave him a slight nod. He obeyed without hesitation, powering down the engine.</p>

<p>The men moved in fast, circling the car. One of them yanked at the door handle.</p>

<p>&quot;Locked,&quot; one of them growled. &quot;Unlock the doors. Now.&quot;</p>
