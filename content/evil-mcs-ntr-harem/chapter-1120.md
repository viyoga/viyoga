<p>The smell of iron filled the hallway instantly.</p>

<p>Ross lowered his arm slowly, as if he had merely swatted a fly.</p>

<p>His expression didn’t change.</p>

<p>He didn’t even bother wiping off the blood splattered across his cheek.</p>

<p>He simply looked at the remaining four men, each one frozen, drenched in the gore of their comrade, shaking like cornered prey.</p>

<p>Ross smiled faintly.</p>

<p>&quot;Two down...&quot; he murmured. &quot;Who’s next?&quot;</p>

<p>Ross’s final word was still echoing—still vibrating through the blood-stained hallway—</p>

<p>when the air behind him bent.</p>

<p>A ripple of shadow</p>

<p>a distortion</p>

<p>a whisper—</p>

<p>Then a man materialized directly at Ross’s back, like a nightmare stepping out of darkness.</p>

<p>Before the echo even died—</p>

<p>SHHKKT!</p>

<p>A knife stabbed toward Ross’s throat with deadly precision, the blade aimed perfectly at the softest part of a human neck.</p>

<p>The attacker’s movement was smooth, experienced, practiced from years of killing.</p>

<p>A guaranteed kill.</p>

<p>—Except it wasn’t.</p>

<p>The blade struck Ross’s skin—</p>

<p>CLANG!</p>

<p>Sparks scattered across the dark hallway as the knife skidded off him, the metal edge vibrating violently like it had struck a tank’s armor.</p>

<p>The hand holding the blade shook from the recoil.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t bleed.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t flinch.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t even feel it.</p>

<p>Slowly... very slowly... Ross turned his head, eyes narrowing.</p>

<p>The attacker’s breath hitched.</p>

<p>He could feel it—</p>

<p>the moment he knew this sneak attack had signed his death sentence.</p>

<p>Ross reached back, lightning-fast, trying to grab the man’s throat—</p>

<p>But the leader vanished again.</p>

<p>His form dissolved into shadows, scattering like black dust before reappearing further down the hall, gasping softly, his knife-hand trembling. His ability—short-range teleportation or shadow-shift—saved him, but only barely.</p>

<p>He stared at Ross with a mixture of awe and cold terror.</p>

<p>&quot;No damage... no reaction... not even a scratch...&quot; he whispered.</p>

<p>Ross turned fully to face him now, steps slow, deliberate, unstoppable.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re quick,&quot; Ross said. &quot;But not quick enough.&quot;</p>

<p>The leader clenched his jaw. He wasn’t shaking like the others had, but fear flickered in his eyes.</p>

<p>He raised his hand sharply to the three remaining men behind him—the last survivors of his squad.</p>

<p>&quot;Listen carefully,&quot; he barked. His voice echoed with authority, even as blood dripped from the ceiling behind Ross.</p>

<p>The three men stood frozen, pale and on the verge of collapse.</p>

<p>&quot;Go back to the base,&quot; the leader ordered. &quot;Find Bruno. Tell him everything about this place. About him.&quot;</p>

<p>He pointed at Ross.</p>

<p>&quot;This man... whoever he is... he’s not someone we can beat tonight.&quot;</p>

<p>One of the men swallowed hard.</p>

<p>The other two stared at their boss like he’d just given them divine salvation.</p>

<p>The leader stepped forward, blades in both hands now, stance sharp and ready despite the tremor in his fingers.</p>

<p>&quot;I’ll stay behind,&quot; he said. &quot;I’ll buy you time. I’ll keep him busy.&quot;</p>

<p>Relief washed over the three survivors so visibly that it almost looked pathetic.</p>

<p>&quot;Thanks, boss!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;W—we won’t forget this!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You’re the best—good luck!&quot;</p>

<p>They turned toward the exit immediately—but not before glancing at Ross one last time.</p>

<p>That was a mistake.</p>

<p>Ross’s eyes were glowing faintly, dark and dangerous, staring at them like prey he hadn’t decided to chase yet.</p>

<p>The three men broke.</p>

<p>They sprinted toward the stairs, nearly tripping over the bloodied wooden stakes, coughing as they pushed past the stench of gore.</p>

<p>One slipped on brain matter and scrambled back up with a scream.</p>

<p>Another shoved aside a broken chair and almost fell down the steps in panic.</p>

<p>They didn’t care.</p>

<p>They just wanted out.</p>

<p>Within seconds, they were gone—</p>

<p>boots pounding out the door,</p>

<p>yelping in terror,</p>

<p>racing into the night like hunted animals desperate to escape the slaughterhouse.</p>

<p>That left only two people in the hallway:</p>

<p>Ross, dripping with the blood of their comrades...</p>

<p>...and the leader, blades drawn, breathing hard, knowing he was about to face something far worse than zombies.</p>

<p>Ross wiped a splatter of blood from his cheek.</p>

<p>&quot;Good,&quot; he murmured.</p>

<p>&quot;Now it’s just the two of us.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross smiled—a small, almost playful curl of the lips—and took a single step forward.</p>

<p>That was all.</p>

<p>One step.</p>

<p>And then he vanished.</p>

<p>Not blurred, not sped up, not hidden—he was simply gone, as though the world had blinked and forgotten he existed.</p>

<p>&quot;So fast!&quot; the leader gasped. His voice cracked, the disbelief raw and unfiltered.</p>

<p>His pupils shrank, and cold sweat immediately formed on his forehead.</p>

<p>His mind raced.</p>

<p>He... he copied our abilities. He copied everything. How? How can someone do that?</p>

<p>&quot;He can copy our abilities at will... impossible...&quot; the leader muttered in his heart, and for the first time since the apocalypse began, true fear settled deep inside him.</p>

<p>This wasn’t a fight.</p>

<p>This wasn’t even a battle.</p>

<p>This was a nightmare.</p>

<p>But he had survived too long to simply freeze in place.</p>

<p>The apocalypse had broken him, rebuilt him, and burned him again.</p>

<p>He had watched friends torn apart by zombies and mutants, fought scavengers over drops of water, slit throats in the dark before the enemy even knew he was there.</p>

<p>His instincts had been sharpened into a deadly blade.</p>

<p>And that blade now screamed at him: Move.</p>

<p>He obeyed instantly, slipping into the shadows of the warehouse.</p>

<p>His body vanished behind scattered crates, debris, pillars.</p>

<p>He moved with practiced silence—rolling, sliding, stepping lightly with absolute precision.</p>

<p>His spirit power wrapped around him, suppressing his presence.</p>

<p>Anyone else would’ve lost sight of him completely.</p>

<p>But Ross wasn’t anyone else.</p>

<p>&quot;Too slow,&quot; Ross’s voice whispered.</p>

<p>The leader’s blood turned to ice.</p>

<p>The voice was right behind him—no, inside his ear, as if Ross were breathing directly across his skin.</p>

<p>&quot;Run faster. Hide better.&quot;</p>

<p>The leader choked on a breath and stumbled forward, hitting the ground and scrambling like a terrified animal.</p>

<p>His hands slipped on dust and grime as he crawled behind a steel support beam, then darted to another shadowed corner without stopping.</p>

<p>He moved again. And again. A frantic pattern of desperate survival.</p>

<p>Hide. Disappear. Get away. Move. Move!</p>

<p>But the feeling of danger didn’t fade.</p>

<p>It grew.</p>
