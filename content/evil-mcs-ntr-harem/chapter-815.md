<p>Mario exhaled slowly and leaned against one of the benches, his usual easy confidence stripped away.</p>

<p>Ross folded his arms and leaned back slightly against the lockers, watching him closely.</p>

<p>&quot;Alright,&quot; he said calmly. &quot;We’re alone. Talk to me.&quot;</p>

<p>Mario rubbed the back of his neck, as if unsure how to begin.</p>

<p>&quot;I wasn’t just here to watch the game today,&quot; he said finally.</p>

<p>&quot;I mean, yeah, you dropped 120 points like it was nothing, and everyone’s losing their minds, but that’s not why I came.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross’s brows lifted slightly. He had expected as much.</p>

<p>&quot;I figured,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>Mario’s expression darkened with hesitation.</p>

<p>He paced a few steps, then stopped and faced Ross directly.</p>

<p>&quot;There’s something I need to tell you,&quot; he said. &quot;Something I should’ve told you a long time ago. I didn’t because... I thought maybe you’d never need to know. Maybe I can still fix it. Maybe I’d get lucky. Maybe it would never come up.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross stayed silent, giving him space.</p>

<p>Mario’s voice lowered. &quot;But now I don’t know what to do and perhaps you’re the only person who could help me.&quot;</p>

<p>There was a long pause.</p>

<p>Ross studied him. &quot;Alright. Then say it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Okay... just hear me out first, alright?&quot; Mario said, his voice already tight with emotion.</p>

<p>&quot;I need you to be patient with me, Ross. What I’m about to say might sound weird, or... pathetic, I don’t know. But I honestly don’t know what to do anymore.&quot;</p>

<p>He shook his head and lowered his face into his hands.</p>

<p>His fingers trembled slightly, and his breathing became uneven.</p>

<p>He wasn’t just distressed—he was unraveling.</p>

<p>All the weight he’d been carrying in silence was finally starting to spill out, and the damn was cracking.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t rush him. He stood nearby, quietly watching, giving his old friend space.</p>

<p>But when he saw Mario’s shoulders shaking ever so slightly, he stepped closer.</p>

<p>&quot;Hey,&quot; Ross said gently. &quot;You’re nearly forty, man. Don’t cry on me now. We’ve been through worse together, haven’t we?&quot;</p>

<p>He offered a half-smile and lightly patted Mario on the shoulder.</p>

<p>&quot;Whatever it is, we’ll figure it out. You’ve got me now. If it’s something I can handle, I will—no hesitation. Just say the word.&quot;</p>

<p>Mario didn’t respond, so Ross continued, trying to lighten the mood, even just a little.</p>

<p>&quot;Let me guess... money problems? You picked up a gambling habit? Creditors banging on your door? Listen, if it’s money, I’ve got you. No questions asked. No interest, no stress. Pay me back whenever. Or don’t pay me back. I really don’t care. At this point in my life, money’s just printed paper lying around the house.&quot;</p>

<p>That actually made Mario lift his head, just a little.</p>

<p>His eyes were red, and though the pain hadn’t faded, the kindness in Ross’s voice seemed to dull the sharpest edges of it.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s not money,&quot; Mario said softly. &quot;And no, I’m not gambling.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross quirked an eyebrow.</p>

<p>&quot;Okay, then if it’s not about money... gotta be a woman thing, right?&quot; He crossed his arms and leaned back against a nearby locker.</p>

<p>&quot;You having a side fling? Some mistress causing trouble? Blackmail? Love triangle? Scandal?&quot;</p>

<p>Mario gave a dry chuckle—a real laugh this time, even if it was weak.</p>

<p>&quot;No, no. Nothing like that,&quot; he said, shaking his head. &quot;I’d never cheat on my wife. You know how I feel about her.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross nodded. &quot;Yeah, I do.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;But...&quot; Mario hesitated, his smile fading. &quot;It’s close.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross’s brow furrowed. &quot;Close?&quot;</p>

<p>Mario sighed deeply and sat down on one of the benches.</p>

<p>He ran a hand through his hair, like he was trying to delay the words that were inevitably going to come out.</p>

<p>&quot;My wife wants a baby, Ross.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross blinked, waiting for more. &quot;Okay... and?&quot;</p>

<p>Mario looked up at him, tired and defeated. &quot;And I can’t give her one.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross stared at him.</p>

<p>Mario paused, then finally said it: &quot;I’m impotent.&quot;</p>

<p>The word hit the air like a stone dropped in still water.</p>

<p>Ross was silent.</p>

<p>&quot;I’ve been tested. We’ve tried treatments. Hormones. IVF. We even traveled to some obscure clinic in Europe that claimed it could ’restore vitality through magnetic balance’ or some shit,&quot; Mario muttered with a bitter chuckle.</p>

<p>&quot;I’ve done everything. We did everything. And nothing worked.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross slowly walked over and sat beside him. The energy in the room shifted—less like two friends joking around, more like two men caught in the heaviness of something real.</p>

<p>&quot;I see,&quot; Ross said quietly. &quot;How long have you known?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Years,&quot; Mario whispered. &quot;But it didn’t start hurting until she told me that she wanted to have a baby. Until she said she wanted to try again.&quot;</p>

<p>He let out a shaky breath.</p>

<p>&quot;She’s been so kind about it. So understanding. But I see the way she looks at babies. I see how she touches her stomach when she thinks I’m not watching. I know she’s thinking about what it would be like. To feel life growing inside her. To hear a baby cry in the house. To hold something that’s ours.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross said nothing. He just listened. Sometimes, silence was louder than words.</p>

<p>&quot;And the worst part?&quot; Mario continued. &quot;I feel like less of a man. Like I failed her. Like I was born broken, and now I’ve brought that brokenness into our marriage.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross slowly turned his head to look at him. &quot;You’re not broken, Mario. And you didn’t fail anyone. Biology’s not what makes you a man.&quot;</p>

<p>Mario gave a dry, humorless laugh. &quot;Yeah. Try telling that to the part of me that can’t give the woman I love the one thing she’s always wanted.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross leaned back against the lockers, thinking.</p>

<p>&quot;What about IUI?&quot; Ross asked gently, his tone more serious now. &quot;You’ve tried that, right?&quot;</p>

<p>Mario nodded with a bitter laugh. &quot;Done everything. Multiple rounds. Hormone therapy. Supplements. Even acupuncture and some traditional stuff her aunt swore by. All of it failed.&quot; He rubbed his face in frustration, his voice trembling hard.</p>
