<p>Ross didn’t call cut.</p>

<p>He let the moment live.</p>

<p>He panned slowly across her face, capturing the raw emotion in her eyes—the heartbreak, the disappointment, the fury.</p>

<p>Then, without a word, he adjusted the focus.</p>

<p>It was time for him to step into the scene.</p>

<p>&quot;Cut!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Good take, Cara! Let’s do another one,&quot; Ross called out as he clapped his hands once and started packing up the equipment with practiced ease.</p>

<p>The camera crew, the same team from earlier, didn’t need further instructions—they were already moving toward the swimming pool, chatting and adjusting gear as they went.</p>

<p>Music thumped softly in the background, just enough to give life to the scene without overwhelming it.</p>

<p>Around the pool, several contestants from the house cast were laughing, splashing water, and playfully chasing each other around.</p>

<p>The light from the setting sun shimmered across the water, casting a golden hue over the entire space.</p>

<p>It was the picture of carefree fun—except for one person.</p>

<p>Cara sat off to the side on a lounge chair, alone beneath a wide umbrella.</p>

<p>A half-melted drink rested in her manicured hand, the tiny paper umbrella in the glass drooping sadly.</p>

<p>Her gaze was locked on some invisible point in the distance.</p>

<p>Her lips were still, her expression unreadable.</p>

<p>But the look in her eyes—haunted and hollow—spoke volumes.</p>

<p>Ross noticed it immediately. He wasn’t just the director—he paid attention to people.</p>

<p>To moments like this.</p>

<p>He walked over, quiet but confident, and gently took the seat beside her.</p>

<p>&quot;I thought I was in the wrong place for a second,&quot; he said lightly, adjusting his posture.</p>

<p>Cara didn’t respond right away.</p>

<p>She just blinked slowly and turned her head to look at him.</p>

<p>Her face was as stunning as ever—high cheekbones, perfect skin—but the emptiness in her expression dulled her natural glow.</p>

<p>&quot;...but it seems I’m exactly where I’m meant to be,&quot; Ross continued, his voice softer now.</p>

<p>He extended a hand with a lopsided grin.</p>

<p>&quot;Hi, I’m D.&quot;</p>

<p>Cara stared at the hand.</p>

<p>She didn’t take it right away.</p>

<p>Her eyes flicked to his, studying him, as if unsure whether to let him in—or shut him out entirely.</p>

<p>But then, just barely, the corner of her lips lifted. She reached out and shook his hand.</p>

<p>&quot;Does that pickup line work all the time?&quot; she asked, her voice low and slightly hoarse. &quot;Cara.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Of course,&quot; Ross replied with a wink. &quot;It hasn’t failed me yet, Cara.&quot;</p>

<p>Cara let out a breath that was almost a laugh. Almost. Then she looked back out at the water.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t rush. He leaned back, letting the silence settle naturally before speaking again.</p>

<p>&quot;So... who’s the dumb guy who made you cry?&quot;</p>

<p>She turned her head slowly, caught off guard. &quot;Is it that obvious?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It sure is,&quot; he said, not unkindly. &quot;You’ve got that look.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;What look?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;The kind where you’re trying really hard to keep it together, but you’re one bad song or one wrong word away from falling apart.&quot;</p>

<p>She exhaled deeply and turned her face back to the pool.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s not one guy. It’s... a lot of things. People. Pressure. The expectation always being high. I don’t know. Maybe just me being dramatic.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross didn’t push. He let her speak at her pace.</p>

<p>&quot;You ever feel like... everyone expects you to be a perfect—even when no one really is?&quot; she continued.</p>

<p>&quot;Like you forget who you are in the midst of a crowd.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross nodded slowly. &quot;More than you’d think.&quot;</p>

<p>There was a pause, long and weighty, but not uncomfortable.</p>

<p>The kind of silence that only exists between two people who understand each other without needing to fill every second with noise.</p>

<p>Cara finally looked at him again.</p>

<p>&quot;So what’s your story, D? You always this smooth with sad girls?&quot;</p>

<p>He smirked. &quot;Only the ones who make silence feel like a conversation.&quot;</p>

<p>That got a real smile out of her—small, yes, but genuine.</p>

<p>The music shifted to something slower, and a breeze drifted across the pool deck, lifting strands of her hair.</p>

<p>For the first time since Ross sat down, she tucked the hair behind her ear and relaxed back in her seat.</p>

<p>&quot;Maybe you are exactly where you’re meant to be,&quot; she murmured.</p>

<p>Ross raised his glass of water in a mock toast. &quot;Here’s hoping.&quot;</p>

<p>They clinked their glasses lightly—hers, cold and sweating; his, clear and unassuming.</p>

<p>The scene around them buzzed with life, but in that little pocket of space beneath the umbrella, it felt like the world had slowed just for the two of them.</p>

<p>They talked some more, letting the conversation meander naturally from light-hearted jokes to deeper topics.</p>

<p>It was easy—too easy—and that ease made Cara more curious.</p>

<p>There was something about Ross that didn’t quite fit into the usual mold of overconfident men who came and went like scenes in a show.</p>

<p>After a brief lull, Cara tilted her head, studying him more closely.</p>

<p>Her gaze lingered on the black mask that covered the upper half of his face—sleek, minimal, but clearly deliberate.</p>

<p>&quot;So...&quot; she began, swirling the straw in her drink. &quot;What’s with the mask? Are you a spy or something?&quot;</p>

<p>Ross chuckled, the kind of low, relaxed laugh that made it hard to tell whether he was amused or hiding something.</p>

<p>He reached up and tapped the side of the mask lightly.</p>

<p>&quot;I had an accident when I was younger,&quot; he said casually, though there was a flicker of something more serious in his voice.</p>

<p>&quot;Left me disfigured. Now I’m the beast—just waiting for my beauty to show up.&quot;</p>

<p>He turned slightly toward her, and though she couldn’t see the full expression beneath the mask, his tone softened.</p>

<p>&quot;And judging by the way you’re looking at me, I think I won’t have to wait much longer to hold her in my arms.&quot;</p>

<p>Cara laughed, the sound sweet and bright, chasing away the weight of his words.</p>

<p>She shook her head, smiling as she took another sip from her drink.</p>

<p>&quot;I find that hard to believe,&quot; she said, eyebrows raised in mock disbelief.</p>

<p>&quot;With how smooth you are, I’d bet half the women here have already fallen head over heels.&quot;</p>
