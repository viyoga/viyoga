<p>The party was long over. What remained was silence—thick and undisturbed, like a veil drawn across the house.</p>

<p>Bottles lay tipped over, half-filled glasses abandoned, glitter and confetti scattered like forgotten memories of laughter.</p>

<p>Everyone had disappeared into their rooms, into dreams, or drunken stupor.</p>

<p>Heaven, Tianna, and Lily had curled up in the girls’ room without a word.</p>

<p>They had no questions, no accusations.</p>

<p>They simply slipped into sleep, their minds quiet, unaware that another story was still unfolding in the dim hours of the night.</p>

<p>Down the hall, a shadow moved.</p>

<p>Joan’s steps were light, uncertain, like a woman wading through fog.</p>

<p>Every movement felt surreal, like she was watching herself from outside her body.</p>

<p>Her pulse raced, her breath shallow.</p>

<p>The hallway seemed to stretch endlessly, each step echoing in her ears like a drumbeat of doubt.</p>

<p>She reached the boys’ room door and stopped.</p>

<p>Her hand hovered above the doorknob.</p>

<p>Her mind screamed at her to turn around, to go back, to lock herself in her room and pretend none of this had ever happened.</p>

<p>That Ross hadn’t seen through her. That he hadn’t touched a part of her she’d spent years keeping buried under poise, pride, and practiced indifference.</p>

<p>But she didn’t turn back.</p>

<p>Because even now—especially now—she knew that nothing had ever unraveled her like this.</p>

<p>And so... she pushed the door open.</p>

<p>The room was dim, the only light coming from a soft lamp on the far table.</p>

<p>Shadows danced lazily across the walls, and in that haze stood Ross—waiting.</p>

<p>He hadn’t sat down, hadn’t fallen asleep. He was simply there. Like he knew.</p>

<p>Like he’d always known.</p>

<p>Joan’s breath hitched as their eyes met.</p>

<p>He said nothing at first. He didn’t have to.</p>

<p>&quot;Did you ever doubt I’d come?&quot; she asked, her voice barely above a whisper.</p>

<p>Ross smiled faintly, his eyes never leaving hers. &quot;Not once.&quot;</p>

<p>That answer pierced her. Not just because of its confidence—but because it was true.</p>

<p>He hadn’t doubted her. Not for a moment.</p>

<p>Even when she’d been drowning in indecision, in shame, in self-denial—he had known.</p>

<p>She hadn’t even known herself.</p>

<p>&quot;How did you know?&quot; she asked again, quieter this time, the weight of the question settling heavy between them.</p>

<p>She needed to understand. She needed to know how he’d peeled back layers she hadn’t even known were there.</p>

<p>Ross took a slow step toward her, then another. He didn’t rush.</p>

<p>He was never in a rush.</p>

<p>&quot;Because I’m good at reading people,&quot; he said. &quot;And with you... the signs were all there.&quot;</p>

<p>Joan stood frozen, staring up at him, her voice dry. &quot;What signs?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You hide behind control,&quot; Ross said. &quot;Behind sharp words and distance. But I could see it. The way you stiffen when someone gets too close. The way you pretend to be unaffected... but your eyes betray you.&quot;</p>

<p>Joan opened her mouth to speak, to deny it, but he cut her off—gently, with his tone.</p>

<p>&quot;You don’t want to be in control,&quot; he said, voice like velvet. &quot;You want someone to take it from you. To strip you bare and see the real you—the messy, trembling, wild part you’re terrified to let out.&quot;</p>

<p>His words wrapped around her like a noose made of silk.</p>

<p>She wanted to argue. She wanted to scream that he was wrong. That he didn’t know her.</p>

<p>That she wasn’t like that.</p>

<p>But she couldn’t.</p>

<p>Because he was right.</p>

<p>He was terrifyingly right.</p>

<p>&quot;I hate how right you are,&quot; she breathed.</p>

<p>Ross took another step forward. Now they were inches apart.</p>

<p>He looked down at her—not with arrogance, but with the quiet authority of a man who had already won.</p>

<p>&quot;You don’t hate it,&quot; he murmured. &quot;You hate that you love it.&quot;</p>

<p>Joan’s chest rose and fell, her breathing unsteady, the war inside her raging louder than ever.</p>

<p>But with every second, her walls were crumbling. Not with force—but with truth.</p>

<p>&quot;Say it,&quot; Ross said softly. &quot;Say what you want.&quot;</p>

<p>Her lips trembled. &quot;I don’t know how.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Yes, you do.&quot;</p>

<p>She shook her head. &quot;I’ve never said it before.&quot;</p>

<p>He reached out, slowly, and tucked a strand of hair behind her ear. His fingers were gentle.</p>

<p>Warm. Grounding.</p>

<p>&quot;You don’t have to be perfect here,&quot; he said. &quot;You just have to be honest.&quot;</p>

<p>Joan’s eyes welled, just a little, from the pressure of it all. Of being seen. Truly seen.</p>

<p>It felt like standing naked under a spotlight—exposed and fragile.</p>

<p>&quot;I want...&quot; she whispered, her voice cracking. &quot;I want to let go.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross leaned in closer, his voice deep and steady. &quot;And you want me to take it from you.&quot;</p>

<p>She nodded.</p>

<p>That was all he needed.</p>

<p>Ross stepped behind her slowly, wrapping one arm gently around her waist, the other brushing down her arm.</p>

<p>His touch wasn’t rushed. It wasn’t harsh. It was deliberate—commanding, yet patient.</p>

<p>He wasn’t claiming her like a conquest.</p>

<p>He was accepting her surrender.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re mine tonight,&quot; he whispered, lips near her ear. &quot;And I’ll take care of everything.&quot;</p>

<p>Joan closed her eyes and nodded again, this time with more certainty.</p>

<p>Her body relaxed, the weight of decision finally lifted.</p>

<p>She wasn’t sure what tomorrow would bring. Or how she’d feel in the daylight.</p>

<p>But for now... she didn’t care.</p>

<p>She had found something intoxicating in the storm that was Ross—not just desire, but freedom.</p>

<p>And tonight, she would drown in it willingly.</p>

<p>&quot;Mmm...&quot; Ross kissed her, his lips firm and commanding.</p>

<p>Joan responded instinctively, almost desperately, her body pressing into his as if drawn by gravity itself.</p>

<p>It was her first kiss—and it was nothing like she imagined. It wasn’t gentle or timid.</p>

<p>It was intense, consuming, and utterly electrifying.</p>

<p>A rush of heat flooded through her, curling in her stomach, lighting every nerve on fire.</p>

<p>So many men had tried to win her over before. Some had begged, others had bragged.</p>

<p>All of them failed. They were boys playing at being men—soft, uncertain, and far too easy to dismiss.</p>

<p>She’d always sensed they couldn’t handle her, couldn’t tame her, couldn’t command the hidden part of her she guarded so carefully.</p>
