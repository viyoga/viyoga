<p>Karen looked around her room and sighed softly, the sound barely audible in the still air.</p>

<p>The walls were smooth and metallic, without a single window or hint of the outside world.</p>

<p>There was no sunrise, no moonlight, only the constant hum of machinery buried somewhere deep in the walls.</p>

<p>She rubbed her arms and tried to calm her thoughts. There really was no other choice left.</p>

<p>Whatever freedom she’d once had ended the moment she was brought here.</p>

<p>Taking a slow breath, she stood and walked to the tall cabinet near the far wall.</p>

<p>When she opened it, a small gust of cold air brushed her face. Inside were several neatly folded sets of clothes—jackets, shirts, boots, and even accessories, all arranged as if waiting specifically for her.</p>

<p>Whoever prepared them knew her size, her preferences, even the way she tied her hair.</p>

<p>The realization made her uneasy.</p>

<p>&quot;This place...&quot; she murmured. &quot;It’s too much.&quot;</p>

<p>The room was absurdly large for one person—bigger than her entire apartment back in Parkland.</p>

<p>She glanced toward the far end, where a minimalist bed sat under a soft blue light.</p>

<p>The walls gleamed faintly, reflecting her movement as she walked, and she wondered just how deep underground they really were.</p>

<p>They said the base was near Parkland City, but beyond that, no one knew the exact location.</p>

<p>It was as if they had been erased from the world above.</p>

<p>Karen sighed again, a sound that carried both frustration and fear.</p>

<p>She pulled out a simple black jacket and a matching cap from the cabinet and slipped them on.</p>

<p>The fabric was oddly comfortable—too comfortable, as if made from something more advanced than normal cloth.</p>

<p>She adjusted the collar, took a deep breath, and headed for the door.</p>

<p>The hallway outside was dimly lit, the overhead lights pulsing faintly every few seconds.</p>

<p>The metallic walls stretched endlessly in both directions, cold and sterile.</p>

<p>It felt like walking inside the belly of some enormous machine.</p>

<p>Her footsteps echoed softly, reminding her just how alone she was down here.</p>

<p>The silence pressed against her ears until—</p>

<p>&quot;Please follow me.&quot;</p>

<p>The sudden voice made her jump, her heart leaping to her throat.</p>

<p>She spun around, eyes wide, scanning the corridor.</p>

<p>&quot;W-where are you?&quot;</p>

<p>From the shadows, a shape emerged—a tall, broad-shouldered figure who moved with measured precision.</p>

<p>As he stepped into the weak light, she saw the gleam of armor plating and the matte black of a uniform.</p>

<p>His entire face was concealed beneath a sleek, high-tech helmet with a tinted visor that revealed nothing of the person beneath.</p>

<p>Karen took a nervous step back. &quot;You scared me,&quot; she said, her voice barely steady.</p>

<p>The man didn’t respond.</p>

<p>The faint hum from his helmet’s built-in respirator was the only sound between them.</p>

<p>Then, with a slow, mechanical turn of his head, he gestured toward the corridor ahead.</p>

<p>&quot;Please follow me,&quot; he repeated, this time with a sharper tone—polite but commanding, a voice that carried the weight of discipline and authority.</p>

<p>Karen hesitated, her pulse still racing.</p>

<p>She swallowed hard, pulling her jacket tighter around her.</p>

<p>&quot;Fine,&quot; she said quietly. &quot;Lead the way.&quot;</p>

<p>The man turned and began walking, his boots making precise, heavy thuds on the metallic floor.</p>

<p>Karen followed a few steps behind, trying to match his pace.</p>

<p>As they moved deeper into the corridor, the lights dimmed even further, and the air grew colder.</p>

<p>Strange hums echoed from behind the walls—machines working, vents breathing, perhaps something more alive.</p>

<p>After a while, she dared to ask, &quot;Where are we going? Where is Ross?&quot;</p>

<p>The man gave no answer.</p>

<p>His visor flickered faintly with blue light, perhaps scanning, recording, or transmitting.</p>

<p>Karen frowned and looked away. She didn’t know whether to feel watched or protected.</p>

<p>The man guided Karen through a labyrinth of dimly lit corridors, his steps silent yet deliberate, as if he had memorized every twist and turn of the bunker.</p>

<p>The walls pressed in slightly, metallic and cold, reflecting the faint glow of emergency lights overhead.</p>

<p>Most of the people in here were asleep, their quiet breaths and distant murmurs echoing faintly down the hallways.</p>

<p>Occasionally, someone stirred, and the man would subtly adjust their path, keeping them hidden in the shadows.</p>

<p>Karen’s heart pounded with every movement, every step feeling heavier than the last, as if the walls themselves were pressing her toward the inevitable.</p>

<p>The walk lasted barely five minutes, yet to Karen it felt like an eternity.</p>

<p>Her palms were clammy, and every instinct screamed for her to turn back, to run, to somehow escape this subterranean maze.</p>

<p>But the man’s steady pace and unwavering presence kept her moving forward, even as the fear in her chest grew.</p>

<p>Finally, they reached a reinforced door, one that seemed far heavier than the others.</p>

<p>Its surface was matte, unyielding, and there was an almost imperceptible vibration in the air, like power humming just beneath the steel.</p>

<p>&quot;He’s inside,&quot; the man said quietly, his voice devoid of emotion but carrying authority.</p>

<p>Without another word, he pivoted, melted into the shadows, and vanished as if he had never been there.</p>

<p>Karen blinked, the emptiness of the corridor swallowing him instantly.</p>

<p>Her hand hovered over the handle, trembling slightly despite her effort to steady herself.</p>

<p>She drew a slow, shaky breath, trying to summon the courage she knew she needed.</p>

<p>Her pulse thundered in her ears, every beat echoing the apprehension and anticipation swirling inside her.</p>

<p>Finally, she turned the handle and pushed the door open.</p>

<p>Inside, the room was startlingly familiar.</p>

<p>The walls were bare but polished, the floor smooth, the lighting soft but precise, and the furniture minimal yet functional—almost identical to her own quarters.</p>

<p>The sense of symmetry was unsettling.</p>

<p>It made her realize that all the rooms in this underground bunker were likely designed the same way, each one sterile, controlled, and intimate in its own quiet way.</p>

<p>And there, standing at the small table, was Ross.</p>

<p>He exuded an effortless dominance, his posture relaxed but commanding.</p>

<p>In his hand was a glass of wine, which he drained to the last drop with a single, fluid motion.</p>

<p>The faint clink of the empty glass on the table seemed to reverberate through the room, announcing his presence in a way nothing else could.</p>
