<p>Eric was the name of Anita’s husband.</p>

<p>He had been missing for months—captured during a mission that had gone terribly wrong.</p>

<p>When the rescue team finally brought him back to the base, bruised but alive, everyone called it a miracle.</p>

<p>Everyone except him.</p>

<p>Because the moment he stepped through the door of their quarters and saw Anita standing there, something felt... off.</p>

<p>She smiled. She hugged him. She even cried.</p>

<p>But it felt rehearsed. Measured.</p>

<p>As if she were performing the role of a relieved wife instead of being one.</p>

<p>A week had already passed since his return, yet the warmth he had clung to during his darkest hours in captivity never truly came back to him.</p>

<p>The base felt colder. Their room felt unfamiliar. And Anita—his Anita—felt like an alien wearing her face.</p>

<p>She avoided his gaze more often than not. Conversations died quickly.</p>

<p>When he tried to reminisce about old memories, she would nod politely, but her eyes seemed distant, distracted.</p>

<p>Like she was somewhere else.</p>

<p>&quot;Anita,&quot; he said one evening, his voice gentler than he felt.</p>

<p>She stood near the small counter, pretending to organize things that didn’t need organizing.</p>

<p>He stepped closer, closing the space between them. &quot;Can we talk?&quot;</p>

<p>She didn’t answer immediately. That hesitation—so small, yet so sharp—cut deeper than anything.</p>

<p>He reached for her hand.</p>

<p>She dodged him.</p>

<p>It wasn’t dramatic. It wasn’t violent. It was subtle. But it was deliberate.</p>

<p>&quot;Not tonight, Eric. I’m tired,&quot; she said, keeping her eyes fixed on the counter.</p>

<p>The same line. The same tone. The same careful distance.</p>

<p>For days now, every attempt at closeness had ended the same way. No kisses.</p>

<p>No lingering touches. No quiet conversations in the dark like they used to have. She went to bed early.</p>

<p>Woke up early. Left before he could say much.</p>

<p>He felt like a guest in his own marriage.</p>

<p>That night, something inside him cracked.</p>

<p>&quot;What’s happening with you, Anita?&quot; His voice rose before he could stop it. &quot;Why are you so distant since I got back?&quot;</p>

<p>She stiffened but didn’t turn around.</p>

<p>&quot;I survived,&quot; he continued, his words tumbling out, raw and uncontrolled. &quot;I came back to you. I thought you’d be happy. I thought we’d—&quot; His voice faltered, then hardened. &quot;Are you cheating on me? Is that it? Just tell me!&quot;</p>

<p>Silence.</p>

<p>The kind of silence that felt louder than shouting.</p>

<p>She finally turned, and for a split second, he saw something in her eyes—not guilt, not anger... but fear.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t,&quot; she whispered.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t what?&quot; he demanded. &quot;Don’t ask? Don’t notice that my own wife won’t even let me touch her?&quot;</p>

<p>His fists clenched at his sides. &quot;Every night it’s ’I’m tired.’ Every conversation ends before it begins. You won’t look at me. You won’t hold me. What am I supposed to think?&quot;</p>

<p>His anger filled the room, but beneath it lay something far more fragile—terror.</p>

<p>Because what scared him most wasn’t the possibility that she was cheating.</p>

<p>It was the possibility that she no longer loved him.</p>

<p>And the worst part?</p>

<p>The way she looked at him now... almost as if she were the one afraid of him.</p>

<p>&quot;I think we need time and distance, Eric. I can’t be with you like this—full of bitterness and accusations.&quot;</p>

<p>Anita’s voice didn’t rise. That hurt more than if she had screamed. It was calm. Controlled. Decided.</p>

<p>Eric stared at her as if she had just spoken in another language. &quot;What are you saying?&quot; he asked, his voice cracking despite himself.</p>

<p>She didn’t answer immediately. Instead, she walked past him to the closet and pulled out a travel bag.</p>

<p>The sound of the zipper sliding open felt sharp in the silence.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m saying I can’t live like this,&quot; she said at last. &quot;Every conversation turns into suspicion. Every touch feels like an interrogation. I’m tired, Eric. Not physically. Emotionally.&quot;</p>

<p>He watched her fold her clothes with mechanical precision.</p>

<p>&quot;Anita, don’t do this,&quot; he said, stepping closer. &quot;I was just angry. I didn’t mean it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You did mean it,&quot; she replied quietly. &quot;You wouldn’t have said it otherwise.&quot;</p>

<p>The words struck him harder than any blow.</p>

<p>&quot;I survived,&quot; he whispered hoarsely. &quot;I came back to you. I thought—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You came back different,&quot; she cut in, finally turning to face him. Her eyes glistened, but no tears fell. &quot;And I tried to be patient. I told myself you needed time. That what happened out there changed you. But you don’t talk to me, Eric. You look at me like I’m hiding something. Like I betrayed you.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I was scared!&quot; he burst out. &quot;I lost control of everything out there. I needed something stable. I needed you.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And instead of leaning on me, you pushed me away,&quot; she said.</p>

<p>The bag was full now. She zipped it shut.</p>

<p>&quot;Anita, wait. Please.&quot; His composure shattered. &quot;I’m sorry. I’ll fix it. I’ll get help. Just don’t leave.&quot;</p>

<p>For a moment—just a moment—she hesitated at the door.</p>

<p>But hesitation wasn’t enough.</p>

<p>&quot;I need space,&quot; she said softly. &quot;For both of us.&quot;</p>

<p>Then she walked out.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>The quarters felt too big without her.</p>

<p>Too quiet.</p>

<p>The bed seemed colder. The air heavier.</p>

<p>Eric told himself he would respect her wishes. That he would give her the distance she asked for.</p>

<p>By the second day, he broke.</p>

<p>He started noticing her schedule again. The times she left for duty.</p>

<p>The routes she took through the base. He told himself it wasn’t stalking.</p>

<p>He told himself he just wanted reassurance.</p>

<p>But deep down, he knew what it was.</p>

<p>Obsession born from fear.</p>

<p>He followed from afar—never close enough to confront her, but close enough to see.</p>

<p>In the cafeteria, she sat alone or with colleagues, speaking only about work.</p>

<p>In training simulations, she was sharp, focused, professional.</p>

<p>When her shift ended, she returned directly to her newly assigned quarters.</p>

<p>No secret glances.</p>

<p>No hushed meetings.</p>

<p>No unfamiliar men lingering near her door.</p>

<p>Another week passed.</p>

<p>Nothing.</p>

<p>Eric leaned against a wall one evening, watching her disappear into her room again, and something inside him deflated.</p>

<p>&quot;Was I truly wrong?&quot; he muttered under his breath. &quot;She’s not cheating... she never was.&quot;</p>

<p>He had spent seven days searching for evidence of betrayal and found none.</p>

<p>Only proof of his own insecurity.</p>

<p>Memories from captivity crept into his mind—interrogations, manipulation, the constant feeling that trust was a weakness.</p>

<p>Somewhere along the way, that poison had followed him home.</p>

<p>And he had poured it onto the one person who had waited for him.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m an idiot,&quot; he whispered bitterly.</p>

<p>The realization didn’t bring relief.</p>

<p>It brought shame.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>He approached her again a few days later, this time without anger, without suspicion.</p>

<p>She stood outside the command building reviewing a data pad when he stopped a few feet away.</p>

<p>&quot;Anita,&quot; he said quietly.</p>

<p>She looked up. No hostility. Just caution.</p>

<p>&quot;I owe you more than an apology,&quot; he continued. &quot;I let my fear control me. I accused you because I was scared of losing you... and in doing that, I pushed you away.&quot;</p>

<p>She listened without interrupting.</p>

<p>&quot;I was wrong,&quot; he said firmly. &quot;You didn’t deserve that.&quot;</p>

<p>A long silence followed.</p>

<p>&quot;I know you were hurting,&quot; Anita said at last. &quot;I know what you went through changed you. But it changed me too.&quot;</p>

<p>He frowned slightly. &quot;What do you mean?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I spent months thinking you were dead,&quot; she replied. &quot;I learned how to function without you. I built walls just to survive the possibility that you weren’t coming back.&quot;</p>

<p>Her voice wavered for the first time.</p>

<p>&quot;When you returned, I was relieved. But I was also... afraid. Afraid to let those walls down again. And when you started accusing me, it felt like confirmation that we weren’t the same people anymore.&quot;</p>

<p>Eric felt the weight of her words settle into his chest.</p>

<p>&quot;I don’t recognize the man who looks at me like I’m guilty before I even speak,&quot; she said softly. &quot;And I don’t think you recognize the woman who had to learn how to live without you.&quot;</p>

<p>He swallowed hard.</p>

<p>&quot;So that’s it?&quot; he asked quietly. &quot;We just end?&quot;</p>

<p>She shook her head gently. &quot;I’m not saying it’s over forever. I’m saying we need space. Real space. To figure out who we are now.&quot;</p>

<p>Her gaze met his fully this time—sad, but steady.</p>

<p>&quot;You’ve changed. And I’ve changed too. I think we’re better off apart for now.&quot;</p>

<p>The words weren’t cruel.</p>

<p>They were honest.</p>

<p>And that honesty hurt more than any accusation ever could.</p>

<p>Eric stood there long after she walked away, the weight of his own mistakes pressing down on him.</p>

<p>For the first time since his rescue, he understood something clearly:</p>

<p>Coming back alive wasn’t the same as coming back whole.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>What Eric didn’t know—what he could never have suspected—was that Ross had been fucking his wife in secret.</p>

<p>&quot;Ahhhhh...&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Ohhhhh...&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Ughhhh...&quot;</p>

<p>Anita’s moans spilled shamelessly through the bedroom, raw and unguarded, rising in pitch each time Ross drove deeper.</p>

<p>The headboard tapped the wall in slow, deliberate rhythm.</p>

<p>She clutched the sheets, knuckles white, thighs trembling around his hips as he pinned her exactly the way she’d begged him to the first time they crossed the line.</p>
