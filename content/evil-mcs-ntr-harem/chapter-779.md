<p>By evening, the lights dimmed, the music thumped, and the scent of alcohol filled the house.</p>

<p>Another party was in full swing.</p>

<p>Drinks were passed around, laughter echoed from room to room, and the stress of the week dissolved in a wave of unrestrained energy.</p>

<p>The mood was electric—hands wandered, lips met, and boundaries blurred.</p>

<p>Ross stood near the edge of the room, glass in hand, surrounded by his women.</p>

<p>They clung to him, giggling, glowing with pride and pleasure.</p>

<p>Their dresses were tight and low, showing off the curves he’d memorized in the dark.</p>

<p>They whispered in his ear, touched his chest, and kissed his neck.</p>

<p>He smiled, giving them just enough attention to keep them close—but his eyes were scanning.</p>

<p>Because Ross wasn’t done.</p>

<p>Even in the middle of the party, even as bodies danced and the music pulsed like a heartbeat, he was stalking.</p>

<p>He watched the others with the calm precision of a hunter.</p>

<p>Every smile, every shy glance cast in his direction, every flick of a wrist or bite of a lip—it didn’t go unnoticed.</p>

<p>He was calculating who would fall next. Who would want to taste what his women already had. Who would be the next to melt under his touch and beg for more.</p>

<p>And they were already watching him too.</p>

<p>A few girls near the bar kept sneaking glances his way.</p>

<p>One even dropped her gaze and blushed when their eyes met.</p>

<p>Another leaned closer to her friend and whispered something, both of them laughing softly—but the second girl’s gaze lingered on Ross’s chest.</p>

<p>He saw all of it.</p>

<p>The party raged on around him, wild and carefree.</p>

<p>But Ross stood at the center like a king surveying his domain.</p>

<p>The bed had barely cooled from the last round of passion... and already, new prey was falling into his web.</p>

<p>Because for Ross, there was always another game.</p>

<p>And he always played to win.</p>

<p>Ross began moving across the crowded room with that same effortless confidence that made people either admire him or envy him—or both.</p>

<p>His women watched him go with knowing smiles, already familiar with the hunt.</p>

<p>The music thumped in the background, bodies danced, drinks clinked, but all of it seemed to slow down as people noticed where Ross was heading.</p>

<p>&quot;Damn! Big D really moves fast,&quot; one of the guys muttered under his breath, nudging his friend as they both turned to look.</p>

<p>&quot;Yup, looks like he’s got his eyes on someone new again. Doesn’t he realize eviction’s tomorrow night?&quot; another whispered, keeping his voice low but his gaze locked on Ross.</p>

<p>&quot;That guy’s pushing his luck too far. One of these days it’ll blow up in his face,&quot; a third added, sipping his drink but unable to tear his eyes away from the unfolding scene.</p>

<p>They weren’t wrong. Ross had already stirred up the house with his conquests, his dominance, and the way he carried himself like he owned every room he walked into.</p>

<p>But this time, he was aiming higher—a woman who had, so far, remained untouchable.</p>

<p>She was one of the five most beautiful women in the house, no question.</p>

<p>But unlike the others, she kept her guard up. She didn’t flirt. She didn’t gossip.</p>

<p>She didn’t play games.</p>

<p>She was seated alone on one end of the party lounge, legs crossed gracefully, wearing a sleek dark red dress that hugged her curves without begging for attention.</p>

<p>Her long hair was tied up loosely, strands falling over one shoulder.</p>

<p>Her expression was calm, unreadable—her eyes scanning the room as if everyone else were playing a game she had no interest in.</p>

<p>Ross, of course, found that irresistible.</p>

<p>He approached her without hesitation, cutting through the loose crowd like a knife.</p>

<p>People stepped aside instinctively.</p>

<p>The music pulsed, lights flickered, but the focus shifted entirely onto the two of them.</p>

<p>He came to a stop in front of her, holding a glass of red wine in one hand, and a charming, slightly mocking grin on his lips.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re drinking Coke?&quot; he said, raising an eyebrow. &quot;What are you, a baby?&quot;</p>

<p>Without missing a beat, the woman looked up at him—calm, cool, and unimpressed.</p>

<p>Her eyes flicked to the wine, then back to Ross’s face.</p>

<p>She accepted the glass with a delicate hand but didn’t raise it to her lips.</p>

<p>&quot;Thanks,&quot; she said evenly, &quot;but no thanks.&quot;</p>

<p>She set the glass down on the side table beside her untouched, her fingers still wrapped around her soda.</p>

<p>&quot;I don’t drink,&quot; she continued. &quot;And if not drinking alcohol makes me a baby... then I guess I’m the most mature baby in this entire house.&quot;</p>

<p>Her tone was smooth, her voice soft but edged with steel.</p>

<p>She didn’t raise her voice or get defensive—she simply pushed back with cool, quiet confidence.</p>

<p>Around them, a few people overheard and exchanged looks.</p>

<p>Some chuckled under their breath. Others waited to see what Ross would do. No one dared interrupt.</p>

<p>Ross’s grin widened, not offended in the slightest. In fact, her response only intrigued him more.</p>

<p>&quot;You know,&quot; he said, easing himself down to sit beside her, &quot;most people in this house either giggle when I speak to them or melt on the spot. You’re not like most people.&quot;</p>

<p>She gave him a side glance, unbothered. &quot;Is that your line for every woman who doesn’t jump into your bed?&quot;</p>

<p>He let out a low chuckle. &quot;No. You’d be surprised how rarely I get told no.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m not surprised,&quot; she replied. &quot;You walk around like you’re collecting trophies.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross tilted his head, amused. &quot;And what would that make you?&quot;</p>

<p>She turned to him fully now, her gaze sharp and unflinching. &quot;A mirror. One you don’t want to look too long into.&quot;</p>

<p>For a moment, the tension thickened. Even the music seemed to fade.</p>

<p>Ross leaned back slightly, swirling his wine. &quot;You’re good,&quot; he said. &quot;I like that.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m not here to be liked,&quot; she replied. &quot;Especially not by someone who treats women like a hobby.&quot;</p>
