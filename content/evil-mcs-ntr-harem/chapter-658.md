<p>After the intensity of the day, a strange, uneasy quiet settled between them—less awkward than it was heavy, the silence of two people trying to process too much, too fast.</p>

<p>Sasha had turned on the television for Ross before heading to the bathroom, hoping the noise would fill the stillness.</p>

<p>She needed space. Time. In the bath, she sat submerged beneath the steaming water, the soft scent of lavender soap curling into the air.</p>

<p>She closed her eyes, trying to release the tension still coiled tight in her shoulders.</p>

<p>Her mind replayed everything—Wesley&#x27;s voice, the gunshots, Ross&#x27;s calm, the violence that followed, and the way he&#x27;d held her afterward like she was something fragile.</p>

<p>By the time she stepped out, wrapped in a thick robe with her hair damp and clinging softly to her neck, the clock read just shy of 8 p.m.</p>

<p>She padded softly into the main room, expecting to find Ross still lounging on the couch, maybe mindlessly flipping through the channels. But instead, she stopped short.</p>

<p>He wasn&#x27;t on the couch.</p>

<p>The TV was still playing, but Ross wasn&#x27;t watching.</p>

<p>He was in the kitchen—moving with unexpected grace between the stove and the countertop, like he&#x27;d done this a hundred times before.</p>

<p>He had his sleeves rolled up, shirt slightly unbuttoned at the collar, and was taste-testing something from a wooden spoon.</p>

<p>Sasha blinked, taken aback.</p>

<p>The table was already set, not hastily or carelessly, but with a surprising level of attention. Plates arranged with purpose.</p>

<p>Steam rising from silver serving dishes. Napkins. Cutlery. Wineglasses. The scent of garlic, herbs, and something buttery and rich lingered in the air.</p>

<p>It didn&#x27;t just look good—it looked amazing. Like something out of a fine dining spread.</p>

<p>She walked forward slowly, her brows lifting. &quot;Where did you get all this?&quot;</p>

<p>Ross glanced over his shoulder at her, a flicker of amusement in his eyes.</p>

<p>&quot;It&#x27;s nothing. There&#x27;s nothing money can&#x27;t buy—or make rush delivery for, if you know where to look.&quot;</p>

<p>He turned back to the stove, swirling the contents of a small pan with practiced ease. &quot;There&#x27;s a 24/7 gourmet grocery three blocks away. Paid someone to do a quick run. Wasn&#x27;t going to let you eat instant noodles on a night like this.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha stepped closer, still a little stunned. &quot;You… cooked all this?&quot;</p>

<p>Ross gave a small shrug. &quot;What can I say? I&#x27;m full of surprises.&quot;</p>

<p>She didn&#x27;t reply right away, her gaze shifting from the dishes to him.</p>

<p>He looked completely at ease in the space, as if the events from earlier had already faded into distant memory.</p>

<p>There was no sign of the man who had just broken another&#x27;s face hours ago.</p>

<p>Only this calm, collected, oddly domestic version of Ross—his expression relaxed, movements smooth and unhurried.</p>

<p>&quot;You should go change,&quot; he said gently, without even glancing back at her. &quot;Dinner will be ready in five.&quot;</p>

<p>He didn&#x27;t even spare her a second look—even as she stood there in nothing but her robe, skin still damp, the curve of her legs bare beneath the hem.</p>

<p>Most men would have stared. Ross didn&#x27;t.</p>

<p>And somehow, that made her feel even more exposed.</p>

<p>Sasha lingered for a moment longer, watching him.</p>

<p>Something about it was disarming. He wasn&#x27;t trying to impress her.</p>

<p>He wasn&#x27;t angling for gratitude or praise. He was just… taking care of her. Quietly. Like it was the most natural thing in the world.</p>

<p>Her heart squeezed unexpectedly.</p>

<p>&quot;Alright,&quot; she whispered, her voice barely audible. &quot;I&#x27;ll go get dressed.&quot;</p>

<p>And as she turned and disappeared into her bedroom, she realized something unsettling.</p>

<p>She didn&#x27;t want Ross to leave anymore.</p>

<p>Not tonight.</p>

<p>Not tomorrow.</p>

<p>Maybe… not for a while.</p>

<p>They ate slowly, enjoying each other&#x27;s company.</p>

<p>The tension that had hung between them earlier gradually melted away with every shared laugh, every story exchanged across the candlelit table.</p>

<p>For a while, it felt like the world outside didn&#x27;t exist—no gunshots, no chaos, no lingering fear. Just two people, sitting close, talking like old friends who had somehow wandered into something deeper.</p>

<p>By the time they finished their meal, it was nearing ten o&#x27;clock. The plates were mostly cleared, the wineglasses half-full, and the room was comfortably quiet.</p>

<p>Sasha let out a soft yawn and leaned back in her chair, the warmth of the food and the emotional toll of the day settling heavily on her.</p>

<p>The adrenaline from earlier had completely worn off, leaving her feeling physically drained, yet strangely content.</p>

<p>Ross noticed and stood up, stretching slightly. &quot;Looks like it&#x27;s time for bed,&quot; he said, his voice gentle. &quot;Just point me to the guest room—I&#x27;ll find my way. You go rest, Sasha. You&#x27;ve had a hell of a day.&quot;</p>

<p>But Sasha didn&#x27;t move.</p>

<p>Instead, she looked up at him, and something playful flickered in her eyes.</p>

<p>A slow smile tugged at her lips—a smile that didn&#x27;t match the exhaustion, but something else entirely.</p>

<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s scratch that,&quot; she said, her voice low and teasing. &quot;I have a better idea.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross raised an eyebrow, curious. &quot;Oh?&quot;</p>

<p>Without answering, Sasha stood and walked over to him.</p>

<p>That same smile—mischievous, warm, and a little daring—decorated her face as she reached for his hand.</p>

<p>&quot;Come with me,&quot; she said simply.</p>

<p>Ross didn&#x27;t resist. He let her take his hand, and she led him down the short hallway to her bedroom.</p>

<p>The lights were dim, casting long shadows across the walls. The bed sat waiting—soft sheets, fluffed pillows, the scent of lavender still lingering faintly in the air.</p>

<p>They stood at the foot of it for a moment, facing each other in silence.</p>

<p>No words were needed.</p>

<p>Sasha stepped closer, her hands brushing gently against his chest. Her gaze was steady, inviting.</p>

<p>She wasn&#x27;t uncertain, and she wasn&#x27;t rushing either. This wasn&#x27;t about gratitude or adrenaline or needing to feel something after nearly losing everything. This was a choice.</p>

<p>And Ross understood.</p>

<p>Anything could happen from here on out.</p>

<p>And neither of them seemed in the mood to stop it.</p>
