<p>It was one of her favorite places in the refuge, a calm sanctuary away from the noise of survival and whispers of gossip.</p>

<p>The refuge, known as Aegis, was enormous—almost like a self-contained city.</p>

<p>It had everything one could need to live comfortably: a library, a gym, a cafeteria, a massive swimming pool, even a luxury cinema that still played old films to keep morale high.</p>

<p>At times, it felt less like a bunker and more like a luxury hotel hidden underground.</p>

<p>As she walked through the library aisles, her fingers lightly brushing the spines of old books, she spotted someone sitting quietly in a far corner.</p>

<p>Her steps slowed when she recognized him.</p>

<p>Ross.</p>

<p>He was alone, reading silently, his expression calm and unreadable.</p>

<p>The light filtering from the overhead lamps cast a soft glow over his face, making him look strangely peaceful—almost gentle, a far cry from the rumors surrounding his name.</p>

<p>Karen’s mother lingered for a moment, observing him quietly.</p>

<p>She wasn’t sure what to think of the man her daughter had chosen.</p>

<p>Yet, seeing him like this—calm, collected, lost in a book—made her wonder if perhaps there was more to him than what others said.</p>

<p>Without a word, she turned and walked to another section of the library, leaving Ross to his solitude.</p>

<p>For now, she would continue to trust her daughter’s choices... even if she didn’t fully understand them.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Two hours later, Marissa closed the last page of her book and leaned back in her chair.</p>

<p>The library was peaceful—soft lights, the faint hum of air conditioning, and the quiet rustle of pages being turned.</p>

<p>Yet, her mind wasn’t entirely on the story she’d just read.</p>

<p>Ever since she’d caught sight of Ross earlier, something about him had lingered in her thoughts.</p>

<p>His calm presence, his posture, the way he seemed lost in another world—it all stirred a strange curiosity in her.</p>

<p>After a few minutes of hesitation, she found herself glancing toward the far end of the room where he had been seated.</p>

<p>&quot;What am I even doing?&quot; she muttered under her breath, shaking her head.</p>

<p>Still, her feet seemed to move on their own.</p>

<p>With quiet steps, she made her way through the aisles, the faint scent of old books and polished wood filling the air.</p>

<p>When she finally reached the corner, her heart gave a startled flutter—Ross was still there.</p>

<p>Sitting exactly where he’d been before, a thick hardcover open in his hands, his expression calm and focused.</p>

<p>The light from the overhead lights fell perfectly on his features, highlighting the sharp line of his jaw and the depth of his eyes.</p>

<p>She froze.</p>

<p>He’s still here?</p>

<p>For a brief moment, she considered turning around and walking away before he noticed her, but it was too late.</p>

<p>Ross lifted his head. Their eyes met.</p>

<p>&quot;Ah...&quot; Marissa’s breath caught. Time seemed to stop.</p>

<p>Up close, Ross was far more striking than she’d imagined—his face was clean-cut, his gaze steady yet oddly magnetic.</p>

<p>For a second, she swore she felt him undressing her with just his eyes, his quiet confidence washing over her like heat.</p>

<p>Her pulse quickened, her palms grew damp, and she could barely remember how to breathe.</p>

<p>&quot;Marissa, right?&quot; Ross said, his voice low and smooth, carrying easily in the stillness of the library.</p>

<p>He smiled, a faint curve of his lips that made her heart skip again.</p>

<p>He stood and closed the book gently before walking toward her.</p>

<p>Up close, his presence was overwhelming.</p>

<p>She could smell him now—something fresh and manly, a mix of clean soap, wood, and warmth.</p>

<p>It wasn’t overpowering, but it was enough to make her head swim.</p>

<p>&quot;Y-yes,&quot; she managed, her voice barely more than a whisper as he extended his hand.</p>

<p>She took it automatically, feeling the warmth of his skin against hers.</p>

<p>His grip was firm but gentle, and for some reason, she couldn’t let go right away.</p>

<p>Ross’s eyes lingered on her face for a second longer before he said softly,</p>

<p>&quot;Nice to finally meet you, Marissa.&quot;</p>

<p>Her heart thudded in her chest.</p>

<p>Finally meet me? she thought, dazed.</p>

<p>She wanted to ask what he meant, but her tongue felt heavy.</p>

<p>Instead, she could only nod, still holding his hand as if in a trance, wondering how a simple meeting could make her feel so unsteady inside.</p>

<p>A soft, incredulous breath was all that escaped Marissa’s lips.</p>

<p>&quot;...&quot;. Words had utterly failed her, receding like a tide and leaving her mentally stranded.</p>

<p>Ross, of course, was a man who read silences as clearly as headlines, and he knew precisely when to capitalize.</p>

<p>A subtle, knowing smile touched his eyes more than his lips, and he seamlessly stepped into the space her silence had created.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s the first time I’ve seen you here,&quot; he began, his voice a low, pleasant rumble that seemed to absorb the surrounding quiet of the bookstore.</p>

<p>His gaze was intent, making her feel like the only other person in the world. &quot;Do you read a lot?&quot;</p>

<p>Marissa managed a slight nod, her fingers tightening around the spine of the novel she held. &quot;I... yes. When I can.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I myself am fond of the old masters,&quot; Ross continued, gesturing vaguely toward the classic literature section.</p>

<p>&quot;There’s a certain weight to their prose, a permanence. But I have a soft spot for discovering new voices, too.&quot; He plucked a book from a nearby display, examined it with a critical yet appreciative eye, and set it back with a quiet thud.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s like meeting someone new. You never know what world you’re about to step into.&quot;</p>

<p>That small, seemingly innocuous observation was the key that unlocked the conversation.</p>

<p>What began as polite, hesitant replies from Marissa soon blossomed into a genuine exchange.</p>

<p>They drifted from books to travel, from travel to a shared, wry amusement about the absurdities of modern life.</p>

<p>Ross was a skilled conversationalist, never monopolizing, but guiding and probing with thoughtful questions that made her feel fascinating.</p>

<p>He listened not just to her words, but to the spaces between them.</p>
