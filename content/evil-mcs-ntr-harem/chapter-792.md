<p>A small group began to gather around the TV, already throwing out random ideas.</p>

<p>&quot;What about a horror film? We got lots of beautiful girls here to cast, lots of screaming, not much acting needed.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Rom-com might be easier. We’ve got couples in the house. We can build around real chemistry.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Nah, action! Let’s do stunts, fight scenes—get physical!&quot;</p>

<p>Corey tuned out the chatter for a moment, his mind elsewhere.</p>

<p>He wasn’t worried about acting or the camera. He was thinking about Cara.</p>

<p>The moment this task dropped, he knew she’d be part of it too—and that meant Big D would find a way to get close to her under the excuse of rehearsing, acting, or building chemistry.</p>

<p>&quot;Shit...&quot; he muttered under his breath.</p>

<p>This task wasn’t just about teamwork or creativity anymore.</p>

<p>It was another battlefield—and the stakes were growing by the hour.</p>

<p>Back in the living area, one of the producers entered with a stack of printed instructions and props.</p>

<p>A production kit had been wheeled in as well—costumes, camera gear, a few lighting rigs, and sound equipment.</p>

<p>&quot;Oh damn... this is legit,&quot; someone whispered, eyes wide.</p>

<p>&quot;Three days,&quot; the producer said, smiling.</p>

<p>&quot;Plan your scenes, cast your roles, write your script, rehearse, shoot, and edit. Everything’s in your hands now.&quot;</p>

<p>The room went silent again.</p>

<p>Then, slowly but surely, the wheels started turning.</p>

<p>A couple of hours later, after much debate and a few disagreements, the team finally settled on their genre: romantic comedy.</p>

<p>It wasn’t the most original idea, but it was simple, relatable, and easy to execute within the confines of the house.</p>

<p>The setting itself gave them all they needed—cozy corners, awkward run-ins, group dynamics, and just enough drama to pull something interesting off.</p>

<p>The premise was straightforward.</p>

<p>A group of close friends decide to take a vacation together, thinking it would be a relaxing escape.</p>

<p>But as the days pass, secrets begin to unravel, misunderstandings snowball, and unspoken feelings rise to the surface.</p>

<p>The story would center on a couple—best friends who were just on the verge of becoming more—whose relationship is put to the test as the chaos unfolds.</p>

<p>Everyone seemed content with the direction, gathering their props, organizing the shoot locations inside the house, and handing out roles.</p>

<p>Laughter echoed through the living room as someone tried to choreograph a scene involving spilled drinks and mistaken confessions.</p>

<p>For a moment, the atmosphere felt lighthearted and united.</p>

<p>Then Ross finally spoke up.</p>

<p>&quot;This isn’t going to turn out well,&quot; he said calmly, arms folded across his chest.</p>

<p>&quot;Seth’s a decent actor, I’ll give him that. But directing? That’s a whole other game. He’s not ready for it. I could take over. I’ve got experience in this field.&quot;</p>

<p>His words cut through the buzz of conversation like a blade. The room went quiet almost instantly.</p>

<p>Everyone turned toward him. Tension brewed in the silence.</p>

<p>A few exchanged uneasy glances, while others looked away, unsure of what to say.</p>

<p>Ross’s confidence was undeniable, but so was his intimidating presence.</p>

<p>The house had grown used to his unpredictable nature and domineering aura, and not everyone liked it.</p>

<p>Only his women looked calm, sitting comfortably as if they already knew the outcome.</p>

<p>They didn’t even flinch.</p>

<p>They had seen this before—Ross taking control, reshaping everything around him with sheer will.</p>

<p>The first to speak up was Corey, the de facto leader of the project and the one who had put the most effort into organizing things.</p>

<p>He met Ross’s gaze and forced a smile, though his jaw was slightly clenched.</p>

<p>&quot;Thanks, Big D,&quot; Corey said, trying to keep things cordial.</p>

<p>&quot;But we’ve got it handled. The group already agreed on the plan. I think most of us are satisfied with how it’s shaping up.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross didn’t argue. He just gave a small shrug, his expression unreadable.</p>

<p>He knew how these things worked. No need to force his way in. Not yet.</p>

<p>&quot;I see,&quot; he said simply, then turned his attention elsewhere—specifically to Cara, who was helping adjust a prop in the background.</p>

<p>His gaze lingered a moment too long, and a small smirk tugged at the corner of his lips.</p>

<p>Her laugh, the way she moved, the curve of her back as she bent over to fix something—it all caught his attention effortlessly.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t mind being brushed off. He had patience.</p>

<p>He didn’t need to push; he just needed to wait.</p>

<p>Opportunities always came to those who watched quietly.</p>

<p>And he was very good at watching.</p>

<p>Soon enough, something would go wrong.</p>

<p>A scene would fall apart, someone would mess up, and the group would start looking for a solution.</p>

<p>When that moment came, he’d step in—cool, calm, and capable—and they’d welcome him with open arms.</p>

<p>And Cara? Ross was confident. She wouldn’t resist him for long.</p>

<p>Very soon, everything he wanted would be within reach.</p>

<p>Evening fell, and the group finally gathered in the main room to begin filming.</p>

<p>The lights were set up hastily, casting uneven shadows against the walls.</p>

<p>Someone held a boom mic overhead while another balanced the camera on a borrowed tripod.</p>

<p>&quot;Lights.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Camera.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Action.&quot;</p>

<p>With the cue given, the first scene began.</p>

<p>At first, spirits were high.</p>

<p>Laughter bubbled up between takes, and some of the cast threw themselves into their roles with wide smiles and dramatic gestures.</p>

<p>The opening scene was supposed to be a lighthearted moment between friends—easy, low-stakes.</p>

<p>But the problems started almost immediately.</p>

<p>&quot;What the fuck are you doing, Hugh? Focus!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Put more emphasis on your facial expression! You look like a corpse!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Alice, that’s not right! You missed the cue again. Start over!&quot;</p>

<p>Seth’s voice, which had begun cool, collected, and full of creative excitement, slowly lost its luster.</p>

<p>He’d taken the role of director seriously, determined to prove he could lead the group and make something worth watching.</p>

<p>But as the hours dragged on, that enthusiasm gave way to visible exasperation.</p>

<p>Take after take went wrong. Lines were mumbled or forgotten.</p>

<p>Blocking was all over the place.</p>
