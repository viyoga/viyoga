<p>By the time dessert arrived, Amanda realized the evening had flown by.</p>

<p>The quartet in the corner had shifted to a slower tune, soft jazz drifting through the air like smoke. Ross set down his glass and stood, extending his hand toward her.</p>

<p>&quot;May I have this dance?&quot;</p>

<p>Amanda blinked. &quot;Here? There’s not even a dance floor.&quot;</p>

<p>He glanced around. &quot;Then we’ll make one.&quot;</p>

<p>For a moment, she hesitated. But then her fingers slid into his, and he guided her to a small open space near their table.</p>

<p>The other guests barely looked up, too absorbed in their own lives.</p>

<p>The world felt narrowed down to just the two of them.</p>

<p>The dance was simple, slow. Ross’s hand rested at the small of her back, his other holding hers gently.</p>

<p>Amanda followed his lead, uncertain at first but quickly finding rhythm.</p>

<p>His touch wasn’t demanding—it was warm, steady. Comforting.</p>

<p>&quot;This isn’t what I expected,&quot; she said quietly, half to herself.</p>

<p>&quot;Good or bad?&quot; he asked.</p>

<p>She looked up into his eyes, then quickly looked away. &quot;I haven’t decided yet.&quot;</p>

<p>He chuckled softly. &quot;That’s fair.&quot;</p>

<p>When the song ended, they lingered for a moment before returning to the table, hearts beating a little faster, breaths a little shallower.</p>

<p>The rest of the evening passed in a kind of haze—comfortable, unhurried.</p>

<p>And when the check came, Ross paid without a second thought.</p>

<p>&quot;I’ll take you home,&quot; he said, already on his feet.</p>

<p>Amanda opened her mouth to protest but stopped.</p>

<p>There was something about his presence tonight—gentle, nonintrusive—that made her want to say yes, if only just this once.</p>

<p>The car ride was quiet, the city lights casting golden patterns across the windshield.</p>

<p>Amanda leaned her head slightly against the window, her thoughts swirling.</p>

<p>She still couldn’t quite figure him out. Was this the real Ross Oakley?</p>

<p>Or just another version he showed to women he wanted?</p>

<p>They pulled up in front of her building, and Ross stepped out to open the door for her.</p>

<p>He walked her to the front steps, the silence between them filled with things neither had yet dared to say.</p>

<p>&quot;Well,&quot; she said, unsure of how to end the night.</p>

<p>Ross leaned in—not bold, not pushy—and kissed her gently on the cheek. It was a brief touch, but it sent a soft warmth through her chest.</p>

<p>&quot;Goodnight, Amanda,&quot; he said with a small smile.</p>

<p>&quot;Goodnight,&quot; she replied, her voice quieter than she expected.</p>

<p>She watched him walk back to the car. He didn’t look back.</p>

<p>Amanda stood there for a long moment, one hand brushing against her cheek where he’d kissed her.</p>

<p>It had been a simple night. No grand declarations. No promises.</p>

<p>And yet, something inside her had shifted.</p>

<p>She turned slowly and walked into her apartment, closing the door behind her.</p>

<p>Upstairs, as she slipped out of the red dress and into something soft and loose, Amanda caught her reflection in the mirror.</p>

<p>Her lips were curved in the faintest smile, and her eyes... they were still glowing.</p>

<p>She lay in bed that night unable to sleep, staring at the ceiling, her thoughts circling one quiet truth.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley wasn’t at all what she expected.</p>

<p>And that might be the most dangerous thing of all.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Another week passed, and once again, Amanda received a message from Ross. A simple invitation.</p>

<p>No pressure. No assumptions. Just another friendly outing—like all the others.</p>

<p>She stared at the screen for a full minute before responding.</p>

<p>Yes.</p>

<p>The answer came too quickly. Again.</p>

<p>Amanda groaned, tossing her phone on the bed as she sat down, clutching a pillow to her chest. &quot;I’m such an easy girl. I shouldn’t have done that,&quot; she whispered to herself.</p>

<p>And yet... her heart fluttered at the thought of seeing him again.</p>

<p>This had become a pattern now. A rhythm of sorts. An unspoken agreement between them.</p>

<p>What had started as a one-time dinner had turned into a full-blown routine—an emotional habit that was getting harder and harder to break.</p>

<p>They saw each other at least once a week. Sometimes twice. Always casual. Always &quot;just friends.&quot;</p>

<p>But it never felt casual to Amanda. Not anymore.</p>

<p>There had been movie nights, where they shared popcorn and soft glances in the dark.</p>

<p>Rooftop bars with glittering city lights and whispered jokes over cocktails.</p>

<p>Dance clubs where Ross would offer his hand and hold her close without a hint of impropriety.</p>

<p>And dinners—so many dinners—where he would listen to her stories as if they were the most important thing in the world.</p>

<p>Not once had he crossed the line. Not once had he touched her inappropriately.</p>

<p>He was always respectful, always patient. That, more than anything, was what made it so confusing.</p>

<p>Because now... she wanted him to.</p>

<p>Amanda had spent weeks trying to bury her feelings under excuses.</p>

<p>It’s just admiration... it’s just loneliness... it’s just the thrill. But the truth had become harder to ignore with every passing day.</p>

<p>She was falling in love with him.</p>

<p>Not the powerful billionaire, not the tabloid icon, not the man with wives and fame and mystery.</p>

<p>She was falling for Ross. The man who knew how she liked her tea.</p>

<p>Who remembered her birthday without a reminder. Who texted her silly memes at midnight just to make her laugh.</p>

<p>The man who looked at her like she was a secret he couldn’t wait to explore.</p>

<p>And it was eating her alive.</p>

<p>She had a boyfriend. He had multiple wives. Yet here they were, orbiting each other like two stars waiting to collide.</p>

<p>It all came to a head on a cool Friday evening.</p>

<p>They had just finished a casual dinner at a quiet little spot Ross had discovered.</p>

<p>They walked along the cobbled sidewalk, passing quiet cafés and glowing streetlamps.</p>

<p>Amanda could barely hear what Ross was saying anymore. Her mind was too loud.</p>

<p>Without thinking, she stopped walking.</p>

<p>Ross noticed immediately. &quot;Amanda?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Do you like me, Ross?&quot; she asked, voice tight. Her heart pounded so loud it echoed in her ears.</p>
