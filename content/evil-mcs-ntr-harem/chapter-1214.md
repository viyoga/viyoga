<p>One week had stretched into a tense, repetitive purgatory.</p>

<p>Every night, without fail, the same ritual played out behind the thin partition wall that separated everyone’s bunk from Sebastian and Bella’s quarters.</p>

<p>It began quietly enough—murmurs, soft laughter, the rustle of sheets—then built steadily into something primal and unapologetic.</p>

<p>The bedframe would start its steady, rhythmic thumping against the shared wall, slow at first, then faster, harder, like a heartbeat accelerating toward collapse.</p>

<p>Bella’s voice followed: breathy gasps turning into sharp cries, then full-throated moans that climbed higher with every passing hour.</p>

<p>Ross never spoke much—his answers came in low, guttural sounds, satisfied rumbles, the occasional growled command that made the air feel thicker.</p>

<p>Sometimes Bella laughed through it, delirious, sometimes she begged, sometimes she simply shattered into wordless sound.</p>

<p>The barracks had given the whole performance a name by day four: &quot;The Midnight Symphony.&quot;</p>

<p>By day seven it was simply &quot;the show.&quot;</p>

<p>Soldiers swapped timestamps and highlights over lukewarm coffee the way they once traded stories about firefights.</p>

<p>&quot;She hit that high note at 0217—swear to God it rattled my locker.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;He flipped her twice last night, you could tell by the change in the rhythm.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I timed it—three hours straight before they even slowed down.&quot;</p>

<p>The details grew more lurid with each retelling: Ross’s cock described in increasingly mythical proportions, Bella’s stamina matched only by her volume, the two of them fucking like the war outside didn’t exist, like the only mission left was to ruin each other until dawn.</p>

<p>Sebastian endured it all in near-total silence.</p>

<p>He still rose early in the morning. Still shaved with mechanical precision.</p>

<p>Still ran his PT laps in perfect formation. But the light behind his eyes had dimmed.</p>

<p>He ate alone at the end of the farthest table, back to the room.</p>

<p>When someone tried small talk he answered in monosyllables, then excused himself.</p>

<p>The hurt was so obvious it felt like a violation to witness it—his shoulders carried a permanent hunch, his jaw stayed clenched even in sleep.</p>

<p>No one joked about it in his earshot anymore.</p>

<p>Not because they’d suddenly grown compassion, but because the sight of a man quietly bleeding inside made the laughter taste sour.</p>

<p>June watched it all and could do exactly nothing.</p>

<p>Ross had aced every test the evaluators could throw at him. He passed them all.</p>

<p>June’s only countermeasure was exhaustion.</p>

<p>She loaded him with missions that would have broken lesser men.</p>

<p>Cross-country convoys hauling classified medical isotopes through contested backroads.</p>

<p>High-priority resupply drops to other bases.,</p>

<p>Emergency extractions where he drove blacked-out Humvees through zero-visibility sandstorms with bleeding soldiers screaming in the back.</p>

<p>She paired him with the heaviest trailers, the longest routes, the tightest deadlines.</p>

<p>If he was bone-tired, maybe he’d collapse into sleep instead of into Bella.</p>

<p>Maybe the symphony would finally go quiet for one goddamn night.</p>

<p>It never did.</p>

<p>Ross rolled back in after thirty-six-hour runs looking like he’d just stepped out of a barber shop.</p>

<p>Dust on his boots, yes. Sweat stains under his arms, sure.</p>

<p>But his eyes were clear, his movements economical, his smirk still in place when he caught her watching him from the ops tent.</p>

<p>He carried the fatigue like a medal instead of a burden.</p>

<p>He leaned against the hood of the nearest truck, arms crossed, uniform streaked with the red clay of three different states.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m starting to think I’ve become your favorite soldier on base, Commander.&quot; His voice carried that familiar half-teasing lilt, but there was a thread of real weariness underneath it now. &quot;I don’t know how much more of this abuse I can take and I’m not even a soldier in the first place.&quot;</p>

<p>June didn’t look up from the clipboard. She kept marking off damaged suspension components.</p>

<p>&quot;I know,&quot; she said, calm as ever. &quot;But your skill set is too valuable to waste. You’re the only person I trust to move critical cargo—medical, intel, personnel—without turning it into a rolling disaster. You’ve pulled off rescues no one else would even attempt. You’ve saved lives I can’t even count anymore. So yes, Ross. I’m going to keep using you. Until you tell me you’re done.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross watched her for a long moment.</p>

<p>The teasing faded from his expression, replaced by something quieter, almost respectful.</p>

<p>He pushed off the truck, stepped closer.</p>

<p>&quot;When I finally drop,&quot; he said, voice low, &quot;you’re buying the first round. And the second. And maybe the third.&quot;</p>

<p>June let the corner of her mouth lift—just barely.</p>

<p>&quot;Deal.&quot;</p>

<p>He gave her a small nod, then turned and walked toward the barracks, already rolling his shoulders like a man shaking off the day instead of carrying it.</p>

<p>That night the sounds started again. Right on schedule.</p>

<p>The thump of the headboard. Bella’s rising voice. Ross’s steady, unrelenting rhythm.</p>

<p>June lay on her bed, eyes fixed on the ceiling cracks that had become as familiar as constellations.</p>

<p>She didn’t reach for her headphones. Didn’t try to drown it out.</p>

<p>She just listened.</p>

<p>And somewhere between the third hour and the fourth—when Bella’s cries turned raw and pleading, when Ross answered with that low, possessive sound that vibrated through the wall—June felt the exhaustion finally catch up.</p>

<p>Not enough to sleep.</p>

<p>But enough to close her eyes and let it hurt.</p>

<p>Because some nights, that was all there was left to do.</p>

<p>June had once believed that the situation in the world would slowly but surely improve.</p>

<p>After all the sacrifices made and the countless lives lost, it felt only natural to cling to the hope that the worst had already passed.</p>

<p>That fragile hope shattered the day the report arrived.</p>

<p>The moment June finished reading it, her blood ran cold. The implications were too vast, too terrifying to ignore—news so grave that it threatened every surviving nation at once.</p>

<p>&quot;Oh no...&quot; June muttered, her voice trembling as her hands tightened around the document.</p>

<p>The room felt suddenly smaller, the air heavier, as the full weight of the information settled on her shoulders.</p>

<p>She knew, even then, that the world was about to be thrown into further chaos once more.</p>
