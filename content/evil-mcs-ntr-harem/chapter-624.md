<p>But Thomas didn&#x27;t relent. He needed to believe there was still a way out.</p>

<p>He repeated his threats louder, angrier, throwing out names, codes, high-level contacts—anything that might spark fear or hesitation.</p>

<p>But Ross simply kept walking, unbothered, his pace calm and unwavering.</p>

<p>They reached a secure door.</p>

<p>Ross paused, his hand pressing against the scanner. With a soft beep and a hiss, it slid open.</p>

<p>Inside, the lights were dim, casting a warm glow on the small, clean room.</p>

<p>There, lying curled up on the huge bed, was Jade.</p>

<p>Her chest rose and fell gently with sleep. She was untouched by all of this—safe. For now.</p>

<p>Ross gazed at her quietly.</p>

<p>&quot;She didn&#x27;t let me down,&quot; he murmured to himself, almost fondly. &quot;Still untouched. Still pure.&quot;</p>

<p>Then he turned to Thomas, a smirk tugging at his lips.</p>

<p>&quot;But you?&quot; Ross said, voice dropping into something cold and sharp. &quot;You had me entertained for a while. I&#x27;ll give you that.&quot;</p>

<p>He chuckled softly. The kind of laugh you hear when the joke has ended and the consequences begin.</p>

<p>The door closed behind him with a soft click, sealing Jade off from the horrors to come.</p>

<p>He&#x27;d make sure she remembered none of this.</p>

<p>There was no need to burden her with trauma she didn&#x27;t deserve. She was innocent.</p>

<p>Thomas, on the other hand?</p>

<p>He would carry every memory—every second—until the very end.</p>

<p>Ross turned toward the next door. It was plain, unmarked, ominously silent.</p>

<p>He placed his hand on it, and it opened for him like the maw of some beast inviting its master in.</p>

<p>Without a word, Ross stepped through.</p>

<p>Thomas followed, still paralyzed, still floating—a condemned man unable even to resist his own execution.</p>

<p>He passed through the threshold like a corpse being delivered to its coffin.</p>

<p>The door slid shut behind them with a final, echoing hiss.</p>

<p>And in that room—sealed away from the world—justice would not be found.</p>

<p>Only punishment.</p>

<p>Thud!</p>

<p>Thomas slammed into the far wall with the force of an invisible hand, his body sticking there like a trapped insect beneath glass.</p>

<p>His limbs refused to budge. It was as if he&#x27;d been fused to the wall itself—helpless, paralyzed, and entirely at Ross&#x27;s mercy.</p>

<p>The door behind them whispered shut, the soft hiss of compressed air sealing off the world outside.</p>

<p>They were alone now. There would be no escape. No witnesses. No help.</p>

<p>Ross ignored him for a moment, stretching his arms above his head before leaping effortlessly onto the room&#x27;s pristine bed.</p>

<p>He sighed in genuine pleasure as he sank into the premium mattress, running his fingers over the finely woven sheets.</p>

<p>&quot;Mmm… high thread count. Custom foam. Reinforced frame.</p>

<p>They didn&#x27;t spare any expense, did they?&quot; Ross murmured, lying back with the satisfaction of a king in his palace. &quot;Government money really is magic.&quot;</p>

<p>He turned his head and looked at Thomas, still glued to the wall like a man-sized painting of defeat.</p>

<p>The sharp suit. The clenched jaw. The storm behind those eyes.</p>

<p>Thomas was still trying to move—Ross could see the tension in his neck, the twitching of muscle trying to rebel against whatever unnatural force bound him.</p>

<p>&quot;You look ridiculous, you know,&quot; Ross said casually, resting an arm behind his head.</p>

<p>&quot;All that training. All that discipline. And now? You&#x27;re just… decor. Very expensive decor, mind you. But still.&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas could speak—barely.</p>

<p>&quot;You won&#x27;t get away with this,&quot; he growled, his voice thick with restrained rage. &quot;They&#x27;ll come for you.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross smirked, not even bothering to sit up. &quot;Oh, I&#x27;m counting on it. But they&#x27;ll be too late, of course. They always are.&quot;</p>

<p>His smile faded, replaced by something colder.</p>

<p>&quot;The U.S. government,&quot; he said thoughtfully, as if savoring the name.</p>

<p>&quot;It looks like I&#x27;ll have to shake their tree a little harder. Cause a few cracks in their little illusion of control. I&#x27;ve been too lenient lately. Time to fix this issue permanently this time.&quot;</p>

<p>He stood, suddenly serious, his footsteps soundless on the floor as he approached Thomas.</p>

<p>&quot;Now, let&#x27;s talk about you.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross stopped just in front of him, so close Thomas could feel the subtle heat radiating off his body, the strange pressure that seemed to distort the air around him.</p>

<p>&quot;Thomas Trump,&quot; Ross said, his voice low, deliberate.</p>

<p>&quot;Brilliant young lawyer. First in your class. Topped the bar with a perfect score. You were supposed to change the world—make headlines, sit on the Supreme Court one day.&quot;</p>

<p>He tilted his head, eyes gleaming with something unreadable.</p>

<p>&quot;But the world changed you, didn&#x27;t it? You offended the wrong people. You played your game too close to the fire. The mafia took notice. And in the end, your family paid the price.&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas&#x27;s face darkened. The mention of that night brought a flicker of something into his eyes—pain, loss, hatred.</p>

<p>&quot;A car bomb,&quot; Ross continued softly, like a bedtime story. &quot;Mom. Dad. Your girl. All gone. In one second. Just like that.&quot;</p>

<p>He snapped his fingers for emphasis.</p>

<p>&quot;Boom.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross&#x27;s tone turned wistful, even sympathetic.</p>

<p>&quot;You wanted justice. But there wasn&#x27;t any. So you became something else. Traded in your law books for guns. Entered the Special Forces. Killed your way to revenge. You became the government&#x27;s favorite weapon.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross gave a soft laugh.</p>

<p>&quot;Pity. All that rage, all that effort… and here you are. Stuck to a wall, like a poster of a man who used to matter. But since your men gave me a bracelet before, it&#x27;s my turn to give you a gift. Two of them.&quot;</p>

<p>He paused, stepping back slightly, just enough to gesture with a casual flick of his wrist.</p>

<p>The air shimmered.</p>

<p>A moment later, they arrived—two stunning women who seemed to materialize from nothing, their beauty so surreal it made the room feel colder by contrast.</p>

<p>They stood with unnatural grace, eyes empty of life and then with every breath after, their eyes blinked as if confused at their surroundings.</p>

<p>Ross turned toward them with the ease of a man stepping into a long-planned performance.</p>

<p>&quot;I&#x27;ve been thinking,&quot; he said slowly. &quot;Torture is an art, Thomas. And every artist needs a muse.&quot;</p>

<p>He turned back toward his captive, smiling.</p>

<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s see how long that strong little mind of yours lasts.&quot;</p>
