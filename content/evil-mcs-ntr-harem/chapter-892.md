<p>Their combined charm was overwhelming, and under the haze of alcohol, Sarah’s resistance crumbled piece by piece.</p>

<p>She told herself she wouldn’t actually go through with it, that she would just pretend, but deep inside she knew she was being cornered.</p>

<p>Refusing once was brave; refusing three of them, again and again, would make her look stubborn and ungrateful.</p>

<p>And so, after half an hour of playful pressure disguised as sisterly encouragement, Sarah’s lips curved into a weak, resigned smile.</p>

<p>She set her glass down, feeling her pulse hammering in her throat.</p>

<p>&quot;Fine... just once. Then we drop it,&quot; she murmured, her voice barely audible over the music.</p>

<p>The three managers exchanged knowing glances and victorious smiles, as though they had won a game.</p>

<p>They straightened her clothes, adjusted her hair, and whispered last-minute encouragements into her ear.</p>

<p>And so it was that thirty minutes later, Sarah stood up.</p>

<p>Her legs were unsteady from both nerves and drink, but she forced herself forward.</p>

<p>The room seemed to quiet as she walked, or perhaps it was only in her imagination.</p>

<p>Every step toward Ross’s table felt heavier than the last, as though her shoes had been filled with lead.</p>

<p>Ross sat at the center of attention, surrounded by executives and sycophants, laughter booming around him as glasses clinked.</p>

<p>He looked relaxed, powerful, untouchable—a king at his throne.</p>

<p>And now, Sarah was being pushed forward to play the role of entertainment.</p>

<p>Her heart pounded, her palms sweated, and yet she forced herself to keep going.</p>

<p>All eyes seemed to follow her, curious, expectant.</p>

<p>And when Ross finally turned his head and saw her approach, his smile froze—just slightly—before his eyes narrowed with interest.</p>

<p>Sarah didn’t hesitate—she wanted to get this over with quickly before she lost her nerve.</p>

<p>The place was pulsing with heavy bass, lights flashing across the packed dance floor, and the smell of alcohol and perfume thick in the air.</p>

<p>Pushing past swaying bodies, she made her way toward Ross.</p>

<p>When she finally reached him, she leaned in close, her lips almost brushing against his ear as she spoke, her voice firm but urgent.</p>

<p>&quot;Boss, do you have a minute? I’ve got something important to tell you.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross turned, his sharp eyes catching hers even in the dim lighting.</p>

<p>His expression softened with a faint smile.</p>

<p>&quot;Sure. Come with me, Sarah. And don’t call me boss—call me Ross.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Yes, boss.&quot; Sarah answered automatically, then gave a small, teasing smile, almost apologetic.</p>

<p>It was an open secret that Ross had long wanted everyone to call him by his given name, but over the years not a single person had followed his request.</p>

<p>Out of respect—or perhaps reverence—they all insisted on calling him boss.</p>

<p>He had earned the title.</p>

<p>With dozens of wives and influence that stretched across continents, Ross had more power than anyone she knew, yet what truly set him apart was his work.</p>

<p>His philanthropy, his vision, and the sheer scope of what he had achieved went beyond anything history had ever seen.</p>

<p>To many, he wasn’t just a leader—he was a living legend.</p>

<p>Ross gave her a knowing look but said nothing more.</p>

<p>He placed a guiding hand at the small of her back and began leading her through the press of bodies.</p>

<p>Wherever he walked, the crowd seemed to part naturally, as if his presence alone demanded space.</p>

<p>Sarah followed close behind, her pulse quickening with every step.</p>

<p>They left the thundering music behind, slipping into a quieter hallway lined with offices.</p>

<p>The silence felt almost jarring after the chaos of the club floor.</p>

<p>Ross pushed open a door to one of the empty rooms and held it for her.</p>

<p>She stepped inside, nerves bubbling in her chest.</p>

<p>The office was dimly lit, with a sleek desk, leather chairs, and the faint scent of cigars lingering in the air.</p>

<p>Closing the door behind them, Ross leaned casually against the desk, his arms crossed, his gaze steady on her.</p>

<p>&quot;Alright, Sarah,&quot; he said, his voice lower now, stripped of the noise and distractions.</p>

<p>&quot;We’re alone. What’s so important that you had to fight through all that noise just to tell me?&quot;</p>

<p>Sarah swallowed, suddenly aware of how close they were, how intense his eyes looked when nothing else competed for her attention.</p>

<p>Her fingers twisted nervously, but she forced herself to meet his gaze. This was it.</p>

<p>Although Sarah could have leaned in and stolen the kiss—just to follow through with the challenge—the thought alone made her heart pound with guilt.</p>

<p>The temptation was there, yes, but the truth was stronger: she was a happily married woman, and she wasn’t about to betray her vows, not even for a harmless dare.</p>

<p>Instead, she took a deep breath, steadied her nerves, and decided to come clean.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m sorry, boss,&quot; she confessed, her voice dropping low.</p>

<p>&quot;The managers gave me a challenge to kiss you. I...&quot; Her lips trembled slightly as the words faltered. She couldn’t bring herself to finish.</p>

<p>Ross leaned back, his expression calm and unreadable, yet his eyes revealed the faintest spark of amusement.</p>

<p>He picked up where she had left off, his tone smooth.</p>

<p>&quot;I see. That’s fine then. Let’s just stay here for a couple of minutes. When you go back, you can tell them you did it. That should be enough to keep them satisfied.&quot;</p>

<p>He crossed the room with the ease of someone who owned every place he walked into.</p>

<p>Sliding into the chair behind the desk, he pulled out his phone and began idly scrolling, his demeanor casual, as though none of this mattered to him in the slightest.</p>

<p>Sarah lingered by the door for a moment, unsure whether to stay or excuse herself.</p>

<p>But Ross’s composure had a gravity of its own, pulling her in.</p>

<p>She walked across the room and took a seat in one of the leather chairs opposite him.</p>

<p>Her back was straight, her hands clasped neatly together on her lap, but her eyes refused to behave.</p>

<p>They kept drifting toward him.</p>
