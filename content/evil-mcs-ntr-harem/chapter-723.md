<p>It was maddening and was making Heaven crazy.</p>

<p>She had brushed it off at first, thinking it was just a temporary illness or maybe stress.</p>

<p>But one full day had passed, and her taste buds still betrayed her. It wasn’t normal.</p>

<p>She wasn’t sick. She was fine—at least physically.</p>

<p>Which left only one possibility.</p>

<p>Her eyes narrowed as her mind returned to one person.</p>

<p>That smug bastard with the arrogant smirk and outrageous confidence. Big D.</p>

<p>She stood up abruptly, her chair scraping back sharply against the floor, drawing a few glances her way. But she didn’t care.</p>

<p>She marched straight toward him, finding him seated casually near the lounge, sipping something as if the world was at peace.</p>

<p>He looked up just as she reached him, seemingly unsurprised.</p>

<p>&quot;What did you do to me?&quot; Heaven demanded, her voice low but sharp, cutting through the room like a knife.</p>

<p>Ross raised an eyebrow, his smile slow and infuriating.</p>

<p>&quot;Hello, Heaven,&quot; he said coolly, ignoring her question. &quot;Didn’t like your food?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Fix it,&quot; Heaven snapped, her voice low but trembling with fury.</p>

<p>&quot;Or I swear, I’ll summon the house boss and tell him everything. We’re all surrounded by cameras in this house. You think you can get away with this? You’ll end up in jail—or worse.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross didn’t flinch. If anything, her threat seemed to amuse him.</p>

<p>He leaned back leisurely in his seat, his fingers idly spinning the rim of his glass as he met her glare without so much as a blink. His voice, when it came, was maddeningly calm.</p>

<p>&quot;I doubt it,&quot; he said with a shrug. &quot;But by all means, let the house boss know. I’m not stopping you.&quot;</p>

<p>Heaven stared at him, her breath caught somewhere between a gasp and a scream.</p>

<p>That arrogance—how could someone look so relaxed while being accused of poisoning someone?</p>

<p>She spun on her heel and stormed out of the lounge area, fists clenched.</p>

<p>The silence left in her wake was thick, broken only by the whispering of the others.</p>

<p>&quot;Did he really do something to her?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Man, that Ross guy is weird...&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;She looked serious. She hasn’t eaten properly in days.&quot;</p>

<p>Within the hour, the house boss had been summoned, and with him came the house doctor, a stern-faced woman in her fifties who had seen her share of contestant meltdowns and psychosomatic episodes.</p>

<p>Heaven sat tensely on the couch while the doctor ran through her routine.</p>

<p>She checked her vitals, ran a quick neuro exam, even did a few taste tests using a kit that had sugar, salt, vinegar, and various food items.</p>

<p>Ross sat a few meters away, sipping his drink without a care in the world, watching it all unfold like a mildly entertaining sitcom.</p>

<p>After a thorough evaluation, the doctor stood and addressed the group.</p>

<p>&quot;There’s nothing physically wrong with you, Heaven,&quot; she said, professional but gentle. &quot;You’re in excellent health. No signs of foodborne illness, no toxins, no neurological abnormalities. Your taste buds are working perfectly. That leaves only one possibility—this is likely a psychological issue, stress-related. Not uncommon in high-pressure environments like this.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Are you saying I’m imagining this?&quot; Heaven asked, her voice tight, restrained.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m saying that sometimes, the mind can influence how the body perceives things,&quot; the doctor replied. &quot;If you’d like, we can remove you from the house for rest or psychological support.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No,&quot; Heaven said sharply. &quot;That won’t be necessary.&quot;</p>

<p>Because she knew she wasn’t crazy.</p>

<p>The second she stood up from the examination, she could feel their eyes on her—some with pity, some with curiosity, some with suspicion.</p>

<p>But one pair of eyes—his—held something else entirely. Triumph.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t gloat out loud. He didn’t have to. The way he looked at her said it all.</p>

<p>Heaven turned and walked past him, but not before pausing right beside his chair. She didn’t even look at him when she whispered:</p>

<p>&quot;I don’t know what you did... but I will find out.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross smiled, slow and confident, without looking up.</p>

<p>&quot;I hope you do,&quot; he murmured.</p>

<p>She walked away, her jaw clenched, her body shaking with a storm of anger, confusion, and something she hated to admit—fear.</p>

<p>This wasn’t just a prank or a fluke. It wasn’t some harmless trick to throw her off her game.</p>

<p>Ross had done something to her.</p>

<p>Something she couldn’t explain.</p>

<p>Something no one could trace.</p>

<p>And worst of all... something she couldn’t undo.</p>

<p>Not yet.</p>

<p>But she would figure it out sooner or later.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Thursday came, bringing with it a slow descent into madness.</p>

<p>Every time Ross stepped into the kitchen, Heaven could feel her willpower crumble just a little more.</p>

<p>The moment the first notes of sizzling oil or roasted spices hit the air, her senses betrayed her. Her mouth would fill with saliva.</p>

<p>Her nose would twitch as if trying to catch more of the scent.</p>

<p>Her stomach would tighten painfully, begging for something—anything—that could satisfy the void left by him.</p>

<p>And it wasn’t hunger. Not really.</p>

<p>She ate. She ate what others cooked.</p>

<p>She even tried delivery once, hoping the variety might shock her taste buds back to life.</p>

<p>But nothing worked.</p>

<p>No matter how expensive or beautifully presented the food was, it all tasted like sawdust to her.</p>

<p>The only thing that made her body react, that made her feel, was Ross’s cooking.</p>

<p>She hated it.</p>

<p>She hated him.</p>

<p>Hated the way he smirked when she entered the room, as if he knew exactly what she was going through.</p>

<p>Hated how he didn’t even try to flaunt it—he just cooked like it was no big deal, served himself a plate, and walked away.</p>

<p>Never offering her anything. Never even asking if she wanted a bite.</p>

<p>By mid-afternoon, she couldn’t focus on anything else.</p>

<p>People were talking to her, but she barely registered their words.</p>

<p>She smiled when expected, nodded on cue, but her mind was somewhere else entirely.</p>

<p>It was in the kitchen, picturing Ross standing over the stove, seasoning, tasting, plating.</p>

<p>She tried to fight it. Again.</p>

<p>She chewed gum, drank cold water, even washed her face with ice-cold towels.</p>

<p>Nothing worked.</p>

<p>By dinner, she couldn’t take it anymore.</p>
