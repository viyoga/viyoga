<p>Carrie’s mom’s words struck deep.</p>

<p>She wanted to argue, to insist she was being careful, but guilt crept in instead.</p>

<p>Because her mom wasn’t wrong—Carrie was sneaking around, and she hadn’t been completely honest.</p>

<p>In the end, all she could do was nod.</p>

<p>&quot;Okay, Mom.&quot;</p>

<p>But as she retreated to her room later that night, her thoughts swirled.</p>

<p>How could she tell her mother that the boy she was seeing wasn’t just some ordinary guy, but Ross—the Ross—whose name alone could send a crowd screaming?</p>

<p>If her mother knew, she wouldn’t just be cautious; she’d lock Carrie inside the house.</p>

<p>And yet, Carrie also knew she couldn’t keep lying forever.</p>

<p>That night, Carrie texted Ross about what had happened with her mother and how she was being pushed to reveal who he was.</p>

<p>She typed everything honestly, her thumbs moving quickly across the screen, then stared at her phone as if his reply would pop up instantly.</p>

<p>Though she was already twenty years old, she still lived under her parents’ roof, still followed their rules, still asked for permission far too often.</p>

<p>A part of her hated how reliant she remained, how little freedom she had compared to some of her classmates who were already working part-time jobs and living in shared apartments.</p>

<p>&quot;Maybe it’s time I found a place of my own,&quot; she muttered under her breath, staring at the dim glow of her phone.</p>

<p>The thought startled her. It wasn’t something she would normally even consider.</p>

<p>She had always been the type to stay close to home, close to her family, and especially close to her best friend.</p>

<p>That best friend had been her secret reason all these years—the girl she quietly loved but could never confess to.</p>

<p>Staying nearby had always meant keeping that thread of hope alive, no matter how faint.</p>

<p>But things were different now.</p>

<p>Ross had entered her life, and his presence had shifted everything.</p>

<p>She pulled up her banking app and scrolled through the numbers.</p>

<p>Ten thousand dollars—give or take. It wasn’t much, but it was something.</p>

<p>Enough to get started.</p>

<p>With that, she could rent a small studio or share a place, then pick up part-time work to keep herself afloat.</p>

<p>Her classmates did it, so why couldn’t she?</p>

<p>The more she thought about it, the more the idea made sense. Moving out meant freedom.</p>

<p>No more lying about &quot;group projects,&quot; no more sneaking home just before midnight.</p>

<p>She could come and go as she pleased, see Ross whenever she wanted, and start building a life that was truly her own.</p>

<p>Still, the thought scared her. Independence was alluring, but it also meant responsibility.</p>

<p>Bills. Rent. Balancing work with studies. And what would her parents think?</p>

<p>Would they be angry, disappointed? Would they see it as a betrayal?</p>

<p>Carrie hugged her knees to her chest, sitting in the quiet of her room.</p>

<p>For the first time, she realized how much Ross was changing her—not just her emotions, but the very way she thought about her future.</p>

<p>Before, she was a girl holding on to a hopeless love, content to stay in place.</p>

<p>Now, she was imagining steps forward, choices for herself.</p>

<p>Her phone buzzed in her hand, startling her. Ross had finally replied.</p>

<p>His reply was simple, but it struck Carrie like a bolt of lightning.</p>

<p>&quot;I’ll be there. But how are you going to introduce me to your parents? Tell me, Carrie—am I your boyfriend now?&quot;</p>

<p>Her lips parted as she stared at the message.</p>

<p>The words were straightforward, yet the meaning behind them made her chest tighten.</p>

<p>Boyfriend.</p>

<p>The word sounded heavier than she expected, like a label she had been waiting for but never dared to claim out loud.</p>

<p>Carrie held her phone for almost ten minutes, her fingers hovering above the keyboard, deleting and retyping over and over.</p>

<p>She thought about her mother’s sharp eyes, her father’s quiet authority, the questions they would ask.</p>

<p>She thought about Ross—the celebrity, the man who had captured her heart, the man with more wives than she could count.</p>

<p>Did she have the right to call him hers?</p>

<p>In the end, her heart won.</p>

<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>

<p>She whispered it as she pressed send, her voice trembling but her smile radiant.</p>

<p>It felt like stepping off a cliff and discovering she could fly.</p>

<p>Her phone rang almost immediately, Ross’s name flashing on the screen.</p>

<p>She answered without hesitation.</p>

<p>&quot;Carrie,&quot; his voice came through, low and full of warmth. She could almost see his smile. &quot;You don’t know how happy I am to hear that. You made my night.&quot;</p>

<p>Carrie giggled, her cheeks flushing. &quot;You sound like you’re the one blushing, not me.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross chuckled, and the sound filled her with a giddy warmth.</p>

<p>They fell into conversation easily, their words flowing late into the night.</p>

<p>Sometimes it was teasing, sometimes it was serious, but the undercurrent of happiness never left.</p>

<p>Ross was genuinely excited, and that excitement was contagious.</p>

<p>Carrie found herself smiling so much her cheeks began to hurt.</p>

<p>She knew, of course, about his other wives.</p>

<p>The knowledge lingered in the back of her mind, but tonight it didn’t matter.</p>

<p>Ross was the first man who had made her feel wanted, the first to make her believe she could be someone’s first choice, even if only in his heart for a moment.</p>

<p>The first love was always the strongest, and Carrie clung to that feeling.</p>

<p>Even though her first love didn’t work out, her first boyfriend, Ross, she hoped would turn out to be everything she had ever wished for.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>By the time the clock struck midnight, they had already made plans.</p>

<p>Ross would come to her city that weekend—not in disguise, not as a hidden lover, but openly.</p>

<p>He would meet her parents face-to-face.</p>

<p>When the call finally ended, Carrie lay in bed with her phone pressed against her chest, her mind racing.</p>

<p>She could hardly sleep.</p>

<p>Thoughts of Ross’s smile, the sound of his laughter, the way he called her name—all of it played in her head like a song she never wanted to end.</p>
