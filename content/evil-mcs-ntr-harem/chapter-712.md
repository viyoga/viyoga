<p>&quot;You should tell your boyfriend about us, Amanda. And soon. There’s no sense dragging this out. The longer it goes on, the more it’s going to hurt him,&quot; Ross said, his voice low but firm.</p>

<p>He was sitting back against the sofa, legs slightly spread, watching her with that calm, unreadable look he always wore when they touched on something serious.</p>

<p>They were already a month into their secret relationship—if it could even be called that—and the line they were toeing had long since disappeared.</p>

<p>Amanda stood by the window, arms folded, her thoughts churning.</p>

<p>Her boyfriend, Chris, was due back in a few days.</p>

<p>She had told herself—lied to herself—that this was just a mistake, a moment of weakness.</p>

<p>But a month had passed. And here she was, not wanting it to end.</p>

<p>&quot;I know,&quot; she whispered, barely loud enough to be heard. She exhaled slowly, the weight of her guilt sitting heavy in her chest. &quot;I’ll take care of it.&quot;</p>

<p>She turned away from the window, walked back toward Ross, and dropped onto the sofa beside him.</p>

<p>The silence between them was thick, not awkward but charged—like something unspoken was always hanging in the air.</p>

<p>Amanda leaned in and rested her head briefly against his shoulder. He didn’t speak.</p>

<p>He just let her be. That was one of the things she liked about Ross—he never pushed. Not unless he had to.</p>

<p>After a moment, she tilted her head up and looked at him, her fingers already moving toward his belt, wordlessly.</p>

<p>Ross raised an eyebrow. &quot;Right here?&quot; he asked, a small smirk playing on his lips.</p>

<p>She nodded, her voice barely above a murmur. &quot;No one’s going to see. And right now... I don’t want to think about anything else.&quot;</p>

<p>Her fingers worked quickly, unbuckling his belt, unzipping his pants.</p>

<p>She reached in and slowly pulled him out. Her breath hitched.</p>

<p>No matter how many times she saw it, the size of him still took her by surprise.</p>

<p>Amanda licked her lips, then lowered her head.</p>

<p>&quot;God...&quot; she murmured under her breath before taking him in.</p>

<p>Her mouth wrapped around him, her head bobbing gently at first, tongue swirling, eyes fluttering shut as she lost herself in the moment.</p>

<p>She couldn’t take all of him—not even close—but what she could manage was more than enough.</p>

<p>She used her hands to work what her mouth couldn’t reach, eager, hungry, as though this act was the only thing tethering her to sanity.</p>

<p>Ross leaned his head back, one hand drifting down to her hair. He didn’t force her, just held her lightly, his breathing growing heavier.</p>

<p>&quot;Mmm, just like that...&quot; he muttered.</p>

<p>Amanda moaned softly around him, the sound vibrating through him.</p>

<p>It was messy, intimate, addicting—and for those minutes, everything else faded away. No boyfriend.</p>

<p>No guilt. Just the two of them and the tension crackling between their bodies.</p>

<p>It didn’t take long. Ross tensed, and Amanda braced herself as the first warm spurt hit the back of her throat.</p>

<p>She swallowed without hesitation, keeping her mouth on him until he was done, until his fingers relaxed in her hair and he let out a long, satisfied breath.</p>

<p>She pulled back slowly, wiping the corner of her mouth with her thumb, then looked up at him.</p>

<p>&quot;That should keep you quiet for a bit,&quot; she teased with a half-smile.</p>

<p>Ross chuckled, reaching down to cup her cheek. &quot;You’re dangerous, you know that?&quot;</p>

<p>Amanda didn’t answer. She leaned in again, resting her cheek on his thigh, eyes closed.</p>

<p>For now, she didn’t want to think about consequences. She didn’t want to feel guilty.</p>

<p>She just wanted to be here, in this quiet moment, with him.</p>

<p>The secret relationship between Amanda and Ross continued behind closed doors, unspoken but undeniably real.</p>

<p>Every kiss, every night spent tangled together on her couch, made the lie she was living grow heavier on her shoulders.</p>

<p>And somewhere far across the ocean, a man still believed in the promises they once made.</p>

<p>He held onto them like a lifeline, unaware that the ground beneath his world was already crumbling.</p>

<p>He had counted down the days on a calendar taped to the inside of his locker.</p>

<p>Each red mark brought him closer to home—closer to her. In his mind, Amanda was still waiting. Still smiling. Still his.</p>

<p>Until the moment everything changed.</p>

<p>Standing on the deck of his ship under the pale light of early morning, he casually unlocked his phone during a break, expecting a sweet message or maybe a photo.</p>

<p>What he got instead was a gut punch that knocked the air out of his lungs.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s over between us. I’m sorry. I’ve found someone new. Please forget me. You deserve someone better.&quot;</p>

<p>He read it once. Then again. And again.</p>

<p>&quot;What?&quot; he whispered, his voice hoarse with disbelief.</p>

<p>No greeting. No explanation. Just four lines that sliced through him like a blade.</p>

<p>He stared at the message for a full minute, fingers frozen above the screen.</p>

<p>Then he tried calling. Straight to voicemail. He opened their chat and typed:</p>

<p>Amanda? What is this? Please tell me this isn’t real.</p>

<p>But the moment he hit send, the screen flashed. Message not delivered.</p>

<p>Her profile was gone. Her display picture—once a smiling photo of the two of them at the beach—was now just a gray silhouette.</p>

<p>She had blocked him.</p>

<p>He stood there in stunned silence, his breath shallow and shaky. Slowly, he lowered the phone.</p>

<p>&quot;Fuck,&quot; he said under his breath, voice trembling.</p>

<p>Then louder: &quot;Fuck!&quot;</p>

<p>His fist slammed into the railing, knuckles scraping against cold metal, but he didn’t feel the pain.</p>

<p>He could only feel the ringing in his ears, the suffocating pressure in his chest.</p>

<p>He had been out at sea for weeks, sleeping in cramped bunks, eating canned food, thinking of her every night.</p>

<p>He had planned everything.</p>

<p>The dinner. The ring. The words.</p>

<p>He had practiced the proposal in front of the mirror like an idiot, over and over again, imagining her face lighting up when he asked her to marry him.</p>

<p>He had even written the damn speech down in a notebook.</p>

<p>And now?</p>

<p>Now, she was in someone else’s arms.</p>

<p>Someone she had clearly been seeing for a while.</p>

<p>His throat tightened as the realization sank in. She hadn’t waited. She hadn’t even hesitated.</p>

<p>She’d moved on.</p>

<p>While he was out here fighting storms and loneliness, Amanda was with someone else—laughing, kissing, moaning his name.</p>

<p>And just like that, everything they had meant nothing.</p>

<p>His hands gripped the railing as he stared blankly out at the sea.</p>

<p>The waves rolled gently, mocking his pain with their calm.</p>

<p>The ocean didn’t care.</p>

<p>And neither, apparently, did she.</p>
