<p>The door to June’s office sealed with a soft pneumatic hiss, the sound too quiet for comfort, too final for casual conversation.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t wait for an invitation. He crossed the threshold like he owned the room—because in every way that mattered inside these reinforced walls, he probably did.</p>

<p>The overhead lights caught the sharp line of his jaw, the faint scar that curved under his left eye like a signature he’d never bothered to explain.</p>

<p>He moved with the loose, cool economy of someone who knew exactly how much space his presence required.</p>

<p>June stayed behind her desk a beat longer than necessary, fingers resting on the cool edge of the smart-glass surface.</p>

<p>She’d rehearsed this moment in her head a dozen times since the after-action reports came in.</p>

<p>Gratitude. Professional respect. Firm boundaries.</p>

<p>She should’ve known better.</p>

<p>&quot;I don’t know how to thank you, Ross,&quot; she said, voice steady despite the quickening pulse at the base of her throat. &quot;The lives you saved today... they’re countless.&quot;</p>

<p>He didn’t sit right away.</p>

<p>Instead he prowled to the sideboard, trailing a fingertip along the edge of her commendations display—each plaque and holo-medal a silent testament to battles she’d fought long before he ever set foot on this base.</p>

<p>Then he turned, and that slow, knowing smile unfurled across his face.</p>

<p>&quot;Countless, hm?&quot; His voice was smoke and gravel, pitched low enough that it seemed to stroke the air between them.</p>

<p>&quot;I can think of one or two ways you could make the number feel a little more... personal.&quot;</p>

<p>June’s jaw tightened. She’d heard variations of this line from him before—half-teasing, half-deadly serious—but never quite so nakedly after he’d pulled off something this impossible.</p>

<p>&quot;That can’t happen, Ross.&quot; She forced the words out cleanly. &quot;You know I’m engaged.&quot;</p>

<p>He tilted his head, studying her like she was a tactical schematic he hadn’t quite cracked yet.</p>

<p>Amusement danced in the depths of his eyes, dark and patient.</p>

<p>&quot;Engaged,&quot; he repeated, tasting the word. &quot;Funny thing about rings and promises—they don’t stop the body from wanting what it wants.&quot; He took one measured step closer.</p>

<p>&quot;If you really loved him, June, you would’ve had me pull strings weeks ago. One call. One favor. Your precious fiancé would be standing on this rock right now instead of rotting somewhere away from you.&quot; Another step. &quot;But you didn’t.&quot;</p>

<p>The air between them thickened, charged with the low hum of the base’s environmental systems and something far more dangerous.</p>

<p>&quot;You didn’t want him here,&quot; Ross continued, voice dropping to a murmur that seemed to settle against her skin. &quot;Because deep down—right where you pretend you’re still in control—you know the truth.&quot;</p>

<p>He closed the last of the distance with lazy confidence, stopping just inside her personal space.</p>

<p>Close enough that she could smell the faint tang of gun oil, smoke, and the dark spice of whatever cologne he wore when he wanted to be noticed.</p>

<p>&quot;I think,&quot; he said, leaning in until his breath ghosted her cheek, &quot;you’re starving for a man who doesn’t ask permission. A man who can actually subdue you. Conquer you.&quot; His lips curved, wicked and certain. &quot;And lucky for you... I know exactly who that man is.&quot;</p>

<p>He dipped his head, slow, deliberate, giving her every fraction of a second to stop him.</p>

<p>She didn’t.</p>

<p>Not with words.</p>

<p>Instead, her skin shimmered—violent, sudden, beautiful. A bright metallic sheen raced across her body like liquid mercury poured over flame.</p>

<p>It flowed upward from her fingertips, across her arms, her shoulders, her throat, hardening into sleek, lethal plates as it went. Head to toe in heartbeats.</p>

<p>The transformation was seamless, merciless: curves accentuated and weaponized, joints articulated for lethal grace, every line of her now a darker, fiercer evolution of the ultimate Iron Man suit.</p>

<p>The light in the room bent around her, refracting off the chrome-bright surface in sharp, dangerous gleams.</p>

<p>Ross froze mid-motion, lips a breath from hers.</p>

<p>Then he exhaled—a low, appreciative sound that bordered on a growl.</p>

<p>&quot;Nice trick,&quot; he murmured, voice thick with something that wasn’t quite surprise and wasn’t quite lust, but a hungry fusion of both.</p>

<p>His gaze raked over her new form, unhurried, shameless. &quot;I do love my women hard.&quot;</p>

<p>He lifted a hand, not quite touching—just hovering a centimeter from the gleaming pauldron that now capped her shoulder.</p>

<p>The heat of his palm radiated through the infinitesimal space.</p>

<p>&quot;But I’ll bet...&quot; His eyes flicked back to hers, pupils blown wide. &quot;...you’re still soft. Right where it counts.&quot;</p>

<p>For a long heartbeat neither of them moved.</p>

<p>Somewhere down the corridor, boots echoed—personnel changing shifts, oblivious.</p>

<p>The base kept breathing, indifferent to the private war being waged in this soundproofed room.</p>

<p>Ross finally stepped back. Not retreat—never retreat. Just recalibration.</p>

<p>He had options. The base crawled with willing bodies; he could have a warm lap and eager mouth within minutes if he crooked a finger.</p>

<p>But June...</p>

<p>June was the chase.</p>

<p>The one who fought.</p>

<p>The one who might, just might, make him bleed a little before she gave in.</p>

<p>He liked taking his time.</p>

<p>And she—armored, radiant, untouchable—was worth every second of the wait.</p>

<p>He gave her one last lingering look, the kind that promised this conversation was only paused.</p>

<p>Then he turned toward the door, pausing with his hand on the release.</p>

<p>&quot;When you’re ready to stop pretending, Commander,&quot; he said without looking back, &quot;you know where to find me.&quot;</p>

<p>The door hissed open.</p>

<p>And closed.</p>

<p>Leaving her alone with the low thrum of her own heartbeat, the cool weight of her armor, and the lingering echo of his voice.</p>

<p>She didn’t exhale until the corridor outside went silent again.</p>

<p>As for Ross, the moment he stepped out of June’s office, his mind had already shifted to new opportunities—new prey.</p>

<p>With the events in the hall still fresh in everyone’s minds, his status within the base had skyrocketed overnight.</p>

<p>After saving countless lives, he had become something of a legend—an untouchable figure that people couldn’t help but admire, envy, or fear.</p>
