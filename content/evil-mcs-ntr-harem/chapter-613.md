<p>Eventually, Jade exhaled softly and gave a small nod. &quot;Okay,&quot; she said, her tone calm, composed.</p>

<p>There was no smile, no hint of playfulness in her voice — just a businesslike acceptance. Still, that was a victory in itself.</p>

<p>She wasn&#x27;t pushing him away. She was keeping him close, if only just enough to remind him that she wasn&#x27;t someone to underestimate.</p>

<p>&quot;I figured you&#x27;d see the value in that,&quot; Thomas said, lips curving into a self-assured grin.</p>

<p>Jade turned away from him without answering and picked up the office phone. She pressed a button, waited a beat, and then spoke.</p>

<p>&quot;Call Brandon up. I&#x27;m going to need his services today.&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas tilted his head slightly, intrigued by the name. Brandon?</p>

<p>She hung up and sat back, meeting his eyes with a faint, unreadable smile.</p>

<p>&quot;Bringing backup?&quot; he asked casually, his curiosity piqued.</p>

<p>&quot;Let&#x27;s just say,&quot; Jade replied, her voice cool as glass, &quot;I prefer not to drink alone with men who bring billion-dollar deals and hidden intentions.&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas chuckled under his breath. &quot;Fair enough. I admire a woman who stays ten steps ahead.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I&#x27;m always ten steps ahead, Thomas,&quot; she said, folding her arms across her chest. &quot;You&#x27;d be wise to remember that.&quot;</p>

<p>Just then, there was a knock at the door. Her secretary peeked in. &quot;Mr. Brandon is on his way up.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Thank you,&quot; Jade said, then glanced at Thomas again. &quot;Shall we head to the lounge?&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas rose from his seat and adjusted his cufflinks. &quot;Lead the way.&quot;</p>

<p>As they walked out of the office, Jade&#x27;s heels clicked with sharp precision across the polished floor, echoing through the quiet hallway.</p>

<p>Thomas followed beside her, one hand in his pocket, the other gently adjusting his tie. The deal had been made, the money was on the table — but now came the real negotiation.</p>

<p>And neither of them was planning to lose.</p>

<p>Naturally, the walk to the lounge wasn&#x27;t silent.</p>

<p>Thomas, ever persistent, filled the air with his smooth, measured charm.</p>

<p>His words danced between flirtation and wit, never overtly aggressive but always suggestive — each sentence carefully crafted to poke at Jade&#x27;s composure.</p>

<p>He complimented her laugh, her business acumen, even her stride, all under the guise of harmless conversation. But both of them knew better.</p>

<p>Jade didn&#x27;t fall for it. Not outwardly. Her responses were clipped, professional, peppered with just enough amusement to keep things civil.</p>

<p>She didn&#x27;t scold him or call out his intentions — that would&#x27;ve meant acknowledging them. Instead, she let his words slide past her like water off polished marble.</p>

<p>Still, Thomas wasn&#x27;t discouraged. He wasn&#x27;t the type to give up after one failed pass — especially not when he could still feel the tension in the air.</p>

<p>It was faint, but there. A spark buried beneath her cool exterior, smothered by years of discipline and pride, but not fully extinguished. He&#x27;d seen it before.</p>

<p>He could read it in the way she paused a heartbeat too long after a compliment, or how her eyes occasionally flicked toward his mouth when she thought he wasn&#x27;t looking.</p>

<p>She was still affected. Just like all the others. She just didn&#x27;t want to admit it.</p>

<p>They reached the exclusive executive lounge on the upper floor — a hidden gem within the building known only to a few.</p>

<p>The moment the glass doors slid open, the atmosphere shifted.</p>

<p>The soft, rich sound of jazz music floated through the air, and warm, ambient lighting cast a golden sheen across the plush leather seating and polished mahogany finishes.</p>

<p>It was the kind of place where deals were whispered over aged scotch, not hammered out in boardrooms.</p>

<p>Behind the marble-topped bar stood a quiet, middle-aged bartender in a crisp white shirt and black vest, polishing glasses with practiced rhythm. He glanced up and gave them a nod of acknowledgment — friendly but reserved.</p>

<p>This lounge rarely saw traffic. It belonged to Jade&#x27;s company, and since most of her employees were young women more focused on climbing the corporate ladder than sipping cocktails, the space was often empty.</p>

<p>Still, the bartender was content. The pay was generous, the hours were easy, and the company — when it did arrive — was interesting.</p>

<p>Thomas surveyed the room approvingly. &quot;Now this,&quot; he said, &quot;is a proper setting for a celebration.&quot;</p>

<p>Jade simply nodded, leading him to one of the private rooms tucked away near the back.</p>

<p>It was intimate but not too small — dimly lit, with velvet-lined walls, a low table, and two curved leather armchairs placed just close enough to invite conversation, but not quite close enough for comfort.</p>

<p>A beautiful hostess entered shortly after with menus in hand.</p>

<p>She greeted them with a soft smile and waited patiently as they placed their orders — whiskey neat for Thomas, and a gin and tonic for Jade. Once she was gone, a brief silence settled between them.</p>

<p>Then the door creaked open again.</p>

<p>But it wasn&#x27;t the hostess returning.</p>

<p>A hulking figure stepped through the doorway, immediately altering the entire energy of the room.</p>

<p>He was massive — well over six feet, with shoulders like boulders and a chest that stretched the seams of his dark, custom-fitted suit.</p>

<p>His face was hard, expressionless, with eyes that scanned the room in a slow, calculated sweep.</p>

<p>Brandon.</p>

<p>He didn&#x27;t speak. He didn&#x27;t smile. He simply nodded once at Jade, then positioned himself in the corner of the room, arms crossed over his broad chest. Like a statue — or a warning.</p>

<p>The temperature seemed to rise with his presence, or maybe it was just the sudden pressure of being in the same room as someone who looked like he could snap a man in half with his bare hands.</p>

<p>Thomas raised his eyebrows, slowly turning to Jade. &quot;So this is Brandon?&quot;</p>

<p>Jade sipped her drink without looking back. &quot;My insurance policy.&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas let out a low chuckle. &quot;Remind me never to miss a payment.&quot;</p>

<p>Brandon didn&#x27;t move. Didn&#x27;t blink. He just watched, impassive and silent — a human monolith whose only job was to be there and make his presence known.</p>

<p>Thomas wasn&#x27;t intimidated, exactly, but he respected the move. It was smart.</p>

<p>A woman in Jade&#x27;s position couldn&#x27;t afford to let her guard down, especially with someone like him in the room.</p>

<p>Thomas had heard plenty about Ross Oakley&#x27;s infamous bodyguards — they supposedly numbered in the hundreds, a small army hidden in plain sight.</p>

<p>At first glance, Brandon looked like just another one of them. Another overpaid, overbuilt watchdog trained to follow orders and throw punches when needed.</p>

<p>But then Thomas took a longer look.</p>

<p>There was something different about this one — something subtle, yet unmistakable.</p>

<p>He&#x27;d seen it before, countless times during his missions overseas. It was in the posture, the stillness, the eyes.</p>

<p>&quot;This man has seen blood,&quot; Thomas realized, narrowing his gaze.</p>

<p>Brandon wasn&#x27;t just muscle. He was a killer — the kind who didn&#x27;t flinch, didn&#x27;t hesitate, and didn&#x27;t lose sleep over a broken neck.</p>

<p>His eyes weren&#x27;t just cold — they were dead. Void of emotion. Void of hesitation. The kind of eyes that had stared down the barrel of a gun more times than Thomas could count.</p>

<p>And right now, those eyes were locked on him.</p>

<p>Brandon was looking at him like he wasn&#x27;t even a threat — just another corpse waiting to happen. A dead fish, ready to be gutted and fried.</p>

<p>Thomas held his gaze a moment longer, then leaned back slowly and took another sip of his drink.</p>

<p>So this wasn&#x27;t just another dog.</p>

<p>This one had teeth.</p>
