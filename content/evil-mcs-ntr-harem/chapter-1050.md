<p>Miku looked at Ross waiting for a reply.</p>

<p>He sat a few feet away, relaxed but alert, his broad shoulders outlined by the faint light of the tv screen.</p>

<p>There was something commanding about him, something that made the room itself feel smaller when he was in it.</p>

<p>His face was striking—too perfect, almost unreal.</p>

<p>Even with the faint stubble along his jaw and the exhaustion that came from surviving a world gone mad, his handsomeness was undeniable.</p>

<p>&quot;I saved you from those five bastards,&quot; he said simply.</p>

<p>&quot;They were planning to make you their slave, you know. If I hadn’t shown up, you wouldn’t be sitting here breathing right now.&quot;</p>

<p>His tone wasn’t cruel, just matter-of-fact, as though he were stating a weather report.</p>

<p>&quot;I think I’ve helped you more than anyone ever has in your life,&quot; he added after a pause.</p>

<p>&quot;If you want more, you’re free to stay. I’ve got food, clean water, and enough supplies to last through the next few months. I even make fresh burgers. Help yourself.&quot;</p>

<p>He gestured toward the kitchen counter, where several freshly cooked patties still steamed beside a loaf of bread and a jar of pickles.</p>

<p>Miku blinked. The smell of real food—warm, meaty, seasoned—was almost too much to process.</p>

<p>For so long, all she had known were dry rations, canned beans, and cold air.</p>

<p>The scent filled the room like a memory of the old world, of dinners with Ace before everything went wrong.</p>

<p>Her stomach growled, betraying her hesitation.</p>

<p>She hesitated for a long moment before standing up and moving toward the kitchen.</p>

<p>&quot;Thank you,&quot; she said softly.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t answer right away. His eyes were now fixed on the television screen.</p>

<p>The newscaster’s voice was grainy and hollow, barely cutting through the static as the footage showed burning cities and collapsing buildings.</p>

<p>Entire highways were packed with unmoving cars—each one a coffin.</p>

<p>&quot;The infection continues to spread across all major continents,&quot; the anchor’s voice droned. &quot;Governments have fallen silent. Containment efforts are failing. Humanity’s survival... remains uncertain.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross adjusted the volume slightly. The signal flickered in and out, but it still held strong enough to deliver bad news.</p>

<p>Zombies were mindless creatures; they didn’t destroy communication lines or satellites.</p>

<p>The internet, against all odds, still survived—one of the last remnants of the old world.</p>

<p>It was eerie, seeing glimpses of civilization persist while the streets outside were filled with death.</p>

<p>Miku stood there, holding a burger in her hands, staring blankly at the screen.</p>

<p>The images of fire and blood blurred together.</p>

<p>Ross glanced at her from the corner of his eye. &quot;You should eat,&quot; he said quietly. &quot;You’ll need your strength.&quot;</p>

<p>She nodded and took a small bite. It was warm, soft, and rich with flavor.</p>

<p>Tears pricked at the corners of her eyes before she could stop them.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t say anything more.</p>

<p>He turned his gaze back to the news, one arm resting on the back of the couch, his expression unreadable.</p>

<p>For a long time, only the faint hum of the television filled the silence between them.</p>

<p>Knowing she had been unfair earlier, Miku sat for a long while in silence, her hands clasped tightly on her lap.</p>

<p>The warmth of the burger had faded, replaced by a gnawing sense of guilt.</p>

<p>She had spoken rashly before, perhaps too emotionally, and Ross... he hadn’t deserved that.</p>

<p>He’d saved her life, after all.</p>

<p>Taking a quiet breath, she turned to him. Her voice was gentle, almost hesitant.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m Miku.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross finally glanced at her, his expression calm but distant. &quot;Ross,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>And just like that, the silence returned.</p>

<p>The television continued to hum softly in the background, the image flickering between static and chaos—collapsed cities, burning cars, survivors screaming as hordes of the undead flooded the streets.</p>

<p>The world was ending, yet here in this small, dimly lit living room, time seemed frozen.</p>

<p>Five minutes passed. Miku stared at the floor, trying to gather the courage to speak again.</p>

<p>Her heart was pounding.</p>

<p>She didn’t know if it was desperation or foolish hope that made her open her mouth, but the words came out before she could stop them.</p>

<p>&quot;Please, Ross...&quot; she began, her voice trembling, &quot;please save my husband.&quot;</p>

<p>For a moment, Ross didn’t respond.</p>

<p>He simply stared at her—his eyes unreadable, his expression almost pitying.</p>

<p>Then, slowly, he exhaled and leaned back on the couch, the leather creaking under his weight.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s a wild world out there, Miku,&quot; he said finally, his tone quiet but firm.</p>

<p>&quot;Even if I wanted to, I’m not risking my skin for someone I don’t know.&quot; His gaze shifted toward the window, where faint light bled through the curtains.</p>

<p>&quot;I can’t even save my own family, you know.&quot;</p>

<p>His words carried a heaviness that silenced her.</p>

<p>Miku’s lips parted as if to say something—but nothing came out.</p>

<p>The room felt colder suddenly. She bit her lip, realizing how selfish her plea must have sounded.</p>

<p>Ross had his own battles, his own losses. Who was she to demand more from him?</p>

<p>&quot;...I understand,&quot; she whispered.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t reply.</p>

<p>He only turned back toward the television, his face illuminated by the pale glow of the screen.</p>

<p>The news anchor’s voice droned on, lifeless and grim.</p>

<p>Outside, the distant moans of the undead rose and fell like the sound of the ocean—an unending reminder that safety was fragile, temporary.</p>

<p>The night passed slowly.</p>

<p>Miku ate the rest of the burgers Ross had prepared, though the taste barely registered.</p>

<p>Her thoughts kept drifting to Ace—where he might be, whether he was still alive, whether he’d given up searching for her.</p>

<p>Every bite she took felt like betrayal, like moving one step farther from him.</p>

<p>When she finally went to bed, exhaustion overtook her. The sheets were clean, the pillow soft.</p>

<p>For a brief moment, she almost felt human again.</p>

<p>She showered, washed away the grime and blood of the road, and changed into clean clothes Ross had left folded on the bed.</p>
