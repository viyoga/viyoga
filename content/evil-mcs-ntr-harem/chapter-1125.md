<p>The group finally reached a massive, once-bustling supermarket that occupied nearly an entire city block.</p>

<p>Its towering sign was cracked, flickering weakly, but the structure itself still stood firm like a stubborn monument refusing to fall to the apocalypse.</p>

<p>This place had become the base of Giana and the rest of her group—a decision made not out of luxury, but pure practicality.</p>

<p>Food was abundant, storage rooms were packed, and the building’s thick concrete walls made it far safer than most shelters scattered around Parkland City.</p>

<p>As Ross and the others approached, they could see the subtle signs of human effort layered over the ruins.</p>

<p>Shopping carts were welded together into makeshift barricades.</p>

<p>Steel shelves had been dragged to block entrances. Wooden planks had been hammered across broken windows to prevent crawlers from slithering inside.</p>

<p>A faint trail of smoke drifted from a burn pit where zombies had been thrown and cremated, leaving behind that sharp, unmistakable scent of scorched undead flesh.</p>

<p>Inside, the atmosphere felt strangely calm compared to the dying city outside.</p>

<p>Lanterns hung from hooks. Several survivors were patrolling with spears and sharpened metal pipes.</p>

<p>The air carried the warmth of cooked food and the distant chatter of people—something rare and almost nostalgic in a world where silence usually meant danger.</p>

<p>The chaos consuming Parkland City hadn’t lessened at all.</p>

<p>If anything, it had grown worse the farther they walked.</p>

<p>Abandoned cars lined the roads like gravestones. Blood trails marked the sidewalks.</p>

<p>Somewhere in the distance, a lone gunshot echoed, followed by the shrill, haunting cry of a zombie pack.</p>

<p>The military still hadn’t reclaimed the city; that much was obvious.</p>

<p>It wasn’t because they didn’t want to—it was because they couldn’t.</p>

<p>Soldiers had been dying in droves ever since the outbreak.</p>

<p>The ones who survived were exhausted, stretched thin, and constantly forced to choose between duty and the desperate need to check on their own families.</p>

<p>Many who attempted to reach their homes never returned.</p>

<p>Their routes had turned into slaughterfields, littered with smashed vehicles and torn uniforms.</p>

<p>Parkland City had become a maze of death where every wrong turn could end in a scream.</p>

<p>Yet despite everything, humanity hadn’t collapsed.</p>

<p>A sliver of hope remained, small but blazing stubbornly.</p>

<p>The discovery of the heart stones had changed the tide, even if only slightly.</p>

<p>People awakened strange powers—some strong, some weak, but every ability counted.</p>

<p>These stones gave humanity an edge against the monsters, a way to fight back instead of just running and hiding.</p>

<p>Little by little, safe zones grew. Survivors were found and rescued.</p>

<p>Certain districts were slowly cleared out and reclaimed from the undead.</p>

<p>It wasn’t a grand victory, not yet, but it was progress.</p>

<p>A step forward in a world that had almost forgotten what progress felt like.</p>

<p>That was why places like this supermarket mattered.</p>

<p>They weren’t just shelters; they were reminders that people could still work together, adapt, fight, and survive.</p>

<p>As Ross looked around, seeing the determined faces of the survivors and the fortified walls they had built with their own hands, he understood something clearly:</p>

<p>This wasn’t just a base.</p>

<p>It was proof that humanity refused to be erased.</p>

<p>&quot;Let’s go.&quot; Ross stopped the vehicle with a sharp tap of the brakes and stepped out, the door swinging open with a heavy thud.</p>

<p>His boots hit the cracked asphalt, and the air around him seemed to shift—dense, oppressive, full of a quiet pressure that made even Giana and the others shiver.</p>

<p>The rest hurried after him, forming a loose line behind his tall figure.</p>

<p>They barely got the chance to adjust to the new surroundings before the undead began shambling toward them.</p>

<p>What happened next was the same terrifying pattern from earlier.</p>

<p>Every zombie that got within ten meters of Ross froze mid-step... and then collapsed.</p>

<p>Bodies imploded, skulls flattened, rib cages caved in as though an invisible hammer descended from the sky.</p>

<p>The creatures were crushed so completely that their corpses resembled broken pies splattered across the pavement.</p>

<p>Blood fanned out like dark petals around them.</p>

<p>It was effortless.</p>

<p>It was casual.</p>

<p>And it was terrifying.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t even look at them.</p>

<p>He simply continued forward, walking straight toward the supermarket’s main entrance—a pair of thick metal sliding doors that had been barricaded from the inside.</p>

<p>Heavy beams, chains, and welded plates reinforced it. It was supposed to withstand a horde.</p>

<p>It did not withstand Ross.</p>

<p>The moment he approached, the metal groaned loudly, shaking violently before both doors bent inward as if struck by a giant’s fist.</p>

<p>With a deafening bang, they flew open, the reinforced locks snapping like they were nothing more than old twigs.</p>

<p>Inside, five armed guards jumped up in shock.</p>

<p>&quot;What the—?!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Who are you?!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Wait—stop—what do y—&quot;</p>

<p>They didn’t even get to finish their words.</p>

<p>The moment they raised their guns, something invisible and brutal slammed them downward.</p>

<p>Five bodies hit the ground with sickening cracks.</p>

<p>Their limbs twisted at impossible angles, bones tore through skin, and blood pooled instantly beneath their broken frames.</p>

<p>Not a single one survived.</p>

<p>&quot;Ross!&quot;</p>

<p>Giana, Ace, Joy, and the rest stiffened, their faces pale as sheets.</p>

<p>Their hearts hammered painfully in their chests.</p>

<p>They had expected resistance from the supermarket’s defenders, but they never imagined Ross would eliminate them in the blink of an eye.</p>

<p>Zombies were one thing—mindless monsters.</p>

<p>Humans were another.</p>

<p>Seeing Ross kill people with the same casual cruelty sent chills down their spines.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just strength.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just power.</p>

<p>It was something darker... colder... primal.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t pity them.&quot; Ross didn’t even spare the corpses a glance. His voice was low, steady, laced with a cold anger that felt deeper than rage. &quot;They’re animals wearing human skin.&quot;</p>

<p>He stepped forward, his boots splashing in their pooled blood, and paused only for a moment.</p>

<p>His eyes narrowed, as if peering into the memories of the dead.</p>

<p>He could see.</p>

<p>He didn’t need proof.</p>

<p>Their crimes clung to them like a foul stench.</p>
