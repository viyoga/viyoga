<p>Bits of brain and fractured bone splattered across the floor.</p>

<p>The body collapsed, twitching as it hit the ground.</p>

<p>Before the others could process what had just happened, Ross pivoted, his eyes burning with cold fury.</p>

<p>He launched forward with terrifying speed, closing the distance between himself and a second guard.</p>

<p>&quot;Hrrgh!&quot; Ross grunted, driving his fist into the man&#x27;s stomach with devastating force.</p>

<p>The impact echoed like a gunshot. His arm didn&#x27;t stop at the stomach—it sank through, punching through muscle, bone, and organ like wet clay.</p>

<p>His fist burst out the man&#x27;s back with a sickening squelch, a spray of blood arcing into the air.</p>

<p>The man screamed once before his body went limp, sagging over Ross&#x27;s arm like a broken doll.</p>

<p>&quot;Fuck!&quot; someone shouted, panic surging through the room.</p>

<p>Then all hell broke loose.</p>

<p>Muzzle flashes lit the hall as gunfire erupted in every direction.</p>

<p>The sharp, deafening tat-tat-tat of automatic rifles echoed off the concrete walls.</p>

<p>Bullets tore through the air, ripping chunks out of walls and leaving sparks in their wake.</p>

<p>But Ross was already gone.</p>

<p>He dropped the limp body and dove into a roll, bullets trailing inches behind him.</p>

<p>As he came up, he grabbed a fallen rifle—spinning with the motion—and hurled it like a spear into a third guard&#x27;s face.</p>

<p>Crack! The heavy weapon collided with the man&#x27;s skull, snapping his neck and sending him sprawling.</p>

<p>Another guard took aim. Ross was already there.</p>

<p>He seized the barrel of the rifle, yanked it to the side, and drove his elbow into the man&#x27;s throat.</p>

<p>The impact crushed the windpipe with a dry crunch.</p>

<p>As the man choked and stumbled backward, Ross ripped the rifle from his hands and smashed the stock into his skull, cracking it open like a melon.</p>

<p>Two remained.</p>

<p>They hesitated—just for a second—but that was all Ross needed.</p>

<p>He ducked low, almost gliding across the floor as he closed the gap.</p>

<p>One of the guards fired, but the rounds went wide. Ross slid beneath the barrel and came up inside the man&#x27;s guard.</p>

<p>He grabbed the man by the mask and slammed his head into the concrete pillar with enough force to cave it in. The man dropped like a sack of meat.</p>

<p>The last one tried to run.</p>

<p>Big mistake.</p>

<p>Ross launched after him, grabbing him by the back of his vest. He spun him around and delivered a thunderous blow to the chest.</p>

<p>The man&#x27;s ribs shattered, and he flew backward, crashing into the wall with a hollow boom before collapsing, wheezing and bloodied.</p>

<p>Silence fell.</p>

<p>Blood pooled across the floor. Bodies twitched—or didn&#x27;t. Smoke and dust hung in the air, blending with the metallic scent of blood and gunpowder.</p>

<p>In the center of it all stood Ross Oakley.</p>

<p>His shirt was soaked with blood—none of it his own. His chest rose and fell steadily, his breath calm, collected. Not a trace of fear. Not a single scratch on him.</p>

<p>Across the room, Thomas stared in stunned silence.</p>

<p>Six guards.</p>

<p>Six trained men.</p>

<p>Slaughtered in under thirty seconds.</p>

<p>Ross turned slowly to face him, eyes gleaming with unspoken wrath.</p>

<p>&quot;This,&quot; Ross said, his voice low, dangerous, &quot;was just the introduction.&quot;</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>&quot;…!&quot;</p>

<p>Thomas&#x27;s entire body tensed, a chill running down his spine as every hair stood on end.</p>

<p>His eyes were wide, fixed on the nightmare unfolding before him—Ross, drenched in blood but moving like a predator at play, tearing through his men like paper dolls.</p>

<p>Screams echoed, sharp and fleeting, before they were silenced by the sound of bones snapping, throats being crushed, and skulls splitting open.</p>

<p>It was a massacre. And Ross… he looked like he was enjoying every second of it.</p>

<p>Thomas had seen war. He had watched men die, had killed more than a few himself. But this?</p>

<p>This wasn&#x27;t a firefight. This wasn&#x27;t combat. This was one man playing god, and everyone else was just waiting to be snuffed out.</p>

<p>Fear gripped Thomas&#x27;s chest like a vice. His breathing quickened, but his training kicked in.</p>

<p>He was a professional. Panic got you killed—he knew that better than most.</p>

<p>He didn&#x27;t know how Ross was doing what he was doing. He didn&#x27;t care if it was tech, drugs, or something else entirely. What mattered was survival.</p>

<p>He stepped back slowly, trying not to draw attention, his boots crunching softly on the shattered glass and blood-soaked floor.</p>

<p>Then, in one smooth motion, he reached for his sidearm. A matte-black pistol with a suppressor already attached. It had never failed him.</p>

<p>Pft. Pft. Pft.</p>

<p>Three shots. Center mass.</p>

<p>Thomas was a marksman. Each bullet hit Ross dead-on, punching into his chest with mechanical precision.</p>

<p>Ross looked down. Then up.</p>

<p>Nothing. Not a stagger. Not a wince. Not even a blink.</p>

<p>&quot;What the f—&quot; Thomas muttered under his breath, his voice trembling. This wasn&#x27;t possible.</p>

<p>Ross tilted his head slightly, a smile slowly forming on his lips.</p>

<p>&quot;It tickles,&quot; he said, his tone casual—playful even. &quot;Thank you.&quot;</p>

<p>He licked the blood off his thumb like it was barbecue sauce.</p>

<p>Thomas&#x27;s heart pounded. He&#x27;d fought monsters before—people with no conscience, no fear—but Ross was something else entirely. Something wrong.</p>

<p>Thomas gritted his teeth and recalibrated. Chest shots were out. Go for the head.</p>

<p>Pft.</p>

<p>A fourth shot cracked out. This one aimed directly at Ross&#x27;s forehead.</p>

<p>The bullet struck with a sharp hiss. Thomas prepared for the explosion of blood, the collapse, the end. But instead…</p>

<p>Ross didn&#x27;t fall. Didn&#x27;t bleed. Didn&#x27;t so much as blink.</p>

<p>A trail of smoke rose from his brow, and Ross reached up almost lazily.</p>

<p>He plucked the bullet from his skin like it was lint on a jacket. No damage. Not a single drop of blood.</p>

<p>Thomas&#x27;s mouth went dry.</p>

<p>&quot;Cute,&quot; Ross said, inspecting the still-hot slug between his fingers before discarding it with a flick.</p>

<p>Thomas stared, frozen in place, mind racing. There was no protocol for this.</p>

<p>No amount of training prepared you for a man who shrugged off bullets like mosquito bites.</p>

<p>He needed a new plan.</p>

<p>Or a miracle.</p>
