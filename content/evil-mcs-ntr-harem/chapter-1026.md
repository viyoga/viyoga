<p>A massive steel hatch built into the ground groaned open, the mechanical hum echoing across the empty landscape.</p>

<p>A second later, heavy gears locked into place, and a gust of stale air burst outward from the underground facility.</p>

<p>Then came the rumble—deep, rhythmic, and growing louder by the second.</p>

<p>One after another, armored vehicles began to climb out of the bunker.</p>

<p>The sunlight gleamed off their reinforced plating and mounted gun turrets.</p>

<p>Twenty-four of them in total, each fully armed and ready for battle.</p>

<p>Their thick tires crushed the cracked concrete beneath as they assembled in formation.</p>

<p>They had chosen to move in daylight.</p>

<p>Some might have called it a reckless decision—after all, the undead were most active under the sun’s glare—but Ross Oakley had reasons of his own.</p>

<p>Fighting at night would have been far too easy for him and his men.</p>

<p>They weren’t ordinary humans at all; they were stronger, faster, sharper.</p>

<p>A few million zombies were hardly enough to make them sweat.</p>

<p>But Ross understood one thing very well: power was most valuable when hidden.</p>

<p>He would not reveal his full hand unless the situation truly demanded it.</p>

<p>Until then, he would play the role of a well-prepared leader—confident, capable, but seemingly human.</p>

<p>The world didn’t need to see a god walking among them. Not yet.</p>

<p>The armored convoy rolled out of the bunker in perfect synchronization, their engines rumbling like a pack of beasts hunting prey.</p>

<p>They drove past the empty land in the outer area of the city.</p>

<p>Parkland City stretched ahead of them—a dead metropolis that once housed over two million souls.</p>

<p>Now, it was a home for shuffling corpses and the resistance that wanted to restore order amidst the chaos.</p>

<p>No orders were given. None were needed.</p>

<p>Brandon, Ross’s trusted right-hand man, led the column with stoic focus.</p>

<p>Every soldier in the convoy knew their role down to instinct.</p>

<p>Ross himself sat in the second vehicle, relaxed and unconcerned.</p>

<p>He had reclined his seat slightly, a comic book spread open on his lap, the corners of his lips curling faintly as he turned the pages.</p>

<p>The hum of engines was the only sound for ten long minutes. Then, without warning, it began.</p>

<p>&quot;Contact—north and east!&quot; a voice barked through the radio.</p>

<p>The calm morning shattered.</p>

<p>A roar of gunfire erupted as the mounted turrets came alive, spinning and firing in synchronized bursts.</p>

<p>The chatter of high-caliber machine guns thundered through the streets, each burst cutting through the moaning swarm ahead.</p>

<p>Brass casings poured down from the turrets like golden rain.</p>

<p>From every direction, zombies flooded into view—crawling out of shattered windows, bursting through broken doors, climbing over wrecked cars.</p>

<p>The stench of decay filled the air as thousands became tens of thousands.</p>

<p>But numbers meant nothing.</p>

<p>The convoy never slowed.</p>

<p>The trucks plowed through the streets, shredding undead flesh to ribbons.</p>

<p>Heavy gunfire, rocket bursts, and grenades painted the battlefield with fire and gore.</p>

<p>Severed limbs flew through the air. Skulls burst apart like rotten fruit.</p>

<p>Inside the armored cabin, Ross barely looked up.</p>

<p>He turned another page of his comic, one hand casually resting on the window frame as explosions flashed outside.</p>

<p>Brandon’s voice crackled over the radio. &quot;Clear. Moving to the central district!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Good,&quot; Ross murmured, not taking his eyes off the page. &quot;Keep the formation tight. Let them come.&quot;</p>

<p>Outside, the slaughter continued. The convoy carved through the horde like a blade through mist.</p>

<p>Every few seconds, another wave of the undead was reduced to ash and blood.</p>

<p>The earth trembled from the constant barrage of firepower.</p>

<p>But through it all, Ross remained serene—unbothered, unreadable.</p>

<p>His men fought like soldiers, but he sat like a man waiting for something bigger.</p>

<p>He knew this was only the beginning.</p>

<p>He still needed to harvest more heart stones for his people.</p>

<p>Until then, he let the sound of gunfire play like music while he finished his comic, a faint smirk forming on his lips as the convoy bathed the city in fire and death.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>An hour passed, and the world above the bunker had turned into a living apocalypse.</p>

<p>The thunder of gunfire never ceased.</p>

<p>It rolled across the plains like a storm that refused to end, shaking the ground and echoing off the ruined shells of distant buildings.</p>

<p>The scent of burning gunpowder mixed with rot and blood until it became a heavy, choking perfume of death.</p>

<p>Around the convoy, the battlefield was no longer recognizable—there was no ground, only a vast carpet of mangled corpses stretching in every direction.</p>

<p>Every inch of the landscape was painted in crimson and black.</p>

<p>The bodies piled so high that the gunfire began to chew through the dead just to reach the ones still standing.</p>

<p>Yet the horde kept coming, endless and insane, crawling over mountains of their own kind just to die again.</p>

<p>And through it all, the guns never stopped.</p>

<p>The barrels of the mounted turrets glowed a dull red, yet they did not melt.</p>

<p>Ammunition belts that should have run dry after minutes of constant firing never seemed to end.</p>

<p>The feed systems didn’t jam, the engines didn’t falter, and the gunners didn’t tire.</p>

<p>It was unnatural, eerie—like something out of a dream.</p>

<p>But no one said a word.</p>

<p>Everyone knew.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley didn’t fight fair.</p>

<p>His undead men all knew it so well.</p>

<p>Ross had a way of bending the impossible until it obeyed him.</p>

<p>Infinite ammunition, weapons that never overheated, armor that never broke—it was just another one of his miracles or great luck.</p>

<p>And no one, not even his closest men, dared to question him.</p>

<p>They just fired, killed, and kept moving.</p>

<p>It was shocking to see one man ignoring the carnage, walking directly to the dead zombies to collect heart stones from their bodies with complete absence of fear or hesitation.</p>

<p>Then, through the storm of gunfire, a new sound broke the rhythm.</p>

<p>&quot;Tog-tog-tog-tog!&quot;</p>

<p>The rhythmic pounding of rotor blades.</p>

<p>Heads snapped upward.</p>

<p>A dark shadow cut through the clouds—one helicopter, then another, circling above the smoke like vultures.</p>
