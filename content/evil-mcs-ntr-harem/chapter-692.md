<p>&quot;Don’t play dumb with me, Jane. You didn’t land a senator by accident. I know you know exactly what I’m talking about,&quot; Ross said, his voice thick with implication, laced with a smug arrogance that made Jane’s skin crawl.</p>

<p>&quot;...&quot;</p>

<p>It took Jane three steadying breaths to process the venom behind his words.</p>

<p>She wasn’t slow—far from it. In fact, her instincts were usually razor-sharp.</p>

<p>But the sheer audacity of what Ross was suggesting caught her off guard.</p>

<p>Her fingers tightened around her phone. &quot;Are you insinuating that you’ll help free my husband... if I sleep with you?&quot; she said, her voice trembling with fury and disbelief. &quot;You’re really a fucking lowlife, Ross. Go to hell!&quot;</p>

<p>She hung up, slamming the phone down on the kitchen counter so hard that it bounced slightly.</p>

<p>Her chest was heaving now, not from fear, but from raw indignation.</p>

<p>&quot;The guts of that man...&quot; she muttered through clenched teeth, pacing the room.</p>

<p>&quot;I should sue his ass off. Sexual coercion, blackmail—he’s asking for a goddamn lawsuit!&quot;</p>

<p>Jane’s pulse pounded in her ears.</p>

<p>She wrapped her arms around herself, not because she was cold, but to stop herself from shaking.</p>

<p>Ross’s words had been a slap to the face, an insult to everything she had fought for.</p>

<p>Yes, she was beautiful. Striking, even. Her curves turned heads.</p>

<p>Her smile could melt the hardest of men. And yes—she knew it. She had always known it.</p>

<p>She wasn’t born into power or privilege.</p>

<p>She had clawed her way out of poverty with her wit, her charm, and her unshakable will.</p>

<p>She played the game the way it was meant to be played, using every asset she had.</p>

<p>But never—not once—had she compromised her dignity.</p>

<p>She had slept with one man in her life: her husband.</p>

<p>It hadn’t been about love at first. It was about survival, about security, about making sure she would never be that poor, helpless girl again.</p>

<p>Over time, affection had bloomed. She had grown to respect him, even admire him.</p>

<p>He gave her a life she could only dream of as a child.</p>

<p>And now, Ross wanted to reduce it all to a filthy transaction.</p>

<p>She walked over to the mirror in the hallway and stared at her reflection.</p>

<p>Her eyes, still fierce. Her lips, tight with anger. She looked like a woman ready to fight back.</p>

<p>&quot;No,&quot; she said aloud, her voice calmer now, but steel beneath the surface.</p>

<p>&quot;You don’t get to use me like that, Ross. I’m not some trophy you can buy with power.&quot;</p>

<p>Her phone buzzed again—another message from Ross, no doubt.</p>

<p>She didn’t even look at it. Instead, she picked it up, calmly deleted the number, and then set the phone aside.</p>

<p>If he wanted a game, she’d show him what it was like to play against someone who had everything to lose—and nothing left to give.</p>

<p>Jane held out longer than most people thought she could.</p>

<p>For an entire month, she fought tooth and nail to survive the aftermath of the scandal.</p>

<p>She cut expenses, sold jewelry, even pawned her designer bags—anything to scrape together enough to buy time.</p>

<p>But time wasn’t on her side.</p>

<p>The silence from her so-called social circle was louder than any condemnation. Her name—once spoken with admiration—was now a punchline in political satire shows.</p>

<p>She reached out for help, swallowing her pride to call old friends and even one time acquaintances she hadn’t spoken to in years.</p>

<p>She begged—something she never imagined herself doing.</p>

<p>But all she heard was the same rehearsed line:</p>

<p>&quot;I’m sorry, Jane. I really am. But it’s too risky.&quot;</p>

<p>They were afraid. Afraid of being associated with a fallen senator’s wife.</p>

<p>Afraid of subpoenas, frozen accounts, and guilt by association.</p>

<p>No one wanted her burden.</p>

<p>That was when the vultures started circling.</p>

<p>Wealthy men she’d once scoffed at suddenly reappeared, cloaked in concern and false empathy.</p>

<p>They invited her to &quot;private dinners&quot; or offered &quot;help&quot; with a knowing smirk.</p>

<p>Their eyes didn’t see her pain—only her body.</p>

<p>Her beauty had always drawn attention, but now it attracted something worse: opportunists. Predators.</p>

<p>They offered money. Not loans. Deals. One man even slid a black card across the table, whispering that it would be hers—if she became his.</p>

<p>She had slapped him. Walked out in tears. But the thought lingered, cruel and persistent.</p>

<p>She didn’t just need money. That wouldn’t fix anything.</p>

<p>She needed power. She needed her husband free. She needed her life back—the life she had built through grit and beauty and calculated charm. The power couple image.</p>

<p>The respect. The access to circles only the elite could enter.</p>

<p>She needed Ross.</p>

<p>As much as she loathed him, he was the only man who had both the influence and lack of morality to make things move behind the scenes.</p>

<p>He had connections in many places. He had leverage. And worst of all, he knew it.</p>

<p>&quot;I shouldn’t have deleted his number,&quot; she muttered for the tenth time that week.</p>

<p>She sat curled up on the couch, wearing an old oversized sweatshirt, hair messy, makeup smudged from yesterday’s tears.</p>

<p>She stared at the blank screen of her phone like it might magically display his name.</p>

<p>But it never did.</p>

<p>Day after day passed with no message, no call, no email. The silence felt cruel.</p>

<p>Intentional. As if Ross was punishing her for rejecting him the first time.</p>

<p>Or worse—testing how far she’d fall before she came crawling back on her own.</p>

<p>Another week passed.</p>

<p>Jane found herself standing in front of the mirror again.</p>

<p>Her reflection stared back—tired, thinner, but still undeniably beautiful.</p>

<p>She was a woman who had spent years learning how to use her looks, how to disarm, how to win.</p>

<p>But right now, she looked like a queen in exile. And no one came to save queens who had been dethroned.</p>

<p>The thought came unbidden, and it disgusted her: Maybe someone else could help. Not Ross... someone else. Someone who wouldn’t make me beg.</p>

<p>Her stomach churned.</p>
