<p>Pressing down from every direction like an invisible storm.</p>

<p>The leader’s heart thrashed violently in his chest. His breath became ragged.</p>

<p>His vision blurred at the edges from pure adrenaline.</p>

<p>He tried to steady himself, but his hands were trembling uncontrollably.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s no fun at all.&quot; Ross’s sigh echoed from the darkness, almost disappointed. &quot;You’re far too slow to play with me.&quot;</p>

<p>That single sentence crushed the last fragment of hope in the leader’s mind.</p>

<p>He spun around wildly, trying to pinpoint Ross’s location.</p>

<p>Nothing.</p>

<p>No footsteps.</p>

<p>No presence.</p>

<p>Not even a shadow.</p>

<p>Only the sound of his own breath, too loud in his ears.</p>

<p>Then—</p>

<p>Bang!</p>

<p>Something struck him in the stomach.</p>

<p>Not a punch.</p>

<p>Not a kick.</p>

<p>Not even a blow.</p>

<p>It was a collision—as if a car speeding at full force rammed into him.</p>

<p>His abdomen caved inward with a horrible crunch, ribs bending and snapping like brittle sticks.</p>

<p>Air blasted out of his lungs in a broken gasp.</p>

<p>His feet lifted off the ground as his entire body was launched upward.</p>

<p>His back slammed into the ceiling with explosive force.</p>

<p>Plaster cracked. Dust rained down. His bones groaned under the sudden, violent impact.</p>

<p>For a moment he hung there, suspended awkwardly like a broken puppet—</p>

<p>—and then he fell.</p>

<p>He hit the floor head-first, then collapsed in a heap, arms twisted, legs limp.</p>

<p>The breath wheezed out of him in short, weak bursts.</p>

<p>Blood trickled from his mouth, pooling on the cold concrete beneath his cheek.</p>

<p>He couldn’t move.</p>

<p>He couldn’t even think.</p>

<p>His vision flickered like a dying flame.</p>

<p>And when the world finally steadied...</p>

<p>Ross was standing over him.</p>

<p>Hands casually in his pockets, face relaxed, eyes calm—looking down at him as though he were nothing more than a toy that broke too easily.</p>

<p>&quot;This is the end,&quot; Ross sighed, sounding almost bored.</p>

<p>He shifted his weight, lifted one leg, and stomped down on the man’s skull.</p>

<p>Puchi!</p>

<p>The sound was wet, revolting, final.</p>

<p>The man’s head exploded beneath Ross’s boot—bone fragments, blood, and grey matter splattering across the dirt like crushed overripe fruit.</p>

<p>The body twitched once, twice, then went still. The metallic scent of blood thickened the air, coiling around the room like a suffocating fog.</p>

<p>Ross exhaled softly, as though even killing had become routine.</p>

<p>&quot;That wasn’t as fun as I thought it would be,&quot; he muttered, wiping a speck of gore from his cheek with lazy fingers.</p>

<p>His eyes held no excitement, no satisfaction—only disappointment, as if the night had failed to amuse him.</p>

<p>He sauntered toward the window, footsteps unhurried, the floorboards creaking beneath his weight.</p>

<p>The cool night breeze touched his face as he pushed the shutters open.</p>

<p>Then his gaze fell upon the chaos outside.</p>

<p>Screams echoed through the night.</p>

<p>&quot;Ahhhhhhh!&quot;</p>

<p>The men who had escaped earlier were being hunted down, trapped in a narrow alley by Brandon and his crew.</p>

<p>The moonlight illuminated the scene in pale silver, turning the violence below into a haunting tableau.</p>

<p>Brandon’s men were no ordinary men—hungry eyes, twisted grins, movements too predatory to belong to humans.</p>

<p>They circled the terrified survivors like wolves savoring the moment before the kill.</p>

<p>Each man who had fled now lay on the ground, pinned beneath clawed hands or crushed under brutish strength.</p>

<p>&quot;Please—no—no—NO—!&quot; one of the fleeing men cried out.</p>

<p>His plea only made Brandon grin wider.</p>

<p>What followed was not mercy.</p>

<p>Not a swift end.</p>

<p>But prolonged suffering.</p>

<p>It began with screams—raw, agonized, high-pitched. Brandon’s group tore into the survivors’ clothing, skin, sanity.</p>

<p>Their minds broke far earlier than their bodies did.</p>

<p>The attackers laughed, taunted, mocked their prey as they played with them in grotesque, degrading ways.</p>

<p>The agony stretched on and on.</p>

<p>Minutes felt like an eternity.</p>

<p>Their cries grew raspier, weaker, dissolving into sobs and choked gasps.</p>

<p>Still, the torment continued.</p>

<p>Ross watched it all with calm, detached eyes, leaning slightly against the window frame.</p>

<p>His heartbeat didn’t quicken. His expression didn’t shift.</p>

<p>To him, the horror outside was no different than watching rain fall from the sky.</p>

<p>Eventually, after their &quot;fun&quot; was thoroughly exhausted, Brandon and his men unleashed the final part of their nature.</p>

<p>The feeding.</p>

<p>The alley filled with a new set of sounds—wet tearing, crunching, slurping—ghastly noises that echoed off the walls as the monstrous group devoured the bodies.</p>

<p>Piece by piece.</p>

<p>Scream by scream.</p>

<p>Life by life.</p>

<p>The bottomless hunger in their bellies drove them until nothing remained but smeared stains on the dirt.</p>

<p>Ross closed the window slowly as the last gurgling cry was swallowed by the night.</p>

<p>A faint breeze rustled the silence that followed.</p>

<p>Another night ended.</p>

<p>Another massacre completed.</p>

<p>And the world, uncaring, continued turning beneath the moon.</p>

<p>As for Ross, he made his way back down into the basement, wiping a smear of dust from his sleeve as if he had simply stepped outside to breathe some fresh air.</p>

<p>The atmosphere below was thick—fear clung to the women like another layer of skin.</p>

<p>Every pair of eyes snapped toward him the instant he reappeared.</p>

<p>Ross raised a hand casually, offering a small smile that seemed out of place in the tense room.</p>

<p>&quot;They’ve gone away,&quot; he announced.</p>

<p>For a moment, there was complete silence.</p>

<p>No one moved.</p>

<p>No one breathed.</p>

<p>Then—</p>

<p>&quot;Impossible...&quot; Giana whispered. Her voice cracked at the edges, her face pale and strained.</p>

<p>She hugged herself tightly, as if expecting the door to burst open any second.</p>

<p>&quot;There were too many... even if some left, there should’ve been noise... something...&quot;</p>

<p>Ross shrugged lightly.</p>

<p>&quot;You can check upstairs.&quot;</p>

<p>His tone was so casual, so completely relaxed, that it only made the women more suspicious.</p>

<p>Still, desperation pushed them to move.</p>

<p>One by one they climbed the stairs, each step hesitant, as if they expected to see corpses piled at the top.</p>

<p>But what they found froze them on the spot.</p>

<p>The house’s main floor was spotless.</p>

<p>The tables were arranged neatly.</p>

<p>The floor gleamed with fresh cleaning oil.</p>

<p>The lights were lit and steady.</p>
