<p>Miku’s eyes shined—not with fear, but with conviction.</p>

<p>&quot;And since when did being powerless mean being useless? Even normal people can stand up for what’s right. And Ross said he can guarantee our safety. I trust him.&quot; She lifted her chin stubbornly. &quot;I’m going. Period.&quot;</p>

<p>Without waiting for a response, Miku yanked open the van door and climbed inside, her jaw set in determination.</p>

<p>Ace stood frozen.</p>

<p>The world around her fell silent except for the pounding of her own heart.</p>

<p>She wanted to argue—wanted to pull Miku back, force her to stay in the relative safety of this house.</p>

<p>But Miku’s eyes had made it clear: nothing Ace said would stop her.</p>

<p>And deep down... Ace knew she couldn’t let her friend face danger alone.</p>

<p>With a defeated exhale, she muttered, &quot;Fine... fine,&quot; and climbed in, sliding the door shut behind her.</p>

<p>Ross watched them settle into their seats.</p>

<p>For a moment, he said nothing—only smiled that calm, infuriatingly confident smile that suggested he had already predicted exactly how the argument would end.</p>

<p>Then he gripped the steering wheel and turned the key.</p>

<p>The engine growled to life, loud and powerful.</p>

<p>The sound echoed through the empty streets like a thunderclap.</p>

<p>Birds scattered from rooftops. Dust drifted from broken windows.</p>

<p>And from somewhere deeper in the city, a chorus of guttural groans responded.</p>

<p>The horde had heard them.</p>

<p>One by one, decaying silhouettes emerged from alleyways, drawn by noise, hunger, and instinct.</p>

<p>Dozens at first. Then more.</p>

<p>Their dragging footsteps scraped against the asphalt as they converged toward the van.</p>

<p>Inside, the women stiffened.</p>

<p>Ace clutched Miku’s arm so tightly her knuckles turned white.</p>

<p>Miku swallowed hard, trying to look brave but unable to hide the fear flickering in her eyes.</p>

<p>The five rescued women pressed against the windows, breath shallow, hands trembling.</p>

<p>&quot;Ross...&quot; one whispered, her voice barely audible. &quot;We should go. Now.&quot;</p>

<p>But Ross showed no sign of panic.</p>

<p>Instead, he slowly opened the driver’s door.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay inside,&quot; he said, stepping out as if he were simply stretching his legs.</p>

<p>The women watched with wide, terrified eyes as he walked a few steps into the street.</p>

<p>The undead swarm approached—dozens of rotting bodies stumbling closer, jaws slack, arms reaching.</p>

<p>Ross rolled his shoulders.</p>

<p>Cracked his knuckles.</p>

<p>And smiled.</p>

<p>It was then—right there, in the golden light of the dying sun, surrounded by an army of the dead—that the women finally witnessed what Ross Oakley was truly capable of.</p>

<p>They had thought they understood him.</p>

<p>They were wrong.</p>

<p>And they were about to see why the world whispered his name with equal parts awe, fear... and disbelief.</p>

<p>&quot;Go away,&quot; Ross whispered.</p>

<p>His voice carried barely any force, barely any emotion—just a simple command given to the universe itself.</p>

<p>Then he flicked his fingers.</p>

<p>BOOOOM!</p>

<p>The world reacted instantly.</p>

<p>An earth-shaking blast of invisible pressure erupted forward, ripping through the asphalt like a giant’s hammer.</p>

<p>A massive pathway tore open across the street—at least twenty meters wide—flattening everything in its way.</p>

<p>Cars crumpled like tin cans. Streetlights bent as if made of soft metal.</p>

<p>Zombies exploded into dust and pulp, smeared flat into the ground like insects under a boot.</p>

<p>The shockwave didn’t just clear a path—it erased everything that blocked Ross’s way.</p>

<p>The blast stretched so far that the horizon shimmered with distortion.</p>

<p>Giana, Joy, Avery, and the rest of the women stared, mouths open, unable to see the end of the destruction.</p>

<p>It vanished into the distance like a god had drawn a straight line across the land.</p>

<p>The echo of the blast faded, leaving only a ringing silence.</p>

<p>Dust rolled over the van in slow waves.</p>

<p>Then Ross turned around, hopped lightly back into the driver’s seat, and closed the door as if he had only swatted a fly.</p>

<p>&quot;Alright,&quot; he said casually, hands sliding onto the wheel. &quot;Road’s clear. Let’s go.&quot;</p>

<p>The van moved forward, rolling smoothly onto the flawless empty lane Ross had carved. No obstacles.</p>

<p>No threats. Just a clean, open road stretching endlessly forward.</p>

<p>Inside the van, no one breathed for several seconds.</p>

<p>Their hearts were still trying to catch up with what their eyes had just witnessed.</p>

<p>Then the questions burst out.</p>

<p>&quot;What... what kind of ability IS THAT!?&quot; Giana shouted, her hands gripping her seat.</p>

<p>&quot;That’s not broken—that’s BEYOND broken!&quot; another woman blurted, shaking her head in disbelief.</p>

<p>&quot;He flicked his fingers!&quot; someone whispered. &quot;Just a flick... and he did that...&quot;</p>

<p>They couldn’t process it. They couldn’t understand it. Ross’s power didn’t feel human.</p>

<p>It didn’t feel mutant, evolved, or magical. It felt like something that belonged to legends.</p>

<p>Joy sat with her eyes closed for a moment, her breathing slow and steady as she activated her sensory ability.</p>

<p>Joy could perceive everything within a full mile radius—movement, living beings, undead threats, even changes in energy.</p>

<p>She scanned the path Ross created.</p>

<p>Her eyes snapped open in pure shock.</p>

<p>&quot;I–It’s still active...&quot; Joy stammered.</p>

<p>Avery turned to her sharply. &quot;What do you mean still active?&quot;</p>

<p>Joy pointed nervously out the window. &quot;Look. Look at the sides of the road.&quot;</p>

<p>Everyone craned their necks to see.</p>

<p>On both sides of the path Ross had blasted open, the zombies remained crushed—completely immobilized, flattened as if the air itself continued pressing down on them.</p>

<p>Many weren’t even twitching. Some looked like they had been ironed into the ground.</p>

<p>&quot;They’re not moving,&quot; Joy whispered. &quot;They can’t move. The pressure Ross released... it’s still holding them down. I can feel it. A massive force is still lingering in the area. It’s like the world’s gravity changed.&quot;</p>

<p>Avery’s jaw dropped. She stared at Ross with wide, trembling eyes. &quot;That’s... impossible. Ross—just what are you?&quot;</p>

<p>Another woman added softly, almost reverently, &quot;No human can do that...&quot;</p>

<p>Joy nodded, swallowing hard. &quot;Even from inside the van, I can sense it. There’s a divine-level force still pressing everything flat. Ross isn’t just strong. He’s... something else.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross didn’t look up.</p>

<p>He didn’t answer their questions.</p>

<p>He simply kept driving, one hand casually on the wheel, the other tapping rhythmically against the window as the van cruised through the impossible path he had carved.</p>

<p>A slight smile curved his lips—quiet, mysterious, and terrifyingly confident.</p>

<p>Whatever Ross Oakley was...</p>

<p>He was far beyond anything they could understand.</p>
