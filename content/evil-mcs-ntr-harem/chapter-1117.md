<p>“W-What meat is this?”</p>

<p>“It tastes like premium beef but… not.”</p>

<p>“It’s too tender to be normal!”</p>

<p>“And the flavor—how is it this good?!”</p>

<p>The women turned toward Miku expectantly, but Miku could only chuckle before her eyes shifted toward Ross.</p>

<p>“Ross cooked it?”</p>

<p>“No wonder!”</p>

<p>“I should’ve known!”</p>

<p>Miku nodded proudly. “Well, I cooked the base, but Ross added his own spices and ingredients. And when Ross finishes a dish…” She exhaled deeply, almost reverently. “It becomes something else.”</p>

<p>Ace leaned back, arms crossed as he spoke like a man who had been blessed for years. “After eating Ross’s cooking, everything else feels dull. The flavors… they’re on another level.”</p>

<p>The newcomers took another bite.</p>

<p>“This tastes magical…”</p>

<p>“No—divine!”</p>

<p>“It feels like my entire body is warming up!”</p>

<p>“My fatigue is disappearing!”</p>

<p>One of them lifted her bowl to drink straight from it.</p>

<p>“Who is this man?!”</p>

<p>Their heads turned toward Ross again.</p>

<p>He sat quietly on a wooden bench, still looking a little pale, hands folded over his lap. A tired, humble smile rested on his lips.</p>

<p>To them, he looked like a fragile hero forcing himself to stay strong despite the blood he had spilled minutes earlier.</p>

<p>But inside—</p>

<p>Ross was smiling in triumph.</p>

<p>Just as planned.</p>

<p>His cooking was another weapon, another way to make them admire him, trust him, rely on him.</p>

<p>The women continued eating, savoring every spoonful.</p>

<p>“It’s so flavorful…”</p>

<p>“Each sip tastes different—deeper, richer!”</p>

<p>“I feel like crying. Is food supposed to be this good?”</p>

<p>“I didn’t know soup could taste like heaven…”</p>

<p>Miku laughed softly. “Welcome to Ross’s cooking.”</p>

<p>Ace nodded. “You get used to it. Eventually.”</p>

<p>All five newcomers stared at Ross with stars in their eyes—gratitude, awe, and budding infatuation mixing into a single overwhelming emotion.</p>

<p>Libby pressed her bowl to her chest like it was sacred. “Ross… thank you for saving me… and for this…”</p>

<p>Another girl whispered, “He’s… amazing.”</p>

<p>Another murmured, “He risked his life… to save someone he barely knows.”</p>

<p>And another added quietly, cheeks slightly red, “And he can cook like this…”</p>

<p>Their admiration deepened, solidified.</p>

<p>Ross kept his smile soft, humble… perfectly innocent.</p>

<p>Inside his mind, however—</p>

<p>Step two complete, he thought with satisfaction. Healing act successful. Cooking supremacy confirmed. They’re hooked.</p>

<p>And so he sat there, surrounded by warm gazes and blushes, while the aroma of his heavenly soup filled the room.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>An hour later, when the last bowls were wiped clean and everyone had settled into the warm, gentle glow of a full meal, the atmosphere finally felt calm enough for the five women to speak.</p>

<p>They had eaten quietly at first, as if afraid the food might disappear if they made too much noise.</p>

<p>Now, huddled together on the long couch, they exchanged looks—silent encouragements—before deciding it was time to tell the truth.</p>

<p>Giana sat at the center of the group, her fingers intertwined tightly with the others’.</p>

<p>Her eyes flickered from Ross to the rest of the room. She swallowed once, hard, and began.</p>

<p>They told Ross and the others everything.</p>

<p>They had been survivors—lucky ones, or so they thought.</p>

<p>Their camp had once been a refuge, a walled compound built from scrap metal, broken cars, and whatever else they could shove into place.</p>

<p>More than a hundred people had lived there—families, loners, elderly survivors, even a few children.</p>

<p>Life had been difficult, but it had been life, and in the apocalypse, that was priceless.</p>

<p>For a while, they managed. They hunted, scavenged, grew what little crops they could.</p>

<p>Their leader, an older man named Donovan, kept order and made sure disputes were settled quickly and fairly.</p>

<p>He wasn’t perfect, but he was humane—a rare quality in a dying world.</p>

<p>Everything fell apart the morning Donovan was found dead.</p>

<p>He had been murdered brutally, his throat slit, his belongings ransacked.</p>

<p>The camp fell into chaos, and in that chaos, a new leader rose—Bruno.</p>

<p>A hulking man with a shaved head, scarred knuckles, and a voice that silenced arguments by sheer force.</p>

<p>He declared Donovan weak and claimed that only strength could keep them alive.</p>

<p>At first, people accepted it. They were scared. They wanted direction. But then the whispers began.</p>

<p>Women waking up with bruises they couldn’t explain.</p>

<p>Girls going to sleep and never leaving their tents alone again.</p>

<p>A handful of women confiding in each other about being cornered, groped, threatened.</p>

<p>But none dared speak publicly.</p>

<p>Bruno had loyal men—thugs who patrolled the camp like guard dogs.</p>

<p>The first woman who tried to report Bruno… disappeared.</p>

<p>The second was found dead near the fences.</p>

<p>The third had her entire family threatened.</p>

<p>And so fear became the new law of the camp.</p>

<p>Giana’s voice shook as she continued.</p>

<p>“We… we thought maybe it wouldn’t reach us. That if we kept our heads down, if we didn’t draw attention, we’d be safe.”</p>

<p>Her hands trembled. One of the other women, Valery, squeezed her arm gently.</p>

<p>“But then,” Valery whispered, “Bruno started choosing. Like we were… things.” Her breath hitched.</p>

<p>“He would stare at us every day. We knew it was only a matter of time.”</p>

<p>They planned their escape for weeks.</p>

<p>They scavenged a map, gathered small food portions, identified weak points in the patrol schedule.</p>

<p>But even then, escaping was nearly impossible.</p>

<p>The camp was surrounded by makeshift walls and guarded by men who enjoyed violence.</p>

<p>They escaped anyway.</p>

<p>In the middle of the night, while a fire broke out on the west side, diverting most of Bruno’s men.</p>

<p>They ran barefoot through brush and dirt, not daring to stop, even as debris tore at their legs and lungs burned for air.</p>

<p>For an hour, they wandered, starving, exhausted, always looking over their shoulders and hiding from zombies.</p>

<p>They heard voices once—Bruno’s men searching for them—but they hid until the footsteps faded.</p>

<p>“And then… we saw the lights here,” Giana said, voice barely above a whisper. “We didn’t know if anyone kind lived here. But we had no choice.”</p>

<p>Her eyes glistened with tears she tried to blink away. “We just… hoped.”</p>

<p>She opened her mouth to continue—but froze.</p>

<p>THUD!</p>
