<p>Ross, of course, did as he promised. A week later, he arranged the meeting between the estranged couple—Ashley and Cyril.</p>

<p>It was quiet when they arrived, a subdued tension hanging in the air like a storm that had already passed but left everything broken in its wake.</p>

<p>Cyril looked good at first glance.</p>

<p>He was still dressed in designer clothes, his shoes polished, his watch expensive, and his hair carefully styled.</p>

<p>From afar, he might&#x27;ve looked like the same man who used to walk into boardrooms with confidence and command respect without speaking a word.</p>

<p>But up close, it was clear that something was missing. The light in his eyes had dulled. His once-proud shoulders slumped slightly.</p>

<p>There was stubble on his face, several days old, and it didn&#x27;t suit him—he had always been meticulous, clean-cut.</p>

<p>Now, he looked like a man weighed down by the weight of choices he couldn&#x27;t take back.</p>

<p>Despite still having access to his millions, it was painfully obvious: Cyril had lost something far more valuable.</p>

<p>He had lost his woman. He had lost Ashley.</p>

<p>And in the corner of the room, like a silent ghost pulling strings from the shadows, Ross sat—calm, detached, almost unreadable.</p>

<p>He was, in many ways, the reason all of this had happened. If fate were a writer, it clearly had a wicked sense of irony.</p>

<p>Ashley was the first to speak. She didn&#x27;t shout. She didn&#x27;t cry. She simply asked a single question.</p>

<p>&quot;Why?&quot;</p>

<p>Just one word. One syllable. But it was laced with everything she felt—anger, confusion, pain, betrayal.</p>

<p>Cyril&#x27;s lips parted, and he tried to speak. The truth was right there, teetering on the edge of his tongue.</p>

<p>But no sound came out. He tried again, a little more desperately, but something within him refused to let the words form.</p>

<p>His mouth moved, but his voice failed him.</p>

<p>Panicking, he grabbed a piece of paper from the table and a pen, trying to write down the truth.</p>

<p>His hand shook.</p>

<p>The pen barely touched the paper. He then reached for his phone, trying to type it out in a message—but his fingers felt heavy, almost paralyzed, like they belonged to someone else.</p>

<p>His mind screamed at his body to move, to act, to do something, but nothing worked.</p>

<p>It became painfully clear—Cyril was no longer in full control of himself.</p>

<p>Finally, with what little strength he had left, he forced out the only words he was seemingly allowed to say.</p>

<p>&quot;I&#x27;m sorry, Ashley. It was all my fault.&quot;</p>

<p>And with that, he broke. His voice cracked, and he began to cry—not with the quiet, dignified tears of a proud man, but the raw, messy sobs of someone who had finally accepted the depth of his failure.</p>

<p>He covered his face with his hands, ashamed, his body trembling with grief.</p>

<p>Ashley didn&#x27;t move. She didn&#x27;t say a word. She simply watched him, her expression unreadable.</p>

<p>Cyril, in the throes of his regret, could only replay the moment in his mind when his daughter had introduced Ross to the family.</p>

<p>At the time, Ross had been charming, polite—but ordinary. Cyril hadn&#x27;t paid him much attention.</p>

<p>He&#x27;d dismissed him as a phase, a fleeting crush, someone who didn&#x27;t belong in their world.</p>

<p>But oh, how wrong he&#x27;d been.</p>

<p>If only he had seen what Ross truly was—what he was capable of.</p>

<p>If only he had treated him with the respect he apparently deserved. Maybe things would&#x27;ve turned out differently.</p>

<p>Maybe Ashley wouldn&#x27;t be sitting across from him now, her heart broken and her trust shattered.</p>

<p>Looking at Ross now, Cyril felt something strange rise in his chest. It wasn&#x27;t just guilt—it was awe.</p>

<p>Fear. Helpless admiration.</p>

<p>If he had known then what he knew now, he might have dropped to his knees and addressed Ross like he was some kind of god.</p>

<p>Like Ross was his daddy or something.</p>

<p>But it was too late. The past couldn&#x27;t be bought—not even with all the money in the world.</p>

<p>And no apology, no wealth, no desperate plea could undo what had already been done.</p>

<p>Cyril was a man with everything… and yet, somehow, he&#x27;d ended up with nothing.</p>

<p>&quot;That&#x27;s all you can say? That you&#x27;re sorry?&quot; Ashley&#x27;s voice cracked. &quot;What the fuck were you thinking, Cyril?!&quot;</p>

<p>It was the first time she had ever cursed at him in all their years together.</p>

<p>Once, she might have thought herself too composed, too restrained for that kind of emotional outburst.</p>

<p>But now?</p>

<p>Now she felt like everything she&#x27;d believed about their marriage, about the man she married, had crumbled beneath her feet.</p>

<p>Cyril recoiled like he&#x27;d been struck. His eyes, usually calm and calculating, were glassy and unfocused.</p>

<p>He opened his mouth, desperation flickering in his expression as he tried to respond.</p>

<p>&quot;I… ammmm…&quot; he stammered, the sound escaping his lips weak and broken.</p>

<p>It was pathetic. And heartbreaking.</p>

<p>This wasn&#x27;t the Cyril she had known. The Cyril she married had been a man who commanded attention, who negotiated business deals with sharp intellect and disarming charm.</p>

<p>But now he stood in front of her like a prisoner locked inside his own body, unable to speak, unable to explain, unable to fight for what was left of their relationship.</p>

<p>&quot;Why are you stuttering?&quot; Ashley&#x27;s voice softened for a brief second, confusion overtaking her fury.</p>

<p>&quot;This… this isn&#x27;t like you at all, Cyril.&quot;</p>

<p>She took a step closer, eyes searching his face for some kind of answer, some glimmer of recognition—of the man he used to be.</p>

<p>But there was nothing there. Just a hollow, broken shell who looked like her husband but couldn&#x27;t even lift his voice to defend himself.</p>

<p>&quot;Say something. Please,&quot; she whispered.</p>

<p>But the silence stretched.</p>

<p>And with each second that passed, the anger returned, stronger than before.</p>

<p>She shook her head slowly, tears threatening to fall but never quite spilling over.</p>

<p>&quot;This is useless,&quot; she muttered bitterly.</p>

<p>With that, she turned and walked out, her footsteps echoing down the hallway. She didn&#x27;t look back.</p>

<p>There was no point. The man she had loved was gone—and what remained couldn&#x27;t even tell her why.</p>

<p>By the time she arrived home, she was drained. Her heart felt heavy, her limbs tired.</p>

<p>The day had taken more out of her than she expected.</p>

<p>She had gone there wanting closure—something, anything to help her make sense of everything that had fallen apart.</p>

<p>But she left with more questions than answers.</p>

<p>And a growing suspicion that something far darker was at play.</p>

<p>She stepped into the apartment and paused.</p>

<p>The aroma hit her immediately—warm, rich, and inviting.</p>

<p>A mix of sautéed garlic, rosemary, butter, and something slightly sweet.</p>

<p>It smelled like comfort. Like safety. Like home.</p>

<p>She blinked, almost in disbelief. She hadn&#x27;t realized how hungry she was until that very moment.</p>

<p>Ross was in the kitchen, as effortlessly put together as ever.</p>

<p>He wore a crisp white shirt with the sleeves rolled up to his elbows, and an apron that looked like it belonged on the cover of some upscale cooking magazine.</p>

<p>He moved with practiced ease, flipping something in a pan while humming to himself like he didn&#x27;t have a care in the world.</p>

<p>When he noticed her, he turned with that familiar, confident smile that never seemed to waver.</p>

<p>&quot;So,&quot; he said, spoon in hand, &quot;how did the meeting go?&quot;</p>

<p>Ashley just stood there for a moment, staring at him.</p>

<p>Everything about him was so controlled, so calm—so opposite from the broken mess she had just left behind. It was disorienting. Infuriating. Comforting. All at once.</p>

<p>She opened her mouth to respond, but nothing came out.</p>

<p>Ross tilted his head, watching her with curious amusement. &quot;That bad, huh?&quot;</p>

<p>Ashley finally moved, stepping inside and closing the door behind her.</p>

<p>She didn&#x27;t answer, not right away.</p>

<p>She felt like she was still carrying the weight of that meeting on her shoulders, and now she was standing in the middle of a world that seemed completely untouched by it.</p>

<p>Ross returned to the stove, casually stirring a simmering sauce.</p>

<p>&quot;Well,&quot; he continued, &quot;I hope you didn&#x27;t expect too much. Cyril&#x27;s been… unstable for a while now.&quot;</p>

<p>Ashley narrowed her eyes. &quot;What did you do to him?&quot;</p>

<p>Ross chuckled lightly, not even bothering to pretend he didn&#x27;t know what she meant.</p>

<p>&quot;I didn&#x27;t do anything. He did it to himself. All I ever did was give him the opportunity to show who he really was.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You broke him.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No,&quot; Ross said, turning toward her now, his eyes cool and sharp. &quot;He was already broken. I just stopped pretending otherwise.&quot;</p>

<p>There was something terrifying about the way Ross said it—calm, measured, like he was simply stating a fact. And maybe he was. Maybe that&#x27;s what made it worse.</p>

<p>Ashley looked away, her throat tightening. She didn&#x27;t want to admit it, but part of her felt… safer here. With Ross. Even after everything.</p>

<p>And that scared her more than anything else.</p>

<p>What was most interesting, though, was how the night ended.</p>

<p>After the meal was finished and the plates were cleared, a different kind of hunger would soon be satisfied.</p>
