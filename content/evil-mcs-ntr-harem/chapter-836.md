<p>A heavy silence fell over the car.</p>

<p>The city lights blurred past, but the weight of his words hung between them like a storm cloud. Lois shifted in her seat, refusing to meet his gaze.</p>

<p>Billy glanced at her again, this time softer. &quot;Lois, this isn’t just a story. It’s Ross Oakley. He’s got influence that reaches farther than you can imagine—politicians, police, billionaires. Even people in our own network.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m not afraid of him,&quot; Lois said quietly, though the firmness in her tone was laced with something else—something personal.</p>

<p>Billy noticed. &quot;This isn’t just about journalism for you, is it?&quot;</p>

<p>Lois stayed silent a beat too long before answering.</p>

<p>&quot;No. Of course it is. I just don’t like how millions of people are practically kissing the ground Ross Oakley walks on. He’s not a saint, and I’m going to expose him for what he truly is. A big bad wolf in sheep’s clothing.&quot;</p>

<p>That was all she gave him—yet it was more than enough to make Billy’s stomach sink.</p>

<p>A cold heaviness settled in his gut, because deep down, he knew the truth.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley wasn’t the kind of man you challenged... and lived to tell the tale.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>One week passed in a blur, and Monday arrived before Lois knew it.</p>

<p>She strolled into the office, coffee in hand, expecting the usual start-of-the-week drudgery—half-asleep coworkers, last-minute script edits, the occasional grumble about deadlines.</p>

<p>But today... was different.</p>

<p>The air practically crackled with tension. People were huddled in small clusters, whispering fiercely, eyes darting toward the TV screens on the walls.</p>

<p>Some clutched their phones like lifelines, scrolling for updates.</p>

<p>The atmosphere was so thick with unease that Lois slowed her steps.</p>

<p>&quot;What’s going on?&quot; she asked a nearby colleague, her frown deepening.</p>

<p>It wasn’t often the newsroom looked like someone had just announced the end of the world.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re late—and it’s big,&quot; the woman replied, leaning forward as if afraid to say it too loudly. &quot;Someone bought out the entire network.&quot;</p>

<p>Lois blinked. &quot;Bought out... what? Our network?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Yeah. As of this morning, the ink’s already dry.&quot; The colleague hesitated for a beat before adding, &quot;It’s Ross Oakley.&quot;</p>

<p>The name landed like a stone in Lois’s stomach. &quot;Ross... Oakley?&quot; she repeated, almost hoping she’d misheard.</p>

<p>&quot;He’s expanding into news media,&quot; the colleague continued. &quot;Apparently, this is just one of several networks he’s acquired. We’re part of his portfolio now.&quot;</p>

<p>Lois’s hand tightened around her coffee cup until she nearly crushed it.</p>

<p>Her mind whirled, replaying the interview, the heated words, the smug curve of Ross’s smile.</p>

<p>This wasn’t just business.</p>

<p>This felt personal—like a chess move made solely to remind her who controlled the board.</p>

<p>Around her, the newsroom buzzed with speculation, excitement, even admiration for their new, impossibly wealthy owner.</p>

<p>But Lois barely heard any of it.</p>

<p>The world seemed to tilt beneath her feet, a slow, dizzying realization sinking in.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley wasn’t just in her story anymore. He owned it.</p>

<p>Lois didn’t leave the office that day.</p>

<p>She stayed long after most people had clocked out, nursing a cup of coffee she didn’t even like just to give herself an excuse to linger.</p>

<p>Her desk sat near the glass wall, and every so often she caught glimpses of shadows moving in the executive wing—Ross Oakley’s territory now.</p>

<p>She’d seen him earlier in the conference room, flanked by high-ranking board members, his presence pulling everyone’s attention like gravity.</p>

<p>Even from across the hall, he seemed in control of the air itself.</p>

<p>No wonder people followed him. No wonder so many were too afraid to stand in his way.</p>

<p>Three o’clock came, and with it, the doors to the conference room opened.</p>

<p>The last batch of executives spilled out, laughter and handshakes all around, grins plastered on their faces like they’d just been promised a future paved in gold.</p>

<p>Whatever Ross had told them, they believed it—and they were thrilled about it.</p>

<p>Lois waited until the hallway was clear before stepping in.</p>

<p>Her heels clicked against the polished floor with sharp precision, each step a deliberate act of defiance.</p>

<p>Ross was exactly as she remembered him—leaning back in a leather chair as if the entire room belonged to him, exuding an unshakable calm that bordered on arrogance.</p>

<p>The faintest smirk tugged at his lips when he saw her, as though her arrival was the final move in a game he’d been orchestrating all along.</p>

<p>&quot;Well, well, well,&quot; he drawled, his voice carrying that lazy confidence that made people lean in just to catch every word.</p>

<p>&quot;Miss Johnson. What a surprise, seeing you again so soon.&quot; His gaze moved—slowly, unapologetically—from her eyes down the curve of her figure, lingering just long enough to make her pulse spike.</p>

<p>&quot;And I must say... you’ve only grown more beautiful with time.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Cut the crap, Mr. Oakley.&quot; Lois didn’t bother with pleasantries. She didn’t sit, didn’t even blink. &quot;Did you do this because of me? You couldn’t take losing to me in a spar of words, so you just bought the company I work for?&quot;</p>

<p>Ross’s smirk deepened, his eyes glinting with something dangerously close to amusement.</p>

<p>&quot;Right on the mark,&quot; he said without hesitation, as if her accusation were a compliment. &quot;And what’s wrong with that?&quot;</p>

<p>He leaned forward slightly, elbows on the table, the predator in him fully awake now.</p>

<p>&quot;In fact,&quot; he continued, voice dropping to a lower, more intimate tone, &quot;I’ll make you an offer. How does a hundred thousand dollars a month sound? Too little? Name your price, and I’ll double it.&quot;</p>

<p>The words hung between them, thick with implication.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just a job offer—it was a challenge. A line in the sand.</p>

<p>&quot;And in exchange, what do you want?&quot; Lois’s tone was cold enough to frost glass. Her gaze didn’t waver.</p>

<p>&quot;You want me to sleep with you, is that it?&quot; The words came out steady, but her pulse quickened.</p>

<p>In her experience, men like Ross Oakley never handed out generosity without expecting something in return—and that something was usually expensive, degrading, or both.</p>
