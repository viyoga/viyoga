<p>Everyone gathered around the common area, jotting down the supplies they wanted to buy for the week—snacks, toiletries, comfort items, and maybe a few luxury treats if the budget allowed.</p>

<p>The list slowly filled up as voices called out suggestions, but the mood was far from cheerful.</p>

<p>There was a tension in the air that couldn’t be ignored.</p>

<p>It clung to them like humidity before a storm, thick and suffocating.</p>

<p>Conversations were quieter. Smiles were tighter.</p>

<p>Every laugh seemed forced, a thin mask barely covering the anxiety underneath.</p>

<p>Tonight was Nomination Night—and everyone knew it.</p>

<p>Except Ross.</p>

<p>While most of the contestants stole uneasy glances at one another, wondering who would turn on whom, Ross remained perfectly calm.</p>

<p>He leaned back in his chair, relaxed, a small, knowing smile playing on his lips.</p>

<p>He scribbled down his requests—confidently, leisurely—as if this was just another day in paradise.</p>

<p>Unlike the others, his mood hadn’t shifted one bit.</p>

<p>Not even a flicker of doubt crossed his face.</p>

<p>Because Ross already knew how tonight would end.</p>

<p>And that made him the most dangerous person in the room.</p>

<p>After dinner, a heavy silence blanketed the house.</p>

<p>The dishes had barely been cleared when the atmosphere shifted, thick with unease.</p>

<p>It was 8 o’clock—nomination night had officially begun.</p>

<p>Everyone knew what was coming, and no one felt safe.</p>

<p>Contestants sat stiffly in the living room, each on edge, some nervously tapping their feet, others pretending to be calm while stealing glances at their housemates.</p>

<p>The tension was palpable, like a storm hanging in the air.</p>

<p>Everyone, that is, except Ross.</p>

<p>He lounged back in his seat with a quiet smirk, exuding the kind of confidence that either made you admire him or hate him.</p>

<p>The cuff that had once kept him attached to Tianna was finally gone.</p>

<p>It felt good—liberating, even. Not that he needed it anymore.</p>

<p>He had already claimed Tianna, body and soul.</p>

<p>There was no longer a need for symbols of possession. The cuff had served its purpose.</p>

<p>Now, it was just an obstacle—one that stood between her and the wild, uninhibited nights he intended to give her.</p>

<p>Ross stretched his arms behind his head, completely unfazed by the drama about to unfold.</p>

<p>In fact, he welcomed it.</p>

<p>A minute past eight, the booming voice of the house boss echoed through every speaker in the house, sharp and commanding.</p>

<p>&quot;Get ready, house contestants. Tonight marks your very first nomination night,&quot; the voice announced.</p>

<p>&quot;You will each vote for the person you believe is not suited—or does not belong—in my house.&quot;</p>

<p>The words hit like a slap. Whispers died. Breaths were held.</p>

<p>&quot;You have three votes to cast,&quot; the house boss continued.</p>

<p>&quot;One worth three points, another worth two points, and the last one worth one point. Choose carefully. These votes will determine who faces next week’s eviction.&quot;</p>

<p>Some contestants looked around, suddenly unsure who their friends were.</p>

<p>Bonds forged over meals and shared chores began to feel fragile.</p>

<p>Every smile now seemed suspicious.</p>

<p>&quot;The three contestants who receive the highest number of points will be nominated for eviction,&quot; the house boss declared. &quot;Your names will be called at random.&quot;</p>

<p>A pause. Hearts raced.</p>

<p>&quot;Joan, come into the boss room.&quot;</p>

<p>Joan froze. Her face went pale as she slowly stood up.</p>

<p>She forced a smile that didn’t reach her eyes, then walked stiffly toward the boss room door like a prisoner headed to trial.</p>

<p>No one dared speak. All they could do was watch—and wonder.</p>

<p>Who would be next?</p>

<p>And more importantly... who would still be here at the end of the week?</p>

<p>Ross simply chuckled to himself. This was just the beginning—and he already knew how the game would play out.</p>

<p>One by one, the house contestants took their turn, disappearing into the boss room to cast their votes.</p>

<p>The atmosphere inside the house grew heavier with each passing minute.</p>

<p>The sound of the door opening and closing echoed ominously, a reminder that alliances meant little when survival was on the line.</p>

<p>No one spoke. They only exchanged glances—some nervous, some calculating, and a few filled with quiet resentment.</p>

<p>Inside the boss room, the votes flowed like confessions in a church booth.</p>

<p>&quot;I give 3 points to Big D,&quot; one contestant said, trying to sound firm. &quot;I don’t like his attitude. He’s always arrogant, always trying to be the center of attention. It’s exhausting.&quot;</p>

<p>Another one followed. &quot;I give 2 points to Jason. He farts a lot—like, a lot. And he does it on purpose when there are people around. It’s gross and disrespectful.&quot;</p>

<p>A third voice: &quot;I give 1 point to Lily. She hasn’t lifted a finger since day one. No dishes, no sweeping, nothing. We’re all tired of it.&quot;</p>

<p>Others echoed similar sentiments, mixing petty grievances with strategic moves.</p>

<p>Some picked their targets based on personal annoyances.</p>

<p>Others simply saw an opportunity to eliminate competition.</p>

<p>There were even a few who voted for friends, just to keep the heat off themselves.</p>

<p>After nearly an hour, the voting was complete.</p>

<p>The contestants returned to the living room, faces blank, trying to read each other without giving anything away.</p>

<p>Every smile looked fake now. Every friendly glance felt suspicious.</p>

<p>Then, the house boss’s voice came on again—loud, slow, and heavy like a judge delivering a verdict.</p>

<p>&quot;The votes have been counted.&quot;</p>

<p>A few contestants straightened up. Others visibly flinched.</p>

<p>&quot;The three nominated house contestants are...&quot;</p>

<p>The room went still. You could hear a pin drop.</p>

<p>&quot;Big D, with 45 points.&quot;</p>

<p>A collective gasp swept the room. Big D’s face remained passive.</p>

<p>His muscles fit not tense at all nor his jaw twitch.</p>

<p>He stared straight and smiled and no one could understand how he seemed to not care one bit about this bad result for him.</p>

<p>&quot;Jason, with 30 points.&quot;</p>

<p>Jason’s eyes widened. &quot;What the fuck?&quot; he muttered under his breath. He looked around the room, betrayed. &quot;I thought y’all liked me.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Lily, with 19 points.&quot;</p>

<p>Lily let out a small, stunned breath.</p>

<p>Her lips trembled, and her hands gripped the edge of the couch.</p>

<p>&quot;Seriously? Nineteen points?&quot; she whispered. &quot;All because of some dishes?&quot;</p>
