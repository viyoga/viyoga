<p>Kristine gaped at her. &quot;You did not just say that.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I did,&quot; Sasha said, taking a slow sip from her glass. &quot;Look, I was just playing. It&#x27;s not like I&#x27;m going to join his little collection of wives. But come on, Kristine—admit it. He got under your skin too.&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine looked away, her expression flustered. &quot;He&#x27;s just... good at talking. That&#x27;s all.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha smirked. &quot;He&#x27;s good at a lot of things, I bet.&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine shot her a look. &quot;You&#x27;re impossible.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Maybe,&quot; Sasha said, laughing. &quot;But I&#x27;m not boring. And tonight? That was the most fun I&#x27;ve had in a long time.&quot;</p>

<p>There was a pause, the kind that settles in after a storm—calm, reflective, uncertain.</p>

<p>&quot;You really think you can handle someone like him?&quot; Kristine asked softly.</p>

<p>Sasha looked at her, her expression suddenly more thoughtful. &quot;No. But I don&#x27;t have to. I just want to play the game. And Ross? He&#x27;s the kind of opponent who makes it worth playing.&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine frowned. &quot;He&#x27;s not playing, Sasha. That man—he&#x27;s serious. He knows exactly what he wants.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha&#x27;s smile faded slightly as she stared into her glass, watching the crimson wine catch the ambient light.</p>

<p>&quot;Yeah,&quot; she murmured, almost to herself. &quot;That&#x27;s what makes him dangerous. But that&#x27;s also what makes it fun.&quot; She shrugged, feigning indifference, even as her heart betrayed her with a quiet thrum of excitement.</p>

<p>She couldn&#x27;t stop thinking about Ross.</p>

<p>About the cool confidence he carried like a second skin, the way his voice had a rhythm that made everything he said sound like poetry dipped in power.</p>

<p>There was something about him that pulled at her—compelled her—even though she knew better than to fall for that type.</p>

<p>Kristine watched her closely, concern flickering in her eyes. &quot;I hope he doesn&#x27;t call you.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha arched an eyebrow. &quot;Why?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Because you act like you&#x27;re playing with him, but I think he&#x27;s playing a deeper game,&quot; Kristine said softly. &quot;He&#x27;s not someone who just flirts and walks away. Be careful, okay?&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha sighed, tossing her hair back as if to brush off the weight of the warning. &quot;I will. I can handle him. Heck, I can handle ten of him,&quot; she said with a wink, trying to lighten the mood.</p>

<p>Kristine didn&#x27;t laugh. She just shook her head.</p>

<p>Sasha giggled anyway, letting the sound fill the space between them.</p>

<p>It was easier to laugh than admit she was nervous.</p>

<p>After that, they paid the bill, stepped out into the cool night, and parted ways under the streetlights, the city buzzing softly around them.</p>

<p>But Sasha&#x27;s thoughts stayed locked on Ross.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>One Week Later</p>

<p>Sasha tried not to think about him.</p>

<p>She told herself she was fine, that she didn&#x27;t need validation from some man with too many wives and too much charm.</p>

<p>She had other things to worry about. Work. Friends. Life. Her boyfriend.</p>

<p>And yet… every time her phone buzzed, she felt it. That flash of hope.</p>

<p>She hated it. Hated how he&#x27;d gotten under her skin with just a few hours of smooth talk and intense stares.</p>

<p>She&#x27;d replayed that dinner more than once in her head—his voice, the way his eyes lingered, the way he made her feel like she was the only woman in the room.</p>

<p>Still, a week passed. No calls. No texts. Nothing.</p>

<p>She told herself it was for the best.</p>

<p>Then, late one Friday evening, as she sat on her couch in her pajama shorts with a half-eaten tub of ice cream in her lap, her phone buzzed.</p>

<p>Want to go have fun tonight? — R.</p>

<p>Sasha stared at the message, frozen.</p>

<p>No context. No preamble. Just a direct invitation from a number she didn&#x27;t recognize—but she knew it was him. She knew.</p>

<p>A thousand thoughts ran through her head.</p>

<p>He waited a whole week.</p>

<p>Did he forget about her? Was this some casual text he sent to every girl he met?</p>

<p>Why did her heart flutter like a schoolgirl&#x27;s?</p>

<p>She set the phone down. Picked it back up. Set it down again.</p>

<p>Her heart was racing now. Her hands were clammy. She bit her lower lip and muttered to herself, &quot;I won&#x27;t reply. Ignore. Ignore.&quot;</p>

<p>She tossed the phone onto the couch and marched into the kitchen like that would somehow clear her head.</p>

<p>Opened the fridge. Stared at nothing. Closed it again. Her thoughts spun.</p>

<p>A minute passed.</p>

<p>Then two.</p>

<p>Then she walked back to the couch and picked up the phone again.</p>

<p>She reread the message.</p>

<p>Once.</p>

<p>Twice.</p>

<p>Ten times.</p>

<p>He didn&#x27;t even say her name. Just assumed she&#x27;d know.</p>

<p>And of course, she did.</p>

<p>&quot;Goddammit,&quot; she whispered, running a hand through her hair. &quot;Why do I want to go?&quot;</p>

<p>She tapped her fingers against the screen, then locked her phone. Then unlocked it again.</p>

<p>Finally, after a long, drawn-out battle with her pride, her curiosity won.</p>

<p>She typed:</p>

<p>Where and when?</p>

<p>And hit send.</p>

<p>Instantly, her heart sank and soared all at once.</p>

<p>There was no going back now.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Sasha came to see Ross—but she didn&#x27;t come unarmed, and she didn&#x27;t come to lose.</p>

<p>Ross had offered to pick her up, his tone casual but loaded with hidden promise.</p>

<p>She&#x27;d refused with a smile, her words sweet but firm. There was no way she&#x27;d let him dictate the pace from the very beginning. That wasn&#x27;t her style.</p>

<p>Instead, she arrived at the meeting place with three bodyguards—silent, well-dressed men who moved like shadows and didn&#x27;t flinch under scrutiny.</p>

<p>Some might say it was overkill. Sasha called it insurance.</p>

<p>The location Ross had chosen was nothing short of luxurious.</p>

<p>A rooftop lounge perched above the glittering skyline, with soft jazz humming in the background and a private corner table reserved just for the two of them.</p>

<p>A full moon hung above them, bathing the city in silver light, while a soft breeze carried the scent of roses from the garden terrace nearby.</p>

<p>And there he was.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley stood near the edge of the balcony, hands tucked into his pockets, framed by the night like something out of a movie.</p>

<p>His dark suit hugged his form perfectly, his expression unreadable but deeply confident—like he already knew how the night would end.</p>
