<p>&quot;How did it taste, Heaven? Was it as good as it smelled?&quot; one of the girls asked, leaning forward, her curiosity barely contained.</p>

<p>All around the room, the others paused what they were doing.</p>

<p>Conversations died down, utensils hovered mid-air, and the soft rustle of movement quieted.</p>

<p>It was as if the entire room had collectively held its breath to hear her answer.</p>

<p>Everyone had been wondering what that mysterious, mouth-watering aroma had been, and now they were about to find out.</p>

<p>Heaven, sitting with crossed legs and her expression unreadable, slowly set down her fork.</p>

<p>She dabbed the corner of her lips with a napkin, her eyes flicking up at the audience she now held in the palm of her hand.</p>

<p>&quot;Yup,&quot; she finally said, her voice carrying just the right amount of dramatic pause.</p>

<p>&quot;In my 25 years on this planet, that is hands down the most delicious thing I’ve ever tasted. Nothing else even comes close.&quot;</p>

<p>A few gasps and murmurs of agreement rippled through the crowd.</p>

<p>&quot;But...&quot; she added, her lips curling into a half-smirk before turning into a scowl. &quot;It’s a shame the man who made it is a complete bastard.&quot;</p>

<p>Her declaration sent a wave of surprise through the listeners. Eyebrows raised. Heads turned.</p>

<p>&quot;What? Why?&quot; someone asked.</p>

<p>&quot;What did he do?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Did he say something nasty?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Spill!&quot;</p>

<p>Their voices overlapped as curiosity morphed into a full-blown demand for gossip.</p>

<p>It didn’t take long before Heaven, clearly enjoying the attention, recounted the brief but fiery exchange she’d had with Ross.</p>

<p>Within minutes, the story had circulated throughout the house.</p>

<p>People were whispering behind their hands, shooting glances at Ross, who was now sitting at the far end of the room with a relaxed grin on his face, as if all the drama unfolding around him didn’t bother him in the slightest.</p>

<p>A lanky man with a cocky grin who was popular in his circle of friends spoke up with a scoff.</p>

<p>He leaned back in his chair and put his feet on the table, making a show of it.</p>

<p>&quot;Hah... don’t make me laugh, Ross. You really think you’ve got a shot with Heaven? That girl’s way out of your league. You trying to get with her is a joke. Just close your eyes and jack off to the idea like everyone else.&quot;</p>

<p>The room burst into laughter. A few people clapped, others whistled. Ross didn’t blink. His smirk only grew deeper.</p>

<p>He set down his drink and finally spoke, his voice calm but charged with something heavier—conviction.</p>

<p>&quot;I don’t think so,&quot; he said, rising slowly to his feet. The sudden shift in posture made a few people quiet down.</p>

<p>&quot;Let me tell you what’s going to happen instead.&quot; He scanned the room, eyes locking with Heaven’s for just a second.</p>

<p>&quot;Before this weekly task is over... Heaven is going to come to me. Not just come to me—she’s going to beg me to fuck her.&quot;</p>

<p>Gasps filled the room like a wave crashing through a crowd.</p>

<p>Heaven’s eyes narrowed instantly, but a flicker of something—amusement? Interest?—crossed her face before she scoffed and looked away.</p>

<p>&quot;Oh please,&quot; someone muttered.</p>

<p>&quot;Arrogant bastard,&quot; another whispered.</p>

<p>But deep down, whether they wanted to admit it or not, everyone was now waiting to see if Ross’s insane prediction would come true.</p>

<p>Dinner arrived, and it didn’t take long for someone to finally understand what Ross had meant with his bold statement earlier.</p>

<p>As the group settled around the long dining table, laughter and idle chatter filled the air.</p>

<p>Plates clinked, spoons scraped, and the aroma of freshly prepared dishes wafted across the room.</p>

<p>Everyone was enjoying the meal—everyone except one person.</p>

<p>&quot;Heaven? What’s the matter?&quot; a girl sitting beside her asked, eyeing her untouched plate. &quot;Don’t you like the food?&quot;</p>

<p>All eyes shifted toward Heaven, who sat motionless, her fork barely lifted, a faint sheen of sweat glistening on her forehead.</p>

<p>She looked pale—unnaturally so—and her expression was tight, like she was trying not to gag.</p>

<p>&quot;No... I’m fine. Just not feeling well,&quot; Heaven said, forcing a weak smile. Her voice lacked conviction.</p>

<p>A few more heads turned her way. It was obvious something was wrong.</p>

<p>Trevor, who had been watching her with concern from across the table, slammed his glass down louder than necessary.</p>

<p>&quot;I knew it,&quot; he declared, looking righteously indignant. &quot;That big dickhead probably put something in your food earlier. He must’ve messed with it to mess with you. We should call the house boss and get a doctor in here, Heaven. Seriously.&quot;</p>

<p>He leaned forward, trying to sound protective but also making sure everyone heard him.</p>

<p>After all, he liked Heaven. A lot. And who wouldn’t?</p>

<p>She was easily a 10 out of 10—beautiful, confident, and the center of everyone’s attention.</p>

<p>If he could score some points now, maybe she’d finally notice him the way he noticed her.</p>

<p>Heaven shook her head, her expression unreadable.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s nothing,&quot; she muttered.</p>

<p>But her hand trembled slightly as she picked up her spoon.</p>

<p>She forced herself to take a bite, chewing slowly like she was eating paper instead of food. Every swallow looked like a struggle.</p>

<p>The atmosphere at the table shifted. The earlier noise and cheer died down, replaced by quiet glances and whispered suspicions.</p>

<p>Trevor leaned closer, still watching her. &quot;Are you sure? You really don’t look okay.&quot;</p>

<p>Heaven didn’t answer. She just kept chewing, eyes vacant, trying to hold herself together.</p>

<p>But even she couldn’t deny it anymore—the taste she once called the most delicious thing she’d ever had now tasted like ash on her tongue.</p>

<p>And Ross, from across the room, was watching her with a smile that said everything.</p>

<p>The night passed, and soon the third day of the week arrived.</p>

<p>Breakfast came and went. Then lunch. Still, nothing had changed.</p>

<p>Heaven pushed the food around on her plate with growing frustration.</p>

<p>No matter what she tried—sweet, savory, spicy—everything tasted bland, dull, almost offensive.</p>

<p>What once brought her joy now felt like chewing cardboard soaked in bitterness.</p>
