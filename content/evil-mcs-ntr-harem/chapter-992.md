<p>The city was dark and nearly empty at that hour.</p>

<p>The cold wind brushed against her face as she drove through the quiet streets of Chicago, her thoughts racing.</p>

<p>She didn’t know why she was doing this — maybe to end it, maybe to face him once and for all.</p>

<p>Ross had told her where to meet, and finding the place wasn’t difficult.</p>

<p>It was an old park on the edge of the city, nearly deserted at night, the streetlights casting long, lonely shadows across the pavement.</p>

<p>When she arrived, he was already there — leaning casually against a bench, his car parked nearby.</p>

<p>As she turned off her engine, Ross stepped forward, a grin spreading across his face.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s good of you to come, Jennifer,&quot; he said, his voice smooth and confident. &quot;This will be a night of revelation for the two of us.&quot;</p>

<p>Jennifer’s hands tightened on the steering wheel, her heart pounding.</p>

<p>Jennifer stepped out of the car and locked it behind her, the soft click echoing faintly in the quiet evening air.</p>

<p>The city park stretched before them, dimly lit by scattered lamps that flickered like distant fireflies.</p>

<p>The wind carried a faint chill, rustling the fallen leaves around her heels.</p>

<p>&quot;Ross, what happened l—&quot; she began, but before she could finish, Ross pulled her close and kissed her.</p>

<p>It wasn’t gentle—it was urgent, claiming, the kind of kiss that stole her breath and left her thoughts spinning.</p>

<p>For a moment, she forgot where she was, forgot the streetlights, the world, even her own name.</p>

<p>When he finally drew back, her lips tingled, and her heart thudded painfully in her chest.</p>

<p>She looked up at him, dazed, her cheeks flushed a deep pink.</p>

<p>The look in his eyes—calm, steady, and unyielding—made her remember all too vividly the side of him she’d seen before: confident, dominant, impossible to resist.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s pulse raced as she tried to gather herself.</p>

<p>She noticed, belatedly, a few onlookers nearby—two junkies loitering near the benches, a man sleeping by the fence.</p>

<p>They’d seen the kiss. Shame burned in her chest, and she quickly turned away.</p>

<p>&quot;Ross...&quot; Her voice cracked slightly. &quot;I love my husband. Please, if you want this... whatever this is, to continue, no one can ever know. Not a soul.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross tilted his head, studying her. A faint, knowing smile tugged at the corner of his lips.</p>

<p>&quot;Of course,&quot; he said, his tone smooth and composed. &quot;If that’s what you want, then it’s done.&quot;</p>

<p>He paused, his eyes glinting beneath the park lights.</p>

<p>&quot;But you’ll have to do everything I say, Jennifer. No questions, no hesitation. That’s the only way this works.&quot;</p>

<p>Jennifer’s breath hitched.</p>

<p>For a moment, she wanted to argue, to remind him she wasn’t some puppet—but she couldn’t.</p>

<p>She knew too well what it meant to defy him, and deep down, she wasn’t sure she wanted to.</p>

<p>Ross extended his hand, his expression unreadable. After a brief hesitation, she took it.</p>

<p>His grip was firm, almost possessive, yet somehow reassuring.</p>

<p>Without another word, he guided her down the narrow path that wound through the park, the distant city lights fading behind them.</p>

<p>The silence between them was heavy—charged with something neither could name.</p>

<p>Jennifer could feel her heart pounding with every step, torn between guilt and an unshakable pull toward the man walking beside her.</p>

<p>After a minute of walking, they found an empty bench tucked away beneath a pair of tall maple trees.</p>

<p>The night air was still, the faint rustle of leaves the only sound breaking the silence.</p>

<p>Jennifer thought for sure Ross had brought her here for one reason—there was hardly any other explanation for wandering into such a secluded part of the park—but instead of stopping, he simply brushed past the bench, his hand still firmly wrapped around hers.</p>

<p>&quot;Where are we going?&quot; she asked softly, though her voice trembled slightly.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t answer.</p>

<p>He just led her a few steps further, toward the shadows behind the bench, where a lamppost stood watch above them.</p>

<p>Its dim, golden light spilled across the ground, just bright enough to make them visible to anyone who might wander nearby.</p>

<p>The idea of being seen sent a strange thrill down Jennifer’s spine.</p>

<p>She waited for him to speak, to do something—anything—but before she could ask again, Ross turned and pressed her back gently against the rough bark of a tree.</p>

<p>The sudden contact stole her breath.</p>

<p>Then he kissed her, deeply, urgently, with the same fire that had burned between them the night before—maybe even stronger.</p>

<p>It was the kind of kiss that made the world shrink to nothing but the warmth of his breath and the sound of her own heartbeat pounding in her ears.</p>

<p>His hands moved instinctively, tracing the outline of her waist, her back, her hips.</p>

<p>The thin fabric of her white blouse and the snug fit of her jeans did little to stop the heat of his touch.</p>

<p>She could feel his strength in the way his fingers guided her, the way his touch seemed to both demand and cherish at once.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s thoughts scattered like leaves in the wind. Part of her knew this was reckless—they were standing right under a lamppost where anyone could see—but another part of her didn’t care.</p>

<p>There was something magnetic about Ross, something that drew her closer even when logic told her to stay away.</p>

<p>He pulled back just enough to look at her. His breath brushed against her lips as he murmured her name, his voice low and rough.</p>

<p>The way he said it made her shiver.</p>

<p>In that quiet, under the faint glow of the streetlight, the rest of the world felt impossibly far away.</p>

<p>&quot;Hmm...&quot; Jennifer’s lips parted under his, her breath catching as Ross pressed closer.</p>

<p>Their kiss deepened, and for a moment, the rest of the world seemed to vanish.</p>

<p>The faint glow of the lamppost above them cast soft shadows across their faces, but she didn’t notice.</p>

<p>She didn’t care. Ross was simply too good at making her not care.</p>
