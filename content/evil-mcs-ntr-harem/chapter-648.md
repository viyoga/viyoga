<p>&quot;Are you okay, girlfriend? Why the long face?&quot; asked a stunning woman with icy blue eyes and high cheekbones, her Russian roots evident in both her accent and elegance.</p>

<p>She placed a manicured hand gently on her friend&#x27;s arm, concern flickering in her expression.</p>

<p>Kristine, the beautiful Filipina sitting across from her, gave a faint smile, but it didn&#x27;t quite reach her eyes.</p>

<p>She looked down at her drink—a tall glass of mango juice with a small umbrella poking out—and stirred it absentmindedly with the straw.</p>

<p>&quot;I don&#x27;t know, Sasha,&quot; she said quietly, her voice carrying the weight of something deeper. &quot;Colton&#x27;s just… not the same lately. Ever since he lost in the NBA Finals, he&#x27;s been different.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha raised a perfectly shaped brow. &quot;Different how? You mean like... sad? Distant?&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine let out a soft, humorless laugh. &quot;I wish it were just that. He&#x27;s become more controlling—paranoid, even. He asked for all the passwords to my social media accounts last week. Then yesterday, he wanted the passcode to my phone. I didn&#x27;t think much of it at first, but... it&#x27;s getting weird.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha&#x27;s expression shifted from concern to mild disbelief. &quot;Wait, what? That doesn&#x27;t sound like Colton at all. He used to be so confident. Cool. Always the guy who walked into a room like he owned it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Exactly,&quot; Kristine said, leaning back in her seat. &quot;That&#x27;s what made me fall in love with him. He had this unshakable presence. But now… it&#x27;s like the loss shook him to his core. He doesn&#x27;t trust me anymore. Or maybe he doesn&#x27;t trust himself.&quot;</p>

<p>The women sat in silence for a moment, the sounds of clinking glasses and soft lounge music filling the upscale café around them.</p>

<p>&quot;I&#x27;m trying to be patient,&quot; Kristine continued, her voice softer now. &quot;I know losing the championship hit him hard. He poured everything into that season. But I didn&#x27;t expect it to affect us like this.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha nodded slowly, thoughtful. &quot;Men and pride—it&#x27;s a complicated thing. Especially for someone like Colton. When they lose something that defines them, it rattles their sense of identity. But still… there&#x27;s no excuse for him to take it out on you.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I keep wondering if I&#x27;m overreacting,&quot; Kristine admitted. &quot;Maybe it&#x27;s just a phase. Maybe I need to give him space. Or maybe…&quot; Her voice trailed off.</p>

<p>&quot;Maybe he needs a wake-up call,&quot; Sasha finished for her. &quot;He&#x27;s lucky to have you, Kristine. Don&#x27;t let him make you feel otherwise.&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine gave a grateful smile. &quot;Thanks, Sasha. You always know what to say.&quot;</p>

<p>Sasha smiled back, then clapped her hands lightly. &quot;Alright, enough doom and gloom. Let&#x27;s forget about insecure husbands and paranoid basketball stars. You need a distraction. Shopping spree, my treat. And then maybe some ice cream?&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine laughed—really laughed—for the first time that day. &quot;God, yes. Let&#x27;s go crazy. I need a new pair of heels anyway.&quot;</p>

<p>The two friends stood up, linking arms as they left the café behind, their laughter trailing through the air like sunlight breaking through storm clouds.</p>

<p>They followed through on all their plans—and more.</p>

<p>From a luxurious boutique in the fashion district to a surprise stop at their favorite gelato spot, Sasha and Kristine made the most of the day.</p>

<p>As dusk settled over the city, they ended their outing with dinner at an elegant rooftop restaurant, its panoramic view of the skyline bathed in golden twilight.</p>

<p>It was a perfect setting—soft jazz playing in the background, candlelight dancing on wine glasses, and the quiet murmur of conversation from other diners adding to the relaxed ambiance.</p>

<p>They&#x27;d dressed up for the occasion, not for anyone else, but for themselves.</p>

<p>Kristine wore a pale blue dress that complimented her complexion, while Sasha&#x27;s sleek red number turned more than a few heads.</p>

<p>They laughed over old college memories—Sasha&#x27;s dramatic break-up in the middle of the library, Kristine&#x27;s failed attempt at baking cookies for a guy she liked—and toasted to the years that had flown by since.</p>

<p>They had been inseparable back then, and even now, after careers, marriages, and changes, they were still each other&#x27;s sanctuary.</p>

<p>They were more than best friends; they were each other&#x27;s constants.</p>

<p>The type of bond that didn&#x27;t need daily texts or constant communication to remain strong.</p>

<p>They could pick up after weeks apart and fall right back into sync. It was rare, and they both knew it.</p>

<p>But there was an unspoken understanding that this season of their lives wouldn&#x27;t last forever.</p>

<p>Kristine was married now. Her responsibilities had shifted.</p>

<p>Sasha, ever the free spirit, was still enjoying her independence, but even she spoke more often about settling down, about finding someone serious.</p>

<p>And when children came into the picture—because they would, eventually—days like this would be harder to come by.</p>

<p>Schedules would conflict. Priorities would change.</p>

<p>So tonight, they savored the moment like a final glass of wine—full of warmth, bittersweet around the edges.</p>

<p>And then, just as their food arrived and they were debating over whether to share a dessert, a voice interrupted their bubble.</p>

<p>&quot;Hello, ladies. Mind if I join you for the night?&quot; came the smooth, unfamiliar voice of a man.</p>

<p>They looked up, caught off guard. The man was tall, dressed in a sharp navy blazer with the casual charm of someone used to getting his way.</p>

<p>His smile was confident—too confident—and before either of them could respond, he pulled out the chair beside Kristine and sat down without waiting for permission.</p>

<p>&quot;My date stood me up,&quot; he added, as if that somehow justified the intrusion. &quot;Hate to eat alone.&quot;</p>

<p>Kristine blinked, momentarily stunned. Sasha, on the other hand, narrowed her eyes slightly. Her lips curled into a polite smile, but there was a flicker of ice behind her gaze.</p>

<p>&quot;Wow,&quot; Sasha said slowly, setting her wine glass down. &quot;That&#x27;s… bold.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;We were actually in the middle of something,&quot; Kristine added, trying to keep her voice light but firm. &quot;A girls&#x27; night.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Even better,&quot; the man said with a chuckle, completely undeterred. &quot;I&#x27;ve always been told I have great energy. Besides, three&#x27;s a party, right?&quot;</p>
