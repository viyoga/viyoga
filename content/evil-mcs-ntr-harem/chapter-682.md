<p>“That’s Ross…” Colton muttered, eyes locked on the image.</p>

<p>“Yup,” a teammate confirmed, giving a grim nod. “And word is, that wasn’t just a one-time thing. There’s more. A lot more.”</p>

<p>Another teammate chimed in with a smirk. “Heard that dude’s packing a goddamn anaconda between his legs. Your wife Kristine probably walked funny for days.”</p>

<p>The court exploded with another round of laughter—some taunting, some nervous, others just cruel.</p>

<p>Colton’s hands curled into fists.</p>

<p>His knuckles turned white as the blood drained from his face.</p>

<p>He tried to breathe, tried to calm himself down, but the rage rising inside him was like a volcano ready to blow.</p>

<p>His teammates’ voices blurred into static. All he could see was Kristine’s face—her smile.</p>

<p>That same smile she used to greet him with after long days, now turned toward another man in freefall.</p>

<p>A man who wasn’t just famous—but infamous.</p>

<p>And Ross Oakley? He had the reputation. The resources. The reach.</p>

<p>Colton knew he couldn’t compete with that. Not in money. Not in lifestyle. Not in size.</p>

<p>The thought made his stomach churn.</p>

<p>“That bitch…” Colton hissed, voice cracking with fury.</p>

<p>“Fuck!”</p>

<p>He hurled the basketball across the court, sending it bouncing violently off the far wall.</p>

<p>Then, without another word, he tore off his practice jersey and stormed toward the locker room.</p>

<p>“Damn…” someone muttered as he passed.</p>

<p>“You guys really shouldn’t have said anything,” another said quietly, but no one stopped him.</p>

<p>The court went silent behind him as Colton disappeared through the exit.</p>

<p>What none of them knew was that he wasn’t going home to talk things out.</p>

<p>He was going to find Kristine.</p>

<p>And this time, it wasn’t going to end in laughter.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Colton took the first flight back to his home city, his mind churning the entire way.</p>

<p>The seatbelt felt like a restraint, holding him back from the confrontation he knew was coming.</p>

<p>As soon as the plane touched down, he didn’t wait—he rushed straight home, his heart pounding with dread and fury.</p>

<p>But when he stepped through the front door, silence greeted him.</p>

<p>The house was cold, quiet—too quiet.</p>

<p>Lights were off, and not a single trace of life lingered inside.</p>

<p>It didn’t feel like a home anymore. It felt like a shell.</p>

<p>His jaw clenched as he tossed his bag onto the couch and pulled out his phone.</p>

<p>He dialed Kristine.</p>

<p>No answer.</p>

<p>He cursed under his breath and called again.</p>

<p>Still nothing.</p>

<p>By the third try, his blood was boiling.</p>

<p>When the call finally connected, thumping bass nearly burst his eardrum.</p>

<p>People were laughing in the background, shouting over the music.</p>

<p>It didn’t sound like a quiet night in with friends—it sounded like a party.</p>

<p>“Where the fuck are you?” Colton snapped, barely able to keep his voice steady.</p>

<p>“What?” Kristine’s voice came through, muffled and distracted.</p>

<p>“I said—where the fuck are you?” he shouted, his voice cracking with rage.</p>

<p>“Oh… I’m with Sasha right now,” she said after a moment, her tone too casual, too unconcerned. “We’re at a birthday party with some friends.”</p>

<p>“A birthday party?” Colton repeated, his voice low and dangerous. “Who else is with you?”</p>

<p>Kristine hesitated just a second too long. “Just me and Sasha and a few friends from college,” she answered.</p>

<p>“Lies!” he exploded. “You’re with Ross Oakley, aren’t you? Tell me—is he there with you?”</p>

<p>“No, he’s not,” she replied quickly, but there was a beat—a small pause that made Colton’s stomach twist.</p>

<p>He didn’t believe her. Not entirely.</p>

<p>“Tell me where you are,” he said through gritted teeth. “I’ll come get you. We need to talk. Now.”</p>

<p>Kristine was quiet for a moment.</p>

<p>The music still played, voices still shouted and laughed in the background—but she sounded different now.</p>

<p>Tired. Maybe even resigned.</p>

<p>“I’ll come home later,” she said at last, her voice quieter, more grounded. “And you’re right, Colton. We do need to talk.”</p>

<p>Her words sent a chill through him. Something about her tone… it wasn’t just guilt.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just defensiveness. It was finality.</p>

<p>And for the first time since boarding that plane, Colton wasn’t angry.</p>

<p>He was scared.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>The hours crawled by, each minute like a drop of acid eating away at Colton’s patience.</p>

<p>The house remained dark and still, the silence so thick it rang in his ears.</p>

<p>He had tried pacing, tried drinking, tried breathing just to keep from exploding, but nothing worked.</p>

<p>He kept glancing at the front door, as if sheer willpower would force it to open.</p>

<p>It was nearly 3 a.m. when he finally heard the sound he’d been waiting for—the soft jingle of keys, the click of the lock.</p>

<p>She was home.</p>

<p>Colton rose to his feet slowly, his pulse pounding in his neck.</p>

<p>The anger that had simmered all night surged forward, demanding release.</p>

<p>But when the door swung open, what he saw stopped him cold.</p>

<p>Kristine stepped inside first, her eyes red-rimmed but dry.</p>

<p>She looked exhausted, her face unreadable. But it wasn’t her that held Colton’s gaze.</p>

<p>It was the man beside her.</p>

<p>Ross Oakley.</p>

<p>The same smug bastard Colton had hated for years.</p>

<p>The same arrogant, backstabbing prick he had suspected all along.</p>

<p>And now here he was—standing in Colton’s house, looking comfortable, even casual, as if he belonged.</p>

<p>“Hello, Colton,” Ross said with a wide grin, his voice smooth and dripping with fake politeness. “Long night?”</p>

<p>Colton’s hands curled into fists at his sides.</p>

<p>His jaw clenched so tightly it felt like his teeth might crack. But he didn’t look at Ross.</p>

<p>He looked at Kristine.</p>

<p>Straight into her eyes.</p>

<p>“Why is he with you?” Colton asked, voice low and bitter.</p>

<p>Kristine hesitated, then took a deep breath.</p>

<p>Her gaze didn’t waver, but there was a flicker of guilt—of shame—just behind her eyes.</p>

<p>“There’s something I need to tell you,” she said.</p>

<p>He waited. The tension stretched the air like a wire, ready to snap.</p>

<p>“I’m pregnant,” she continued. Her voice trembled slightly, but she pushed through. “And the baby… it’s Ross’s.”</p>

<p>The room fell into a deathly silence.</p>
