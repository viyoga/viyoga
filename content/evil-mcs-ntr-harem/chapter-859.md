<p>Relatives asked about flower themes, colors, the guest list, and even the seating arrangements for the press.</p>

<p>Lois sat quietly, nodding here and there, but her mind wasn’t on the decorations or the cake flavors.</p>

<p>She was waiting—watching for the right opportunity.</p>

<p>Finally, after what felt like hours, her chance arrived.</p>

<p>&quot;Why don’t I show Ross around our game room?&quot; Lois said, her tone casual though her pulse raced.</p>

<p>&quot;I remember hearing in one of your interviews that you’re into games. We’ve got a decent setup.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross leaned back in his chair, a thoughtful hum escaping his lips.</p>

<p>Then he flashed that signature smile, the kind that seemed to light up a room and disarm even the harshest critics.</p>

<p>&quot;Hmmmm... that would actually be a nice break for me. Thank you, Lois.&quot;</p>

<p>Laura, sitting beside him, giggled. &quot;Don’t let him beat you, sis. He’s competitive.&quot;</p>

<p>Ross chuckled, rising smoothly to his feet. &quot;Only when it’s worth it.&quot;</p>

<p>Lois stood too, forcing a smile as her stomach tightened.</p>

<p>She gestured toward the hallway, and together they walked away from the buzzing conversations in the living room.</p>

<p>The voices of their family faded into the background, leaving just the two of them moving side by side.</p>

<p>Ross walked easily, hands in his pockets, his presence calm but commanding.</p>

<p>&quot;You know,&quot; he said lightly, glancing at Lois, &quot;it’s rare I get a moment away from all the noise. I appreciate this.&quot;</p>

<p>Lois gave a small nod, her mind whirling.</p>

<p>She hadn’t invited him just to play games—she needed answers.</p>

<p>And maybe, just maybe, she would finally see through the mask Ross Oakley wore so perfectly.</p>

<p>Along the way, they talked like friends—Ross asking her about her favorite games, Lois teasing him about whether he was as good as he claimed in those interviews.</p>

<p>Outwardly, it was easy, lighthearted. But inside, Lois was bracing herself for the conversation she truly wanted to have.</p>

<p>Thud!</p>

<p>The game room doors shut behind them, muting the excited voices and laughter from the crowded living room.</p>

<p>The silence between them was thick, charged, and suffocating.</p>

<p>Lois took a sharp breath, her nails digging into her palm as she clenched her fists.</p>

<p>&quot;Why?&quot; she demanded, her voice low and trembling with restrained fury.</p>

<p>Ross tilted his head slightly, pretending not to understand.</p>

<p>&quot;Why what?&quot; he asked with a casual shrug, strolling to the pool table like he had all the time in the world. He</p>

<p>leaned against it lazily, his sharp eyes never leaving her.</p>

<p>&quot;If you’re asking about Laura, then I already explained. You weren’t available to me, Lois. So I took your sister instead. And since you’re twins...&quot; His grin widened, wolfish and cruel. &quot;It’s almost as if I’ve already had you, too.&quot;</p>

<p>Lois’s breath caught in her throat.</p>

<p>Heat rose to her face, anger and disbelief twisting inside her chest.</p>

<p>She wanted to scream at him, slap him, anything—but her body stayed rooted to the floor.</p>

<p>Ross chuckled at her silence, his voice dropping to a near whisper as he leaned closer.</p>

<p>&quot;But now that I’ve seen your mother...&quot; His words dripped like honey, poisoned and sweet at the same time.</p>

<p>&quot;I might just go after her once I’m done with Laura. And eventually—&quot; his gaze roamed slowly down her figure, deliberate and taunting, &quot;—I’ll probably have you as well.&quot;</p>

<p>For an instant, Lois’s mask slipped. Triumph flickered in her eyes, as if she had finally caught him revealing his true intentions.</p>

<p>She thought she had him cornered—exposed.</p>

<p>Ross noticed. He always noticed.</p>

<p>His smirk sharpened, the predator in him savoring her tiny victory before tearing it apart.</p>

<p>&quot;Ahhh,&quot; he said smoothly, straightening up and fixing his jacket as though he were about to step onto a stage.</p>

<p>&quot;So that’s what you were hoping for, wasn’t it? That I’d say something incriminating. That I’d hand you the weapon you need to destroy me.&quot;</p>

<p>Her lips pressed into a thin line, but her silence betrayed her.</p>

<p>Ross’s eyes narrowed knowingly.</p>

<p>He tapped the pocket of her blouse lightly, right where her phone rested.</p>

<p>The gesture sent shivers up her spine.</p>

<p>&quot;I know you’re recording this, Lois.&quot;</p>

<p>His voice grew louder, theatrical now, meant to be heard clearly by anyone listening through the device.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s just a joke, everyone. Don’t take me seriously. I love Laura more than I love life itself.&quot;</p>

<p>He laughed softly, a confident, disarming laugh that filled the room.</p>

<p>A laugh that sounded genuine enough to fool anyone who might later hear this recording.</p>

<p>Lois’s heart sank.</p>

<p>The little hope she had of exposing him was slipping through her fingers like sand.</p>

<p>Her chest tightened, her throat dry, but her eyes remained locked on him, refusing to look away.</p>

<p>Ross took a slow step forward, closing the distance between them. His voice lowered again, intimate, meant for her alone.</p>

<p>&quot;You see, Lois... I always play three steps ahead. Don’t think for a second you can outsmart me.&quot; He leaned in, his breath brushing against her ear.</p>

<p>&quot;And one day, when you least expect it... you’ll come to me willingly. Just like your sister.&quot;</p>

<p>Lois flinched, her breath shaky, but she didn’t back away. She hated him.</p>

<p>She hated how calm, how untouchable he seemed.</p>

<p>But what terrified her most wasn’t his arrogance—it was the small, unshakable part of her that feared he might be right.</p>

<p>Ross smiled again, his victory complete, and turned back toward the door as though nothing had happened at all.</p>

<p>Lois stood frozen, her recording now useless, her anger burning hotter than ever.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re impossible.&quot; Lois exhaled sharply, her thumb pressing the screen as she killed the recording. Her chest rose and fell, her pulse hammering in her ears.</p>

<p>Ross’s smile curved darker, sharper—predatory.</p>

<p>&quot;Is the recording off?&quot; he asked lightly, almost teasing. Then, as the room seemed to shrink around her, his voice dropped into a chilling whisper.</p>

<p>&quot;Good. Because I want to make this absolutely clear to you, Lois. I want you. And I will have you. Either you come to me willingly... or I’ll take your mother first, and save you for last. That’s the only choice you’ll get.&quot;</p>
