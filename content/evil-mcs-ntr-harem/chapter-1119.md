<p>Ross took another step.</p>

<p>And another.</p>

<p>Each footfall made the hunters retreat a half-step without realizing it.</p>

<p>Ross’s eyes gleamed with a disturbing calm—like someone who had already decided what everyone’s fate would be.</p>

<p>The hallway felt smaller suddenly, as if the walls were closing in around the intruders.</p>

<p>&quot;Come,&quot; Ross said softly. &quot;Let’s dance.&quot;</p>

<p>His voice held no excitement—only a quiet, chilling anticipation.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s been... far too long since I felt blood warm on my hands.&quot;</p>

<p>The leader’s heart skipped.</p>

<p>The telekinetic’s jaw clenched.</p>

<p>The big man instinctively raised his fists.</p>

<p>And Ross kept walking, unhurried, unstoppable, stepping toward the six men who realized—far too late—that they hadn’t cornered prey.</p>

<p>Prey didn’t smile like that.</p>

<p>Predators did.</p>

<p>&quot;Well, if you want to die that badly, then be my guest! I’ll help you get to hell myself!&quot;</p>

<p>The telekinetic’s voice cracked with rage as he thrust both hands forward, veins bulging on his temples.</p>

<p>Pffft!</p>

<p>The wooden arrow shot like a bullet, slicing through the air fast enough to whistle.</p>

<p>It struck Ross dead center on the forehead—so perfectly aimed that the other hunters didn’t even bother to blink.</p>

<p>They had seen that attack pierce sheet metal and shattered bone clean through.</p>

<p>But this time...</p>

<p>Thud.</p>

<p>The arrow simply stopped.</p>

<p>Like it had hit a slab of solid iron.</p>

<p>Ross didn’t flinch.</p>

<p>Didn’t blink.</p>

<p>Didn’t even sway.</p>

<p>The projectile clattered onto the wooden floor with a harmless tap.</p>

<p>The hallway went silent.</p>

<p>&quot;This... this is impossible...&quot; the telekinetic whispered.</p>

<p>His face twisted in disbelief as he stumbled back, eyes wide as saucers.</p>

<p>&quot;That’s my strongest shot... it should have blown your head open...!&quot;</p>

<p>Ross rubbed the spot on his forehead as casually as someone wiping away a raindrop.</p>

<p>&quot;No bleeding... no crack... not even a scratch,&quot; he murmured, amused. Then he looked up with a wide, toothy smile.</p>

<p>&quot;Well, it did tickle. I’ll give you that.&quot;</p>

<p>His smile grew darker.</p>

<p>&quot;Why don’t I give you a taste of your own medicine?&quot;</p>

<p>He flicked his fingers.</p>

<p>Snap.</p>

<p>Whoooosh!</p>

<p>The wooden floor beneath the telekinetic buckled violently.</p>

<p>A jagged stake erupted upward as if launched by an underground explosion.</p>

<p>The man barely had time to widen his eyes.</p>

<p>SCHLK!</p>

<p>The stake impaled him from below—ramming straight up to his ass first then through his pelvis, shredding his insides, piercing organs, spine, throat—then exploding out his mouth in a shower of blood and shattered teeth.</p>

<p>His scream died instantly, trapped behind the blood filling his lungs.</p>

<p>The stake didn’t stop.</p>

<p>It carried him upward, lifting him clean off the ground, suspending him like a butchered animal on display.</p>

<p>His legs twitched. His fingers curled. A wet gargle escaped what remained of his throat.</p>

<p>Blood poured down the stake, dripping rhythmically onto the floorboards.</p>

<p>Tap...</p>

<p>Tap...</p>

<p>Tap...</p>

<p>The hunters stared in horror.</p>

<p>The smell of iron filled the hallway.</p>

<p>The telekinetic’s lifeless body hung there, mouth stretched unnaturally wide around the stake, eyes frozen in a mix of disbelief and terror.</p>

<p>Ross tilted his head.</p>

<p>&quot;Well... that looks uncomfortable,&quot; he said lightly, as if critiquing a painting.</p>

<p>He stepped forward, brushing past the corpse like it was nothing more than furniture in his way.</p>

<p>The blood soaking into the wood didn’t faze him.</p>

<p>The shocked expressions of the remaining five hunters didn’t faze him.</p>

<p>He simply walked—slow, calm, deliberate.</p>

<p>&quot;Now then...&quot; Ross said softly, wiping a fleck of blood off his cheek.</p>

<p>&quot;Who wants to go next?&quot;</p>

<p>The remaining hunters finally realized:</p>

<p>They hadn’t cornered a man.</p>

<p>They had awakened a monster.</p>

<p>&quot;FUCK YOU!&quot;</p>

<p>The big man erupted like a volcano, spittle flying from his lips as rage twisted his features.</p>

<p>His massive frame tensed, muscles bulging like coiled steel cables beneath his skin.</p>

<p>He charged forward, shaking the entire hallway with his steps.</p>

<p>Every stomp sounded like a hammer smashing through the floor.</p>

<p>Every breath was a growl.</p>

<p>Every intention screamed murder.</p>

<p>He pulled his arm back, gathering every ounce of strength in his monstrous body.</p>

<p>His knuckles whitened. His veins throbbled. He wanted Ross’s head—wanted to crush it in a single devastating blow.</p>

<p>He swung.</p>

<p>B A N G !</p>

<p>His fist slammed into the side of Ross’s skull with a thunderous crack.</p>

<p>And Ross didn’t move.</p>

<p>Not an inch.</p>

<p>Not a wobble.</p>

<p>Not a twitch.</p>

<p>Instead—</p>

<p>The big man staggered back, his eyes widening in pure disbelief.</p>

<p>His entire arm vibrated violently, the shockwave of the impact traveling all the way up to his shoulder.</p>

<p>The pain hit a heartbeat later—white-hot, sharp, agonizing.</p>

<p>&quot;What... what the hell are you...?&quot; he gasped, clutching his wrist as the bones inside screamed.</p>

<p>His fist felt like it had struck solid molten iron—something impossibly hard, impossibly hot, something that rejected all force.</p>

<p>He had smashed through concrete with those fists.</p>

<p>He had folded car hoods like paper.</p>

<p>He had caved in skulls with a single swipe.</p>

<p>But this—</p>

<p>Ross’s head—</p>

<p>felt like punching a demon’s throne.</p>

<p>Ross slowly tilted his head back toward him, calm and eerily patient.</p>

<p>There was no anger in his eyes.</p>

<p>No excitement.</p>

<p>Just... curiosity.</p>

<p>&quot;My turn,&quot; Ross said softly, almost gently.</p>

<p>The big man’s instincts screamed. He tried to step back. Tried to raise his guard. Tried to do anything.</p>

<p>Too late.</p>

<p>Ross stepped forward, barely shifting his weight, and threw a single punch.</p>

<p>PUCHI!</p>

<p>The sound wasn’t just loud—</p>

<p>It was wet.</p>

<p>Heavy.</p>

<p>Final.</p>

<p>Ross’s fist connected with the man’s skull, and the result was grotesque.</p>

<p>The head didn’t simply split open—it detonated. Bone fragments shot out like shrapnel.</p>

<p>A geyser of blood blasted upward, splattering the ceiling, painting the walls, raining down onto the remaining hunters.</p>

<p>The man’s jaw flew one direction.</p>

<p>An eye another.</p>

<p>Brain matter slid down the hallway in thick ropes of red and white.</p>

<p>For a single surreal second, the giant’s headless body remained standing—legs shaking, arms trembling as though the body refused to accept death.</p>

<p>Then—</p>

<p>THUD!</p>

<p>The corpse collapsed like a felled tree, blood pumping from the ragged stump of his neck in thick spurts.</p>
