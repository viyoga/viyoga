<p>Jenny had never felt so wanted, so thoroughly claimed.</p>

<p>Like she was the only thing keeping him tethered to something human.</p>

<p>Not even her late husband has driven her like this crazy.</p>

<p>Without a doubt, Jenny would now do anything for Ross. Anything.</p>

<p>During the day, while Ross was out on runs—scavenging fuel, reinforcing the outer barricades, or putting down stragglers that got too close—she kept herself busy in the women’s dorm.</p>

<p>The place had become her second home again, just without the bunk she used to sleep in.</p>

<p>She helped wherever she could: sorting ammo crates, patching gear, boiling water for the communal showers, even helping her friends do the inventory the dwindling medical supplies.</p>

<p>The teasing never stopped.</p>

<p>She’d walk in mid-morning, still flushed and walking with that telltale hitch, and the room would erupt.</p>

<p>&quot;Morning, Screamer,&quot; Martinez called from her bunk, grinning like a shark. &quot;How’s the dick appointment schedule looking today? Fully booked?&quot;</p>

<p>Jenny rolled her eyes but didn’t blush anymore. She’d gotten used to it. &quot;Jealous much?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Extremely,&quot; Torres shot back, tossing her a clean rag for wiping down rifles. &quot;I heard you last night. Sounded like he was trying to rearrange your organs.&quot;</p>

<p>Priya snorted while she counted bandages. &quot;The whole east wing heard. Ventilation’s a bitch. You two ever gonna invest in a gag?&quot;</p>

<p>Chen, braiding her hair nearby, smirked. &quot;Nah, let her scream. It’s better than white noise. Reminds us we’re still alive.&quot;</p>

<p>Jenny just laughed now—open, easy, unbothered.</p>

<p>The first few days she’d wanted to sink through the floor every time someone mimicked her moans or asked for &quot;play-by-play updates.&quot;</p>

<p>But a week in? She owned it.</p>

<p>The apocalypse had stripped away shame faster than it stripped away the weak.</p>

<p>And the weak had gone first.</p>

<p>When the outbreak hit, civilization collapsed in weeks.</p>

<p>The soft ones—the ones who cried too long, who couldn’t pull a trigger, who froze when the dead came shambling—were culled quickest.</p>

<p>The survivors were the ones who adapted fast: learned to shoot, learned to run, learned to fuck like tomorrow might never come.</p>

<p>Because most days, it felt like it wouldn’t.</p>

<p>Sex wasn’t taboo anymore. It was fuel. A way to feel something other than terror.</p>

<p>Couples formed overnight, broke up at dawn, reformed with someone else by dusk.</p>

<p>Doors stayed unlocked.</p>

<p>Moans drifted through the halls at all hours like a fucked-up soundtrack to survival.</p>

<p>Nobody batted an eye. It was a miracle that Jenny held up this long without sex also and she was glad that she waited and found Ross.</p>

<p>Jenny had toughened up too. She learned a few things also.</p>

<p>She could field-strip an AR-15 in under a minute now, spot a shambler’s weak points from fifty yards, and—most importantly—take everything Ross gave her without flinching.</p>

<p>She craved it.</p>

<p>The ache between her legs had become a constant companion, a reminder she was still breathing.</p>

<p>Ross, meanwhile, was out there being the hero the base needed.</p>

<p>He led small teams beyond the wire almost every day: clearing blocked roads, raiding abandoned trucks for supplies, putting down hordes before they massed against the fence.</p>

<p>He came back dirty, blood-splattered, sometimes with fresh cuts or bruises, but always alive.</p>

<p>Always calm. Always looking for her the second he stepped through the gate.</p>

<p>At night, though, the hero mask dropped.</p>

<p>He’d shower off the day’s grime, then find her waiting—naked on his bunk or already on her knees.</p>

<p>He fucked her like she was the only woman left on earth. Slow and deep when he wanted to savor her.</p>

<p>Hard and punishing when the day had been particularly brutal.</p>

<p>He’d whisper things against her skin—&quot;You’re mine,&quot; &quot;Scream for me,&quot; &quot;Take it all&quot;—and she did.</p>

<p>Every time.</p>

<p>One night, after a long tiring day. Ross went at her hard.</p>

<p>He just stripped her slow, laid her out on the mattress, and ate her out until she was sobbing his name—then fucked her face-down, one hand clamped over her mouth so the whole base wouldn’t hear how broken and desperate she sounded.</p>

<p>Afterward, he held her tighter than usual. She felt his heartbeat against her back, steady but fast.</p>

<p>&quot;You okay?&quot; she whispered.</p>

<p>He kissed the nape of her neck. &quot;Never been better.&quot;</p>

<p>She smiled into the dark. &quot;Good. Because I’m not done with you yet.&quot;</p>

<p>He laughed—low, rough—and rolled her over for round two.</p>

<p>Outside, the world kept trying to end.</p>

<p>Inside, Jenny and Ross kept proving it hadn’t—not yet.</p>

<p>And every scream she let out was another small, defiant fuck-you to the dead things scratching at the gates.</p>

<p>She’d always known Ross wasn’t exclusive. Hell, nobody on the base was anymore.</p>

<p>The dead didn’t care about monogamy, and neither did the living.</p>
