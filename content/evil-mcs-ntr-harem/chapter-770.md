<p>&quot;I’m here,&quot; Lily said softly.</p>

<p>Her words cut through the charged silence like a spark in dry grass, and Ross turned to her with a smile—genuine, knowing, and just a little amused.</p>

<p>Lily had followed her gut. She didn’t know why, exactly. Maybe it was foolish.</p>

<p>Maybe it was her youth talking.</p>

<p>But something deep inside her told her this was the right choice.</p>

<p>If she walked away now, she knew she’d lie awake at night wondering what could have been.</p>

<p>That kind of regret—the quiet, lingering kind that eats away at you over time—was something she never wanted to carry.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just about Ross. It was about herself. Her agency. Her choice.</p>

<p>People might call her naïve. Maybe even reckless. But she didn’t care.</p>

<p>She would rather leap and fall than stand on the edge for the rest of her life wondering what if.</p>

<p>And if this turned out to be a mistake?</p>

<p>Then it would be her mistake to own, to learn from, and never apologize for.</p>

<p>Ross chuckled, breaking the tension. &quot;Looks like I need a bigger bed.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I guess you do,&quot; Lily replied with a small grin, her voice steadier now.</p>

<p>She walked across the room and sat next to Tianna, who gave her a subtle, understanding nod.</p>

<p>The energy in the room shifted.</p>

<p>Everyone was still. All eyes moved to Steve, who looked like he’d just been punched in the gut.</p>

<p>He couldn’t believe it. Lily? Of all people?</p>

<p>Just a few minutes ago, he had thought he had a chance—thought she might actually side with him.</p>

<p>But now, she had chosen Ross. Willingly. Boldly.</p>

<p>He had no idea how Ross did it, how he kept turning things in his favor like some kind of magnetic force.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just charm—it was deeper than that.</p>

<p>There was something about him that drew girls in, especially the ones untouched and unsure.</p>

<p>And the worst part?</p>

<p>Ross didn’t even look like he was trying.</p>

<p>&quot;Unbelievable,&quot; Steve muttered under his breath, his jaw tight, fists clenched at his sides.</p>

<p>Ross glanced at him briefly, his smile unfading, as if silently reminding him: This is the difference between us.</p>

<p>And Steve felt it. Sharp and cold. The sting of being outclassed in every way.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, Lily sat calmly, her heart pounding but her face composed.</p>

<p>She had made her decision. And whatever came next, she was ready to face it.</p>

<p>&quot;The floor is yours now, boy. Speak your piece,&quot; Ross said coolly, his voice dripping with amusement as his fingers lazily caressed Heaven’s thigh.</p>

<p>The gesture wasn’t just possessive—it was deliberate, a declaration. And it hit Steve like a hammer to the gut.</p>

<p>His jaw clenched, his fists tightened, and for a moment, rage flashed in his eyes.</p>

<p>But he swallowed it down, forced his breath to steady. Losing his temper wouldn’t get her back.</p>

<p>So, he ignored Ross. Pretended the man didn’t exist.</p>

<p>He turned to Heaven, his eyes filled with wounded betrayal, and stepped forward slowly.</p>

<p>&quot;I can’t believe you did this to me... to us,&quot; Steve said, his voice rough, heavy with emotion.</p>

<p>&quot;After everything we’ve been through. After all the years—every laugh, every fight, every damn memory.&quot;</p>

<p>He took another step toward her, his eyes never leaving hers.</p>

<p>&quot;We had something real, Heaven. Or at least I thought we did. And now, just like that, you’ve thrown it all away?&quot;</p>

<p>She didn’t respond.</p>

<p>Her face remained unreadable, but Steve pushed forward, desperation creeping into his voice.</p>

<p>&quot;We can still make this right. It’s not too late. Just walk out of this damned house with me. Forget all of this. I forgive you,&quot; he said, placing his hand over his chest. &quot;Even after everything, I still—&quot;</p>

<p>He paused, letting his voice crack just slightly, perfectly timed.</p>

<p>&quot;I still love you. More than you’ll ever know.&quot;</p>

<p>His eyes shimmered under the soft lighting, as if on the verge of tears.</p>

<p>His entire posture screamed vulnerability.</p>

<p>He looked like a broken man—someone who had just lost the love of his life and was begging for one last chance.</p>

<p>But it was all an act.</p>

<p>Beneath that soft voice and sorrowful gaze, Steve was seething. He didn’t feel heartbreak.</p>

<p>He felt rage. Humiliation. Heaven had embarrassed him—disrespected him.</p>

<p>And in front of Ross, no less. It wasn’t just betrayal; it was war.</p>

<p>The truth was simple.</p>

<p>If Heaven made the mistake of believing him—if she bought the lie and came back to him—he would make her pay. Slowly. Cruelly.</p>

<p>First, he’d fuck her until he’d taken everything from her: her pride, her light, her hope.</p>

<p>And once he was done—once she was broken and hollow—he’d pass her off to his boys.</p>

<p>One by one. Again and again. Until she begged for death, until she couldn’t even remember what love felt like.</p>

<p>That would be her punishment.</p>

<p>For being a slut.</p>

<p>For spreading her legs for the first man who batted his lashes at her.</p>

<p>For making him look like a fool.</p>

<p>But none of that showed on his face. On the surface, Steve was the picture of sincerity.</p>

<p>The kind of man who had just watched the love of his life slip through his fingers and was praying for a miracle.</p>

<p>&quot;Please,&quot; he whispered, voice low and trembling. &quot;Choose me.&quot;</p>

<p>The room fell silent. Even Ross, for once, said nothing.</p>

<p>All eyes were on Heaven, who now sat frozen between two paths—one lined with promises, the other with unspoken danger.</p>

<p>And somewhere in that silence, Ross’s smirk deepened just a little. Because he could see it too.</p>

<p>Steve’s mask was cracking.</p>

<p>And it was only a matter of time before everyone saw the monster underneath.</p>

<p>Heaven was quiet for a long time.</p>

<p>Her eyes lingered on Steve, searching his face for something—maybe sincerity, maybe the boy she used to know.</p>

<p>But whatever she was hoping to find, it wasn’t there. Not anymore. Too much had changed.</p>

<p>Maybe she had, too.</p>

<p>Finally, she drew in a breath and spoke, her voice soft but steady.</p>
