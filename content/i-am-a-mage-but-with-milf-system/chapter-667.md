<p>The chamber was completely silent except for the soft shift of the water and the faint hiss of the oil lamps.</p>

<p>Julian watched Helena’s face during those seconds and found it extraordinary. She was fighting with herself, reconsidering everything and ultimately, loosing in her own battle.</p>

<p>She was outnumbered now.</p>

<p>Finally, steeling herself, she reached out and placed her hands on her son’s.</p>

<p>Thomas’s fingers closed around hers—gently, carefully, the way you close your hand around something fragile. He pulled her forward without rushing, giving her the space and Helena came willingly. One step, then another, then a third, until she was standing between her children, her hair loose around her shoulders exactly as Kraven had requested.</p>

<p>She kept her eyes on Thomas’s face.</p>

<p>Didn’t look at the sofas. Didn’t look at Mara. Just her son’s face, as if it were the only fixed point available to her.</p>

<p>Thomas’s free hand rose slowly.</p>

<p>He touched her hair first—a careful, tentative touch. His fingers found a loose strand and he moved it back from her face, tucking it gently behind her ear. His fingertips trailed along her hairline, then down the curve of her jaw, the same path they had traveled along Sarah’s face minutes ago.</p>

<p>Helena’s eyes didn’t close. She kept watching his face.</p>

<p>“Mother,” Thomas said quietly.</p>

<p>The word fell into the room like a stone into still water.</p>

<p>Kraven made a low sound, barely audible. His hand found his wine glass without looking and lifted it, but he didn’t drink. Just held it.</p>

<p>Julian said nothing. He turned his glass slowly between his fingers and watched Helena’s face—the way the word landed on her, the slight tension in her jaw, the way she swallowed once before she steadied herself again.</p>

<p>Thomas leaned forward slowly and pressed his lips to her forehead.</p>

<p>It was a gentle thing. Almost innocent, except for everything surrounding it.</p>

<p>Helena’s hands came up.</p>

<p>Slowly. Almost reluctantly, as though her arms were moving against her conscious, they rose and came to rest on Thomas’s chest.</p>

<p>Thomas moved lower, his lips finding her cheek, then the corner of her jaw, then her neck. Helena’s chin lifted with each kiss, the way it does when someone has stopped resisting something and allowed their body to respond on its own terms.</p>

<p>Sarah, who had been standing slightly apart, moved closer. She closed the distance until she was right beside her mother, and then her hand found Helena’s back, caressing it gently.</p>

<p>Helena’s eyes were still open. She looked at the ceiling and for a moment she was simply lost.</p>

<p>Julian understood what she was doing. She was anchoring herself.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, Kraven had finally leaned back into his sofa at last, his earlier tension fading. It was the posture of a man who has confirmed that what he came for is genuinely happening and can now allow himself to simply enjoy it. His wine glass rested on the arm of the sofa, completely forgotten now.</p>

<p>He turned his head and looked at Julian.</p>

<p>“Well,” he said quietly, his voice low enough that it didn’t carry to the pool’s edge. “Your thoughts, Lord Julian?”</p>

<p>Julian considered the question genuinely before answering. His eyes drifted back to the pool, studying the three.</p>

<p>“I think,” Julian said quietly, “that there is nothing quite as fascinating as watching someone cross a threshold they believed that they never would.”</p>

<p>Kraven looked at him.</p>

<p>Julian kept his eyes on the pool for another moment before turning to meet Kraven’s gaze directly.</p>

<p>“The moment itself is never the most interesting part,” he continued. “What’s actually worth watching is the time before. The last moment of the person they were.” A slight pause. “Everything after that is just aftermath. But that instant—” He turned back toward Helena. “That instant contains everything.”</p>

<p>Kraven was quiet for a moment.</p>

<p>Then he made a sound that was as close to genuine as Julian had heard from him</p>

<p>“You’re more interesting than I gave you credit for,” he said finally.</p>

<p>Julian smiled and lifted his wine.</p>

<p>“I get that often,” he said.</p>

<p>At the pool’s edge, Thomas’s hands had found the ties at the back of Helena’s dress. He paused there as if unsure whether he should continue or not.</p>

<p>Helena had lowered her eyes from the ceiling. She was looking at Thomas now. At their joined hands and the space between their bodies that was closing breath by breath, heartbeat by heartbeat.</p>

<p>Helena held his gaze for a long moment.</p>

<p>Then she reached back and moved her hair over one shoulder, clearing it from the ties.</p>

<p>Giving him room.</p>

<p>Thomas began to loosen the ties.</p>

<p>Kraven’s hand tightened slightly on his wine glass.</p>

<p>Mara allowed herself the smallest possible smile.</p>

<p>Thomas worked from the top, each loosened fastening opening a little more of the fabric. He wasn’t rushing. Julian had expected him to rush but Thomas wasn’t rushing at all.</p>

<p>He was taking his time.</p>

<p>Helena stood very still. Her hair was over her shoulder and the nape of her neck was exposed—pale and smooth in the amber lamplight. She had her eyes forward now, moving away from Thomas.</p>

<p>The third tie came loose.</p>

<p>The fabric shifted just a bit and Thomas’s hands stilled for a moment.</p>

<p>Julian watched his face.</p>

<p>The boy was looking at his mother’s back. At the parting fabric and the skin it was revealing—the line of her spine continuing downward, the slight curve where her back swept into her waist. He was very still, and his expression had changed from guilt to pure desire.</p>

<p>He was looking at her the way a man looks at a beautiful woman.</p>

<p>Thomas exhaled slowly through his nose, then continued with the ties.</p>

<p>The fourth came apart. The fifth. The dress was barely held now, the fabric gathered loosely around Helena’s figure.</p>

<p>One movement and it would fall.</p>

<p>But he didn’t find the courage to take that step. Instead, his hands moved to her shoulders, resting there lightly. Leaning forward, his lips met her neck and her breath broke immediately.</p>

<p>It was a small sound but in the silence of the chamber it was perfectly clear.</p>
