<p>She narrowed her eyes, measuring him carefully. “Simple, you say? Do you think the weight of such a command is simple? To ask a mother to take her son into her womb?”</p>

<p>Kain looked down at her, his smirk deepening. “We already live in chains, mother. Bound to a curse that mocks us every day we breathe. You’ve carried it longer than anyone—you should know. And now, you tell me there is a way to end it. A way to bring hope.”</p>

<p>His voice dropped lower, conviction hardening each word. “And you expect me to step back?”</p>

<p>He halted for a moment, staring down at her transformed face, at her glowing belly.</p>

<p>“I won’t,” he said firmly.</p>

<p>Her brows lifted ever so slightly in amusement, though she gave no more emotion than that.</p>

<p>“So quick to accept. Are you truly so devoted to the village, or is there something else lurking in you, Kain?”</p>

<p>The words slithered between them, and for a moment his composure faltered. But then, his smirk returned.</p>

<p>“I am devoted,” he answered sharply. But then his eyes softened “…and I am not blind. Annie has beauty. Strength. You think I haven’t noticed?”</p>

<p>That earned the faintest curl of a smile from her lips. “So you admit it.”</p>

<p>His jaw clenched, and he exhaled sharply through his nose. “Desire or duty—does it matter? If she must lie with her son, then so be it. Rael will do his part. And Annie…” He hesitated “…Annie will not be able to resist forever.”</p>

<p>Rael’s grandmother studied him in silence for a long while, her hand gliding lazily across the swell of her stomach. Finally, she spoke:</p>

<p>“Be careful, Kain. A man who convinces himself too quickly often hides fear in his heart.”</p>

<p>Kain’s eyes met hers, hard and unwavering. “Fear?” He leaned in closer, his voice dropping to a low growl.</p>

<p>“No, mother. Not fear. Only certainty. You asked me to believe this was the Patriarch’s will. I do. And if that is true…” His lips twisted into a cold smile. “…then I will make it happen.”</p>

<p>He straightened again, his presence almost suffocating in the room now.</p>

<p>“Annie is ours to guide. Rael is young, uncertain, but he will follow if we give him no other path. And if the prophecy demands their union…” He spread his arms, as though embracing the weight of it. “…then I’ll see it through.”</p>

<p>Her eyes gleamed at his words. “Good. That’s what I wanted to hear. The Patriarch was right to show himself in my dream. He knew you would not falter.”</p>

<p>But as she spoke, she could not entirely hide the faint smile that tugged at the corner of her mouth—the smile of a woman who had just seen the last piece of her scheme click into place.</p>

<p>Kain noticed it. And though he smirked in return, the question lingered unspoken in the depths of his mind: was this really for the prophecy’s sake?</p>

<p>Turning away, he walked toward the door and did not even look back, not once. The door creaked open, then closed behind him, leaving the room with silence once again.</p>
