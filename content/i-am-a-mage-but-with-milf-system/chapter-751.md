<p>The invitation was no longer veiled in anything.</p>

<p>Julian remained still. His gaze stayed where she had directed it, fixed on the soft pale curves rising and falling with each breath she drew. There was something almost hypnotic about the rhythm of it… the way each slow inhale deepened the view just slightly before the exhale let it settle again.</p>

<p>They seemed somehow more tantalizing with each passing moment, as though the longer he looked the more the room shrank around that single point of focus.</p>

<p>Louisa did not move. She simply held the position and let him look with the patience of someone who understood that stillness could be its own kind of pressure.</p>

<p>And Julian looked.</p>

<p>For nearly two full minutes, his gaze remained there without apology, without hurry, without any indication that he felt the need to be anywhere else or do anything else. The clock on the far wall counted every second of it.</p>

<p>Neither of them spoke.</p>

<p>Finally, Louisa sat upright again, drawing herself back to a composed posture and allowing the neckline of her corset to settle into something slightly more contained.</p>

<p>“Since you must be motivated now,” she said, “I hope you can answer my question, dear.”</p>

<p>Julian let a beat pass, then he finally raised his eyes to meet hers. A faint, dangerous smile played at the corner of his lips.</p>

<p>“Well,” he said, drawing the word out slowly. “Where should I start?”</p>

<p>He leaned back slightly in his chair, giving the question what appeared to be genuine consideration, as though he were deciding carefully how much of the truth to allow to the surface.</p>

<p>“Ezakael,” he began at last, “you can consider that city to be very lucky for me.”</p>

<p>Louisa raised an elegant brow but said nothing, letting him continue.</p>

<p>“I was lucky enough to meet a certain someone there,” Julian continued, the lie moving through him as smoothly as water. “Someone who saw something in me worth their time. They imparted knowledge onto me. Knowledge that changed everything.”</p>

<p>The effect on Louisa was immediate and visible.</p>

<p>Her eyes widened, and for the first time since they had entered this room together, her composure broke in a way she could not fully reclaim before he had already seen it. A flash of genuine shock moved across her face before she caught it and pulled it back beneath her controlled surface.</p>

<p>But he had seen it.</p>

<p>The implication had landed exactly where he intended. Someone powerful enough to take a mediocre mage and reshape him into an Arch Mage within the span of a few months. That kind of mentorship did not simply exist at the edges of possibility. It existed somewhere closer to myth.</p>

<p>She leaned forward, her voice fractionally tighter than it had been.</p>

<p>“You mean someone made you their disciple?”</p>

<p>Julian gave a small, unhurried smile, neither confirming nor denying it. He reached for his wine and took a slow sip, letting the silence stretch just long enough to watch her lean imperceptibly closer before he set the glass back down.</p>

<p>“Something like that,” he said. “They taught me things that no one in this kingdom even knows exist. Techniques that rewrite how mana moves through the body. Ways to break through bottlenecks that would take ordinary mages decades to overcome on their own.”</p>

<p>Louisa’s fingers tightened around the stem of her wine glass. She was working to keep her voice even and not entirely succeeding.</p>

<p>“Who was this person?” she asked. “A hidden master? Some ancient cultivator living entirely outside any known circle?”</p>

<p>Julian shrugged, keeping his expression relaxed.</p>

<p>“I don’t know their full identity. They preferred it that way. But their power existed on a completely different level from anything I had encountered before. What I showed in the duel with Commander Aldric was only a fraction of what I learned from them.”</p>

<p>He paused.</p>

<p>“And I am still learning. Every single day.”</p>

<p>Louisa was quiet for a long moment. She lifted her glass and took another sip, but her hand was no longer entirely steady. The revelation had shaken her more than she wanted to show and she was aware that it showed regardless.</p>

<p>She set the glass down and looked at him directly.</p>

<p>“And this master. Do they have any connection to the royal family? Or to the King’s court in any capacity?”</p>

<p>Julian shook his head once.</p>

<p>“Not that I know of. They seemed to operate entirely outside the normal structures of power. Outside every structure, really. That is partly why they chose someone like me. Someone who had been cast aside and overlooked. They said I had clean potential. Their words.”</p>

<p>Louisa’s eyes narrowed slightly. She leaned back in her chair and recrossed her legs with a slow and deliberate movement, the slit in her skirt parting further to show the glimpse of her thighs.</p>

<p>“You have become quite the enigma, Kraven,” she said softly, studying him with an expression that carried equal measures of admiration and wariness and something else beneath both that she had not yet named.</p>

<p>“Powerful. Mysterious. And now backed by some unknown master who exists entirely outside any structure we can map or anticipate. My husband will be very interested to hear about this.”</p>

<p>Julian smiled faintly, holding her gaze without effort.</p>

<p>“I am sure he will.”</p>

<p>The tension in the room thickened around them again. Louisa studied him with her full attention now, the seductive performance she had arrived with still present but no longer the dominant thing in her eyes.</p>

<p>Her mind was working at full capacity behind that composed and lovely face.</p>

<p>She had come here to draw out information through seduction, but the information she was receiving had turned out to be something far more significant and far more unsettling than she had allowed herself to anticipate.</p>

<p>She decided to push regardless.</p>

<p>“And this master of yours,” she began, the warmth returning to her voice the way a tide comes back in without announcement, “did they teach you anything about controlling desire?”</p>

<p>Her gaze moved briefly to his lips, just a fraction of a second, before returning to his eyes.</p>

<p>“Or do you still find yourself at the mercy of certain urges?”</p>

<p>Julian looked at her for a long moment before he answered.</p>

<p>“There are some desires,” he said, his voice quiet and unhurried and entirely certain of itself, “that were never meant to be governed.”</p>

<p>The clock on the wall kept counting.</p>

<p>Neither of them moved.</p>
