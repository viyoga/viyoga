<p>West had adjusted his grip casually. "Yeah."</p>
<p>There was no strain on his face or tremors in his arms.</p>
<p>Mark chalked it up to adrenaline. Or youth. Or maybe the stress of the last few days doing strange things to perception.</p>
<p>But then it happened again.</p>
<p>And again.</p>
<p>A dining table box that clearly required two people—West took one end and still seemed to be doing most of the work. A refrigerator component that Mark struggled to even tilt—West carried it without complaint.</p>
<p>"You’ve... been working out?" Mark asked at one point, half joking, half confused.</p>
<p>West smiled. "A bit."</p>
<p>Mark laughed weakly and shook his head. "Kids these days."</p>
<p>They continued.</p>
<p>By the time they reached the stairwell again with their largest load yet, Mark’s legs were burning. Sweat dripped from his temple as he adjusted his grip around a massive box filled with shelving parts.</p>
<p>Halfway up the flight, his foot slipped by just a fraction where the sole of his shoe failed to find traction.</p>
<p>The box tipped backwards and momentum pulled it away from his grasp.</p>
<p>Mark’s heart skipped violently as he instinctively reached out—</p>
<p>"West—!"</p>
<p>It was too late... the box fell straight toward West.</p>
<p>West was behind him, already holding another massive box against his chest.</p>
<p>Mark’s mind screamed.</p>
<p>’It’s too heavy... he’ll get crushed.’</p>
<p>But what happened next made time feel like it warped.</p>
<p>West suddenly moved the box he was carrying to one hand and reached out with the other.</p>
<p>The falling box landed squarely on his open palm and it was stopped instantly.</p>
<p>West stood there with his arms slightly bent, holding two enormous boxes like it was nothing more than an inconvenience.</p>
<p>The stairwell fell silent.</p>
<p>Mark stared.</p>
<p>His mouth opened, then closed.</p>
<p>"How—" he began.</p>
<p>West smiled, almost sheepishly. "Guess I caught it just in time."</p>
<p>Mark’s breath came out in a rush. "That box alone must weigh—"</p>
<p>"I’ve really been working out," West said again, widening his smile just a bit.</p>
<p>Mark laughed but it was strained and uneasy.</p>
<p>Because something about that moment didn’t sit right in his chest.</p>
<p>Still, he didn’t press.</p>
<p>They finished the job soon after.</p>
<p>Boxes stacked neatly. Furniture assembled. Appliances plugged in. The apartment slowly transformed from an empty, echoing space into something that actually looked like a home.</p>
<p>By the time the last piece was set in place, Mark sank down onto a chair with his elbows on his knees, breathing hard.</p>
<p>West stood nearby, barely winded.</p>
<p>Mark looked up at him and realized that his son wasn’t a kid anymore.</p>
<p>Somewhere between long work hours, missed dinners, and years slipping by unnoticed, West had changed. His shoulders were broader. His posture steadier. His eyes... sharper.</p>
<p>Mark swallowed.</p>
<p>"West," he said.</p>
<p>West paused mid-step. "Yeah?"</p>
<p>Mark hesitated.</p>
<p>Then he stood, walked over, and rested a hand on the counter... steadying himself more than anything.</p>
<p>"I know I haven’t been... present."</p>
<p>West didn’t respond.</p>
<p>"I know I say that a lot," Mark continued with a low tone. "And I know it doesn’t mean much when it comes this late."</p>
<p>Still silence~</p>
<p>"I can’t keep using work as an excuse. Work is never an excuse to not be there for your son."</p>
<p>West turned slowly.</p>
<p>Mark’s face drooped. "When your mother and I separated... I should’ve been there more. For you. Not just financially—emotionally."</p>
<p>His voice wavered.</p>
<p>"I know that took a toll on you. It took one on me too. And instead of dealing with it, I buried myself in work like a coward."</p>
<p>West’s gaze softened but only slightly.</p>
<p>"I’m sorry," Mark said. "I’m sorry you grew up feeling like you were on your own. I’m sorry I wasn’t the father you needed."</p>
<p>Silence stretched as West stared at the wall behind him.</p>
<p>He was eighteen now...</p>
<p>His parents had been separated for nearly five years.</p>
<p>For a long time, he had waited.</p>
<p>For calls that didn’t come.</p>
<p>For weekends that were always postponed.</p>
<p>For promises that quietly dissolved into excuses.</p>
<p>Eventually, he stopped expecting anything.</p>
<p>But he wasn’t blind.</p>
<p>He understood, in his own way, that the separation had broken his father. That Mark’s silence wasn’t indifference... it was grief poorly handled. That burying himself in work was how Mark survived.</p>
<p>By the time Mark realized how much time had passed and that he needed to be there for his son, he was already trapped.</p>
<p>Promotions stacked. Responsibilities multiplied. He got a position so demanding that stepping back even slightly risked collapse.</p>
<p>And if he lost that job... who would provide for his son? The mother that was never there?</p>
<p>That was the irony.</p>
<p>West had gotten a job at Ash and Crème not just for himself, but to show his father that he didn’t have to carry everything alone.</p>
<p>That West could stand on his own.</p>
<p>But even then, Mark barely reacted.</p>
<p>Now—</p>
<p>Now that West had nearly died—</p>
<p>Everything changed.</p>
<p>"If you’d died..." Mark eyes glistened as he voiced out. "What would any of it have been for?"</p>
<p>West opened his mouth slightly but shut it back, choosing not to say anything.</p>
<p>"I put in a letter at work," Mark continued. "I demanded a couple days off every week. And a vacation week every three months."</p>
<p>West’s eyes suddenly widened. "What?"</p>
<p>"If they refuse," Mark said firmly, "I’ll resign."</p>
<p>West blinked. "Dad—"</p>
<p>"I need to be there for you," Mark said. "Properly. From now on."</p>
<p>West’s expression soured instantly.</p>
<p>"Um," he said awkwardly while rubbing the back of his neck, "there’s really no need for that."</p>
<p>Mark frowned. "What?"</p>
<p>"I mean—" West searched for words. "I’m fine. Really. You don’t need to rearrange your whole life."</p>
<p>Mark shook his head. "I do."</p>
<p>West internally screamed.</p>
<p>You absolutely do not.</p>
<p>Because while Mark was talking about bonding, shared meals, and presence—</p>
<p>West was thinking about something else entirely.</p>
<p>About how his life was about to get very complicated.</p>
<p>About secrets.</p>
<p>About awakenings.</p>
<p>About power.</p>
<p>About girls.</p>
<p>And most importantly—</p>
<p>About how much harder it will get to have girls around if his dad was suddenly around all the time.</p>
<p>West forced a smile. "We’ll... talk about it."</p>
<p>Mark smiled back, relieved.</p>
<p>West sighed inwardly.</p>
<p>’This is going to be a problem.’</p>
