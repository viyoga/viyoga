<p>While I was at the wake, my grandfather, Taira Danjo-nim, called me to his study. What's up? Does he need something from me again?</p>
<p>,This is the only inheritance your father, Saburo-nim, left behind.</p>

<p>,It's 1 percent of the shares of both Taira Bio and Taira Robotics. Our Taira Group's governance structure is complicated, but Taira Bio and Taira Robotics control most of the affiliates. This means you now own 1 percent of each of these two companies.</p>
<p>Wow... How much would this be worth? It's 1 percent, but if I sold it, couldn't I live off it for the rest of my life?</p>
<p>,I said earlier that Saburo-nim didn't leave anything, right? This stock is as good as nothing too. If you want to survive, you have to keep holding onto this stock. You can't cash it out, and you can't sell it to one of your uncles. Think of it as your lifeline and hold onto it tightly. Keep that in mind.</p>

<p>If Grandpa Danjo-nim is right, does that mean I'm safe as long as I have this stock? Well, if I sell it to one person, the other one won't leave me alone. Even if I sell half to each, they'll complain about why I didn't sell all the stock to them. So, I guess I can only hold onto it as Grandpa Danjo-nim said.</p>

<p>Of course, we, who were related by blood, followed him to the crematorium. In Korea, many people cried when the body went into the fire, but here, perhaps because it was a cold-blooded chaebol family or because they were Japanese, there were hardly any people crying.</p>
