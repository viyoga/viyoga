<p>Outside the seal, the world had not been idle.</p>
<p>The staging plain where Kaiden’s dungeon gate once stood had become a forward operating base the size of a small city.</p>
<p>Military-grade mana artillery lined the northern ridge in rows deep enough to blot out the treeline behind them, their barrels aimed at the empty coordinate where the gate had vanished, each one primed and cycling through idle charge rotations that made the air above them shimmer like highway asphalt in August.</p>
<p>Armored transports idled in columns along the access roads.</p>
<p>Helicopter gunships held pattern overhead in rotating shifts, their downdraft pressing the grass flat in overlapping circles that hadn’t been allowed to spring back up in hours.</p>
<p>And beyond the conventional hardware, at a far perimeter distance that suggested someone had done the math on acceptable blast radii, three nuclear warheads sat on mobile launch platforms with their targeting systems locked to the same empty coordinate, each one attended by a crew who had been told exactly one thing about their assignment: if the order is given, fire.</p>
<p>Awakened fighters filled the gaps between the machines.</p>
<p>Hundreds of thousands of them, organized by guild and region, their signatures stacking so dense across the plain that the mana-sensitive equipment along the eastern ridge had been recalibrated twice to stop throwing false alarms.</p>
<p>S-tiers from the Pacific deployment who had been mid-rotation when the recall order hit stood shoulder to shoulder with A-tier strike teams pulled off active dungeon assignments across three continents.</p>
<p>The European Guild Coalition had sent a forward detachment. The Korean Vanguard had sent two. Independent contractors with kill records longer than most guild rosters milled between the formations, because when the president calls in favors, people show up.</p>
<p>The whole thing looked like humanity was preparing to fight God.</p>
<p>Which, depending on what walked out of that seal, was not far off.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The command tent sat two hundred meters back from the perimeter, reinforced with layered barrier plates and staffed by enough aides and analysts to run a small war, which was convenient, because that was exactly what they were doing.</p>
<p>Grace stood at the central holo-table with her interface unfolded across both hands, three comm channels open, a logistics overlay blinking amber on half its nodes, and dark circles under her eyes that had graduated from "concerning" to "structural."</p>
<p>She had not sat down in hours. The coffee at her elbow had gone cold twice and been replaced five times, and she was currently arguing with a quartermaster about ammunition reserves while simultaneously approving a rotation schedule for the southern perimeter’s S-tier coverage.</p>
<p>She was also, against every professional instinct she possessed, monitoring Kaiden’s stream on a minimized pane in the corner of her interface.</p>
<p>The Chairman had ordered her to. Months ago. It remained the single most surreal standing order of her career.</p>
<p>At the head of the table, the Chairman stood with his hands clasped behind his back, coat streaked with the same ash from earlier, watching the tactical overlay with the unhurried patience he applied to every crisis, which was all of them.</p>
<p>Lazarus Crane had his boots on the table.</p>
<p>The Guildmaster of Crimson Dominion had arrived ninety minutes ago with thirty of his best fighters and his secretary, also known as his daughter.</p>
<p>He walked into the command tent like he owned the mineral rights to the dirt beneath it, commandeered a folding chair, put his feet up, and pulled out a bag of dried meat strips that he was currently eating from with the enthusiasm of a man at a sporting event.</p>
<p>He was watching Kira’s intermission stream on a personal display propped against an ammunition report he had not bothered to read.</p>
<p>"BAHAHA! Rewind that! Did you see the crescent? The kid threw it sidearm at a dead sprint and caught the runner two seconds before it reached the line!" He slapped his knee hard enough to rattle the chair. "That’s instinct, not training. You can’t teach that!"</p>
<p>Every aide in the tent had stopped reacting to him ten minutes ago. The Chairman had not reacted to begin with.</p>
<p>Viera Crane stood behind her father’s chair with her hands clasped at the small of her back, mirrored shades folded into her breast pocket, hip-length black hair immaculate, suit pressed to within an inch of its life, and the expression of someone performing a live autopsy on her own dignity.</p>
<p>"I apologize," she said to Grace, quietly, for the tenth time.</p>
<p>Grace, to her credit, had stopped acknowledging the loud barks and just smiled softly at the poor girl.</p>
<p>Viera leaned down to her father’s ear. "You are eating jerky in front of the Chairman of the Awakened Association, two S-tier guild leaders, and a table full of military analysts coordinating a potential extinction-level response."</p>
<p>"It’s amazing jerky," Lazarus said, mouth full. "I can’t put it down."</p>
<p>"Is that so?" Viera looked like she was ready to kill, and not just anyone. "If you do not put your feet down and close your foul mouth in the next three seconds, I am calling Mother."</p>
<p>"What! Traitor of my blood! Betrayed by my own creation!" He screamed, but the boots came off the table so fast the chair nearly tipped.</p>
<p>The jerky bag vanished into his coat.</p>
<p>The stream display folded shut. Lazarus Crane, Guildmaster of one of the three most powerful guilds in America, two now, considering New Dawn’s current state, sat upright with his hands in his lap and his red eyes forward, looking for all the world like a schoolboy who’d just been caught carving obscenities into his desk.</p>
<p>"Anything but that harpy getting on my di-" He caught himself. "...ass again."</p>
<p>Viera’s eyes could have frozen lava. "That’s not much better."</p>
<p>"It’s-" he wasn’t permitted to mount a defense case.</p>
<p>"Stop talking," Viera instructed, and Lazarus stopped talking.</p>
<p>Viera straightened up, adjusted her cuffs, and then paused.</p>
<p>"Lazarus Crane."</p>
<p>"Call me Father. No, call me Papa."</p>
<p>"Lazarus Crane. How much did you donate."</p>
<p>Lazarus Crane started whistling.</p>
<p>"How much," Viera repeated, hissing through teeth that could have ground diamonds, "did you donate to that stream."</p>
<p>"Nothing significant." The whistling increased. "Just some small amounts. Here and there. As a show of goodwill."</p>
<p>Viera’s fists trembled at her sides.</p>
<p>She knew this man.</p>
<p>She had managed this man since childhood.</p>
<p>"Small amounts" and "here and there" meant he had seen something exciting on the stream, felt an emotion that wasn’t boredom for once, and converted that emotion into guild funds leaving the treasury at a speed that would make their accountant weep.</p>
<p>Grace watched the daughter’s trembling fists from across the table and found herself genuinely curious how many more of these reveals the girl could absorb before something gave.</p>
<p>She was holding up remarkably well, all things considered.</p>
<p>Grace made a mental note to recommend her a therapist friend of hers from the good old college days.</p>
<p>’Not that old...’ she caught herself.</p>
<p>They were a long time ago.</p>
<p>Raziel sat opposite Lazarus, upright, composed, studying the tactical display with a focus that suggested he was building a mental model he intended to be flawless.</p>
<p>The co-leader of the Radiant Order had brought a forty-person strike team and Evangeline, the latter of whom was contributing to the preparations by sitting at the far end of the table with her chin propped on one hand, gazing at nothing in particular while her right hand rested beneath the table’s edge and her shoulder shifted in quick, rhythmic motions that nobody questioned because the alternative was engaging her in conversation.</p>
