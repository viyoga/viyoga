<p>Terms of dissolution of marriage between Vespera Ashborn and Magnus Ashborn.</p>
<p>Magnus read the line.</p>
<p>He read it again.</p>
<p>He turned to the second page. Asset division. Co-ownership restructuring. Guild equity clauses. Custody terms for Alice, with Vespera requesting sole guardianship pending review by the Awakened Family Court.</p>
<p>He turned to the third page. Signatures. Vespera’s was already there, precise and unhurried, the same penmanship she used on guild contracts and kill confirmations. Beneath it, a blank line waited for his.</p>
<p>He went back to the first page and read the opening line again.</p>
<p>Terms of dissolution of marriage.</p>
<p>Forty minutes ago. She had sent this forty minutes ago. Before he’d called Eleanora. Before he’d called Vespera. She had watched the stream, seen the confrontation between Magnus and Kaiden play out live, and known with absolute certainty how the rest of the evening would unfold. The papers were already prepared long before...</p>
<p>Every word she’d spoken. Every cold syllable. Every silence that lasted a beat longer than necessary. The "because I felt like it." The "I already know your opinion." The smile.</p>
<p>That gods damned smile.</p>
<p>She hadn’t been arguing with him.</p>
<p>She’d been saying goodbye.</p>
<p>Magnus set the papers down on the desk, centering them precisely between the crystal fragments. His hands were steady. His breathing was even. His expression, had anyone been present to observe it, would have appeared almost serene.</p>
<p>He sat like that for two full minutes.</p>
<p>Then the operational part of his brain engaged, because Magnus Ashborn did not have the luxury of falling apart, and frankly, did not know how.</p>
<p>Dissolution of marriage. Filed, not finalized. These were terms, not a verdict. Vespera had opened a negotiation, and negotiations required two parties. The document would need to be submitted to the Awakened Family Court, reviewed, contested if necessary, and adjudicated through a process that would take months.</p>
<p>Longer, given the complexity of co-owned guild assets, shared family holdings, and the custody of a minor S-tier combatant who was currently registered under a rival guild’s competition roster.</p>
<p>The moment this went public, it would become the most scrutinized divorce in the country’s history. Every rival guild leader, every political opponent, every journalist who had ever wanted a piece of the Ashborn empire would descend like vultures. New Dawn’s stock would plummet. Recruitment would stall. Sponsorship deals would enter review. The perception of stability that Magnus had spent decades constructing would crack overnight.</p>
<p>Vespera knew all of this.</p>
<p>She had filed anyway.</p>
<p>Which meant she had already calculated the cost and decided it was worth paying.</p>
<p>Magnus stared at the papers.</p>
<p>His wife, the coldest woman he had ever known, the shadow-wielder who had once told him that sentiment was a vulnerability she could not afford, had gone soft.</p>
<p>Magnus had never loved Vespera. Their marriage was an arrangement between two powerful families merging their holdings, their political influence, and their bloodlines into a conglomerate that rivaled nations in net worth.</p>
<p>Vespera had not loved him either, and neither of them had ever pretended otherwise. What they had was better than love. It was alignment. Shared ambition. A partnership built on mutual benefit and the understanding that emotion was a liability neither could afford.</p>
<p>She was the perfect woman for the life he’d built. Cold where he needed cold. Ruthless where he needed ruthless. A mother who raised S-tier children into efficient murderers and a co-owner who never once questioned his decisions because she’d always arrived at the same conclusions independently.</p>
<p>That was the woman he’d married.</p>
<p>And it was precisely why this divorce would be catastrophic. Magnus had not married into the Ashborn name as some lovesick nobody grateful for a seat at the table. He’d brought his own empire to the merger. His own wealth. His own political network. Dissolving this marriage meant untangling two decades of co-mingled assets, shared guild equity, joint military contracts, and family trust structures so complex that the legal fees alone would fund a small guild for a decade.</p>
<p>Vespera knew all of that. She’d calculated the cost, just as she calculated everything, and she’d decided that leaving him was worth the destruction.</p>
<p>Because she’d watched their disgraced son’s streams and let guilt rewrite decades of shared conviction. She’d let a boy who chose to become a pornstar and a teenage girl who defied her own guild convince her that the problem was not the children but the parents who had tried to maintain standards.</p>
<p>Kaiden had done this.</p>
<p>Alice had done this.</p>
<p>Those two had infected Vespera with whatever sentimental disease had turned her from the woman he’d married into someone who smiled on communication artifacts and talked about love and delivered divorce papers like party invitations.</p>
<p>His children hadn’t just chosen each other over him.</p>
<p>They had taken his wife.</p>
<p>They were parasites ruining his life.</p>
<p>Magnus’s hand closed over the papers. He folded them, precisely, along the original creases, and placed them in the drawer where the second artifact had been. He closed the drawer.</p>
<p>Then he activated his field operations artifact and connected to two specific signatures.</p>
<p>Mariana picked up instantly.</p>
<p>Chinedu followed a second later.</p>
<p>"New orders," Magnus said. His voice was calm. Perfectly, terribly calm.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The tent flap opened to cold mountain air and a sky that couldn’t decide between grey and purple.</p>
<p>Kaiden stepped out first. The resting period had helped. Two hours of rest with a Pharaoh on his back and five women within arm’s reach, and the bone-deep exhaustion had receded to something manageable. Not gone, just quiet enough to ignore.</p>
<p>The girls filed out behind him. Calypso rolled her shoulders and cracked her neck with the enthusiasm of a woman who treated violence as cardio. Luna was already bouncing on the balls of her feet, Storm flickering in her calves. Aria merely smiled serenely. Nyx simply stood there, spatial awareness already extending outward, reading the terrain the way most people read a room. Bastet emerged last, at her own pace, because the Pharaoh did not rush for mountains.</p>
<p>Alice was already sitting above his head in her Conduit form.</p>
