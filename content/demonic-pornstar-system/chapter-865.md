<p>In a chamber no blueprint had ever recorded, beneath foundations far older than the building they supported, a pair of eyes opened in the dark.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Viktor Thorne, leader of the underground organization known as Blood Pact, hadn’t moved in forty minutes.</p>
<p>He sat where he always sat, one leg thrown over the armrest of his leather chair, his eyes fixed on a point in the air that Olga couldn’t see.</p>
<p>His awakened interface hung in front of him, playing a silent loop of the same stream for the dozenth time. The boy’s gauntlet buried past the elbow in a wound the size of a house, the counter beside his level climbing faster than the eye could follow.</p>
<p>Viktor replayed it. Then again.</p>
<p>"Olga. The teams I dispatched."</p>
<p>His assistant stood at the edge of the room with a leather-bound dossier the width of a brick tucked under one arm, her posture carrying the permanent air of quiet sufferance that defined her existence. "Still unable to establish proximity to the target, sir."</p>
<p>"Still." The word came out flat.</p>
<p>"Vespera Ashborn has made proximity inadvisable." Olga adjusted her glasses. "Since the awakened competition in the hills began, she is no longer protecting him from the shadows. Our operatives cannot approach without being identified, and being identified within reach of that woman is a death sentence."</p>
<p>Viktor said nothing for a moment. On his interface, the Kaiju rolled onto its back and paddled the air, and a chat window beside the feed scrolled so fast the text became a smear of dog emojis.</p>
<p>"My, my. He named it Pebble."</p>
<p>"Sir."</p>
<p>"What?"</p>
<p>"You asked me for a status report. I gave it. You are watching the dog again."</p>
<p>Viktor grinned at her. "Can you blame me? The boy tamed a walking apocalypse on live television and gave it a six-gram name. You have to admire the showmanship."</p>
<p>"What I have to do is inform you that showmanship is now a strategic problem." Olga’s voice didn’t waver. "Before today, Kaiden Grey was the most-watched streamer among the awakened. After today, he is the most recognized human being on the planet. His viewership during the duel surpassed a hundred million concurrent, and the clips are circulating through every platform on Earth, civilian and awakened alike. He has surpassed every national powerhouse in public visibility, and every celebrity from the old world pales next to the numbers he’s pulling in now. Approaching him now carries the risk of drawing the attention of every major organization on the planet, not merely the Association."</p>
<p>The grin stayed. It didn’t reach his eyes.</p>
<p>"Good." Viktor dismissed his interface and stood. "Boring targets aren’t worth the trouble."</p>
<p>He made for the door, and Olga opened her mouth to continue the briefing when the temperature in the room dropped three degrees in a single breath.</p>
<p>Both of them felt it. The operatives on the casino floor below wouldn’t have noticed a thing, but up here, in Viktor’s private study, the cold that crawled up through the floorboards carried a message only two people in the building were cleared to receive.</p>
<p>Olga’s face went white. "Sir. She’s awake."</p>
<p>Viktor’s grin vanished. For one breath, the room held only the distant laughter of operatives below and Viktor’s absolute stillness.</p>
<p>Then he straightened his coat. "Let’s go."</p>
<p>They passed the gutted casino floor where broken roulette wheels gathered dust under chandeliers that hadn’t held light in years, skirted a cluster of operatives playing cards by lamplight who went silent as Viktor walked past, and descended a stairwell Olga had walked exactly twice in the four years she’d served him.</p>
<p>The first landing smelled like the rest of the building. The second did not.</p>
<p>The air turned cold between one step and the next, then colder, then a deep, still chill that settled against the skin and refused to leave. The walls narrowed from plaster to stone to polished dark rock that drank the sound of their footsteps, and the last light fixture died above the third landing without ceremony.</p>
<p>What remained was a luminescence that came from nowhere in particular and illuminated nothing it didn’t want to.</p>
<p>The corridor at the bottom stretched longer than the building above had any right to allow, and at its end, an archway opened into a darkness that breathed.</p>
<p>Viktor walked through without breaking stride. Olga followed, clutching the dossier against her chest with both arms.</p>
<p>The chamber beyond was vast, cold, and lit by a single candelabra of dark iron standing beside a high-backed chair whose crown vanished into the ceiling’s shadow. Shapes stirred in the carvings when the candlelight shifted, or perhaps the carvings stirred on their own.</p>
<p>A woman sat in it.</p>
<p>She was mid-stretch when they entered, one pale arm extended above her head with the languid grace of a cat uncurling from a sleep that had lasted far longer than any normal nap, and as her lips parted around a slow, luxurious yawn, the candlelight caught the briefest glint of something sharp behind them.</p>
<p>The stretch ended. Her hand drifted back to the armrest as if gravity were a suggestion she’d chosen to humor, and white hair pooled against the chair’s arm like water filling a basin.</p>
<p>Lips the color of dark wine settled into the faintest curve as half-lidded eyes found them at the edge of her light with drowsy, appraising interest.</p>
<p>"Tell me." Her voice filled the chamber without effort, low and unhurried, carrying the texture of old silk and very little warmth.</p>
<p>Viktor Thorne dropped to one knee at the border of the candlelight and bowed his head toward the woman on the throne. Beside him, Olga knelt.</p>
<p>"The world did something very amusing today, my Lady."</p>
<p>"I know." Another yawn, smaller, delicate, a closing note on whatever long sleep she had risen from. "I felt it." She rolled her neck once, and the sound it produced was porcelain on porcelain. "Show me."</p>
<p>Viktor glanced sideways. "Olga."</p>
<p>Olga rose on legs that refused to stop shaking, no matter how viciously she willed them still, and crossed the remaining distance to the throne one careful step at a time.</p>
<p>She held the leather-bound dossier out with both hands, head low.</p>
<p>"This contains all our collected information on the phenomenon known as Kaiden Grey, my Lady."</p>
<p>The woman took it with one hand.</p>
<p>The weight that had made Olga’s arm ache for the entire descent might as well have been a single page in her grip.</p>
<p>She settled the dossier on her lap, opened the cover with one long-nailed finger, and looked down at the first page.</p>
<p>A photograph of a young man filled it, recent and printed in full color, sharp enough to read the confidence in his eyes.</p>
<p>Kaiden Grey in the early days of his rise, caught mid-grin with the easy warmth of someone who burned too bright to notice how many people were staring.</p>
<p>Her fingers paused on the image.</p>
<p>One long nail traced the line of his jaw, slowly, the way one traces the edge of a flame to feel where the heat begins.</p>
<p>"This vitality..." she murmured. Her eyes narrowed, as if the photograph had told her something it hadn’t told anyone else. "Is he even human?"</p>
<p>"Confirmed human," Viktor nodded. "Both parents are awakened humans. The mother is Vespera Ashborn, the Shadow Monarch. The father is Magnus Ashborn, head of one of the three great guilds of America. Well, not anymore. The wife and son pretty much sent him into a straight downfall overnight."</p>
<p>"Ashborn." She turned the name over as if testing its vintage, then let it go with a soft exhale that could have been amusement.</p>
<p>Her nail lingered on his face for one more beat before she turned the page.</p>
<p>"Yes. The son, Kaiden, is a dungeon master."</p>
<p>The woman’s eyes widened for a single moment. Then her touch softened.</p>
<p>"The first one..." She murmured softly and turned the page.</p>
