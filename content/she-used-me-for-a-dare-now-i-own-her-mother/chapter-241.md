<p>While diagnostic protocols continued running, automated surveillance systems operated in the background... constantly comparing current satellite imagery to baseline data, flagging anomalies without requiring human intervention.</p>

<p>At 4:09 AM GMT, those automated systems triggered alerts simultaneously across multiple intelligence networks.</p>

<p>ANOMALY DETECTED: HIMALAYAN REGION</p>

<p>GEOGRAPHIC COORDINATES: AETHROS VALLEY</p>

<p>CHANGE CLASSIFICATION: SEVERE ATMOSPHERIC ABNORMALITY</p>

<p>The alerts escalated rapidly. Severity ratings climbed from standard to priority to critical within seconds. Multiple redundant systems flagged the same location. Warning indicators blazed across monitoring stations.</p>

<p>Analysts, still focused on system diagnostics, initially tried to ignore the alerts — just another automated flag to review later, another distraction from the critical task of understanding what had compromised global security.</p>

<p>But the alerts wouldn’t stop. They multiplied. Intensified. Demanded attention.</p>

<p>Finally, someone looked.</p>

<p>"Sir, we have something. You need to see this."</p>

<p>Analysts pulled up the flagged imagery.</p>

<p>The room went silent.</p>

<p>Where the Aethros Valley should have been visible... a familiar landscape of mountain terrain photographed thousands of times by orbital reconnaissance... there was only white.</p>

<p>A perfect circle of dense fog covering exactly two hundred square kilometers.</p>

<p>"When did this appear?"</p>

<p>Technicians ran comparison protocols, pulling up archived imagery from hours earlier.</p>

<p>"Last clear satellite image shows normal terrain at 3:46 AM GMT. First image showing the fog is timestamped 4:05 AM GMT, right after systems came back online."</p>

<p>The implications hit like a physical blow.</p>

<p>The fog had appeared during the seventeen minutes the world was blind.</p>

<p>Within minutes, the discovery propagated across intelligence networks worldwide.</p>

<p>Every nation with orbital assets was seeing the same impossible thing.</p>

<p>And the fog itself was wrong in ways that became immediately apparent upon closer examination.</p>

<p>The boundary formed a perfect geometric circle. Not the irregular, organic shape of natural weather patterns, but mathematical precision that suggested deliberate creation.</p>

<p>The density was uniform across the entire area. Satellite imagery showed no variation in thickness, no thinning at edges, no internal structure. Just solid, impenetrable white.</p>

<p>Technicians attempted infrared imaging. The sensors showed nothing but white.</p>

<p>Thermal scanning revealed no temperature differentials.</p>

<p>Radar pulses simply vanished into the white mass without returning echoes.</p>

<p>Every instrument humanity possessed failed to penetrate the fog.</p>

<p>Intelligence agencies cross-referenced data. Military commands compared notes. Scientific institutions analyzed patterns.</p>

<p>Every analysis reached the same impossible conclusion:</p>

<p>The blackout and the fog were connected. Not coincidentally occurring near the same time, but fundamentally linked. Whatever created the fog had also disabled humanity’s ability to observe its creation.</p>

<p>The panic that had been building... fed by unanswered questions and impossible diagnostics... crystallized into focused dread.</p>

<p>Because now they had a location. A specific place where something had happened. Something that violated their understanding of physics, meteorology, and reality itself.</p>

<p>Within hours, reconnaissance teams from multiple nations began mobilizing toward the Aethros Valley. Intelligence assets embedded in the region received urgent orders: get eyes on the fog boundary, report findings, determine what satellites couldn’t see.</p>

<p>But nobody knew what they would find when they arrived.</p>

<p>Or if they were ready to discover what had required the entire world to look away.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Alex’s hands gripped the book’s edges as the silver text continued materializing.</p>

<p>The pieces were connecting. A seventeen-minute blackout that disabled every monitoring system on Earth. A valley hidden by impossible fog that appeared the moment systems returned. Automated alerts triggering within minutes because the change was too massive to miss.</p>

<p>Something had fought in that valley during those seventeen minutes.</p>

<p>Something powerful enough that its battle required global blindness.</p>

<p>And whatever remained was still there, hidden behind fog that violated the laws of physics, waiting to be discovered.</p>

<p>The text shifted, new sections beginning to form, and Alex leaned closer.</p>

<p>What had the first teams found when they finally reached the fog boundary?</p>

<p>And what had they brought back that changed everything?</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>March 16th, 2025 — 24 Hours After the Event</p>

<p>The fog remained.</p>

<p>Unchanged. Impenetrable. Waiting.</p>

<p>Within hours of the discovery, every major power had mobilized. Elite reconnaissance teams deployed from six continents. Special operations forces. Intelligence operatives. Scientific personnel equipped with the most advanced technology humanity possessed.</p>

<p>Their orders were identical: Enter the fog. Determine what’s inside. Report findings immediately.</p>

<p>Nations prepared their finest. Delta Force operators. Spetsnaz commandos. SAS squadrons. Special reconnaissance units from China, France, Germany, India, Japan. Men and women who had survived impossible missions, who had never failed their countries, who had faced every conceivable threat and emerged victorious.</p>

<p>They entered with full communication equipment. Satellite uplinks. Emergency beacon transmitters. Quantum-encrypted systems. Experimental technology designed to penetrate any interference.</p>

<p>They entered with confidence, training, and equipment that had never failed them before.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Contact lost within seconds.</p>

<p>Some teams managed seventeen seconds of transmission before silence. Others went dark immediately upon crossing the boundary. A few transmitted bursts of static... meaningless noise that might have been screams or system failure or something else entirely.</p>

<p>Then nothing.</p>

<p>No communication signals emerged. No emergency beacons activated. No personnel returned.</p>

<p>Forty-seven elite operatives from eight nations entered the fog.</p>

<p>None came back.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>March 18th, 2025 — Three Days After the Event</p>

<p>Observation posts around the fog perimeter maintained constant watch. Instruments monitored for any change. Any movement. Any sign of the teams that had entered.</p>

<p>The fog remained unchanged.</p>

<p>No communication signals emerged. No emergency beacons activated. No personnel returned.</p>

<p>Command centers worldwide faced the same horrifying reality: their people had gone in, and the fog had swallowed them completely.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>March 20th, 2025 — Five Days After the Event</p>

<p>The panic was no longer hidden behind diplomatic language.</p>

<p>Intelligence agencies compiled lists of missing personnel. Forty-seven elite operatives from eight nations. Gone.</p>

<p>Were they dead? Captured? Lost in some spatial anomaly? Had they been disintegrated the moment they entered? Were they fighting something inside, unable to call for help? Or were they still alive, trapped in something beyond comprehension?</p>

<p>Nobody knew.</p>

<p>The fog revealed nothing. No sounds emerged. No signals escaped. No evidence of what happened inside reached the outside world.</p>

<p>The teams had simply walked into white silence and vanished.</p>

<p>And the not knowing was worse than any confirmed death.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>March 23rd, 2025 — Eight Days After the Event</p>

<p>No additional teams were sent.</p>

<p>After watching their best personnel disappear without explanation, every nation reached the same conclusion: whatever was inside the fog was beyond current capability to investigate.</p>

<p>The exclusion zone expanded to two hundred kilometers. Military forces established defensive perimeters. Not to attack... but to contain. To ensure nothing came out.</p>

<p>Because if the fog could swallow elite military units without a trace, what else could it do?</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>March 25th, 2025 — Ten Days After the Event</p>

<p>United Nations Headquarters, New York — Emergency Security Council Session</p>

<p>The chamber was packed beyond capacity.</p>

<p>Not just Security Council members, but representatives from every nation that had lost personnel to the fog. The atmosphere was thick with barely controlled fear.</p>

<p>Secretary-General António Silva stood at the podium, his usual diplomatic composure strained.</p>

<p>"We are gathered to address an unprecedented crisis. Ten days ago, a phenomenon appeared in the Aethros Valley that defies explanation. Multiple nations deployed reconnaissance teams to investigate. Forty-seven personnel entered the affected area. None have returned. None have communicated. We have no confirmation of their status or location."</p>

<p>He paused, letting that sink in.</p>

<p>"We face something beyond our collective experience. An unknown that has demonstrated the capacity to neutralize our most capable forces without trace or explanation. We must decide how to proceed."</p>

<p>The American representative stood immediately. "With respect, Secretary-General, we’re past the point of careful deliberation. We’ve lost twelve of our finest operators. China lost eight. Russia lost ten. Every major power has paid in blood... or whatever happened to them... and we’re no closer to understanding what we’re dealing with."</p>

<p>The Chinese delegate’s voice was tight with controlled anger. "Our analysis indicates the phenomenon is not natural. The geometric precision, the sensor resistance, the complete communication blackout... this suggests intelligent design. We may be dealing with hostile technology beyond our current understanding."</p>

<p>"Technology?" The British representative shook her head. "Our scientists say it’s not technology. It’s not biological. It’s not atmospheric. It doesn’t fit any known category. We can’t fight what we can’t comprehend."</p>

<p>The Russian delegate leaned forward, face grim. "Then we contain it. Establish permanent military cordon. Ensure nothing enters or leaves. If we cannot understand it, we prevent it from spreading."</p>

<p>"Contain?" The French representative’s voice rose. "Contain something that appeared instantaneously across two hundred square kilometers? Something that disabled every monitoring system on Earth for seventeen minutes? If this phenomenon chooses to expand, what makes you think our ’cordon’ will stop it?"</p>

<p>Voices erupted across the chamber. Fear masked as policy debate. Panic disguised as strategic discussion.</p>

<p>Secretary-General Silva called for order, but the chaos continued.</p>

<p>"We should send more teams..."</p>

<p>"We should evacuate the entire region..."</p>

<p>"We should prepare for worst-case scenarios..."</p>

<p>"We should assume our people are dead and move on..."</p>

<p>"We should..."</p>

<p>"ENOUGH."</p>

<p>The voice cut through the noise like a blade. Cold. Calm. Utterly unbothered by the panic surrounding it.</p>

<p>Everyone turned.</p>

<p>Premier Dmitri Volkov of Russia sat with his arms crossed, expression almost bored.</p>

<p>While every other delegate showed signs of stress... loosened ties, disheveled hair, exhaustion from sleepless nights... Volkov looked like he’d just come from a leisurely lunch.</p>

<p>Secretary-General Silva seized the moment of silence. "Premier Volkov, you have the floor."</p>

<p>Volkov stood slowly, surveying the chamber with thinly veiled contempt.</p>

<p>"I have been listening to this discussion for forty minutes," he said, voice dripping with disdain. "Forty minutes of panic. Forty minutes of fear. Forty minutes of accomplished nothing."</p>

<p>He gestured dismissively at the assembled delegates.</p>

<p>"You are all acting as if this is some unsolvable mystery. As if we are helpless before the unknown." He paused. "We are not helpless. We have never been helpless. Humanity has faced the unknown before and conquered it through simple, direct action."</p>

<p>The American representative frowned. "Premier, with respect, this situation..."</p>

<p>"Is being overcomplicated," Volkov interrupted. "You send reconnaissance teams into unknown phenomenon. They disappear. You panic. You debate. You accomplish nothing." He shrugged. "I ask simple question: Can we resolve this by panicking?"</p>

<p>Silence.</p>

<p>"No? Then perhaps we stop panicking and use practical solution."</p>

<p>The Chinese delegate leaned forward. "What solution are you proposing?"</p>

<p>Volkov’s smile was cold. "If it is really something dangerous, we should just nuke it. Can’t we?"</p>
