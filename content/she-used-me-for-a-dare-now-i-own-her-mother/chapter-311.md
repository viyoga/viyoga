<p>"You don’t need to worry too much about this, darling."</p>

<p>Cassandra placed a warm, reassuring hand on Jennifer’s shoulder, giving it a firm squeeze.</p>

<p>"This is not your burden to carry. We will handle it."</p>

<p>She straightened up, wiping a stray tear from her cheek with a delicate, manicured finger.</p>

<p>With a deep, shuddering breath, she rebuilt her composure, forcing the mask of the invincible socialite back into place... though she left just enough cracks for Jennifer to see the fear underneath.</p>

<p>"So what if we don’t get his support immediately?" she said, her voice light, almost breezy, but with a hollow undertone that made it sound like a lie she was telling herself.</p>

<p>"Richard hasn’t officially become the Head yet. The coronation isn’t for another six months. A lot can happen in six months, can’t it?"</p>

<p>She smiled, a tragic, brave little smile that didn’t reach her eyes.</p>

<p>"We might lose our key contracts... and perhaps the licensing board will freeze our current projects... but we are Vanderbilts. We know how to survive lean years. We can downsize. Sell off a few assets. We’ll rebuild."</p>

<p>She patted Jennifer’s shoulder one last time... a gesture that felt less like comfort and more like a farewell.</p>

<p>"Now, I have to go meet your uncles," she said, standing and smoothing her skirt with practiced efficiency. "We need to sit down and figure out a strategy for Monday morning."</p>

<p>She turned and walked toward the door, her heels clicking against the marble with measured, deliberate steps.</p>

<p>She paused at the door, her hand lingering on the brass handle, and glanced back over her shoulder.</p>

<p>"You go on home. Enjoy your weekend."</p>

<p>The heavy glass door clicked shut.</p>

<p>And Cassandra was gone.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>The silence that followed wasn’t the powerful, contemplative quiet of ten minutes ago. It was heavy. Suffocating. The kind of silence that presses down on your chest and makes it hard to breathe.</p>

<p>Jennifer sat frozen behind the mahogany desk.</p>

<p>The afternoon light had changed. What had been bright and assertive when Cassandra arrived was now slanting, golden, almost apologetic. The office felt different in it... smaller, more uncertain.</p>

<p>The nameplate... Jennifer Vanderbilt, Director of Special Projects... caught the fading light, but it no longer looked like a trophy.</p>

<p>It looked like a tombstone.</p>

<p>Downsize. Sell assets. Survive.</p>

<p>The words echoed in her mind, poisonous and impossible.</p>

<p>She looked at the skyline. At the cranes building the new Vanderbilt complex in the distance, their skeletal frames sharp against the darkening sky. If Richard Blackwood turned on them, those cranes would stop. The stock would plummet. The vultures would circle.</p>

<p>And Sophia Blackwood would be there, watching from her pristine throne, smiling that ice-queen smile as the Vanderbilt name collapsed into rubble.</p>

<p>A surge of anger, hot and acidic, rose in Jennifer’s throat.</p>

<p>She stood abruptly, her chair scraping back, and paced to the window. Her reflection stared back at her... pale, wide-eyed, younger than she wanted to look.</p>

<p>’This wasn’t supposed to happen.’</p>

<p>Ten minutes ago, the path had been so clear. Finish the degree. Solidify the Director role. Push her mother out. Take the throne. She had practically felt the weight of the crown on her head.</p>

<p>She was supposed to inherit an empire, not salvage a shipwreck.</p>

<p>For a fleeting, desperate second, her mind drifted to the offer.</p>

<p>’The Future Head.’</p>

<p>The title whispered to her ego, seductive and dangerous. If she said yes... she would save them.</p>

<p>She would be the martyr. The savior. The woman who sacrificed herself to keep the Vanderbilt name alive. She would be the wife of the most powerful man in the city.</p>

<p>But as the thought settled, her stomach twisted in violent, instinctive rejection.</p>

<p>’Richard Blackwood.’</p>

<p>She knew him. She had seen him at galas, standing with a glass of scotch, looking at women not as people but as livestock to be graded. He didn’t want a partner. He didn’t want a queen to rule beside him.</p>

<p>He wanted a trophy. Something beautiful to display at galas and lock away when the cameras left.</p>

<p>If she married him, she wouldn’t be running the Vanderbilt Empire. She wouldn’t be sitting in board meetings or signing merger deals.</p>

<p>She would be watching her family’s legacy rot from a penthouse window while Richard patted her head and told her not to worry her pretty little mind about business.</p>

<p>She would lose everything she actually wanted. She would lose the chair. She would lose her voice.</p>

<p>"No."</p>

<p>The word came out sharp and final, scraping against the silence.</p>

<p>She slammed her palm against the glass, the sound echoing through the empty office.</p>

<p>"Why did you do this, Mother?" she hissed at her own reflection.</p>

<p>It wasn’t fair. It was Vivienne’s job. Vivienne was the Head. Vivienne was the one who had preached about "duty" and "sacrifice" and "the battlefield" for twenty years. And the moment the first real shot was fired, she had run.</p>

<p>’Coward.’</p>

<p>Cassandra’s word echoed in her mind, louder this time, more certain.</p>

<p>Jennifer turned away from the window, her mind racing, building a fortress of logic to protect herself from guilt.</p>

<p>’She’s already lived her life,’ Jennifer thought, the rationalization taking root like a weed. ’She had her time. She ran the company for decades. Why should I... ’</p>

<p>She stopped pacing, staring at the empty leather chair where Cassandra had sat.</p>

<p>’Cassandra is right. A real leader sacrifices for the family.’</p>

<p>If Mother really cared about the legacy, she would have said yes. She would have seen the strategic value.</p>

<p>She was older. Experienced. She could handle a man like Richard. She could manage the Blackwood household, influence their politics from the inside.</p>

<p>It wouldn’t be a cage for her. It would just be... a new deployment.</p>

<p>A twisted sort of hope began to bloom in Jennifer’s chest.</p>

<p>’Yes,’ she thought, nodding to the empty room. ’It makes sense. It’s the only logical move.’</p>

<p>If she could just find her mother... if she could just talk to her... she could make her see reason. She would explain the stakes. She would *demand* that Vivienne do her duty.</p>

<p>"I have to find her," Jennifer whispered.</p>

<p>She turned back to her desk, her mind scrambling for a plan. She needed resources. She needed tracking. She needed...</p>

<p>’Buzz.’</p>

<p>The phone on her desk vibrated against the wood, a harsh, mechanical sound that made her jump.</p>

<p>She stared at it.</p>

<p>It buzzed again.</p>

<p>Her heart hammering, she reached out and picked it up. The screen lit up, glowing cold blue against the fading twilight.</p>

<p>Unknown Number</p>

<p>She unlocked the phone. The message was short. Clinical.</p>

<p>Subject: V.V.</p>

<p>Location: Roland Estate. Villa Six.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s breath hitched. V.V. Vivienne Vanderbilt.</p>

<p>For half a second, her mind caught on the strangeness of it. Who sent this? How did they know she needed it right now, at this exact moment?</p>

<p>Then the answer came, obvious and reassuring.</p>

<p>Reginald.</p>

<p>Of course. He was tracking Vivienne too. He must have found her and sent Jennifer the location. He knew she’d want to confront her mother first. He was giving her the chance to fix this before it was too late.</p>

<p>She stared at the address. Roland Estate. It was a high-security development on the outskirts of the city. Quiet. Secluded.</p>

<p>Hope, bright and sharp, flooded her system.</p>

<p>She wasn’t trapped. She wasn’t the sacrifice.</p>

<p>She grabbed her purse and coat, nearly knocking the nameplate off the desk in her haste. Her hands were shaking, but her mind was clear. She had a destination. She had a mission.</p>

<p>’I’m coming, Mother,’ she thought, her jaw tight with resolve.</p>

<p>She would go there. She would look Vivienne in the eye. And she would force her to be the leader she claimed to be.</p>

<p>Jennifer Vanderbilt wasn’t going to be sold.</p>

<p>Not today.</p>

<p>She marched toward the door, yanked it open, and stepped into the hallway without looking back.</p>

<p>Behind her, the office sat empty and dark, the nameplate gleaming like a small, golden lie.</p>
