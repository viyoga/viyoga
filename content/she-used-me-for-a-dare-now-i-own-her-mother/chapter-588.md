<p>The rhythmic clatter of the streetcar over the iron rails filled the quiet, dimly lit cabin.</p>

<p>Victoria leaned her head heavily against Alex’s shoulder, her eyes half-closed as the amber streetlights outside swept across the windows in long, rhythmic intervals.</p>

<p>The sheer physical toll of the afternoon had finally caught up to her.</p>

<p>After the street musician’s final chords had faded, they had spent the remaining twilight laughing and hurrying through the crowded market stalls, darting across brick crosswalks in a breathless rush to catch the outward-bound tram before the evening schedule thinned out.</p>

<p>For a woman accustomed to chauffeured sedans, silent elevators, and tailored armchairs, hours of walking, standing, and dodging pedestrians in boots had taken their toll. Madame Blackwood, who routinely commanded twelve-hour board meetings on sheer willpower, looked thoroughly, delightfully worn out.</p>

<p>Her shoes were resting flat on the wooden floorboards, her shoulders had dropped completely, and her right hand stayed buried deep inside her cardigan pocket, fingers lazily curled around the smooth walnut cat.</p>

<p>Alex looked down at the crown of her head, feeling the soft, heavy warmth of her leaning into his side. He could feel just how exhausted she was in the slow, even pace of her breathing.</p>

<p>Yet whenever she stirred and caught his eye, there wasn’t the slightest sign that she was ready to call it a night.</p>

<p>Alex smiled, giving a slow, quiet shake of his head, charmed by her stubborn refusal to let the day slip away.</p>

<p>Outside the rattling glass, the last embers of sunset had vanished entirely.</p>

<p>By the time the streetcar slowed with a deep squeal of its pneumatic brakes, the clock above the motorman’s booth flickered past six-thirty. The sky had turned a deep, ink-black velvet, cold and clear, with the distant foothill peaks looming like dark, jagged silhouettes against the rising stars.</p>

<p>The tram lurched to a final stop at the gravel commuter loop on the dark southern fringe of the district... the quiet, windswept lot where they had left the car hours earlier.</p>

<p>"End of the line," Alex whispered, turning his head slightly so his lips brushed her temple.</p>

<p>Victoria blinked awake, a soft, drowsy breath slipping past her lips as she lifted her head from his shoulder. She looked around the nearly empty tram, offering him a faint, sleepy smile... until her gaze drifted out the window toward the shadowed parking lot.</p>

<p>?"Back where we started," she murmured, a quiet pang of reluctance in her voice. But there was no disappointment behind it... only a deep, lingering warmth.</p>

<p>Today had given her more pure joy than the last twenty years combined, and she couldn’t ask for a single thing more.</p>

<p>Alex smiled, his thumb brushing lightly across her cheekbone as he took her hand. "Come on."</p>

<p>?They stepped down from the streetcar onto the gravel platform, greeted by the crisp, cool stillness of the evening. Out here on the quiet fringe of the foothills, the air was clean and refreshing, carrying the faint, earthy scent of fallen autumn leaves and distant pines.</p>

<p>?Alex kept a gentle hand at the small of her back as they walked across the quiet commuter lot toward his car.</p>

<p>A quick press of the key fob made the headlights flare to life, casting two bright ribbons of white light across the dark gravel. He reached out and opened the passenger door for her.</p>

<p>?Victoria sank back into the leather seat with a quiet sigh, finally resting her tired feet as Alex pulled the door shut behind her.</p>

<p>?A moment later, Alex climbed into the driver’s seat.</p>

<p>He didn’t say a word. Without breaking the quiet, he pulled the shifter back, checked his mirror, and eased the car smoothly out of the gravel lot onto the main road, quickly gathering speed as the asphalt smoothed beneath the tires.</p>

<p>Victoria leaned back against her headrest, watching the familiar landmarks slip past the glass.</p>

<p>Ahead lay the main junction... the familiar, well-lit split where taking an immediate right would lead them straight back home.</p>

<p>She waited for him to tap the brakes and signal right.</p>

<p>Instead, Alex pressed deeper into the throttle.</p>

<p>The indicator clicked left, and with an easy, deliberate sweep of the steering wheel, he guided the car through the opposite fork, veering away from the metropolitan route and straight onto a narrow two-lane road winding upward into the dark silhouette of the hills.</p>

<p>Victoria blinked, straightening against the leather.</p>

<p>"Alex... you took the wrong turn."</p>

<p>Alex kept his eyes on the road ahead, a faint, teasing smirk playing at the corner of his lips as the engine hummed with the rising incline.</p>

<p>Catching the look on his face, Victoria’s eyes widened with sudden realization.</p>

<p>?"Wait... don’t tell me—"</p>

<p>?Whatever sluggishness and fatigue had weighed her down on the streetcar was completely gone.</p>

<p>The short rest against his shoulder, paired with the electric realization that their date wasn’t over, seemed to have revitalized her entirely. She turned sideways in the leather seat, tucking one leg beneath her, her dark eyes wide and shimmering with unguarded excitement.</p>

<p>"Where are we going?" she asked, unable to keep the bright, breathless anticipation out of her voice. She leaned slightly toward the center console, watching his hands. "Alex, tell me."</p>

<p>Alex glanced over at her, his eyes crinkling at the corners as a slow, knowing smile curved his lips. He reached across the console, his thumb lightly brushing the side of her chin.</p>

<p>"Secret," he said simply.</p>

<p>Before she could protest, he pressed deeper into the accelerator, steering smoothly into the rising incline. The engine surged with a low, eager rumble, the headlights carving bright, sweeping arcs through the dark wall of towering pines as they took the first sharp bend.</p>

<p>The road spiraled up the crest of Mount Solitude, a wooded granite bluff that rose like a watchtower over the metropolitan basin.</p>

<p>As the car crested the final switchback, the dark tunnel of pines abruptly broke open to reveal the sprawling stone pavilions of the Skyline Overlook.</p>

<p>Victoria leaned forward in her seat, both hands pressed against the dashboard as her eyes widened in genuine awe.</p>

<p>Even at night, the entrance was breathtaking: twin stone archways lit by low, amber gaslamps framed a massive terrace carved into the natural rock face, tiered like an ancient amphitheater climbing toward the sky.</p>

<p>"Alex," she breathed, her voice rich with childlike wonder. "I never knew this was here."</p>

<p>"A lot of people miss it if they only look down," he said softly, putting the car in park and switching off the headlights.</p>
