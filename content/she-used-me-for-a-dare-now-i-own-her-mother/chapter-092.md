<p>Miranda surveyed the chamber, her green eyes taking in the small flickers of hope that had ignited across the opposition’s faces.</p>

<p>Pierce sat straighter in his chair. Marcus, despite trying to control himself, still carried that barely suppressed gleam of renewed possibility.</p>

<p>Her lips curved into something that wasn’t quite a smile... sharper, more predatory.</p>

<p>The scent of their hope made her pulse quicken.</p>

<p>This was what she lived for... the moment when her opponents thought they’d found their lifeline, just before she cut the rope.</p>

<p>"Let’s ask the men in question themselves why they supposedly lied, shall we?" she said, her voice carrying across the chamber with surgical precision.</p>

<p>She paused, turning slightly toward the panel.</p>

<p>"But before that, let me clarify something once more..."</p>

<p>Miranda moved with deliberate steps toward William and Brad, her approach measured and calm.</p>

<p>Both young men straightened slightly, their injuries making the movement awkward.</p>

<p>"Mr. Thompson, Mr. Martinez," she began, her tone professional but probing.</p>

<p>"I want to be absolutely certain about your testimony."</p>

<p>She stopped directly in front of them, close enough that they could see the sharp intelligence in her eyes.</p>

<p>"Are you completely sure that these three young men..." she gestured toward Alex, Mike, and Danny, "...are the people who attacked you?"</p>

<p>Pierce shot up from his seat, irritation flashing across his face.</p>

<p>"Dr. Whitman, we’ve established this already!" he interjected, his voice carrying frustrated authority.</p>

<p>"Why are you asking questions we already know the answers to? The victims have positively identified their attackers multiple times."</p>

<p>His tone suggested she was wasting everyone’s time with unnecessary repetition.</p>

<p>"We’re not here to relitigate settled facts!"</p>

<p>William and Brad also exchanged a quick glance, confusion flickering in their eyes.</p>

<p>Why was she asking again? But the evidence still seemed to favor them... Alex had been caught in his lie about being on campus.</p>

<p>William adjusted his sling slightly before speaking, his voice carrying renewed confidence.</p>

<p>"Yes, ma’am. We are absolutely sure."</p>

<p>Brad nodded firmly despite his visible discomfort.</p>

<p>"No doubt in our minds. It was definitely them."</p>

<p>"Excellent."</p>

<p>Miranda turned with fluid grace toward Alex’s table, her movement like a chess player advancing on the king.</p>

<p>"Mr. Hale, Mr. Carter, Mr. Morrison," she said, her voice carrying respectful authority.</p>

<p>"Now then, can you clarify for this panel why you lied about being present on campus during the incident time?"</p>

<p>Alex rose slowly, his composure intact and somehow even more unshakeable than before.</p>

<p>When he spoke, his voice carried an edge of something that might have been amusement.</p>

<p>"I never lied about anything, Dr. Whitman."</p>

<p>A small ripple of shocked murmurs ran through the chamber. Someone whispered,</p>

<p>"He’s still denying it?" The brazenness of his continued denial in the face of clear evidence left several panel members blinking in surprise.</p>

<p>Dr. Brennan leaned forward, his expression troubled.</p>

<p>Even Dr. Carlisle looked taken aback by his bold response.</p>

<p>But Alex continued, his voice growing stronger and more confident.</p>

<p>"I never said I was not on campus yesterday."</p>

<p>He paused, letting that sink in before continuing with perfect clarity.</p>

<p>"In fact, I came to college as any normal student would on a weekday. Yes, I was sitting in the cafeteria, exactly as Mr. Reeves testified."</p>

<p>The chamber fell into stunned silence.</p>

<p>The confidence in his voice, the lack of defensive posturing... it suggested something the audience hadn’t considered.</p>

<p>Miranda’s smile widened slightly.</p>

<p>"Then perhaps," she said, her voice carrying the satisfaction of someone about to spring a perfectly laid trap, "we should hear your side of the story."</p>

<p>She moved closer to Alex’s table, her eyes gleaming.</p>

<p>"If you came to campus that morning and then left in such a hurry... which Mr. Reeves somehow interprets as conspiracy... tell us exactly what happened."</p>

<p>The stage was set. Alex’s calm confidence. Miranda’s predatory anticipation. And the growing realization that this case was about to take another dramatic turn.</p>

<p>"We were in the cafeteria that morning, exactly as Mr. Reeves testified," Alex began, his voice carrying the steady confidence that had unsettled his opponents all day.</p>

<p>"But the phone call I received was not coordination for any assault."</p>

<p>He paused, his expression growing more serious, the weight of the memory settling over his features.</p>

<p>"It was Danny calling to tell me that his eight-year-old sister Nina had been rejected by St. Mary’s Hospital."</p>

<p>His voice dropped slightly, carrying genuine emotion.</p>

<p>"She was dying, and they had nowhere else to turn."</p>

<p>Mike rose beside him, his voice firm with conviction and barely controlled emotion.</p>

<p>"We left immediately because a little girl’s life was in danger."</p>

<p>He looked directly at Pierce, his tone sharpening.</p>

<p>"Not to plan violence, but to try to save a child."</p>

<p>Pierce’s voice rose with what sounded like genuine disbelief, but there was an edge of desperation underneath.</p>

<p>"Mr. Hale, you seriously expect this panel to believe that instead of planning assault, you were responding to a medical emergency?"</p>

<p>His tone carried forced institutional skepticism.</p>

<p>"How remarkably convenient that this supposed crisis coincides perfectly with the timeline of your alleged criminal activity."</p>

<p>Pierce’s confidence was visibly wavering, but he pressed on.</p>

<p>"A dying child? Really? That’s your explanation?"</p>

<p>Miranda turned her attention to Danny, her voice softening with professional compassion.</p>

<p>"Mr. Morrison, please tell this panel about your sister’s medical situation."</p>

<p>Danny rose slowly, his hands gripping the table’s edge. He swallowed hard before speaking.</p>

<p>"Nina’s had kidney problems since she was five. We’ve been patients at St. Mary’s for three years."</p>

<p>His voice was steady but strained, clearly uncomfortable sharing family details in this formal setting.</p>

<p>"Tuesday night, they called us around 11 PM. Said they couldn’t help her anymore. That we needed a specialized pediatric unit."</p>

<p>Danny paused, his jaw tightening.</p>

<p>"We spent all night calling hospitals. Nobody would take her without connections or money we don’t have."</p>

<p>Dr. Carlisle leaned forward slightly. "That must have been very difficult."</p>

<p>Danny nodded, his composure wavering for just a moment.</p>

<p>"We came home Wednesday morning. That’s when I called Alex and Mike."</p>

<p>His voice grew steadier, more focused.</p>

<p>"I told them the doctors had given up. That we had nowhere to turn."</p>

<p>He looked directly at Alex and Mike.</p>

<p>"They came immediately."</p>

<p>Mike stood beside Danny, his voice firm.</p>

<p>"When we got to Danny’s house, we could see the family was desperate. Alex promised he would find a way to help."</p>

<p>Miranda’s voice remained gentle but clear.</p>

<p>"So to be absolutely certain, Mr. Morrison, you were never part of any assault plan?"</p>

<p>Danny’s voice grew stronger, anger beginning to replace vulnerability.</p>

<p>"My eight-year-old sister was dying."</p>

<p>He looked directly at William and Brad, then at Marcus.</p>

<p>"I would never waste a single minute on violence when my family needed me."</p>

<p>The chamber fell silent. The stark contrast was clear to everyone present... three young men who had spent that day trying to save a child’s life, versus the elaborate lie constructed to destroy them.</p>

<p>Pierce sat rigid in his chair, watching his fabricated case crumble under the weight of simple human truth.</p>

<p>The cold realization hit him like a physical blow... he had been so focused on orchestrating the perfect trap that he had never bothered to investigate the most basic question: where exactly had the three defendants actually been that morning?</p>
