<p>Jennifer ran barefoot down the corridor, her breath breaking unevenly in her throat as the marble amplified every step. The house felt too large, too still.</p>

<p>She rounded the corner into the foyer and stopped.</p>

<p>Empty.</p>

<p>The front door was closed. The air felt untouched.</p>

<p>"Sir?" she called, quieter than she intended.</p>

<p>No answer.</p>

<p>A spike of cold panic tightened her chest. She turned sharply and retraced her steps to the master suite. The door remained half-open.</p>

<p>Inside, nothing had changed. Her mother was slumped against the window, pale and unmoving. Helena lay face-down on the carpet, one arm stretched toward nothing.</p>

<p>The room smelled of sweat, perfume, and something metallic beneath it.</p>

<p>But he wasn’t there.</p>

<p>Her pulse jumped.</p>

<p>Jennifer pivoted and headed for the stairs, moving faster now, the slick warmth between her thighs making her steps uneven. The reminder of what she’d done on the terrace sent a flash of heat through her stomach.</p>

<p>At the top of the staircase, she froze.</p>

<p>He was halfway down already.</p>

<p>Descending without hurry. Without tension. As if he were leaving a dinner party instead of the wreckage of two women.</p>

<p>Relief hit her harder than fear.</p>

<p>He hadn’t left.</p>

<p>She gripped the banister and forced herself to breathe.</p>

<p>She couldn’t chase him like this — wild, half-dressed, shaking. Not like them.</p>

<p>Jennifer straightened slowly, drawing composure around herself piece by piece. She was still a Vanderbilt. That hadn’t changed just because her body had betrayed her.</p>

<p>Even if it was still betraying her.</p>

<p>She began descending, slower this time. Each step measured.</p>

<p>Her gaze locked onto his back.</p>

<p>The gallery lights traced the clean architecture of him... the broad plane of his shoulders narrowing to his waist, the slow flex beneath his skin as he shifted his weight. A faint sheen still caught the light along his spine.</p>

<p>Her throat went dry.</p>

<p>Through the glass earlier, he had been untouchable. Framed. Distant.</p>

<p>Now there was no barrier.</p>

<p>Her imagination filled in what she couldn’t see directly... the weight of him, the heat of his body, the steady force she had watched reduce two women to silence.</p>

<p>Her breath faltered.</p>

<p>For a split second, she pictured herself in their place... not collapsed, not broken... but taking it. Meeting it. Holding his gaze while he tried to overwhelm her.</p>

<p>The thought made her thighs press together involuntarily.</p>

<p>He reached the bottom of the stairs and turned into the dining room without looking back.</p>

<p>Jennifer hesitated on the final step.</p>

<p>’He knows I’m here.’</p>

<p>’He has to.’</p>

<p>The idea that he was ignoring her... deliberately... sent a sharp, almost humiliating heat across her skin.</p>

<p>Jennifer followed.</p>

<p>The dining room was dim, washed in silver light from the tall windows. He moved to the sideboard, selected one of her mother’s rare bottles without hesitation, and uncorked it.</p>

<p>No glass.</p>

<p>He drank straight from the bottle.</p>

<p>Jennifer watched the movement of his throat as he swallowed. A drop of wine escaped the corner of his mouth and slid down his neck before disappearing against his chest.</p>

<p>He lowered the bottle and exhaled softly.</p>

<p>"Not bad," he murmured, his voice a low, gravelly vibration in the quiet room. He traced the label with appreciation.</p>

<p>"You’ll be good company until those bitches recover."</p>

<p>With the bottle held loosely in his grip, he turned and began to stride toward the door to exit the dining room.</p>

<p>He reached the archway.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s entire body tensed, anticipation crackling through her like electricity.</p>

<p>’This is it. He’s going to see me.’</p>

<p>’He’s going to... ’</p>

<p>Her mind raced through possibilities. Would he be surprised? Shocked? Would that cool control finally crack when he realized she’d been watching? That she’d come down here for him?</p>

<p>’Maybe he didn’t know I was there after all.’</p>

<p>’Maybe when he sees me, wants me... ’</p>

<p>The door swung open.</p>

<p>He stepped through without breaking stride.</p>

<p>And looked directly at her.</p>

<p>No surprise flickered across his face. No shock. No recognition of something unexpected.</p>

<p>Just... assessment.</p>

<p>His eyes traveled down her body with the slow, methodical precision of someone inspecting merchandise. Starting at her flushed face, trailing down her barely-clinging top, pausing at her exposed lower half, lingering on her trembling thighs.</p>

<p>Jennifer felt the weight of his gaze like a physical touch.</p>

<p>Heat flooded her face as reality crashed through her remaining composure.</p>

<p>She was standing in front of him half-naked. Her expensive silk top hanging loose and wrinkled. Nothing below the waist except skin. Her legs bare, still trembling, still wet from...</p>

<p>’Oh god.’</p>

<p>’He can see it.’</p>

<p>His gaze had stopped at her inner thighs. At the evidence still glistening there in the moonlight... the slick proof of what she’d done on the terrace while watching him destroy her mother.</p>

<p>Shame hit her like a tidal wave.</p>

<p>She wanted to cover herself. Wanted to cross her arms, hide the evidence, pretend she was someone else.</p>

<p>But she couldn’t move.</p>

<p>Every carefully constructed layer of composure... the future CEO, the Vanderbilt heir, the woman who’d convinced herself she was walking down here on her own terms... cracked and fell away under the weight of his gaze.</p>

<p>Could only stand there, exposed and trembling, while he looked at her like she was something he was deciding whether or not to purchase.</p>

<p>The wine bottle dangled from his hand, forgotten.</p>

<p>A slow smile curved his lips... not warm, not welcoming.</p>

<p>Amused.</p>

<p>Like he’d just discovered something entertaining.</p>

<p>He tilted his head slightly, that mocking smile widening.</p>

<p>"Who are you, little girl?"</p>

<p>The words landed harder than a slap.</p>

<p>Jennifer stared at him, certain she’d misheard.</p>

<p>’Little girl?’</p>

<p>For a heartbeat she expected recognition to follow... the shift in posture, the narrowing of his eyes, some sign that he understood exactly who stood in front of him.</p>

<p>Nothing came.</p>

<p>His gaze remained steady, faintly amused.</p>

<p>"Are you a maid?" he asked mildly, glancing past her toward the staircase. "But I was told there wouldn’t be any staff in the villa tonight."</p>

<p>The casual dismissal snapped something inside her.</p>

<p>"A maid?" she repeated, disbelief sharpening her voice.</p>

<p>He lifted one shoulder in a lazy shrug. "You look... underdressed for a guest."</p>

<p>Heat rushed to her face... not embarrassment this time, but fury.</p>

<p>"I am not a maid," she said, each word clipped. "I am the owner of this villa."</p>

<p>He watched her as if waiting for the punchline.</p>

<p>Her hands curled into fists at her sides. "Not just some servant you can dismiss with a glance."</p>

<p>She stepped closer, chin lifting despite the tremor in her chest.</p>

<p>"I’m Jennifer Vanderbilt," she said, anger blazing now, steady and unmistakable.</p>

<p>"Daughter of Vivienne Vanderbilt."</p>
