<p>The engine of the Porsche Taycan whined as Jennifer floored the accelerator, weaving through the evening traffic with a reckless precision that matched her mood.</p>

<p>Her knuckles were white against the leather steering wheel. Every mile that blurred past was another rehearsed line in the speech she was preparing.</p>

<p>’You abandoned us.’</p>

<p>’You ran while the house was burning.’</p>

<p>’I am here to save you from yourself.’</p>

<p>She checked the rearview mirror, catching a glimpse of her own eyes. They were hard. Cold. They looked like Cassandra’s eyes.</p>

<p>"I am the only adult left in this family," she muttered to the empty car, the words fueling her righteous anger.</p>

<p>She wasn’t just a daughter anymore. She was a cleaner. A fixer. She was driving into the dark to drag her mother back to the light, kicking and screaming if necessary. Vivienne had preached about duty for years; tonight, Jennifer was going to make her choke on it.</p>

<p>By the time she reached the outskirts of the city, the sun was nearly gone... a thin line of orange bleeding along the horizon, the sky above it darkening from purple to indigo.</p>

<p>The GPS chirped, signaling her arrival.</p>

<p>Roland Estate.</p>

<p>She slowed as the iron gates loomed ahead. They stood open. Not just unlocked—*open*. The heavy wrought iron panels pushed all the way back against the stone pillars, as though someone had driven through hours ago and simply never bothered to close them.</p>

<p>No security booth. No guard. No intercom asking who she was.</p>

<p>’Saturday night,’ Jennifer told herself, easing through. ’Private estate. People who live here don’t need guards at every corner.’</p>

<p>The road beyond curved gently upward, winding through landscaped grounds until it crested a low rise.</p>

<p>And then she saw them.</p>

<p>The villas.</p>

<p>Seven distinct structures carved into the hillside, arranged in a sweeping crescent that overlooked a private, artificial lake. In the fading light, they looked like jewels set against black velvet.</p>

<p>Soft amber lighting washed over the clean, geometric lines of white stone walls. Floor-to-ceiling glass reflected the shimmering water below. The landscaping was immaculate... sculpted hedges, stone pathways glowing with embedded LEDs. It was a sanctuary of quiet, understated power.</p>

<p>Beautiful.</p>

<p>Peaceful.</p>

<p>The kind of place where the noise and chaos of the city felt like a distant memory.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s gaze swept across the crescent, her eyes cataloging each villa as she drove past.</p>

<p>Villa One sat at the highest point... massive, commanding the best view of the lake and city lights beyond. It projected dominance. Power. Everything Jennifer valued.</p>

<p>Villa Two. Dark.</p>

<p>Villa Three. Lit but empty-looking.</p>

<p>Villa Four. Also dark.</p>

<p>Her phone sat in the cup holder, the message still glowing on the screen:</p>

<p>Subject: V.V.</p>

<p>Location: Roland Estate. Villa Six.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s eyes found it near the end of the crescent.</p>

<p>Villa Six sat tucked between Villa Five and Villa Seven, its position unremarkable. Not the biggest. Not the highest. Not the most isolated.</p>

<p>But it had lights on.</p>

<p>Warm light spilling through the floor-to-ceiling windows. A sense of occupancy that the other villas lacked</p>

<p>’There,’ Jennifer thought, turning the steering wheel.</p>

<p>She guided the Porsche into the circular driveway and killed the engine.</p>

<p>The silence that followed was immediate and absolute.</p>

<p>No traffic. No voices. No hum of distant machinery.</p>

<p>Just the faint rustle of leaves in a breeze she couldn’t feel and the soft tick of her car’s engine cooling.</p>

<p>Jennifer sat for a moment, staring at Villa Six’s entrance.</p>

<p>It was beautiful. Elegant. The kind of place that whispered wealth without shouting it.</p>

<p>A place where someone could disappear for a while without anyone asking questions.</p>

<p>’This is it,’ she thought, reaching for the door handle. ’This is where she’s been hiding.’</p>

<p>She stepped out of the car, her heels crunching softly against the gravel.</p>

<p>The air was cool. Clean. She could smell earth and grass and something floral she couldn’t identify.</p>

<p>For a fleeting second, she understood why someone might choose to hide here.</p>

<p>Then she remembered why she’d come.</p>

<p>Jennifer walked toward the front door, her stride purposeful, her jaw set.</p>

<p>She was done being patient. Done waiting for her mother to do the right thing.</p>

<p>Tonight, Vivienne Vanderbilt was going to face her responsibilities.</p>

<p>Whether she wanted to or not.</p>

<p>Jennifer reached the doorbell and paused, her finger hovered over it.</p>

<p>For the first time since leaving the city, doubt flickered through her mind.</p>

<p>’What if this is wrong?’</p>

<p>’What if the message was a mistake? What if someone was playing a cruel joke? What if she’d driven all the way out here based on a text from an unknown number and her mother wasn’t even...’</p>

<p>Jennifer shook her head sharply, cutting off the thought.</p>

<p>No.</p>

<p>She’d come too far to second-guess herself now. Reginald had sent that message. He had to have. Who else would know where Vivienne was hiding? Who else would care enough to help?</p>

<p>And even if this was some kind of prank... which it wasn’t... she was a Vanderbilt. Nobody had the audacity to harm her. Not here. Not anywhere.</p>

<p>She pressed the bell.</p>

<p>The chime echoed faintly from somewhere inside the villa. Soft. Melodic. Expensive.</p>

<p>Jennifer waited.</p>

<p>One second. Two. Three.</p>

<p>Nothing.</p>

<p>No footsteps. No voice calling out. No sound of movement at all.</p>

<p>Her impatience flared.</p>

<p>She pressed the bell again, harder this time, holding it down for a full second.</p>

<p>Still nothing.</p>

<p>"Mother?" she called, her voice sharp. "I know you’re in there. Open the door."</p>

<p>Silence.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s jaw tightened. She reached for the handle... half expecting it to be locked, half planning to pound on the door until someone answered.</p>

<p>The handle turned.</p>

<p>Unlocked.</p>

<p>She hesitated for half a breath, that small voice in the back of her mind whispering again that something about this was wrong.</p>

<p>Then she pushed the thought away and stepped inside.</p>

<p>The interior of Villa Six was warm. Soft lighting. Polished floors. Everything pristine and expensive in that effortless way that only truly wealthy spaces managed.</p>

<p>But it was also empty.</p>

<p>Completely, utterly empty.</p>

<p>"Hello?" Jennifer’s voice echoed slightly in the open space.</p>

<p>No response.</p>

<p>She moved further inside, her heels clicking against the hardwood as she passed through the entryway into the main living area.</p>

<p>The villa was beautiful... floor-to-ceiling windows offering a view of the dark lake beyond, minimalist furniture arranged with designer precision, abstract art on the walls that probably cost more than most people’s cars.</p>

<p>But something about it felt... off.</p>

<p>Not abandoned. Not neglected.</p>

<p>Waiting.</p>

<p>The air itself seemed to hum with anticipation, as though the villa had been holding its breath for someone to arrive.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s unease deepened.</p>

<p>She moved through the living room toward what looked like a dining area. A long table.</p>

<p>Eight chairs. A chandelier overhead that cast soft, golden light across the polished surface.</p>

<p>Beyond that, she could see stairs leading to the second floor.</p>

<p>And that’s when she noticed it.</p>

<p>Flowers.</p>

<p>Fresh flowers arranged in elegant vases on the dining table. On the sideboard. On the small table at the base of the stairs.</p>

<p>Not just flowers... roses. White and red, their petals perfect, their scent subtle but unmistakable.</p>

<p>Jennifer stopped.</p>

<p>Her mother hated roses. Had always hated them. Too cliché, she’d said. Too obvious.</p>

<p>So why...</p>

<p>A faint sound made her freeze.</p>

<p>Footsteps.</p>

<p>Soft. Measured. Coming from upstairs.</p>

<p>The click of heels against hardwood, descending slowly, deliberately.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s pulse jumped. She turned toward the staircase, her breath catching.</p>

<p>A figure appeared at the top of the stairs.</p>

<p>A woman.</p>

<p>Tall. Elegant. Moving with the kind of poise that spoke of years of high-society training.</p>

<p>She wore a tailored dress in deep emerald that hugged her frame perfectly, her dark hair swept up in a style that was both sophisticated and effortless.</p>

<p>She was beautiful.</p>

<p>And she was smiling.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s mind struggled to place the face for half a second before recognition clicked into place.</p>

<p>"Helena?"</p>

<p>Her aunt descended the last few steps with unhurried grace, her smile widening slightly as she reached the ground floor.</p>

<p>"Jennifer," Helena said warmly, as though this were a planned family visit and not a late-night confrontation in an isolated villa. "What a lovely surprise. I didn’t expect to see you tonight."</p>

<p>Jennifer blinked, her confusion sharpening into irritation.</p>

<p>"What are you doing here?" she demanded, her voice harder than she’d intended.</p>

<p>"Where’s my mother?"</p>
