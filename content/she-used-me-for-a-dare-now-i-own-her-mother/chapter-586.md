<p>The sloped aisle of Auditorium Three was dimly lit by low amber floor lamps, guiding them upward into the quiet cavern of the theater.</p>

<p>Victoria followed Alex past a few seated patrons, offering soft, polite apologies as they squeezed through the narrow gap to the center of Row H.</p>

<p>Settling in, Liam took the far seat, the younger Victoria sat beside him, Victoria slid into the tenth seat, and Alex took the end of their cluster.</p>

<p>The vintage seats were plush and deep, upholstered in faded crimson velvet that absorbed the sound around them. Victoria settled the Colossus Tub carefully onto her lap, looking down at the mountainous heap of golden popcorn with quiet amusement.</p>

<p>Before she could search for an awkward way to balance her drink, Alex reached across and flipped up the padded armrest between their seats. With a smooth, practiced motion, he slid closer, pulling her into his side until her shoulder rested comfortably against his chest.</p>

<p>Victoria let out a soft breath, a faint smile curving her lips as she leaned into his warmth.</p>

<p>For someone who was accustomed to private leather recliners separated by wide conference consoles, having their hips and shoulders pressed together in a crowded public auditorium felt startlingly intimate.</p>

<p>Above them, the ambient overhead lights began to dim, sinking the auditorium into velvety darkness until only the cool, shifting luminescence of the silver screen illuminated the room.</p>

<p>The previews rolled, filling the hall with booming surround sound.</p>

<p>As trailers for upcoming blockbusters flashed across the screen, Victoria reached into the tub for a handful of popcorn. At the exact same second, Alex reached down as well, his fingers brushing against hers in the warm, butter-scented depths of the cardboard bucket.</p>

<p>Victoria didn’t pull away. Instead, she turned her head slightly, her dark eyes catching the shifting blue glow of the projector as she smiled up at him. Picking a single, perfectly glazed kernel from the pile, she lifted it to his lips.</p>

<p>Alex caught it with his teeth, his thumb lightly stroking the side of her wrist before resting his hand over hers on the velvet seat.</p>

<p>“Not bad for three pounds of butter,” Alex murmured close to her ear, his breath tickling the skin just beneath her jaw.</p>

<p>“It is entirely excessive,” Victoria whispered back, her tone laced with quiet delight. “And completely delicious.”</p>

<p>A moment later, the Grandview’s vintage curtain graphics faded, and the opening chords of Accidentally Yours filled the auditorium.</p>

<p>The story was classic, unapologetic romantic comedy fare. Callum Hayes played a tightly wound logistics director whose meticulously planned itinerary unravels in an airport transit hub, while Vera Laurent played a spontaneous, chaotic travel photographer whose misplaced luggage forces them into an uneasy partnership.</p>

<p>Within twenty minutes, the entire room was chuckling at the rapid-fire banter and absurd timing.</p>

<p>Yet as the second-act complication arrived — a massive, contrived misunderstanding involving a lost passport and an overheard snippet of a phone call — Victoria couldn’t entirely silence decades of executive habit.</p>

<p>Leaning close enough that her lips brushed the shell of Alex’s ear, she whispered in a deadpan, clinical murmur, “A fifteen-second direct phone call and an expedited courier request would resolve this entire dilemma. Why is she packing a carry-on and boarding a flight to Rome?”</p>

<p>Alex muffled a laugh against the back of his hand, his shoulders shaking with amusement.</p>

<p>He turned his head, whispering back into her dark hair, “Because if they acted with sound corporate logic, the movie would be over in twenty minutes, Madame. Let them suffer for our entertainment.”</p>

<p>Victoria looked back at the screen, biting down on her lower lip to suppress a genuine, bubbling laugh.</p>

<p>“You’re right,” she murmured, resting her temple against his shoulder. “No logic today.”</p>

<p>She stopped forecasting outcomes, stopped evaluating efficiency, and simply allowed herself to be swept along by the story.</p>

<p>When Vera Laurent delivered a blistering, fast-paced monologue in the middle of a crowded Italian train station, Victoria found herself laughing aloud along with the rest of the audience.</p>

<p>From that moment on, all lingering detachment completely dissolved.</p>

<p>Victoria leaned forward in her seat, popcorn forgotten in her hand, her eyes locked onto the screen with rapt, breathless focus. Whenever the script threatened to push the two leads apart, a quiet tension crept into her shoulders; whenever they shared a stolen glance on the platform, a soft, involuntary warmth softened her face.</p>

<p>The woman who could dismantle a hostile corporate takeover without blinking was genuinely, desperately rooting for two fictional strangers to bridge the gap and stay together.</p>

<p>Alex barely watched the movie. Instead, he kept his gaze on the woman beside him.</p>

<p>The shifting silver and indigo light of the projector traced the slope of her profile, illuminating a vulnerability he had rarely seen under the daylight of the real world. Her guard was entirely down.</p>

<p>Glancing just past her shoulder, Alex caught sight of the younger Victoria sitting two seats down.</p>

<p>The visual parallel was striking. Both women were leaned forward at the exact same angle, lips parted in identical suspense, chin tilted toward the screen with the exact same earnest hope.</p>

<p>In the dim, flickering gloom of the theater, it looked as though time itself had folded in on itself… placing the carefree youth Victoria Blackwood had been denied right beside the woman who was finally, beautifully, reclaiming it.</p>

<p>By the time the third act arrived with its sweeping orchestral crescendo and the inevitable, rain-soaked airport confession, Liam was rolling his eyes while the younger Victoria dabbed at the corner of her eye with a crumpled paper napkin.</p>

<p>Victoria didn’t cry, but her fingers tightened around Alex’s hand, lacing their fingers together in an unbreakable, quiet grip.</p>

<p>On screen, Callum Hayes caught Vera Laurent just before the departure gate closed, offering a declaration that was cheesy, improbable, and utterly sincere.</p>

<p>As the credits began to roll against an upbeat pop ballad, the entire theater broke into spontaneous, warm applause.</p>

<p>Caught in the exact same rush of excitement, both Victorias sprang to their feet in near-perfect unison, clapping enthusiastically along with the crowd with pure, uninhibited joy.</p>

<p>Beside them, Alex and Liam rose as well, sharing an amused, knowing chuckle as they joined the applause. Alex kept his eyes entirely on Victoria, captivated by the flushed, brilliant happiness lighting up her face.</p>

<p>Slowly, the golden wall sconces flickered to life, warming the theater back into the real world as the auditorium settled into a satisfied rustle of coats, bags, and departing chatter.</p>

<p>Looking down at their seats, the once-towering Colossus Tub was miraculously almost empty, with only a small scattering of unpopped kernels rattling at the bottom. Both of them had glossy, salt-dusted fingers, and their giant fountain drinks were drained down to the melting ice.</p>

<p>Liam stretched his arms over his head with a groan, grinning across at them. “Alright, I admit it. The airport scene wasn’t terrible.”</p>

<p>“It was perfection, and you know it,” the younger Victoria said, brushing popcorn crumbs off her jeans before beaming at Victoria. “Claudia, tell him! Didn’t Hayes totally nail the speech?”</p>

<p>“He was completely captivating,” Victoria agreed with an effortless smile, smoothing down the front of her knit cardigan before stepping into the aisle. “When he dropped everything just to reach her before the gate closed… it was wonderfully moving. I didn’t want it to end.”</p>

<p>“Right?!” the younger girl laughed, nudging Liam with pure vindication. “See, Liam? That is what a real grand gesture looks like!”</p>

<p>Liam held his hands up in defeat, chuckling as he fell in beside them.</p>

<p>Chatting and comparing favorite scenes, the four of them followed the slow trickle of patrons down the aisle, through the exit doors, and back into the Grandview’s bustling main lobby.</p>

<p>Outside Auditorium Three, Liam checked his phone, offering an apologetic grimace.</p>

<p>”We’ve got to run to catch the cross-town bus before five,” Liam said, extending a hand to Alex. “Man, it was awesome meeting you both. Seriously made the afternoon ten times more fun.”</p>

<p>Alex shook his hand with an easy grin. “Same here, Liam. Take care.”</p>

<p>The younger Victoria gave Claudia a quick, warm squeeze of the arm. “I still can’t believe you two finished almost that entire tub! You guys have the best date, okay?”</p>

<p>”We will,” Victoria answered warmly, watching the young couple hurry toward the front doors with genuine fondness in her eyes. “Thank you.”</p>

<p>Once the pair had vanished into the lobby crowd, Alex turned to Victoria, his eyes dancing with quiet amusement as he looked down at the comically oversized, near-empty cardboard cylinder she was still holding like a prized possession.</p>

<p>”Well, Claudia,” Alex said softly, offering his arm. “Shall we?”</p>

<p>Victoria hooked her arm through his, leaning her weight lightly against his side. “We shall.”</p>

<p>Together, they began navigating their way across the grand art deco lobby toward the exit. The theater had grown considerably more crowded; the evening rush was already pouring in, with lines for the upcoming six o’clock show stretching past the velvet ropes and spilling across the marble floor.</p>

<p>“How was it?” Alex asked quietly, glancing down at her as they guided their way through the bustling crowd toward the doors.</p>

<p>Victoria looked over at him, her dark eyes shimmering with an emotion far deeper than the story they had just watched on screen.</p>

<p>“It was the best movie I have ever seen,” she said softly, her fingers tightening around his arm.</p>

<p>“Every single second of it.”</p>
