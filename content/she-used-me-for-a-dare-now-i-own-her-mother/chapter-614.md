<p>The towering tinted glass doors of 14 Central Corridor parted with a quiet hiss, spilling Alex back out into the bright morning sun.</p>

<p>A cool, steady breeze swept across the open plaza, carrying the distant hum of morning traffic and the clean scent of the manicured greenery lining the boulevard.</p>

<p>It was a sharp, grounding contrast to the metallic stench of opened veins and the suffocating heat of the executive office forty-eight floors above.</p>

<p>Alex rolled his shoulders, tossing his dark coat casually over one arm while the sleek black leather portfolio containing the building’s master deeds rested effortlessly in his grip. His dress shirt was unbuttoned at the collar, his steps unhurried, radiating the lazy satisfaction of a man who had just concluded an exceptionally pleasant business meeting.</p>

<p>Yet beneath his calm exterior, his senses expanded across the entire perimeter.</p>

<p>Almost instantly, his heightened perception picked up dozens of subtle shifts in the surrounding plaza.</p>

<p>Across the street, a man in an ordinary postal uniform suddenly froze mid-stride, his clipboard slipping an inch in his grip.</p>

<p>Inside an unmarked silver sedan parked along the eastern curb, a tinted window cracked open, a pair of wide, dilated eyes staring through high-grade surveillance binoculars.</p>

<p>Even on the stone benches near the central fountain, a well-dressed "executive" holding a paper coffee cup completely forgot to take a sip, his gaze locked onto the main entrance in naked, vein-popping disbelief.</p>

<p>None of them were civilians.</p>

<p>They were the eyes and ears of the district’s prominent syndicates and mid-tier families, stationed like vultures to witness the inevitable.</p>

<p>Everyone in the city’s underworld knew the Mercer family had staked their claim on Raymond’s commercial sector. They knew Karl Shaw’s black-ops breach team had slipped through the freight docks an hour ago, fully equipped to erase an upstart outsider from the board.</p>

<p>Every watcher in this plaza had been waiting to see a clean body bag carried out through the service exits, or a team of Mercer enforcers walking out victorious.</p>

<p>Instead, the only person stepping out into the morning light was Alexander Hale. An unknown outsider whom none of the ruling families had even bothered to catalog on their threat assessments just days ago.</p>

<p>?Yet here he was.</p>

<p>?Untouched. Unbothered. Without a single scratch on his skin or a speck of dust on his polished shoes.</p>

<p>Alex felt the silent tremor of panic rolling through the plaza. In less than three minutes, encrypted comms and emergency reports would be flooding into the executive penthouses and clan mansions across the district. The unknown variable hadn’t just survived the Mercers’ lethal trap — he had walked out looking like he owned the entire block.</p>

<p>A faint, genuinely amused smirk touched the corner of Alex’s lips.</p>

<p>Why bother feeling annoyed by an audience when they were providing the finest public relations service money could buy?</p>

<p>In a single morning, without spending a dime on marketing or making a single public declaration, these frantic spies would broadcast his presence to every ruling faction in the metropolis.</p>

<p>By noon, every boss, patriarch, and elder in the underworld would know that an unknown youth had walked into Raymond Cole’s prize tower, swallowed an elite Mercer kill-team whole, and strolled back out into the sun without breaking a sweat.</p>

<p>Free advertising, delivered with genuine terror by the very hyenas waiting to pick his bones. He couldn’t have planned a better launch for Eclipse Holdings if he tried.</p>

<p>Chuckling softly to himself, Alex made his way across the wide stone courtyard toward his sedan, parked in the reserved executive bay near the curb.</p>

<p>He tapped the door handle, letting the locks click open with a quiet chirp. Tossing his dark coat into the rear cabin, he flicked his wrist and casually threw the thick leather portfolio — the pristine, multi-million-dollar master deed to 14 Central Corridor — onto the passenger seat like a bundle of morning newspapers.</p>

<p>Slipping into the driver’s seat, Alex pulled the door shut, instantly sealing out the distant street noise and the paranoid gazes lingering around the plaza.</p>

<p>He pressed the ignition button, and the sedan’s engine came alive with a deep, muffled rumble that resonated gently through the chassis.</p>

<p>Reaching over to the high-definition console display, he swiped through the district map and locked in his coordinates: The Valenti Estate, South Crest Heights.</p>

<p>One objective was secured, but the board was just starting to heat up.</p>

<p>With thirty days on the System clock to bring the three circling wolves to heel, it was time to pay a little house call to a dying old tiger.</p>

<p>Alex shifted into gear, eased his foot onto the accelerator, and smoothly steered the car into the flow of the late morning traffic.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>The sharp, hurried click of high heels echoed down the quiet corridors of the Valenti estate.</p>

<p>Bianca moved swiftly through the east wing, clutching a secure digital tablet tightly against her ribs. Her tailored navy suit and pulled-back auburn hair looked neat and composed on the surface, but the slight flush on her cheeks and the quickened pace of her stride betrayed the urgency surging through her veins.</p>

<p>At the end of the long gallery stood a set of reinforced double doors forged from matte black steel, guarded by four imposing sentries in dark tactical gear. Each man was a vetted clan loyalist, their hands resting lightly on the grips of their holstered weapons as their sharp eyes swept the hallway.</p>

<p>The moment they recognized Bianca, the guards snapped their boots together in unison, lowering their heads with deep respect.</p>

<p>"Young Miss," the lead sentry called out, stepping aside to key open the biometric lock.</p>

<p>"Is he awake?" Bianca asked, barely slowing down as the heavy doors hissed open.</p>

<p>"The Patriarch has been awake for an hour," the guard replied softly. "His breathing was rough this morning, but he refused his sedatives."</p>

<p>Bianca offered a stiff nod and slipped through the threshold, letting the heavy doors seal the outside world behind her.</p>

<p>The air inside the private medical suite was thick and suffocating, heavy with the sharp, bitter tang of boiled medicinal roots, antiseptic sprays, and the faint, unmistakable trace of decaying vitality.</p>

<p>Sunlight struggled to pierce the semi-closed wooden louvers, casting long, somber shadows across the sterile room and the various heart monitors humming quietly in the corner.</p>

<p>In the center of the chamber, propped up against a mountain of white pillows on a wide medical bed, lay Vincent Valenti.</p>

<p>To the outside world, Vincent was still the formidable patriarch whose word could shake half the commercial district. But stripped of his public aura, the old man looked painfully frail. His silver hair hung thin and limp, his weathered cheeks were deeply hollowed, and his breathing was shallow, whistling faintly through his chest with every intake of air.</p>

<p>Yet, despite the gray pallor of his skin and the IV lines taped to his mottled wrists, his sunken dark eyes still carried the razor-sharp glint of a wounded tiger.</p>

<p>Hearing the rustle of fabric, Vincent slowly turned his head toward the door.</p>

<p>"You’re walking too fast, Bianca," Vincent rasped, his voice rough and gravelly, like stones grinding against each other. "A leader of the Valenti clan does not rush like a startled deer."</p>

<p>"Father," Bianca said softly, the tension in her shoulders easing slightly as she approached the bedside. She pulled up a wooden chair, sitting close enough to see the deep fatigue etched into his brow. "You should be resting, but this couldn’t wait. The watchers we stationed outside 14 Central Corridor just phoned in an emergency report."</p>

<p>Vincent’s brow twitched faintly. He placed a trembling, veiny hand against the sheets, his fingers curling slightly.</p>

<p>"The commercial block," the old man murmured, his gaze steadying on his daughter. "The Mercers made their move on Raymond’s buyer?"</p>

<p>"They did," Bianca said, unable to hide the faint, eager spark of excitement lighting up her hazel eyes. "Karl Shaw led an elite six-man breach team through the service docks early this morning. They were fully armed, and our people confirmed a high-caliber sniper had taken up an overwatch perch on the adjacent rooftop. By all accounts, it was a textbook Mercer execution. Everyone in the district expected Raymond’s young buyer to leave the building in a body bag."</p>

<p>She paused, taking a breath to steady her own racing thoughts before leaning in closer.</p>

<p>"Except he didn’t."</p>

<p>Vincent’s eyes narrowed slightly, the dull fatigue in his expression replaced by acute, calculated focus. "Go on."</p>

<p>"Less than ten minutes ago, the front doors spun open," Bianca continued, her voice dropping into a breathless murmur. "Alexander Hale walked out into the open plaza alone. Untouched. Not a single drop of blood on his shirt, not a scratch on his face, looking as relaxed as a man stepping out from a casual breakfast. And of Karl Shaw’s team? Not a single operative has come down. The entire strike unit went completely dark the second they reached the executive floor."</p>

<p>A rare, triumphant smile touched Bianca’s lips as she placed her tablet on the side table.</p>
