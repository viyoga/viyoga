<p>"The property was purchased by Vivienne Vanderbilt."</p>

<p>The name landed with weight.</p>

<p>Alex’s expression didn’t change, but his mind was already processing. Vanderbilt. He knew that name. How can he not.</p>

<p>Vivienne Vanderbilt... mother of Jennifer.</p>

<p>A dozen tiny things rearranged in Alex’s chest the moment Caroline said the name. And suddenly the purchase was no longer a neutral real‑estate blip but a deliberate move on someone who lived inside the very world that had humiliated him.</p>

<p>Old memories rose slick and immediate: the ballroom laughter, the phone screens, the scoring, the way Jennifer’s voice had counted him like a lesson.</p>

<p>Jennifer had been a mouthpiece in that cruel theater; she had laughed while he bled. He let the thought sit, cold and deliberate:</p>

<p>"Jennifer—your turn will come. Soon, very soon, you’ll understand what happens when games are played with the wrong player."</p>

<p>Caroline continued, reading his lack of recognition for what it was. "Ms. Vivienne is the CEO of Vanderbilt Media Group. She owns three major digital news platforms, two national magazines, and has controlling interest in several regional broadcast networks."</p>

<p>Alex kept his face neutral, but internally he was recalculating. A media mogul. Someone with the kind of reach that could shape narratives, expose secrets, build or destroy reputations with a few strategic stories.</p>

<p>Moving in next door.</p>

<p>"The purchase was handled through a corporate entity," Edmund added. "Standard practice for someone of her profile. But the transaction was... decisive. Full asking price, no inspection contingencies, no negotiation. She wanted the property, and she acquired it within forty-eight hours of expressing interest."</p>

<p>Alex took a slow sip of his coffee, using the moment to think. This wasn’t random. Properties at this level didn’t sell on impulse. Someone like Vivienne Vanderbilt didn’t just stumble into a neighborhood.</p>

<p>She’d chosen this location deliberately.</p>

<p>The question was: why?</p>

<p>Was it coincidence? An opportunistic acquisition? Or was it planned... a planted sentinel, a message, a family mapping the neighborhood as easily as they mapped influence? He couldn’t tell.</p>

<p>The uncertainty prickled at him like cold iron. If Vivienne had arrived on purpose, the meaning rippled in ways he hadn’t yet parsed; if she hadn’t, then why here, why now? Either possibility demanded answers.</p>

<p>He felt anger, yes... old wounds reopening... but colder and sharper beneath it was calculation.</p>

<p>Panic had been useful to them once; it would not be useful now. He checked himself: steady breath, measured face, no loose reaction to feed their gossip mills.</p>

<p>He would learn why she was here, and he would learn how to use it. The thought of exposure now belonged to him, not them.</p>

<p>A slow, dark idea unfurled at the edge of his thinking, not a scream but a quiet, inevitable plan. If the Vanderbilts... or whoever pulled their strings... wanted to play a game, he would change the rules.</p>

<p>"When did she close again?" Alex asked.</p>

<p>"Fifteen days ago," Edmund confirmed. "The timing is... interesting, I’ll admit. It’s possible she heard through industry channels about the sale and decided to investigate the area."</p>

<p>Or she heard about me, Alex thought.</p>

<p>"Has she moved in?" Alex asked.</p>

<p>Edmund shook his head. "Not yet. The property is being renovated currently... security upgrades, primarily. Her team has been very specific about privacy requirements."</p>

<p>Of course she has.</p>

<p>Alex set down his coffee cup and met Edmund’s eyes directly. "I still want the other five properties. This doesn’t change anything."</p>

<p>Edmund, sensing Alex’s calculations, softened his tone into alliance. "If you’re taking the other five, Mr. Hale, I will personally oversee the closings. Discretion, logistics, everything. And if you wish..." he leaned in just enough, "...I can introduce you to certain contacts. It’s beneficial to have the right introductions in place when a name like Vandebilt is in the orbit."</p>

<p>Alex let a slow smile form, small and precise. Edmund wanted connection; Edmund wanted to be useful. That could be arranged.</p>

<p>"I’ll ask if needed," Alex said. "Prepare the contracts. I’ll want pre‑approval papers ready, and I want a schedule for inspection and closing."</p>

<p>Edmund’s relief was almost audible. He reached for a tablet, already pulling up checklists.</p>

<p>"Perfect. I’ll have Caroline coordinate everything. Mr. Hale, this is an exceptional decision... securing five of six will..."</p>

<p>"Secure privacy," Alex finished. "Control." His gaze moved to Caroline for a beat; she met it with professional composure and a nod. Edmund will be eager to earn his place. Useful, he thought. For now.</p>

<p>Alex leaned back slightly, letting the weight of his decision settle in the room. Then, with calm precision, he spoke.</p>

<p>"Edmund," he said, voice even, controlled. "I’ll need keys to one of the villas. For personal review. That should be sufficient for now."</p>

<p>Edmund blinked, a flicker of surprise crossing his features before he masked it with professional composure. "Of course, Mr. Hale. Which property would you like access to?"</p>

<p>"The one closest to the center of the development," Alex replied without hesitation. "The vantage point matters."</p>

<p>Edmund nodded, already tapping on his tablet. "Consider it done. Caroline will prepare the keys immediately. Security protocols will be maintained, naturally."</p>

<p>A soft click of the office door signaled Caroline’s approach. She handed the keys over with practiced efficiency, her expression neutral, but Alex noticed the slight tightening of her jaw... a small, human acknowledgment of the weight of the transaction.</p>

<p>He took the keys in one hand, weighing them lightly, feeling the cold metal press against his fingers. It was more than access... it was leverage, a foothold, a move in a game only he could see.</p>

<p>"Thank you," he said simply, pocketing the keys. The motion was casual, but deliberate. He could already see the first steps of control taking shape, one villa at a time.</p>

<p>Outside, the city moved on... unaware of the small shift in ownership that would rearrange streets and loyalties. Inside the office, Edmund’s enthusiasm became industrious energy; Caroline moved like a trained instrument, notes and schedules materializing.</p>

<p>"Make it clean," he told Edmund quietly. "No leaks. No surprises."</p>

<p>Edmund bowed his head slightly, the consummate professional. "You’ll have nothing but discretion, Mr. Hale."</p>

<p>Alex leaned back, the chair supporting him like a throne. Five villas, he thought. One wildcard. A media owner. Interesting. Lilith’s voice whispered approval at the edges of his thoughts, but he kept his face still.</p>

<p>"Good," he said aloud. "Begin."</p>

<p>Outside, from where he had imagined his compound, the ring of villas waited... some soon to be empty of strangers, some to be occupied by measures of influence.</p>

<p>For Alex, the city was already rearranging itself around an intent. He liked the way that felt.</p>
