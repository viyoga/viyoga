<p>Next Day – Afternoon</p>

<p>The floor-to-ceiling glass of the Vanderbilt Tower turned the city below into a sprawling circuit board of gray steel and crawling traffic.</p>

<p>From the forty-fifth floor, the people were invisible, the noise non-existent.</p>

<p>There was only power.</p>

<p>Jennifer Vanderbilt leaned back in her ergonomic leather chair, her heels resting brazenly on the corner of the mahogany desk.</p>

<p>It was Saturday afternoon... a time when most of her peers at Blackwood University were nursing hangovers or wasting their parents’ money in boutiques.</p>

<p>But Jennifer was here.</p>

<p>"Let them play," she murmured to the empty, pristine office, spinning the heavy pen between her fingers.</p>

<p>"I have an empire to run."</p>

<p>She swiveled the chair slightly, admiring the way the afternoon sun caught the gold lettering on the door.</p>

<p>Jennifer Vanderbilt – Director of Special Projects.</p>

<p>It wasn’t the CEO title. Not yet. But it was a start. It was a foothold. And most importantly, it was something her mother had sworn she wasn’t ready for.</p>

<p>"It’s not just a company, Darling. It’s a battlefield. You need to learn how to spot a trap before you step in it. You need to see how people smile while they’re measuring your back for a knife. Until you understand that everyone is trying to use you, you aren’t ready to lead them."</p>

<p>Vivienne’s voice echoed in her memory, dripping with that infuriating, condescending patience. Her mother treated the family legacy like a sacred artifact that Jennifer was too clumsy to hold.</p>

<p>"Paranoia," Jennifer murmured to the empty office, dismissing her mother’s wisdom with a roll of her eyes. "She just didn’t want to share the spotlight."</p>

<p>Her uncles, Reginald and Arthur, understood that. They saw her potential.</p>

<p>But it was Cassandra who truly understood.</p>

<p>When Jennifer had gone to her, chafing under Vivienne’s suffocating restrictions, her aunt hadn’t lectured her about patience or politics. She hadn’t told her to wait her turn.</p>

<p>She had handed her a kingdom.</p>

<p>It was Cassandra who had fought for this office. It was Cassandra who had signed off on the budget. It was Cassandra who had pulled out a chair at the executive table and told Jennifer to sit, not as an intern, but as an equal.</p>

<p>"Don’t stress yourself too much, darling," Cassandra had told her just yesterday, squeezing her shoulder with a warmth that felt like salvation. "We need you fresh. The future of this family is heavy, and we need someone strong enough to carry it when your mother... steps back."</p>

<p>Jennifer smiled at the memory, tracing the gold lettering on her nameplate.</p>

<p>They knew that the future of the Vanderbilt name rested on her shoulders, not on her mother’s fading relevance.</p>

<p>Her thoughts drifted, as they often did lately, to her competition.</p>

<p>Sophia Blackwood.</p>

<p>A dark, satisfied laugh bubbled up in Jennifer’s throat.</p>

<p>For years, Sophia had walked through the university halls like she owned the very air they breathed. High and mighty. The Ice Queen. Always acting as if the Blackwood name made her untouchable, as if she were destined for greatness while everyone else was just extras in her movie.</p>

<p>"And look at you now, Sophia," Jennifer whispered to the glass skyline.</p>

<p>Rumors were circulating. Whispers that Sophia had no real authority within her family. That she was being sidelined. That while Jennifer was sitting in a corner office on the forty-fifth floor, making executive decisions, Sophia was still just a student with a credit card.</p>

<p>"I beat you," Jennifer said, the words tasting sweet. "I’m the first. The first to ascend. The first to matter."</p>

<p>She tapped the pen against her lip, her mind racing forward.</p>

<p>Graduation was close. Exams were coming up, mere formalities at this point. Once she secured that piece of paper, the "Director" title would just be a stepping stone.</p>

<p>A few years of experience... real, high-level experience, not the mailroom nonsense her mother wanted... and she would be undeniable.</p>

<p>She would inherit it all.</p>

<p>The media arm. The real estate holdings. The political influence.</p>

<p>Her mother refused to hand it over? Fine.</p>

<p>Jennifer would simply take it, piece by piece, with her uncles’ help. She would prove that the daughter was the true lioness of the family.</p>

<p>She closed her eyes, letting the fantasy wash over her. She could see it... the board meetings, the magazine covers, the way men would look at her with fear and desire, the way Sophia Blackwood would have to make an appointment just to speak to her.</p>

<p>It was perfect. It was inevitable.</p>

<p>Click. Click. Click.</p>

<p>The sound was sharp, rhythmic, and deliberate.</p>

<p>Jennifer’s eyes snapped open.</p>

<p>The sound of heels on marble. Coming from the outer corridor.</p>

<p>She frowned, dropping her feet from the desk and sitting up straighter.</p>

<p>It was Saturday. The executive floor was supposed to be empty, save for security. Her uncles were gone. Her mother was... well, wherever her mother disappeared to these days.</p>

<p>The clicking stopped right outside the heavy glass doors.</p>

<p>A shadow fell across the frosted glass. Then, a knuckle rapped gently against the pane.</p>

<p>"Knock, knock."</p>

<p>The voice was melodic, teasing, and instantly recognizable.</p>

<p>"Is the Madam Director accepting visitors? Or do I need to make an appointment with your secretary?"</p>

<p>Jennifer’s shoulders dropped instantly, the tension bleeding out of her frame. A genuine, bright smile broke through her executive mask.</p>

<p>"Aunt Cassandra!"</p>

<p>She stood up, gesturing to the open room.</p>

<p>"Since when do you need an appointment? You know you never need permission to enter."</p>

<p>The door pushed open, and Cassandra Vanderbilt glided in.</p>

<p>She looked radiant. Her eyes were crinkled at the corners with amusement, her lips curved in a soft, affectionate smile that lit up the room.</p>

<p>"One can never be too careful," Cassandra purred, closing the door behind her with a soft, deliberate click. "Protocols are important, aren’t they? Isn’t that what your mother always says?"</p>

<p>Jennifer rolled her eyes, walking around the massive mahogany desk to meet her.</p>

<p>"Please, don’t ruin the mood. I come here to escape her lectures, not relive them."</p>

<p>Cassandra laughed... a warm, throaty sound that filled the sterile office with life. She closed the distance between them, invading Jennifer’s personal space with the ease of a second mother.</p>

<p>Cassandra reached out, her hands landing softly on Jennifer’s shoulders. She turned her niece slightly, catching the afternoon light.</p>

<p>"Look at you," Cassandra whispered, her eyes gleaming with a mixture of pride and something sharper, something possessive.</p>

<p>"You look... powerful. Like you were born to sit in that chair."</p>

<p>She reached up, her manicured fingers brushing against Jennifer’s neck as she adjusted the collar of her blazer. She smoothed the lapel, picking off a speck of invisible lint, her touch lingering just a second too long. It was a grooming gesture... intimate, motherly, but with the subtle weight of ownership.</p>

<p>"So?" Cassandra asked, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper. "How does it feel?"</p>

<p>She gestured to the room, to the view, to the nameplate on the desk.</p>

<p>"The chair. The view. The silence of the forty-fifth floor." She looked Jennifer deep in the eyes. "Does it fit you?"</p>

<p>Jennifer took a deep breath. Her aunt’s jasmine perfume wrapped around her, sweet and overwhelming, the way affection should feel.</p>

<p>She looked back at the skyline, her chest swelling with validation.</p>

<p>"It feels..." Jennifer paused, searching for the word, and then grinned, fierce and arrogant. "It feels right, Auntie. It feels like I was finally allowed to breathe."</p>

<p>"Good," Cassandra said, her smile widening. "Because you look like a Queen sitting up here."</p>

<p>She smoothed Jennifer’s collar one last time, her hands resting on Jennifer’s shoulders, warm and reassuring.</p>

<p>"And Queens..." Cassandra said softly, "deserve to have everything they want."</p>
