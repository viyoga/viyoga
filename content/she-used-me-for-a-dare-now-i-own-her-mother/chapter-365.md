<p>The air in the Main Hall had shifted. The frantic, mid-afternoon peak had bled into a steady, low hum as the sun began to dip lower behind the arched windows.</p>

<p>Alex moved through the aisles with a measured, effortless stride, his skin cooled by a quick wash and his clothes straightened into a mask of casual academic focus.</p>

<p>He spotted them in the same place where he had first seen them upon entering the hall.</p>

<p>Mike and Sarah were hunched over their thick books, buried deep in their studies.</p>

<p>Danny, however, was slumped in his chair, his bored gaze wandering the room until it snagged on Alex.</p>

<p>The change was instantaneous... he practically jolted upright.</p>

<p>"Hey, you finally here," Danny whispered, his voice thick with a genuine, desperate sort of relief. "I seriously thought you were never going to make it."</p>

<p>Alex offered a slow, easy smirk as he pulled out the chair beside him and sat down.</p>

<p>"I got caught up in something in the back. Lost track of the hour."</p>

<p>Mike didn’t even look up from his thick books.</p>

<p>"Do you realize how difficult it is to reserve an extra seat in this rush, Alex? You’re lucky I didn’t auction this spot to a passing freshman."</p>

<p>"I’ll buy the next round of coffee," Alex offered with a small, easy smirk. "Consider it a peace offering."</p>

<p>"Make it double shots and we’re square," Mike grunted, finally marking his page and looking up.</p>

<p>"Alex, You missed something big. " Danny leaned in, his voice dropping into a conspiratorial whisper, his boredom replaced by the spark of campus gossip.</p>

<p>"I saw Sophia Blackwood marching out of here about twenty minutes ago looking like she was ready to declare war. I’ve seen her angry, but this was different. She looked like she’d just seen a ghost."</p>

<p>Alex leaned back, a faint, unreadable smile touching his lips. He didn’t offer a word of explanation; he simply watched the dust motes dance in the light.</p>

<p>"Something interestingmust have happened," Danny continued, eyes wide. "The vibe in here shifted the second she bolted. You didn’t see anything?"</p>

<p>Before Alex could respond, Sarah leaned over and jabbed Danny sharply in the side with her elbow.</p>

<p>"Ow! What was that for?" Danny yelped, clutching his side where her elbow had struck.</p>

<p>"For being a nosy brat," Sarah scolded, finally lifting her gaze to pin him with a sharp look. "You’d have noticed the answer to question four if you spent half as much time looking at your books as you do at who’s coming and going through those doors."</p>

<p>"Leave Sophia to her drama and focus on the fact that we have a final... and we can’t afford to take it lightly," she added, her eyes lingering on him a moment longer before dropping back to her textbook.</p>

<p>Danny pouted, leaning back into his chair. "I’m just saying... the energy was weird."</p>

<p>"The only thing ’weird’ is your lack of a focus," Sarah retorted.</p>

<p>Alex reached forward and opened one of the thick books already waiting for him at the table. The warmth of the group, the mundane bickering, and the safety of the bright hall felt like a different world compared to the carnage he had left behind in Row 42.</p>

<p>"She’s right, Danny," Alex said quietly, his eyes already scanning the dense lines of the text before him. "Focus on the books. Everything else is just... noise."</p>

<p>The silence at the table thickened, but this time it was productive.</p>

<p>For the next hour, the only sounds were the soft rustle of turning pages, the faint scratch of pens against paper, and the occasional quiet shuffle of chairs around the library.</p>

<p>For Alex, the material felt almost insultingly simple. Concepts settled into his mind the moment he glanced at them, clean and permanent, as if they had always belonged there.</p>

<p>Where others paused, reread, and struggled to connect the dots, he moved effortlessly, each page turning into certainty. His thoughts were sharper now... faster, deeper... like a machine tuned beyond its limits. He didn’t need revision.</p>

<p>Beside him, Danny had finally succumbed to Sarah’s scolding; he was hunched over his own book, his brow furrowed in a rare display of genuine concentration, though he still occasionally tapped a restless rhythm against the table leg.</p>

<p>As the library’s overhead lights hummed to life, signaling the transition into the evening shift, Mike finally stretched, his joints popping audibly in the quiet hall.</p>

<p>"Alright, that’s my limit for one sitting," Mike muttered, rubbing his eyes. "If I read one more sentence, my brain’s going to shut down."</p>

<p>"Cafeteria?" Danny asked, his head snapping up instantly, the promise of food jolting him awake. "I badly need coffee."</p>

<p>"Caffeine first," Sarah agreed, finally closing her book with a satisfied thud.</p>

<p>Alex closed his book with a quiet thud. He could’ve gone on... page after page, Chapter after Chapter... but there was no point. He had already taken in everything that mattered.</p>

<p>Instead, he leaned back slightly, letting his gaze drift over the table.</p>

<p>"So," he said, casual but observant, "how are you guys feeling while reading? Any difference?"</p>

<p>Sarah paused, her pen hovering mid-sentence.</p>

<p>"Yeah... actually." She frowned slightly, as if testing the thought while speaking. "The concepts feel... easier to grasp. Like they’re just clicking faster. And I can read longer without getting tired."</p>

<p>She glanced up at the others.</p>

<p>Mike gave a slow nod, stretching his neck. "Same here. I thought it was just me."</p>

<p>Danny blinked, a little surprised. "Yeah... I didn’t zone out even once. That’s... new."</p>

<p>A quiet agreement settled over the table, each of them striking upon the same truth at once.</p>

<p>Alex said nothing, his gaze lingering on them for a heartbeat. Then, he began gathering his things... calm, deliberate, as if the matter required no further discussion.</p>

<p>The others followed suit, slipping books into bags and stacking their notes in a synchronized rhythm. No one asked further questions; they didn’t need to. The answer was already there, settling quietly in their minds.</p>

<p>The library had thinned out, leaving only a scattered few hunched over books and glowing laptop screens, lost in their own worlds.</p>

<p>"Let’s go," Alex said, already stepping forward, his voice low but certain.</p>

<p>They moved through the Main Hall, their footsteps echoing faintly in the quiet as only a handful of students lingered, scattered and unhurried.</p>

<p>The shift from the dense, almost suffocating silence of the library to the more open, relaxed stillness outside felt like a release.</p>

<p>***</p>

<p>Inside, the cafeteria was a cavern of clattering trays and low-frequency chatter.</p>

<p>As they stepped through the entrance, Alex’s gaze didn’t wander; it locked onto a shadowed corner booth.</p>

<p>There, sitting in the dim light, were Madison and Emma. They weren’t eating. They weren’t talking. They were simply sitting, their eyes fixed on the door, waiting for him with a stillness that mirrored the girls he had left in the Archives.</p>

<p>’They are still here,’ Alex thought, a cold flicker of amusement passing through his mind as he remembered their earlier plea.</p>
