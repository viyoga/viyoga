<p>“Ready, aim—fire!”</p>

<p>The target at the end of the makeshift range, a stick figure drawn on a propped-up board, splintered as Lorelei hit it with a<b> </b>Mana Bolt. The Skill drilled a hole through the wood, tearing through it like it was wet paper.</p>

<p>“Bullseye!” the Hearth Spirit cheered, pumping her fist in the air. “I blasted it to pieces!"</p>

<p>Grinning, I had to agree. Mana Bolt was supposed to be a weak offensive spell, but its effects on our property were supercharged thanks to my Class. Even Lorelei’s downgraded version could easily blow apart the junk targets I assembled from dumpster waste.</p>

<p>I set up the target again, propping it against a stump. The backyard of the manor was still a mess, full of wild grass, weeds, and rotting bits of furniture we hadn’t had time to clear out yet. We had even discovered the rusty hulk of a station wagon concealed in the undergrowth.</p>

<p>Lorelei and I had cleared out a narrow strip to use for target practice. I only had one offensive Skill, but after the incident with the wannabe intruders, I had decided we’d spend an hour a day leveling it up.</p>

<p>Aiming, I fired a bolt of colorless light out of my wand.</p>

<p><b>Skill Mana Bolt has reached Lvl 2!</b></p>

<p>The top of the target exploded, shattered by the force of my Skill. I couldn’t help but grin at the destruction I had wrought.</p>

<p>“Keep it up,” I told Lorelei before heading back toward the front of the house. “I’m going to start setting up those signs and mapping the edge of our property.”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit waved happily before turning back to the firing range. I heard another target explode as she fired off our newly improved Skill.</p>

<p>It was early July, but the air was surprisingly cool within my Lair. The temperature always seemed to be ten-degrees colder within Rotwood Manor’s grounds than the rest of Ashling Grove.</p>

<p>Heading out front, I double-checked the security lights I had rigged up with Franks’ help. They weren’t much, but if anyone tried to approach the house at night they’d be lit up. Rotwood Manor wasn’t anybody’s party house anymore.</p>

<p>I grabbed a bag I’d loaded with stakes and a pile of <em>No Trespassing </em>signs. Outside hanging the signs, I wanted to accurately mark the boundaries of my Lair.</p>

<p>Lorelei had only detected the intruders once they were close to the house. She thought, though, that if we had a clearer picture of where the Lair ended, she’d have a stronger detection range.</p>

<p>If nothing else, I’d figure out what was more accurate—my magic or the county surveyor’s office.</p>

<p>Walking up to the road, I once again felt my power slip away. I took a deep breath, centering myself, before squaring my shoulders and getting to work.</p>

<p>I quickly fell into a routine. I’d walk a few paces, then reach out with my hand till I felt the tingle of mana flowing to me from my Lair. I’d plant a stake and wrap the top with blue electrical tape, so I’d never miss it.</p>

<p>Every few stakes I’d stop, find a tree trunk, and staple up a <em>No Trespassing </em>sign. Lorelei had wanted to put up warnings that stated that <em>Trespassers Will Be Blasted</em>, but I had vetoed the idea as being too silly.</p>

<p>After an hour I’d fallen into a nice rhythm, my worries a distant buzzing in the back of my head as I focused on my work.</p>

<p>“Good morning, neighbor!”</p>

<p>Snapping my head around, I saw a man approaching.</p>

<p>He was a few years older than me. The man was dressed like a stock photo model, with beige slacks and a golf shirt. Horn rim glasses and a bad haircut completed his look. If I had to use one word to describe him, it would be <em>inoffensive</em>.</p>

<p>“Morning,” I repeated, putting away the staple gun before brushing my hands on the sides of my jeans.</p>

<p>He extended a hand. “Mitch Felch!”</p>

<p>“Brent Owens,” I replied.</p>

<p>He gave me a limp fish of a handshake. “So, you’re the guy who moved into Rotwood Manor?”</p>

<p>“Yup,” I agreed, mentally kicking myself for not introducing myself to my neighbors earlier. My mom would have chided me for being inhospitable.</p>

<p>“It’s great to meet you,” Mitch said, giving me a big smile. “Really. It’s been rough, seeing so many folks come and go over the years!”</p>

<p>“Don’t worry,” I replied, “I’m here to stay.”</p>

<p>He pointed over his shoulder at the fancy house I’d noticed butting up against my property. “That’s my place. Just me and missus. We’ve been here for years!”</p>

<p>“Great,” I replied, not sure what else to say.</p>

<p>Mitch glanced at the signs I had put up. “You—ran into some kind of trouble?”</p>

<p>“Just some idiots looking for a place to drink,” I answered, not elaborating further.</p>

<p>“Shame, shame,” Mitch clucked. “Well, let me know if you have any more problems. I’m the head of the neighborhood watch!”</p>

<p>“Neighborhood watch?” I gazed up and down the street. There were only four houses in sight, counting the goat farm on the other side of my property.</p>

<p>“Sure, neighbors should watch out for each other!” Mitch said brightly. “Say, why don’t you come over for dinner tonight, I’m sure Candy, the wife, would love to meet you!”</p>

<p>“I’m a bit busy at the moment, but maybe later this week,” I answered vaguely.</p>

<p>I don’t know why, but something about Mitch put me on edge. He seemed nice enough, but I had zero interest in dealing with the guy further. I could almost hear my mother yelling at me for being rude, but—fuck that guy and his bad haircut.</p>

<p>“Ah, too bad,” Mitch replied, his smiling dimming a bit. “Anyway, it’s really nice to meet you! I’m sure I’ll see you around!”</p>

<p>“I’m sure,” I echoed.</p>

<p>I decided I was done putting up signs on that side of my property. Moving on, I began heading toward the property line on the other side of my driveway.</p>

<p>Mitch stood there, watching me for an uncomfortable amount of time.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“We could have had dinner with the neighbors?” Lorelei pouted. “Why did you say no?”</p>

<p>I shrugged, unable to put my unease into words.</p>

<p>“Well, I guess it’s okay, it’s not like I can eat anyway,” she sighed, returning to her tablet.</p>

<p>I’d spent the rest of the day mapping out the edges of my Lair, planting dozens of stakes and using up a whole roll of electric tape. I hadn’t spoken to any more of my neighbors, but I had seen the farmer who lived on the other side of me.</p>

<p>I was once again glad that there was a thick line of forest between my house and his pasture. The entire place reeked of goat shit.</p>

<p>The farmer had ignored me, but the goats had tracked me, pacing my progress as they ran along the fence line.</p>

<p>I was starting to worry that the weirdness of Rotwood Manor extended beyond its borders.</p>

<p>The back of my property was defined by a deep, forested gorge. I guessed the creek that ran through my woods was linked to the waterway that sliced through the bottom of the canyon.</p>

<p>Worryingly, I had noticed a trail, running over a fallen tree and onto my property. The footprints around the makeshift bridge weren’t fresh, but there were <em>a lot</em> of them. Following them into the woods, I discovered a bonfire circle concealed within a ring of boulders.</p>

<p>Layers of ash and charred wood rested in the middle of the circle. The whole area had been flooded with Darkness, Fire, and Death mana.</p>

<p>It gave me the willies. The entire time I had stood there, I had felt the hairs on the back of my neck standing up.</p>

<p>Based on the litter I found, people had been using the area to party and drink for a long time. The rocks had been scratched and painted, with generations of partiers writing their initials or leaving little signs of their passing.</p>

<p>Some of them had appeared to be decades old, left as little more than faint scratches or fading paint against the granite stones.</p>

<p>I had found more Greek letters, indicating DIK had used the spot as well. More worrying, there were more Satanic symbols: bloody halos, pentagrams, and weird glyphs.</p>

<p>Marking the spot on my map, I had resolved to clean the space up, scrub the rocks, and then figure out some way of burying the whole fucking creepy-ass place.</p>

<p>Returning to the log bridge, I had manhandled the end of the fallen tree into the gorge. It had made a hell of a racket as it had fallen, but I felt better when that little path onto my property was gone.</p>

<p>“Brent? Earth to Brent?”</p>

<p>I shook myself out of the daze I had been drawn into.</p>

<p>Lorelei looked at me worriedly. “Are you okay?”</p>

<p>“Yeah, I’m fine,” I said with a sigh. “Come on, let’s get to bed. We’ve got a lot to do tomorrow.”</p>

<p>I’d emailed Ashling State’s Dungeon program, confirming they ran public delves during the weekend. It was expensive, but I was certain that I needed more power to protect what I had obtained.</p>

<p>Lorelei kissed me on the cheek. “Good night, Brent.”</p>

<p>Still feeling the chill of her lips on my cheek, I went into my tent and quickly fell into a pleasant slumber.</p>
