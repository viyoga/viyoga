<p>“Mister Victor—”</p>

<p>“Just Victor,” the man said, kicking away the corpse of a Pond Lizard.</p>

<p>“Victor,” Lorelei agreed, before pointing to the gun holstered at his waist. “I’m really surprised you haven’t used that yet. How come?”</p>

<p>He snorted. “Cause each shot costs me a hundred and fifty bucks. The college doesn’t reimburse me for ammunition or consumables used during these trips. I only carry this in case of emergencies.”</p>

<p>Her eyes went wide. “A hundred and fifty bucks!”</p>

<p>“Magical ammunition?” I guessed.</p>

<p>The guide nodded. “Enchanted for increased penetration and to leave a bleeding debuff on targets.”</p>

<p>When he saw the Hearth Spirit’s confusion, he explained. “If you’re going to take on Monsters above level ten, you’re going to need enchanted ammunition. Using mundane rounds would be like plunking at them with a BB.”</p>

<p>Ayara, hearing our conversation, came over to join us. “Humans haven’t unlocked a Crafter class capable of producing ammunition?”</p>

<p>“Not specifically,” he admitted.</p>

<p>“The Drow never developed black powder arms like Humans, but we have Bowyers and Fletchers. They’re specialized Crafters who can produce enchanted arrows and bolts.”</p>

<p>From what I knew, Human Crafters had learned to produce magical bullets and shells, but it was a slow, costly process requiring a lot of rare materials. Typically, the government snapped those Crafters up, contracting them to produce ammunition for the military. Most adventurers made do with magical melee weapons instead, which were readily available at reasonable prices.</p>

<p>“Magic bullets.” Lorelei chuckled.</p>

<p>Ayara laughed with her. “My people have Archers and Snipers Classes that are able to use their MP to create temporary ammunition for their weapons. It’s not as good as enchanted ammunition, but it’s much easier on their wallets.”</p>

<p>“Maybe Humanity will eventually unlock a Gunner Class,” I mused. “Or at least a Gunsmith or Armorer.”</p>

<p>“Whoever does it first, they’ll be rich.” Victor chuckled. “Come on, let’s keep going, we’re almost there.”</p>

<p>I filed away that idea for later use.</p>

<p>We took out another group of Giant Roaches. Mana Bolt leveled up again, and I felt like I was close to leveling my Class too.</p>

<p>“Level six,” Ayara said, smiling in satisfaction.</p>

<p>“Congratulations!” Lorelei cheered.</p>

<p>“Congrats,” I echoed. “You seem to be making good progress.”</p>

<p>She hefted her mace. “Blacksmiths get bonus progress for using gear they crafted themselves. I’m hoping I can keep running this Dungeon and reach level ten. If I’m lucky, I’ll be offered a Fusion with my secondary Class.”</p>

<p>I paused. “You can—”</p>

<p>“Stop hogging all the XP!”</p>

<p>We all turned to listen as the two Warriors began to argue. The rookie kept poking his friend, accusing the veteran of stealing all the kills and grabbing the <em>XP</em>.</p>

<p>“Idiots,” the Drow snorted.</p>

<p>I had to agree. XP was a popular trope in video games, but as far as anyone knew it didn’t exist within the System. People leveled up by fighting Monsters or carrying out activities in alignment with their Class. Ayara made progress forging gear, and I continued to level Laborer by working on Rotwood Manor’s repairs. When the System felt like you had put in the appropriate effort, gained insight, or had grown enough, you’d reach the next level.</p>

<p>Victor tried to break up their squabbling before it turned into a real fight. The Jack dithered to the side, clearly stressed out by the whole scene.</p>

<p>“Brent,” Lorelei tapped me on the shoulder. “Over there!”</p>

<p>Following her gaze, I saw there was a chest under a half-fallen column. It was far fancier than any we’d seen before.</p>

<p>The few chests we’d encountered had been soggy, wooden boxes containing the equivalent of Dungeon junk: shiny stones, bits of rope, and chunks of cheap ore. Ayara had shown zero interest in any of the crafting material. Victor had bagged them up, proclaiming we’d probably make three dollars off the lot.</p>

<p>The chest Lorelei had spotted, though, looked like it was made from solid wood, with brass fittings and a matching latch. I tried to pull it out of the wreckage, but the column pinned it in place.</p>

<p>“Can I help?”</p>

<p>Turning, I spotted Ayara behind us.</p>

<p>“Please,” I said, making room for her.</p>

<p>She crouched next to me, and with our combined strength we were able to shift the column out of the way. Opening the chest, I was a bit disappointed to see it was just a pile of ore.</p>

<p>“Iron and Copper, Medium Grade,” Ayara said.</p>

<p>“Shoot, I was hoping for a kickass sword or something,” Lorelei pouted.</p>

<p>I shot her a look. “And who would use it?”</p>

<p>“We could always hang it above the mantle back home!”</p>

<p>The commotion behind us grew louder; both Warriors were shouting. I could hear people shoving and fighting.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Ayara grabbed my shoulder.</p>

<p>The floor shook underneath us as cracks suddenly formed.</p>

<p>Lorelei floated down, grabbing onto my back. “What’s going on?”</p>

<p>Looking toward the rest of the group, I saw that the Warriors’ fight had escalated, and the two had begun wrestling. Without paying attention, they had bumped into a moss-covered wall.</p>

<p>The moss had fallen away, revealing a large, reverse lightning-bolt rune carved into the wall. The symbol lit up, shining a bright, clearly dangerous shade of red.</p>

<p>“You two jackasses set off a trap!” Victor yelled, before waving to the rest of us. “Hurry, run!”</p>

<p>I grabbed Ayara’s hand without even thinking and began to sprint toward the doorway the guide had run through. Lorelei raced above us as the two Warriors and the Jack ran ahead of us.</p>

<p>The floor rumbled, and then began to fall away one stone block at a time. Gaps opened ahead of us, forcing us to jump or run around the holes.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Ayara tripped as the floor beneath her feet suddenly vanished.</p>

<p>I tried to drag her up, but she was too heavy. Still, I refused to let go. Digging my heels into the floor, I pulled with all my might. I could feel Lorelei’s arms around mine, trying to lend me strength.</p>

<p>Everything lurched. The stone beneath me began to tumble. I could only grip onto the Drow tightly as both of us plummeted into the depths.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei called between sobs. “Brent!”</p>

<p>I was wet and cold; I couldn't help but shiver. There was something wrong. Pain radiated from the back of my skull, wrapping around my entire head like the world’s worst migraine.</p>

<p>It was impossible to open my eyes, I couldn’t even get my body to move. Somebody grabbed me roughly and dragged me onto some kind of hard surface, propping me up. My jaw was pried open, and some awful, bitter liquid was poured into my mouth. It tasted like bad cough syrup.</p>

<p>I tried to cough, but I felt rough hands massaging my throat, forcing me to swallow.</p>

<p>Warmth rushed through me, soothing away the pain in my head. It went from a painful spike driving into my skull to a dull, manageable ache.</p>

<p>Coughing, I opened my eyes. Lorelei was hovering above me.</p>

<p>“Oh, thank goodness!” She wrapped her arms around me, hugging me tightly.</p>

<p>Glancing about, I saw we were in another ruined room, at the edge of a small pond. Someone had clearly dragged me out of the water; my clothes were still soaked.</p>

<p>Looking behind me, I saw I was resting in Ayara’s lap. The Drow Blacksmith clutched an empty Healing Potion in her trembling hands.</p>

<p>“Thank the End, you’re awake,” she said, working hard to master her voice.</p>

<p>Carefully, I sat up. My head still hurt, and I was a bit dizzy, but I wasn’t dead.</p>

<p>“Those idiot Warriors triggered some kind of trap!” Lorelei fumed.</p>

<p>Ayara considered me with a serious look. “You fell, trying to save me. You should have just run and escaped with the others!”</p>

<p>“I’d never do that,” I argued, gingerly reaching to feel the knot along the back of my head.</p>

<p>My fingers came back with blood. The Drow Blacksmith looked worried.</p>

<p>“I’m fine” I insisted, wiping my fingers on my pants. “I’d never abandon someone like that. We’re in this together.”</p>

<p>Luckily, when the floor had collapsed, we’d fallen into a pond. The water had broken our fall. Unfortunately, I must have hit my head on the bottom of the pool. If Ayara hadn’t been there to drag me out, I might have drowned.</p>

<p>I pointed that out to the Drow, but she was adamant that she was the one in my debt. I had been hurt trying to save her, so she felt like she owed me.</p>

<p>“The real problem is,” I said, trying to break up the argument, “where the hell are we?”</p>

<p>I winced as a new notification popped into my head.</p>

<p><b>Quest Update! – Escape the Dungeon Annex!</b><br><b>Rewards: Class Progress, Class Specific Item</b></p>

<p>“What the hell is a Dungeon Annex?” I muttered, feeling more confused than ever.</p>

<p>“Oh, oh, I know this,” Lorelei said. “I remember watching a YouTouch Dungeon tutorial video. Anyway, an Annex is created when a new space opens in the Dungeon. They usually spring up when the Dungeon is preparing to expand!”</p>

<p>“The Dungeon must be preparing to grow.” Ayara climbed to her feet. “It’s a popular delving spot, but it’s only a few floors deep. This Annex is probably set to become a whole new floor.”</p>

<p>Lorelei took the first-aid kit out of my bag and cleaned up the wound along the back of my head. The potion had healed most of the damage, but the Hearth Spirit insisted on applying ointment and a bandage.</p>

<p>There were two doorways leading out of the room. Checking our supplies, I saw we had the one remaining freebie Healing Potion and the food and water we’d brought into the Dungeon, that was it. We had no clue where the exit was, or how to get back to the Dungeon proper.</p>

<p>But I knew there was no way in hell I’d just give up and die. I had too much to lose.</p>

<p>Picking a door at random, we began our quest to escape the Annex.</p>
