<p>“There, that’s all of them.” I hung the permits on the window closest to the front door.</p>

<p>Lorelei floated closer to the window, reading them all. “Wow, they really cover everything, huh?”</p>

<p>The papers practically blocked the window. There were permits for new doors, new windows, plumbing repair, electrical repair, a new roof, and more. It likely represented years of work, but I was sure we’d get them all done eventually.</p>

<p>The paperwork had been surprisingly easy. I had been prepared for weeks, maybe even months of bureaucratic red tape. Ashling Grove’s historical society hadn’t even raised a single complaint, they’d just rubber-stamped the paperwork and wished me good luck.</p>

<p>I had also done a walkthrough of the house with the county inspector earlier in the day, a morose man named Merle. He was a gloomy son-of-a-bitch, but he was good at his job. He pointed out dozens of issues we'd have to address, including a few problems I had missed.</p>

<p>When the previous owners had tried to fix the place up, they had done a lot of the work themselves. Any good contractor will tell you that DIY home renovators rarely do it right and up to code. The electrical service panel needed to be replaced, along with a dozen other fixes to the house’s wiring.</p>

<p>I was relieved to know that the water and plumbing, what remained at least, was up to code. There was only one functional bathroom, just off the main hall by the servant’s quarters. It was a bit on the small side, but I was happy I didn’t need to build an outhouse. I had a permit to hook up the rest of the house’s plumbing, but that was low on the priority list.</p>

<p>First, we needed to get rid of all the junk. I had already rented a dumpster, which sat near the front of the house. Based on what I’d seen during the inspection, I imagine we’d fill and empty it multiple times before we were done.</p>

<p>Lorelei pumped her fists. “Come on, let’s go!”</p>

<p>Smiling, I followed her into the house.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>We worked through the day and into the evening. Starting on the first floor of the central wing, we went from room to room. We ripped up carpeting, piled garbage, and pulled down the damaged remains of the plaster lathing.</p>

<p>Lorelei’s Hearth Restoration proved itself to be extremely useful.</p>

<p><b>Hearth Restoration</b><br><b>MP Cost: </b>500</p>

<p><em>The Hearth Spirit is a direct representation of the Hearthomancer’s Lair. Clearing ashes and cleaning the hearth is of critical importance.</em></p>

<p><em>“Keep your Lair clean and orderly, like your mind, and none shall ever overcome you.”—Prescilla, Prefect of The Lonely Bastion</em></p>

<p>Despite the Skill’s limited description, Lorelei could use it anywhere on the property. Upon casting it, the Hearth Spirit could scour away dirt and tidy away garbage. The junk moved around as if swept by a giant, invisible broom.</p>

<p>Honestly, it reminded me of a movie I’d seen as a kid. It might not have been as flashy or destructive as other Wizards, but it was the kind of magic I had always dreamt of.</p>

<p>We moved at a furious pace. I resorted to using a snow shovel and wheelbarrow to move all the junk before dumping it outside.</p>

<p>In less than a day we nearly filled the dumpster. We crammed it with ruined carpets, old beer bottles, and broken furniture. Despite our hard work, we’d only managed to clear the main hall and a few side rooms.</p>

<p>Even after using Second Wind, I was exhausted by the end of the day. The water in the bathroom was ice cold. The heater was on the fritz, but I was glad for a chance to clean off all the accumulated grime and dirt.</p>

<p>Stepping out of the bathroom, I surveyed Rotwood Manor’s main hall.</p>

<p>We had cleared away the dirt and junk, revealing a beautiful parquet floor. The house’s mana had protected it, though I was sure it’d need a good sanding before being stained and polished back to its former glory.</p>

<p>Lorelei floated near the hearth. She drifted closer as I headed toward the cot I had set up near the fireplace.</p>

<p>“Thank you, Brent,” she said quietly.</p>

<p>I chuckled. “I should be saying that to you. We’d never have gotten so much done without that Skill of yours.”</p>

<p>She nodded, her eyes shimmering with gratitude. “I’m sure if we work hard enough, we’ll see this place shine once again!”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit faded away, turning into motes of mana, before I could say anything else. Shaking my head, I laid down on the narrow camp bed I had set up. I was asleep before my head even hit the pillow.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It was dark. I was lost in the woods.</p>

<p>Trees like gnarled, obsidian towers rose around me. Thunder hammered through the air. Lightning sporadically illuminated the cloaked figures that chased after me. Each of them carried a wicked, curved knife.</p>

<p>The blades were stained with blood.</p>

<p>I ran for my life, but no matter how hard I pushed myself my pursuers were right behind me. The noise grew louder, the entire world rattling with each thunderclap.</p>

<p>Tripping over a root, I landed on my knees. The masked figures plunged their knives toward my unprotected back.</p>

<p>With a final clap of thunder, my eyes shot open.</p>

<p>It took me a moment to realize I had been dreaming, but the noise of thunder was real. Every few seconds another loud <em>wham</em> would echo through the empty manor.</p>

<p>Climbing out of my tent, I grabbed my phone and turned on its flashlight, panning it around.</p>

<p>“Brent?”</p>

<p>Lorelei materialized above me; she was faintly luminous. She blinked her eyes like she was waking from a bad dream of her own. “What’s going on?”</p>

<p>“I don’t know.” I grabbed a hammer I’d left sitting nearby.</p>

<p>My Lair’s flames burned steadily, reassuring me. Gripping my makeshift weapon, I stepped out of the room, following the banging noise. Lorelei drifted behind me; her hands were clutched against my back.</p>

<p>It should have been a humid summer night, but the interior of the mansion was dry and cold. For some reason, it put me in mind of a tomb.</p>

<p>Stepping into the foyer, I confirmed the front door was locked and closed. None of the windows had been busted out either.</p>

<p>“I don’t think it’s an intruder,” the Hearth Spirit whispered.</p>

<p>I glanced over my shoulder at her.</p>

<p>“Just a feeling I have.” Her face went pale. “Wait, you don’t think it’s a ghost, do you?”</p>

<p>I had to laugh at that. She smacked me lightly on the shoulder.</p>

<p>“That’s not funny! I know what you know, remember? That means I know all the spooky stories about this place!” She shivered.</p>

<p>That gave me pause. “Do you remember any old stories about this place from the original Lorelei?”</p>

<p>She scrunched her face, thinking. “Maybe? I don’t know. A lot of her memories are faded and worn. I mostly remember the good times when the house was full of light, music, and laughter.”</p>

<p>The noise was coming from the second floor. Carefully we made our way up the stairs, wary of any broken treads. Following the sound, we entered the residential wing.</p>

<p>A long hallway spanned ahead of us. Junk and garbage were heaped everywhere, and the walls were covered in Satanic graffiti, pentagrams, bloody halos, and blasphemously twisted bible verses.</p>

<p><em>Fear, for I am with you; be filled with dismay, for I am your God. I will strengthen you and break your enemies.</em></p>

<p>The banging was coming from the end of the hall.</p>

<p>We advanced, the Hearth Spirit clinging to my shirt. The noise got louder and louder.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei closed her eyes and ducked her head against my shoulder.</p>

<p>Stopping at the end of the hall, I just stood there, confused. “Huh?”</p>

<p>As I watched, the door at the end of the hall slowly squeaked open, then slammed itself shut. Over and over again, it kept happening. I didn’t feel a gust of wind or anything.</p>

<p>“It’s a ghost!” Lorelei shivered.</p>

<p>Taking a deep breath, I activated Mana Perception.</p>

<p>The hallway changed. Billowing channels of mana flowed all around us. Bursts of Darkness pushed in and out of the room, the arcane power enough to affect the material world. Each burst of mana slammed the door against its frame.</p>

<p>“What’s in that room?” I asked the Hearth Spirit.</p>

<p>Lorelei gathered her courage and then peered toward the room. “I don’t know, honestly. Should we take a look?”</p>

<p>Venturing forward, I put a hand out, stopping the door. I could feel it shuddering as the mana hit it, but it stopped moving. Leaning past the threshold, I inspected the room.</p>

<p>It was mostly empty, just four badly damaged plaster walls and a scuffed-up floor with only a few shaggy remains of carpet here and there. Nothing stood out to me.</p>

<p>I guessed it had been used as a bedroom or a party space by the frat boys at one point. Beer bottles glinted as the rays from my flashlight passed over them.</p>

<p>Lorelei looked almost disappointed. “Nothing?”</p>

<p>There was a surge of Darkness mana. It rose from the floor and passed through us. Lorelei closed her eyes and screamed in fear. I staggered back, bracing myself against the door.</p>

<p>I swore as the mana passed through us, I saw something, like phantoms standing around the room. They looked like the cloaked figures from my dream, all of them hunched around a glowing ring carved into the floor.</p>

<p>The mana faded, and the vision vanished. We stood there, slightly stunned.</p>

<p>“What was that?” I asked.</p>

<p>Lorelei shook her head. “I don’t know. A memory, a faded echo of something that happened here?”</p>

<p>I glanced down the hall. Even with Mana Perception, I couldn’t see anything. Whatever the weird phenomenon had been, it was gone.</p>

<p>We searched the room but found nothing. The floor in the middle of the room was damaged. It was so badly scratched and torn up that I was afraid we’d have to replace the floorboards. It was impossible to tell if anything had once been carved there.</p>

<p>Still, before we returned to bed, I made sure to firmly close the door.</p>
