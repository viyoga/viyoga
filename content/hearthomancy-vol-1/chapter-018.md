<p>“I’m an idiot. I can’t show Ayara this!” I said as Lorelei and I stood on what would normally be considered our front lawn.</p>

<p>In truth, it was much more like a wild, garbage-strewn field.</p>

<p>There was still a toilet cratered below the window it had been tossed through, and garbage was everywhere. The porch was a wreck too. I had pulled away the damaged and rotted boards, leaving just a few mana-reinforced beams that I had covered with some spare plywood.</p>

<p>I imagined Ayara wouldn’t mind the mess, given her focus on the Crafter’s Boon, but my pride as a new homeowner wouldn’t allow it.</p>

<p>“It’s fine,” Lorelei assured me, her outfit suddenly replaced by a pair of white overalls and a matching tank top. “With a bit of elbow grease and judicial use of Hearth Restoration, I’m sure we can clean this place up.”</p>

<p>I had borrowed a massive, gas-powered weed whacker from a friend of mine who owned a landscaping company. Slowly, I began hacking down the lawn, cutting away the weeds, creepers, and grass that covered the grounds. Lorelei came in behind me, blowing all the yard waste into a heap for her compost pile. The litter and trash were magically filtered out of the clippings, forming a mound off to the side.</p>

<p>Pausing my work, I watched Lorelei cast her spell. For a moment I was back to my childhood, watching that old, animated movie again. Sure, dancing bits of garbage and a broken toilet weren’t quite to the level of magical servants, but just watching the house magically clean itself was amazing.</p>

<p>Using Second Wind, we finished cleaning the front yard in just one day. We repeated the same performance the next day in the backyard. We managed to generate a huge mountain of vegetation and grass clippings. Lorelei insisted on adding all the yard waste to her compost pile.</p>

<p>“Trust me, I watched a tutorial video online on how to do it,” she said as I shoved a bit of topsoil onto the heap.</p>

<p>She gave the pile a speculative look. “And no goat and asparagus casserole!”</p>

<p>I had returned the casserole dish the day after they’d given it to us, dropping it off on Mitch and Candy’s doorstep with a small, polite note. The pair hadn’t shown up since, but I still didn’t trust them or my other neighbors. There was just something off about them.</p>

<p>Miraculously, new signs announcing the neighborhood watch had cropped up along our street. The logo was a pair of glaring binocular lenses, like a set of watchful eyes.</p>

<p>Lorelei finished working on her compost pile and then inspected the newly cleared backyard.</p>

<p>“Yup, this is great!” she said, hands on her hips. “High five!”</p>

<p>I had to admit, we had done an amazing job. Sure, we had to figure out what to do with the wrecked car moldering in the backyard, but we had hacked down the worst of the wild vegetation and tossed out most of the trash. It wasn’t perfect, but for once I was proud of how my house looked.</p>

<p>“One step at a time,” I reminded myself.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>I decided to take Lorelei out on a date that evening.</p>

<p>“You know picking a fancy restaurant would be a waste on me,” she said.</p>

<p>I gave her a quick kiss. “Trust me, there’s more options for a date than dinner and a movie.”</p>

<p>As we drove off the property I noticed Mitch, Candy, and another man I didn’t know standing out on my neighbor’s lawn. All three stared at us as we drove past. Mitch gave a cheerful wave, which I did not return.</p>

<p>Driving into downtown Ashling Grove, I parked my truck just off the main street. It was a weekday evening, but there were still plenty of college kids and locals out. The weather was nice, and quite a few restaurants and bars were taking advantage of the season to seat customers outdoors. Trees lined the streets along with planters; the flowers were in full bloom.</p>

<p>“It’s beautiful,” Lorelei said, looking down the boulevard, the streetlights just flickering on as dusk settled.</p>

<p>“Trust me, this is just the start,” I told her as I offered her my arm.</p>

<p>“Such a gentleman,” she cooed.</p>

<p>Walking arm and arm, we slowly strolled down the street. It was a beautiful summer evening. I noticed the township had already begun setting up decorations for the upcoming town festival. Pennants and signs hung along the main street.</p>

<p>“There’s a solar eclipse this year?” Lorelei asked, pointing to a banner hanging off a lamppost.</p>

<p>“I guess so,” I answered. “Mid-October, if the date on the signs is right.”</p>

<p>She gave a gentle smile. “I’m glad some traditions stayed alive. I remember gathering on the lawn of the manor to celebrate when solar eclipses occurred.”</p>

<p>“Maybe we can try the same thing this year?” I suggested as she nuzzled closer.</p>

<p>Ashling Grove had a lot of silly traditions. The town festival during solar eclipses, the lantern boats we launched each year on Lake Coldwater, and the offerings children were encouraged to leave along the walls of the town’s graveyards. The town’s elders helped maintain those little oddities, keeping our heritage alive.</p>

<p>I was excited for the upcoming festival. I was sure the streets would be crowded with local Crafters and shops, and the townsfolk would shout along with the big countdown before the moon shadowed the sun. It would be a fun time.</p>

<p>Ashling Grove wasn’t a big town, but there were a ton of little shops and hidden nooks and crannies to explore.</p>

<p>I showed Lorelei the local bookshop I enjoyed. There was a whole section of home improvement books that she poured through, looking for ideas for our house. We then made a brief stop at a local cafe so I could grab a cup of coffee. I was a regular, so the owner happily greeted me and smiled widely when I introduced him to Lorelei.</p>

<p>“It smells heavenly in here!” the Hearth Spirit told him.</p>

<p>“Come back any time!” he called as we headed out the door.</p>

<p>Lorelei was particularly taken with the flower shop that I showed her. As a Hearth Spirit, she didn’t need to eat, but she still loved the smell of flowers.</p>

<p>I quietly made note of the ones she enjoyed the most, arranging for a bouquet of them before leaving.</p>

<p>“They’re beautiful, thank you!” she told me as she hugged the flowers against her chest.</p>

<p>Resuming our walk, I waved hello to a few people I knew, mostly locals I had grown up with. A few paused us here and there as I introduced them to my familiar. She quickly charmed them with her easy smile and cheerful attitude.</p>

<p>“This really is a nice little town,” she said as we walked back to my truck.</p>

<p>“Do you recall much about it from Lorelei’s memories?” I asked.</p>

<p>She paused, her feet lifting off the ground as she began to float.</p>

<p>“No, the only things I really remember are the house,” she said finally as she drifted above my shoulders. “Lorelei rarely left the property, especially after her father died. She preferred the silent, familiarity of Rootwood Manor. I remember there being a town, and people occasionally coming to visit, but that’s about it.”</p>

<p>I had done some follow-up research on my Lair’s history, but there was scant information about the woman whose memory she had inherited.</p>

<p>I had learned that Lorelei Ganlts had died young, sometime in the late 1930s. As my Hearth Spirit had recalled, she never married. She had been a popular socialite in the early 1920s but had become a recluse by the time of her death a decade-and-a-half later. She was the last of her line, so the house had ended up being sold in an auction to cover her family’s many debts.</p>

<p>Shaking off these gloomy thoughts, I held open the truck door for my lover. “There’s one more place I want to show you.”</p>

<p>Driving out of town, we circled up the hills until we reached an isolated overlook.</p>

<p>“Brent, did you bring me to Makeout Point?” Lorelei giggled.</p>

<p>I climbed out of the cab and then pointed to a neighboring hill. “Nope. Makeout Point is over there.”</p>

<p>Lorelei phased through the windshield, coming to join me by the front of the truck. She squinted, peering at the other hilltop.</p>

<p>“Ha, you’re right! I think I can spot some teenagers! Hey, get a room!” she shouted.</p>

<p>“Come over here,” I beckoned.</p>

<p>Joining me, Lorelei gave an audible gasp as she looked down at Ashling Grove, which filled the valley below us. It was just reaching full dark, but the town was already glowing brightly. The main boulevard lit up, the yellow glow of streetlights and shops joined by twinkling lights from cars.</p>

<p>In the far distance, we could just make out the twinkle of Lake Coldwater. Even in the dead of summer, it looked spectral and frigid.</p>

<p>To our right, we could see the lights of the college. The dorms and classroom buildings glowed with rows upon rows of neat, orderly windows.</p>

<p>Pointing to the left, I showed Lorelei where our house was. It was impossible to see it, there were too many hills in the way, but we could both feel the tug of Rotwood Manor, its hearth joining us together no matter how far apart we were.</p>

<p>Lorelei curled her arms around my shoulders, hugging me from behind. “Brent. Thank you so much for showing me this. It’s beautiful.”</p>

<p>I wrapped my hand around hers.</p>

<p>She leaned in and whispered in a mischievous tone. “You know, just because this <em>isn’t </em>Makeout Point, doesn’t mean we can’t have a little fun in the back of your truck.”</p>

<p>There were a few blankets and tarps packed in the cab of my vehicle. Tugging her with me, I decided our date night would have to be extended for a bit longer.</p>
