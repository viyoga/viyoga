<p>I was less than thrilled when the cops showed up again the next morning.</p>

<p>It was just after dawn, and I was still feeling rough from the night before, with scratches along my arms and a huge bruise forming over my right knee. Ayara was still asleep, and I hadn’t heard a peep from Ishikana, though I was sure she knew that we were up and about.</p>

<p>The doorbell rang again, insistently. Checking the camera on my phone, I saw Officer Penkle and Decker, their squad car parked in front of the gate.</p>

<p>Limping up the driveway, I was happy when Lorelei materialized behind me.</p>

<p>“Morning, officers,” I called as we approached the gate.</p>

<p>“Good morning, Mr. Owens,” Penkle replied.</p>

<p>He was acting surprisingly innocent considering he had been hammering the crap out of my doorbell a moment before. His partner had been trying to undo the latch on the gate.</p>

<p>Officer Decker stepped back. “Mr. Owens, we received a report from your neighbor, Herb Klosky. He claimed that you trespassed on his property last night and stole one of his goats.”</p>

<p>Lorelei gasped. “We <em>stole</em> his goat? Those lying bastards!”</p>

<p>Both cops regarded the Hearth Spirit suspiciously.</p>

<p>I stood behind my gate and gave both cops a hard look. “Last night I caught my neighbors, Mitch, Josh, and Herb on <em>my</em> property, claiming one of Herb’s goats had run away. I kicked them off my land.” I gestured toward my injuries. “I went looking for the goat, and all I got out of it was a bunch of cuts and bruises.”</p>

<p>“You got anyone that will back up that story?” Decker demanded.</p>

<p>I snorted. “Sure, ask all the people that were standing around Herb’s barn. Apparently, the neighborhood watch had a late-night get-together.”</p>

<p>Lorelei tilted her head as she regarded the cops. “Weren’t you parked just down the street, in your squad car most of the evening? You must have seen the party, right?”</p>

<p>Both cops blinked, then ignored her question.</p>

<p>“And what happened to Mr. Klosky’s goat?” Decker pressed.</p>

<p>“I never saw it,” I answered truthfully. “We searched for a good chunk of the night, then went back to bed. I haven’t even had a chance to go and tell Herb since you two decided to show up so early.”</p>

<p>I did my best to restrain my anger. The cops might have been assholes, but my real anger was directed at Mitch and the so-called neighborhood watch.</p>

<p>The cops regarded me.</p>

<p>“Do you mind if we search your property?”</p>

<p>“For the missing goat?” I asked.</p>

<p>They shared a look.</p>

<p>“Are you aware of the missing person’s cases from the surrounding county?” Penkle asked, his tone instantly putting me on edge.</p>

<p>“Missing persons?” I shared a glance at Lorelei, and she shrugged.</p>

<p>“Three people, transients, have been reported missing in the last few weeks,” Decker replied. “Now, I grew up around here, just like you. We both know the stories around <em>Rotwood Manor</em>. It wouldn’t surprise me if the new owner was a little screwy in the head. He’d <em>have </em>to be to buy a cursed house<em>. </em>Maybe he decided to resume the place's <em>old </em>traditions.”</p>

<p>He glared at me with open hostility. ”We’ve already received complaints about you, we know you’re a <em>bad seed</em>.”</p>

<p>Penkle stood to the side, his hand going to the butt of his gun.</p>

<p>Decker stepped right up to the gate. “Now, is there anything you’d like to tell us, Mr. Owens, before we search your property?”</p>

<p>I stared right back at him. “Got a warrant?”</p>

<p>Penkle frowned harder. “Do we need one?”</p>

<p>“You’re damn straight you do,” I answered, turning around and heading back down the driveway. “And if you really want to find out what’s going on out here, why don’t you go visit my neighbor, Herb, and check out his fucking barn!”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Lorelei and I watched the cops drive away, using the Lair’s Darkness mana as a cloak. As I suspected, they didn’t even stop by Herb’s farm. I didn’t know how, but it seemed the neighborhood watch had the police on their side.</p>

<p>I didn’t know what to think. For a time, we just hung in the shadows, watching the goat farm and the others’ houses, but nothing stirred. There was no sign of Mitch or the rest of the neighborhood watch.</p>

<p>We slunk back to the manor an hour after sunrise. Ayara and Ishikana met us out front. I told them exactly what had happened.</p>

<p>“Preposterous,” the Arachne scoffed.</p>

<p>“How can they be blaming us, it’s insane!” Ayara agreed angrily. “I’ve been in sieges before when Monsters attacked our houses. This morning feels very similar.”</p>

<p>“We’re under attack,” Lorelei said.</p>

<p>“But we don’t know why,” Ishikana pointed out. “There is foulness afoot, of that I have little doubt, but why?”</p>

<p>Animal sacrifices, Mitch and the neighborhood watch, DIK, Baphomet, and even the upcoming solar eclipse. We had a bunch of jumbled pieces, but nothing truly linking them together. It was like we were trying to solve a puzzle but were missing a few of the critical pieces.</p>

<p>Like Eustace Phelps and his role in the whole mystery.</p>

<p>“We could just go to Mitch’s house and crush his skull,” Ayara noted with a reasonable tone.</p>

<p>“Not as long as they’ve got the cops on their side,” Lorelei replied. “I imagine they’ll be sitting outside our house, keeping an eye on us.”</p>

<p>“Sadly, in the Human world, you can’t go around killing your neighbors because you strongly suspect they’re Satanists,” I added. “There was a whole thing about it back in the 1980s.”</p>

<p>Despite the mess we were in, there were more immediate, mundane things we had to do. I still needed to go to the grocery store and Ayara needed to go to work. I even had a few home renovation and repair jobs lined up for that week.</p>

<p>The world kept on spinning no matter what you did.</p>

<p>After cleaning up and grabbing a cup of coffee, I offered to give Ishikana a ride to the college library so she could continue her research. I had a repair job waiting just off campus, a broken rain spout that I could knock out before hitting the grocery store. Afterward, I might stop to see my folks since I still hadn’t heard back from them, and I was starting to grow concerned.</p>

<p>Ayara kissed me goodbye and took off for the steel mill. Lorelei decided to stay home to watch Rotwood Manor, defending it against further intrusions.</p>

<p>I packed up my gear, then invited Ishikana to hitch a ride in the bed of my truck as we headed into town. As predicted, we passed Penkle and Decker. Their squad car was parked in Mitch’s driveway. Both cops stared woodenly at us as we passed. I was glad our home was secure, otherwise, I was sure the two cops would try to break in, warrant or no warrant.</p>

<p>Even without all the answers, I knew things were coming to a head. The town festival was coming up in only a few weeks, and if the visions I’d experienced were true, the madness around Rotwood Manor was tied to the upcoming solar eclipse.</p>

<p>Putting on the AM radio news station, I listened for any broadcasts concerning missing persons. Instead, all I heard about was the ongoing international news cycle about first contact with the Arachne. There was nothing about missing people, so either the cops had been lying or it wasn’t being covered by the news.</p>

<p>Ashling Grove was fully decked out and ready for the festival. But rather than the excitement and anticipation I associated with the town-wide party, it seemed like Ashling Grove was mired in some kind of darkness. Mothers grabbed their children and ushered them away as we drove past, and pedestrians gave us ugly, hateful looks. Everything seemed twisted and wrong, like the entire town was against us.</p>

<p>Driving onto campus, we got more disturbing looks from the college students. Rather than parking the truck, I pulled up outside the library.</p>

<p>“Are you sure you’ll be okay by yourself?” I asked the Arachne as she climbed out of the vehicle.</p>

<p>“Why, Brent, are you worried about me?” She winked coyishly.</p>

<p>“Yes,” I answered flatly. “At this point, I’m worried about all of us.”</p>

<p>She paused, a small smile playing across her lips.</p>

<p>“I’ll be fine, but let’s keep in touch,” she said, pulling her phone out of her pocket. “If I find anything, I’ll let you know.”</p>

<p>“Let’s do a group chat,” I suggested. “I want Ayara in the loop as well.”</p>

<p>Waiting till the Arachne was safely inside the building, I pulled away from the curb and drove back into town. I decided to take care of the handyman job before hitting the grocery store.</p>

<p>I tried to call my client, to tell them I had time to fix their broken rain spout, but they never answered. They didn’t respond when I texted either.</p>

<p>I didn’t need access to their house to fix the problem, so I decided just to get it done and send them the bill afterward. Even with Ishikana’s check bolstering my bank balance I still wanted to keep up a stream of revenue, and an easy job was an easy job.</p>

<p>Pulling onto a quiet, residential street, I parked in front of the Johnson’s house. I had known the couple for years; I had gone to school with the son and then done several odd repair jobs on their nice but aging bungalow.</p>

<p>Parking, I could already see where one of their rain spouts was damaged. The gutter had been pulled away from the fascia by wind damage, which in turn had bent the spout. It’d be an easy job to fix.</p>

<p>Climbing out of my truck, I unhooked a ladder and began walking across their lawn, intending to prop it up next to the busted gutter.</p>

<p>“Go away!”</p>

<p>I paused, turning my head back and forth.</p>

<p>“I said go away!” the man’s voice repeated. “If you set another foot in our yard, I’m calling the cops, you creep!”</p>

<p>Turning toward the house’s door, I spotted a white, fearful face staring at me. Mr. Johnson had cracked it open, and he was staring at me like I was the devil incarnate.</p>

<p>“Hey, Mr. Johnson,” I waved, unsure what was going on. I pointed toward the broken gutter. “I tried to call earlier, but I couldn’t get a hold of you. I had some time this morning, so I figured I’d come out—”</p>

<p>“Go away!” he repeated more loudly.</p>

<p>I heard a commotion inside the house, like someone opening closets and turning out drawers.</p>

<p>“Are you okay, Mr. Johnson?” I asked. “It’s me, Brent, you hired me to fix your rain spout, remember?</p>

<p>An old woman, Mrs. Johnson, pushed her husband out of the way as she stumbled onto the lawn. She was carrying a double-barreled shotgun.</p>

<p>“Get out of here, you damn devil worshiper!” she yelled as she tried to stuff two shells into the weapon’s breach. “We don’t want your evil near our house!”</p>

<p>“Fuck!” I swung the ladder around, knocking the gun from her hands.</p>

<p>She cursed as she fell backward, onto the lawn. Her husband, cowering in the doorway, watched the whole crazy scene.</p>

<p>I didn’t bother to secure the ladder; I just hooked it back in place and jumped into the cab of my truck. Starting the engine, I slammed on the gas and took off.</p>

<p>The back window of a parked car exploded as the shotgun went off.</p>

<p>Ducking low, I took the first corner and got out of there as quickly as possible.</p>
