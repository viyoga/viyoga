<p>Gentle laughter and the <em>clink</em> of glassware echoed down the hallway. Somewhere, a jazz band was playing.</p>

<p>I blinked. Looking down, I saw I was wearing an old-fashioned suit. The material felt gauzy and unreal under my touch.</p>

<p>“What the hell is going on?” I muttered. “Another dream?”</p>

<p>Turning around, I knew instinctively that I was within Rootwood Manor, the house as it had been in its better days. Its mana swirled around me, familiar and empowering.</p>

<p>“Happy New Year!” a group of voices shouted joyfully.</p>

<p>Mana eddied around me as I began to walk toward the noise. <em>Maybe I was trapped in some memory, an event carved into the very fabric of Rootwood Manor?</em></p>

<p>The house certainly seemed to be in perfect shape. The wooden wainscoting along the walls was pristine; the oak had been polished to a mirror-like sheen. Portraits hung along the length of the hall, with small alcoves containing statues and bits of sculpture. Every time I tried to get a better look at them though, the subject of the art shifted, becoming indistinct shapes and colored blobs.</p>

<p>“Brent!” a sweet voice called. “Hurry up, it’s almost midnight!”</p>

<p>My body began to move of its own volition, running down the hall. I only caught brief glimpses of the rooms I passed. There was a study full of leather-bound books, a music room with a grand piano, and a lady’s lounge painted in soft, pastel shades.</p>

<p>Only one room seemed out of place. It was far simpler, more rustic, than the rest. Inside was a single figure, a giant of a man weeping inconsolably over a closed casket.</p>

<p>But before I could consider his plight, more rooms blurred past, all ornate and beautiful. It made my heart ache, knowing what Rootwood Manor had once looked like.</p>

<p>My feet carried me toward the main hall. Stepping inside, I was nearly overwhelmed.</p>

<p>The room was decorated for the holidays. Two huge Christmas trees stood in the back corners of the room, flanking the hearth. The fireplace was draped in garlands and a row of stockings hung along the mantle.</p>

<p>The chandeliers overhead were decorated with even more garlands, and the floor was crowded with well-dressed phantoms. Their features were indistinct, but I could see they were all wearing finely cut suits and slinky, drape-like gowns. Each of them was holding a drink, cheering for the coming New Year.</p>

<p>Based on the banner hanging over the door I had just stepped through, it would soon be 1925.</p>

<p>“Brent!”</p>

<p>Turning my head, I spotted Lorelei.</p>

<p>She was lounging against a doorway to the side of the room. Her pristine, white gown was draped with tiny crystals, which shimmered brilliantly under the room’s light. A matching tiara sat upon her head, the filigree silver standing out gorgeously against her midnight-black curls.</p>

<p>Lorelei looked like a movie starlet from a bygone age, a silver-screen bombshell sculpted from magic and mana.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit grinned and curled a finger, beckoning me. That time I moved entirely under my own volition, drawn to the alluring woman.</p>

<p>“I’m glad you could make it,” she said, curling her hands around my shoulders.</p>

<p>“Ten!” the phantoms suddenly shouted.</p>

<p>“What’s going on?” I asked.</p>

<p>“I’m not sure,” she admitted, placing her head against my chest. “A dream, a memory? I just knew the clock was about to strike midnight, and I didn't want to celebrate alone.”</p>

<p>“Six—Five!”</p>

<p>She looked up at me. Her eyes were limpid pools flecked with glowing embers of mana. “It’s still customary to kiss someone to celebrate the New Year, right?”</p>

<p>“One! Happy New Year!”</p>

<p>I leaned down to kiss her.</p>

<p>The dream burst.</p>

<p>Groggily, I thrashed against my sheets, the camp bed creaking underneath me. Something was wrong, I could feel it. The mana of Rotwood Manor churned angrily.</p>

<p>Lorelei, her face white, materialized above me. “Brent! There’s someone on the property. Intruders!”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Dude, don’t be a pussy!” the girl hissed.</p>

<p>“You didn’t say someone was living here!” the younger man worried. “Look, there’s a truck parked out front, and the broken windows have been boarded up!”</p>

<p>They were crouched by the corner of the house, hiding behind a scraggly bush. It was hard to tell, but I put the two of them in their late teens. I guessed that they were still high school students.</p>

<p>“Nobody is living here,” another voice snorted.</p>

<p>A third figure appeared, stumbling out of the tree line. “This place has been empty for years!”</p>

<p>“Then what about the truck!” the scared boy whispered.</p>

<p>Weak moonlight broke through the canopy, painting everything in shades of watered-down silver.</p>

<p>I crept closer, inspecting the third figure. He was older, I put him in his thirties, with thinning hair and a bushy, untamed beard. There was a grizzled, party-hard look about him; his clothes were worn and stained.</p>

<p>In one hand he carried a plastic shopping bag full of malt liquor bottles. In the other, he had a crowbar.</p>

<p>The man handed the scared boy the crowbar. “Hurry and get that window open!”</p>

<p>“But—”</p>

<p>“Nobody lives here,” the older man repeated forcefully. “Sure, someone bought the place, but Rotwood has changed hands a dozen times. Even if someone bought it, they’ll be dead or gone in a few weeks, trust me.”</p>

<p>“Yeah, come on, Hank!” the girl teased. “Didn’t you claim there was no such thing as ghosts!”</p>

<p>“Oh, I wouldn’t say that!”</p>

<p>All three screamed as Lorelei appeared behind them, floating in mid-air. The party dress she had been wearing in the dream was gone, replaced by a spectral veil and long, diaphanous gown.</p>

<p>She was really hamming it up with the outfit, but at least I had talked her out of bringing a set of chains to rattle. The moonlight added a nice touch though.</p>

<p>The two teens flailed through the bush in their rush to get away, tripping onto the overgrown lawn. The older man dropped the bottles and grabbed the crowbar back from the kid, holding it in front of him.</p>

<p>“Who the fuck are you!” he snarled.</p>

<p>I stepped out of the shadows, my wand in hand. “I’m the property owner, asshole.”</p>

<p>The two high school kids went white, apparently more scared of me than the ghost.</p>

<p>“Please, don’t call the cops!” the girl said, clutching onto the boy’s arm.</p>

<p>“My parents would kill me!” the boy whimpered.</p>

<p>“Nobody is calling the cops!” the man said, holding onto his crowbar more tightly.</p>

<p>I guessed that he was some kind of Warrior based on his stature. He also reeked of whisky; apparently, the malt liquor wouldn’t have been his first drink of the evening.</p>

<p>Lorelei floated to my side as I held my wand, pointing it at the drunk. “Get out of here and never come back. Rotwood Manor has a new owner, and he’s in no mood to deal with idiots or intruders.”</p>

<p>The two teens edged to the side, clearly preparing to run. The drunk hunched his shoulders, going into some kind of fighting stance.</p>

<p>“You’re full of shit,” he snapped. “This is our party spot! Our temple!”</p>

<p>There was a strange look in his eyes, a half-mad gleam as he prepared to swing his makeshift weapon.</p>

<p>“This isn’t your little clubhouse anymore,” I stated, calling upon the manor’s mana.</p>

<p>Before the drunk Warrior could make a decision we’d both regret, I acted first. Snapping my wand, I cast Mana Bolt.</p>

<p><b>Mana Bolt</b><br><b>MP Cost: </b>100</p>

<p><em>The most basic of Wizard Skills, creating a bolt of non-elemental energy to strike his foes.</em></p>

<p>A colorless spark of mana shot out of my wand. For a moment, I was blinded; the bolt was far larger and brighter than it had been in the past.</p>

<p>The drunk flinched in pain as the bolt struck the crowbar, knocking it out of his hands. It tumbled to the ground as he clutched his stung fingers.</p>

<p>“Get out of here,” I repeated, channeling mana for another bolt. “There won’t be a second warning.”</p>

<p>The kids ran, heading right down the driveway. The drunk glanced at his crowbar, then thought better of it. He gave me one last, nasty look, before taking off after his friends.</p>

<p>“Tell everyone, Rotwood Manor has a new owner,” I yelled. “And I’m not going anywhere!”</p>

<p>“And don’t come back!” Lorelei shouted after them.</p>

<p>Watching them take off, I resolved to improve security. I wanted more than just automated lights or a few <em>No Trespassing </em>signs, though those wouldn’t hurt.</p>

<p>Lorelei sighed, her ghostly form fading as she returned to her more Human appearance.</p>

<p>“Damnit, and just when we were getting to the good part!” She gave me a sly look. “I didn’t even get my New Year’s kiss!”</p>

<p>“So, that was your doing?”</p>

<p>“Maybe?” she said mischievously. “I was having a beautiful dream, remembering some grand party the original Lorelei hosted.”</p>

<p>Her expression turned slightly melancholy. “It was New Year’s Eve, but she didn’t have anyone to kiss. She led a surprisingly lonely life, never finding a sweetheart, never marrying. I decided I wanted <em>my </em>life to be different, so I reached out to you.”</p>

<p>Hesitantly, I put an arm around her, and she snuggled close, her head against my chest.</p>

<p>“I wouldn’t mind sharing a dream like that again,” I admitted, thinking of how beautiful the manor had looked.</p>

<p>And how drop-dead gorgeous Lorelei had appeared when I had first spotted her.</p>

<p>But I steeled myself. No matter what was developing between me and Lorelei, there were more immediate concerns. That night’s incident might have been minor, but in a world dictated by the System, there were still idiots like that drunk who thought all problems could be solved with violence.</p>

<p>There were plenty of people who thought Might makes Right, especially with the video-game logic that dictated our lives. We needed the strength to protect our home.</p>

<p>As soon as I finished hanging the new lights and signs, I’d get us signed up to run Ashling State’s Dungeon.</p>
