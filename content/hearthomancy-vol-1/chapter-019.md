<p>Over the next few days, I made a determined effort to fix up the rooms off the main hall. They were on the small side, Lorelei told me they had once been servant’s quarters, but I figured the proximity of a working bathroom made them our best choice for the time being.</p>

<p>If I was going to invite Ayara or other Crafters to come live with us, they’d need proper rooms. And, if I was being honest with myself, I wanted a proper bedroom. Lorelei was proving to be an amorous lover, but fucking on my cot or on the floor was killing my back.</p>

<p>Having a mattress and a real bed became of critical importance.</p>

<p>Reaching out to a vendor I knew, I got a line on some cheap drywall. Apparently, a contractor had bought the stuff, expecting to land a big deal building a housing development. The job had fallen through, and he was losing money just storing the stuff.</p>

<p>I had to dip into my savings, but it was worth it. I even hired a few guys I knew, other contractors, to come out and help me hang the drywall. They were more than happy to help, especially when I promised fair wages and some free beers.</p>

<p>After a few days, and several thousand dollars, I had several serviceable rooms. I called Frank out, and the wildcat electrician helped me wire them up with electricity. They were basically white, undecorated rooms, several with boarded up windows, but it was a marked improvement over the ruins I had initially purchased.</p>

<p>I spent some time and money fixing up the bathroom too. Thankfully, the water boilers were actually in decent shape, and Frank was able to get them running with a bit of his magic.</p>

<p>“I’m not a plumber, and Merle will chew your ass out for the hacks we made, but they’ll work for now,” he said. “Still, I can’t believe my Skills leveled again! There really is something odd about this place.”</p>

<p>“Yeah, super weird,” I chuckled.</p>

<p>As far as Lorelei knew, nobody could see the Lair’s status without my permission. Frank didn’t know about the Boon, but I resolved to be more careful the next time I invited contractors to come in and help with repairs.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Ayara’s weekend visit arrived faster than I expected. I felt like there were still a million things we needed to do. Our blackboard was still crowded with incomplete tasks, but the Hearth Spirit assured me it was fine.</p>

<p>“Trust me,” she said. “She’ll take one look at this place and fall in love.”</p>

<p>I wasn’t so sure about that. The yard had been cleaned up, but the exterior of the house still looked like hell. Most of the windows were boarded up, and the roof was still covered in tarps along with the atrium. Rotwood Manor looked more like a desperate DIY disaster than anything else.</p>

<p>But, as I stood by the front entrance beside Lorelei, waiting for the Drow Blacksmith to arrive, I could feel the mana circulating around the house. The flow of power felt freer, less constricted, when I had first moved it. It rippled across the property, spilling out from the ever-blazing hearth.</p>

<p>I took a deep breath. “Yup, you’re right. It’s a magnificent house, and I’m sure she’ll see that clearly.”</p>

<p>Eventually, Ayara’s car appeared at the end of the driveway. She carefully parked it in front of the house, then stepped out of the vehicle.</p>

<p>She just stood there for a moment, her eyes wide, as she gazed at Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>“The End be praised,” she said faintly.</p>

<p>“Is that a good thing, or a bad thing?” I whispered to Lorelei.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit grinned. “Oh, good, I’m sure. Just see how excited she is!”</p>

<p>The Drow Blacksmith hurried over, a huge smile on her face. “Brent, it’s even more amazing than you said! The mana here is so rich, so powerful. I’ve only seen something like that in the oldest and most venerable of my people’s houses!”</p>

<p>“You have a perception Skill that lets you see mana?” I asked.</p>

<p>“A variant, for Crafters, Eyes of Discernment,” she answered. “I can’t see mana streams as clearly as a Wizard would, but I don’t need to. Your Lair is magnificent!”</p>

<p>Lorelei chuckled. “Told you!”</p>

<p>“Please…” The Drow took my hand. “Can you show me your hearth?”</p>

<p>“Sure. Come inside, I’ll give you the whole tour.”</p>

<p>Any concern I had for the state of our home evaporated as Ayara gawked at the house’s interior. She was incredibly impressed by the mana-reinforcement evident in the house’s struts and supports. The blazing hearth left her breathless.</p>

<p>I think Lorelei purposefully juiced up the flame, filling the fireplace with a kaleidoscopic flame reflecting countless hues of mana.</p>

<p>“It’s amazing.” The firelight played across her dusky, purple skin. “I’ve never seen a hearth like this! Drow Lairs are located within the Dungeons, so their flames are dyed by the dominant element in their Dungeon. House Druxsellaic’s Lair is mostly red and green from Fire and Earth mana from the London Dungeon. Rotwood Manor though? I can’t even identify all the elements present!”</p>

<p>I showed her the rooms we had just so recently fixed up.</p>

<p>My bedroom, just off the main hall, was pretty basic. I had moved all my furniture out of my parent’s place, so at least I had a bed, nightstand, and wardrobe, but that was about it. The other rooms were bare.</p>

<p>The kitchen now featured a stove, one I’d found on Chad’s List, along with the fridge I’d brought over from my parents’ house. The washing machine in the corner once belonged to my folks’ neighbor, and the dryer was another internet find.</p>

<p>“The microwave is new at least.” I chuckled.</p>

<p>Ayara laughed when I admitted I was worried about the spartan living conditions.</p>

<p>“Brent, I grew up in a Dungeon.” She put a hand on my shoulder. “The fact that you have electricity, running water, and there’s no Monsters constantly battering down your doors trying to eat you, is a luxury. There’s a reason why so many of us have moved to your reality.”</p>

<p>We showed her the basement as well. I honestly hadn’t explored it much yet; there simply hadn’t been time. The only area I knew was right by the stairs, where the electrical service panel was installed.</p>

<p>Rooms, twisting hallways, and chambers spun off in every direction. The few I had searched contained all kinds of old junk; steamer trunks full of clothes, dress mannequins, broken furniture, damaged beer kegs, and other junk left by DIK.</p>

<p>There had even been a wine cellar, though, everything not drunk by the frat had soured into vinegar.</p>

<p>I knew we’d need to clear it all out at some point; I was sure the steamer trunks had become prime real estate for the mice. Webs covered the rafters and ceiling joists.</p>

<p>“Unlike the fiction of your world, my people have little affinity for spiders,” Ayara said, looking at another web spun across a doorway.</p>

<p>“Just another thing on the list,” I sighed, feeling guilty.</p>

<p>The one storeroom off the stairs was full of jars for Bottling Mana. Randy had begun giving them to me, ensuring I always had containers to feed his need for crafting materials.</p>

<p>“It’s how I’ve been paying for all the repairs, there’s a huge demand for the stuff,” I explained.</p>

<p>“That makes sense, it’s difficult to unlock new Classes, especially in a world only recently integrated into The System,” Ayara agreed. “There’s a high barrier for the first unlock, but it becomes easier once the initial pioneers obtain an advanced class like Alchemist, Blacksmith—or Hearthomancer.”</p>

<p>“You think there’ll be more Hearthomancers out there?” Lorelei asked, clearly excited by the idea.</p>

<p>“Potentially.” She gazed around the room, taking in the huge flows of Earth mana around us. “This Lair is singular in its nature though, that I’m sure of. I imagine anyone else on the same path as Brent will likely have higher hurdles unless they have the supreme fortune of finding their own Rotwood Manor.”</p>

<p>I doubted there were many homes like mine out there in the world, though, I did wonder what made Rotwood Manor such a singular find.</p>

<p>“What’s that over there?”</p>

<p>Snapping from my thoughts, I saw Ayara was pointing to a door set into the wall.</p>

<p>“No clue.” I racked my brain, trying to remember the door.</p>

<p>Rootwood Manor was a mess of random closets and even a set of servant’s passages that ran through the upper floors of the residential wing. There were random doors galore; I had actually salvaged a number of them, stacking them in the main hall in case I could rehang them later.</p>

<p>The door was fairly mundane looking, especially compared to the more artistically carved doors that had been used in the public portions of the manor. It was a simple panel design, assembled using the same oak found all around the manor. The knob was made of brass, the metal somehow shiny and bright despite how much grime filled the basement.</p>

<p>Grabbing the knob, I felt a strange thrill run through me, like a shock of static electricity. I experienced an upwell of excitement, like something amazing was just behind the door.</p>

<p>Instead, all I found was Stinky, the cat.</p>

<p>He gave me a sour look, then waddled past me, heading back up the stairs.</p>

<p>“Stinky!” Lorelei called. “How did you get in there; did you get stuck!”</p>

<p>She drifted up the stairs, worriedly following the cat.</p>

<p>Ayara peeked around my shoulder. It was just an empty closet, without even a hanger rod or any kind of shelving. The plaster covering the walls was stained with age and riddled with hairline cracks.</p>

<p>“Damn.” I closed the door, somehow feeling utterly disappointed. Putting on a brave face, I turned to the Drow. “Do you want to see the atrium?”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Lorelei rejoined us a moment later, stating that Stinky had run outside and disappeared into the trees. She left out extra food and water for him as way of an apology. She still had no clue how the cat had managed to get stuck in the closet.</p>

<p>“I’ll check it from now on, every hour if I have to, to make sure it doesn’t happen again!” she pledged valiantly.</p>

<p>The atrium occupied almost an entire wing by itself; only a handful of rooms sat alongside the outside of the huge space. Sunlight, dyed blue by the tarps covering the walls and ceiling, made the atrium look extremely gloomy. The tile floor was stained nearly black by dirt. Even Lorelei’s Hearth Restoration Skill couldn’t clean the floor completely.</p>

<p>Metal frames and shelves were stacked against the one wall. From what I could tell, the frat had used the atrium as some kind of indoor sports field and party center. They had trashed the place, making it difficult to tell what the atrium had originally been intended for.</p>

<p>Ayara gazed up at the cracked glass. “I imagine it must have been beautiful, once.”</p>

<p>“It was.”</p>

<p>We both turned back, looking at Lorelei. She was drifting above us, eyes closed.</p>

<p>“Patrick Ganlts, Lorelei’s father, built it for her for her sixteenth birthday,” the Hearth Spirit said as she called upon the house’s memory. “It was to be a greenhouse, populated by the rarest, most beautiful plants from across the globe.”</p>

<p>Mana, bright as noonday sun, gathered around her. Ayara and I were blinded as we ducked away from the glare.</p>

<p>When we looked again, we were deep in Rootwood Manor’s lost memories. The ruined atrium was gone, replaced by a vivid greenhouse. The twisted metal had become planters and shelves full of potted flowers. Vines grew everywhere, and there were even a few miniature fruit trees growing out of oversized pots. The air was humid and scented with pollen.</p>

<p>As we watched, phantoms of Lorelei, the original Lorelei, drifted through the room. She watered plants, trimmed vines, and picked fruit, her form blurring as the past was replayed at high speed. Days, weeks, and years melded together into a single, looping moment.</p>

<p>“It’s beautiful,” Ayara said.</p>

<p>I had to agree.</p>

<p>With a snap, the loop broke and the memory ended. The bright plants and sun-filled air were replaced by blue-tinged gloom. It was nearly heartbreaking, going back to the atrium’s devastated state.</p>

<p>“It was beautiful once,” Lorelei said in a wistful tone as she reappeared. “But it will be beautiful again!”</p>

<p>At her words, mana surged around us. Bright, shining Life mana was joined by the powerful green flow of Earth mana. For a moment the memory came surging back, the plants blooming to phantom life around us.</p>

<p>Ayara turned to me. “When can I move in? Where can I set up my forge?”</p>
