<p>The entrance of the Dungeon led to a huge stone chamber. The walls and floors were roughly carved out of dull, brown bedrock. Torches, set in sconces, ran in regular intervals along the walls. Each glowed with a bright, red flame, powered by Fire mana.</p>

<p>The Jack ran her hands over a series of crude glyphs cut into the wall. They were identical to the ones on the entrance.</p>

<p>“What do they mean?” she asked.</p>

<p>“We’ve only translated the most basic symbols. There are thousands of different characters,” the Drow Blacksmith responded. “The Dungeons speak their own language, but those runes are found all through their halls and chambers. You learn to spot the ones that mean danger, exit, or opportunity.”</p>

<p>“Right, come on, we’re not even on the first floor yet,” Victor called. “I’ll point out the basic runes the lady mentioned, so pay attention!”</p>

<p>Following him, I couldn’t help but scope out the Drow woman. Outside her striking appearance, I was also intrigued by her Class.</p>

<p>The Drow had lived alongside the System for generations. They had unlocked many advanced classes, like Blacksmith, Leatherworker, Enchanter, and more. Apparently, they had access to more powerful combat Classes as well.</p>

<p>The Drow occasionally stole glances my way as well. She seemed particularly interested in Lorelei, who hovered above my shoulder like an overprotective ghost.</p>

<p>The two Warriors pushed and shoved to see who would be the first one to head down the stairs we found at the end of the room. I rolled my eyes, annoyed with their antics, while Victor yelled at them to act like adults.</p>

<p>“This isn’t some fucking weekend excursion into the woods,” the guide snapped. “You fuck up, you don’t just die, you kill everyone else. I don’t want to be killed by two fucking idiots!”</p>

<p>Lorelei gave the two men a silent raspberry when they weren’t looking. I made a point of keeping a healthy distance from them; I didn’t have much choice who I was partied with, but there was no way in hell I was going to let those two idiots get me or Lorelei hurt.</p>

<p>The stairs were made from the same stone as the entry chamber. We slowly descended, the torches appearing every so often to light the way.</p>

<p>“Where’s everyone else, the parties that went ahead of us?” the Jack asked.</p>

<p>Victor shook his head. “They’re following their own routes and paths. When you enter the Dungeon, it sets you on your own distinct course, shifting and changing to meet its own designs. Typically, you won’t cross paths with other adventurers till deeper in.”</p>

<p>“Is it alive?” the Jack asked. “The Dungeon, I mean.”</p>

<p>The Blacksmith shook her head. “Nobody knows.”</p>

<p>Lorelei and I shared a glance. The way the mana flowed around us certainly made me think the place was alive, at least in the same way Rotwood Manor was. Mana flowed around us like blood through a living creature’s veins. I could almost see the earth around us moving, making me think of a creature shifting during slumber.</p>

<p>For a moment, all I could picture was us descending the stairs like a group of fools walking down a dragon’s gullet. I couldn’t help but glance up, looking for teeth or fangs lining the ceiling.</p>

<p>I sighed in relief as we reached the bottom of the stairs. The first floor of the Dungeon looked like a decrepit, waterlogged ruin. Ancient marble pillars and huge, eroded statues loomed out of the gloom. They were covered with glowing moss and lichen.</p>

<p>Torches burned here and there. The Fire mana was diluted by other elements, leaving them with a weak, feeble glow.</p>

<p>“Welcome to the first floor,” Victor said, double-checking his armor and unsheathing his sword. “You should have done your research before signing up, but we’ll mostly encounter bugs and lizard Monsters down here. They should be low-level, but even low-level Monsters can kill, so be careful!”</p>

<p>The battle plan was simple. The Jack and I would attack at range, trying to get some hits, while the Drow and Victor tanked. The two warriors, both armed with short spears, would get hits in when they could while focusing on crowd control.</p>

<p>To their credit, the two warriors looked serious. The rookie was nervous. He paused occasionally to mop his brow, much to his friend’s amusement.</p>

<p>“The layout changes every time, but it’s not exactly a maze, at least for the first few floors,” Victor instructed. “It should be a straight shot, just keep an eye on your surroundings and listen to what I fucking tell you!”</p>

<p>Lorelei actually saluted, which earned a gruff chuckle<em> </em>from the guide.</p>

<p>He was a hardass, but I could respect that, especially in a job that involved life and death. Besides, as far as I could tell, his sharp words were aimed at the two Warriors and the wilting Jack.</p>

<p>Leaving the first chamber, we ventured down a short hallway. The floor beneath us <em>squished</em>, and moisture seeped out of the cracks between the stones. Everything felt wet, and the humidity was unreal. The smell of brackish water and rotting vegetation was very strong.</p>

<p>“Up ahead,” Victor whispered.</p>

<p>We followed him through a narrow doorway, into another ruined chamber. Ahead, sitting around the edge of a small, brackish pond, was a group of Monsters.</p>

<p>The four creatures looked like oversized roaches, each a meter long. Their brown shells were paper-thin, but the pincers they used to tear up and eat swamp weeds looked sharp.</p>

<p>“Ewww, nasty,” the Jack said.</p>

<p>“Giant Roaches, level two.” Victor flexed his fingers around his sword. “Okay, Wizard, Jack, you’re up. Blacksmith, with me.”</p>

<p>Lorelei drifted above me as I held out my wand and cast Mana Bolt.</p>

<p>Unlike our practice at home, the projectile I fired was much smaller, and not nearly as bright. It snapped out of the end of my wand before hitting the back of the nearest roach. The bug chittered in shock and pain as the Skill cracked its chitin.</p>

<p>A moment later, the Jack hurled a knife. It went wide, missing its target but skimming the leg of the bug next to it. All four bugs turned and charged at us, clearly enraged by our unprovoked attack.</p>

<p>Without missing a beat, Victor stepped forward. With a single swing of his sword, he cleaved the foremost Giant Roach<em> </em>in half. The Blacksmith came in behind him, smashing a bug with her mace. The Monster practically exploded under her weighty attack.</p>

<p>The Warriors<em> </em>clambered in, stabbing everything they could with their spears. Lorelei and I got in a few more hits, and within moments all four bugs were dead.</p>

<p>“Hell yeah!” the rookie Warrior<em> </em>cheered.</p>

<p>The Jack’s complexion had a distinct green tinge to it.</p>

<p>Lorelei just shrugged. “That’s it?”</p>

<p>“That’s it,” Victor confirmed, flicking bug guts off his blade. “It’s straightforward, but don’t get cocky. Overconfidence can kill anyone.”</p>

<p>With that in mind, we began clearing the floor. Victor told us none of the Monsters dropped loot, but we kept our eyes peeled for chests that might contain simple but useful items.</p>

<p>“Always figure out a way to divide the loot <em>before</em> you head into the Dungeon,” Victor cautioned us. “Anything you find on this trip will be bought by the college, the reward divided between all of you, but if you go somewhere else, be ready to share.”</p>

<p>We cleared another chamber, defeating a Giant Roach that was accompanied by a Pond Lizard. The Monster had looked like an oversized gecko, but it had taken a surprising amount of damage before finally dying.</p>

<p>The rookie Warrior<em> </em>leveled up. Lorelei resisted the urge to give him a high five.</p>

<p>It was hard to argue against their exuberance. I could feel my Class and Skills growing too, but I tried to listen to Victor’s advice. I had no interest in dying to some oversized bug. Death would mean abandoning Lorelei and Rotwood Manor too, and I couldn’t bear the thought of what would happen to the Hearth Spirit.</p>

<p>“Okay, we’re taking a break,” Victor declared after we defeated a trio of Pond Lizards<em>. </em>“We’re about a quarter of the way through, we should pace ourselves.”</p>

<p>The veteran Warrior<em> </em>was chomping at the bit to keep going, but I could tell his friend needed a break. The younger man pushed himself hard in every fight, stabbing wildly with his spear. I didn’t know much about martial tactics, but he seemed to be overexerting himself.</p>

<p>I quickly scanned the area, looking for the runes Victor had pointed out. There were traps in the area, but they were clearly marked with a specific rune, which looked like an upside-down lightning bolt. We had spotted a few along the floor and walls in previous rooms, but it had been easy to stay clear.</p>

<p>Confirming the area was safe, I took a seat on a broken column. A moment later, the Drow Blacksmith came over.</p>

<p>“Do you mind if I join you?” she asked.</p>

<p>“Not at all,” Lorelei answered before I could respond.</p>

<p>She took a seat next to me. Up close, I could see the plates of her armor were riveted over heavy Teflon rather than the chainmail I’d come to associate with heavy armor. The entire suit seemed to be tailor made for the Blacksmith, I assumed she had crafted it herself.</p>

<p>“I’m Lorelei,” the Hearth Spirit said, extending a hand.</p>

<p>The Drow removed her helmet, then shook her hand. “Drax’Ayarellum of House Druxsellaic.”</p>

<p>Lorelei looked flummoxed. “Drax Ayar–”</p>

<p>The Drow smiled. “Please, just call me Ayara.”</p>

<p>I nodded respectfully. “I’m Brent.”</p>

<p>Ayara glanced at the others. Victor was talking to the Jack while the two Warriors were poking around the far side of the room, looking for treasure.</p>

<p>She leaned in. “I am deeply impressed to meet a Master of the Sacred Flames. I didn’t know that your species had unlocked that Class.”</p>

<p>I was confused. “Master of the Sacred Flames?”</p>

<p>Ayara tilted her head. “Maybe I’m wrong, but Lorelei isn’t just some <em>familiar</em>. She is a spirit of your home’s sacred flames.”</p>

<p>I glanced at Lorelei, realization dawning. “I think we’re dealing with different names for the same thing? But you’re right, Lorelei is a Hearth Spirit, and I’m a Hearthomancer.”</p>

<p>Ayara nodded, smiling. “You’re probably right. Sometimes different species have different names for the same Class.”</p>

<p>Lorelei grinned. “So, the Drow have Hearthomancers and Lairs too?”</p>

<p>“Yes.” Ayara nodded. “The Drow have spent centuries living within the Dungeons. Masters of the Sacred Flames carve out Lairs, areas of safety for our people to make their homes. Their hearths contain ancient, mana-infused flames, carried all the way from our lost homeland.”</p>

<p>“Wow, so cool,” Lorelei gasped.</p>

<p>I had to admit, it sounded pretty amazing.</p>

<p>“I’m only level five, I don’t think I’m quite at that point yet, but I aspire to get there,” I told the Drow. “There’s virtually nothing out there about Hearthomancers, I might be the first Human to have unlocked it. Even if the Masters of the Sacred Flames end up being different from my Class, I’d still love to learn about them.”</p>

<p>“I’ll be happy to tell you what I can,” Ayara answered, her expression suddenly growing sad. “My people are nomads, forced from our homeland. For nearly two centuries we have wandered the Dungeons. The fires the Masters of the Sacred Flames tend are a direct link to our past, to what we’ve lost.”</p>

<p>“Okay, break time’s over!” Victor called, waving to the two Warriors. “Come on, didn’t you say you wanted to clear the first floor? Then get your asses over here!”</p>

<p>Ayara stood, putting her helmet back on. “After this is over, how about we get a beer or something.”</p>

<p>I grinned. “I’d like that a lot.”</p>
