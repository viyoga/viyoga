<p>Ishikana bullied the library staff to bring us lunch from the college’s cafeteria. It wasn’t much, just sandwiches and chips, but the Arachne neatly ate four servings to my one. I mentally upped our meal budget, resolving to ask the Scholar to contribute to our household budget. Check or no check, she’d eat us out of house and home in no time.</p>

<p>I took a break to step outside and contact my parents; I hadn’t heard from them in a couple of days, which was odd. Calling them, I just got their voicemail.</p>

<p>“Maybe I should stop by on the way home,” I muttered to myself.</p>

<p>I texted Ayara, just to make sure she hadn’t gotten in trouble at work for skipping the day before, but she confirmed she was fine. We went back and forth a little bit, and I discovered the Drow Blacksmith had a habit of peppering her messages with cute emojis.</p>

<p>Grinning to myself, I stepped back into the library.</p>

<p>I was surprised to see there was a newspaper and magazine rack by the reception desk; I couldn’t imagine a lot of students browsing through the local paper. Still, that reminded me of something that had been at the back of my mind.</p>

<p>Grabbing the last few editions of the local newspaper, I flipped through their contents. There weren’t any updates about the murder my parents had mentioned, but I found an interesting entry buried in the obituaries.</p>

<p>Larry Gnotts had only been in his thirties, a few years older than me when he had died. The entry about him was painfully short, apparently being kicked out of school had been the crowning achievement of his life.</p>

<p>But I wasn’t interested in the details. Instead, I was focused on the picture included in the obituary.</p>

<p>It had been cropped from a larger image. It showed Larry, with a beer in one hand and a bong in the other. He was wearing a t-shirt, with the Greek letters Delta Iota Kappa written across his chest.</p>

<p>He’d been a member of DIK which meant he had lived in my house.</p>

<p>I got a cold feeling in the pit of my stomach.</p>

<p>The method of his death aligned too closely with all the Satanic graffiti around my property and the disturbing visions I had received. I couldn’t help but think of the first one, of the cloaked figures conducting some strange ritual in the residential wing of the manor. <em>What had they been up to, had they been members of DIK?</em></p>

<p>Despite their infamous history, I realized I knew almost nothing about the frat beyond the rumors I’d heard growing up. I wasn’t even sure the exact reason they’d been kicked out of school, just that there had been some kind of legendarily hedonistic party that had been the final straw for the college.</p>

<p>Returning to the study room, I waved over the head librarian. “I want everything you have on the frat Delta Iota Kappa.”</p>

<p>“What? Why?” she asked suspiciously.</p>

<p>Ishikana glowered at the woman. “I believe we were promised unfettered access to your library. Now shut up and listen to him.”</p>

<p>The Arachne hissed, her spider half shuddering like she was about to strike. The head librarian went deathly pale and rushed from the room.</p>

<p>Grinning, Ishikana took a haughty pose, her hand below her chin.</p>

<p>“You enjoy doing that, don’t you?” I said conversationally as I returned to my seat next to her. “Lording over others.”</p>

<p>“I do,” she agreed, giving me an approving look. “I worked hard to amass the knowledge and power I possess. At times, I enjoy using it as a blunt club against those who stand in my way or annoy me.”</p>

<p>I gave her a hard look. “If you take that attitude with me, I’m kicking your ass out of my house, check or no check.”</p>

<p>Her smile softened. “Naturally. Power respects power.”</p>

<p>I snorted. “I’m not that powerful.”</p>

<p>“Yet,” she corrected. “Given time, I suspect you and your Lair shall bloom to be a true seat of power in this world.”</p>

<p>“Shit, I don’t want that. I just want to be left alone, to fix up my house and live my life,” I replied.</p>

<p>She nodded sagely. “And trust me when I say it’ll take a great deal of power to obtain that dream, but I am <em>certain</em> you will obtain it.”</p>

<p>“If we don’t all die first,” I muttered.</p>

<p>Before we could continue our conversation, the head librarian bustled back in, an embarrassed look on her face. She put down several moldy, water-damaged cardboard boxes.</p>

<p>“What is this?” Ishikana demanded.</p>

<p>“I’m sorry!” the librarian backed up, hands raised. “We don’t have digital records from 2010 or back, they were lost when we changed systems. All we have are paper records!”</p>

<p>I poked a box. It <em>squelched</em>.</p>

<p>“We kept the old disciplinary records for the Greek System in one of the original buildings. They were just kind of tossed in the corner,” the librarian explained hastily.</p>

<p>Ishikana tapped a chitin-covered finger against her arm.</p>

<p>“Anyway, it appears there was a water leak or something and most of the records regarding Delta Iota Kappa were destroyed!” the woman pleaded.</p>

<p>I snorted. “Seriously?”</p>

<p>“I’m so sorry!” the woman repeated. “I’ve sent my staff to check the rest of the campus, including the registrar's office, but—”</p>

<p>“Then you best hope you find more than this,” Ishikana said, gesturing to the soggy boxes.</p>

<p>The head librarian scurried out again.</p>

<p>Ishikana glanced at me. “Now that she’s gone, care to explain why these papers are so important?”</p>

<p>I shook my head. “Just a hunch regarding a local murder.”</p>

<p>She raised a perfectly sculpted eyebrow. “Then we shall devote our full resources to seeing if your hunch bears fruit or not.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Even with Ishikana’s Skill, it had been nearly impossible to recover anything from the water-damaged records. As we worked, I filled her in on my neighbors, Larry Gnotts, and the strange visions we’d experienced.</p>

<p>“You think your neighbors were in the frat?” she asked when I mentioned Larry being on the goat farmer’s property.</p>

<p>“Maybe? But I’d like some kind of proof, a photo or something,” I answered.</p>

<p>“That still doesn’t explain their preoccupation with your home,” she replied. “They were kicked out of college years ago, yet they still flock around their old home, like moths to a flame?”</p>

<p>I grunted in agreement.</p>

<p>At the bottom of one of the boxes, I found the disciplinary committee’s notes on DIK. The actual trial records were reduced to mush, but the committee’s secretary had kept detailed notes.</p>

<p>“Their final party was Feb 7th, 2008—a Thursday? Weird,” I muttered. “Anyway, it looks like the police got called out after some of the attendees fled the party. The frat boys were trying to have an orgy or something?”</p>

<p>I resolved to douse my entire property in bleach at the thought of a bunch of sweaty frat boys fucking <em>anywhere</em> inside the manor.</p>

<p>Ishikana used her Skill to restore another sheet of loose-leaf paper. “Surprise, surprise, there was also something about a goat? Apparently, our frat boys were trying to sacrifice it somewhere out in the backyard.”</p>

<p>I had a flashback to the bonfire circle and the dancing figures. We hadn’t found anything indicating a history of animal sacrifice on my property, but I was dead certain it hadn’t begun with DIK.</p>

<p>“The cops showed up and got into a fight with the frat boys,” I read, picking out details that I could from the water-damaged text. “A couple of them got arrested. When the police raided the house, they found signs of—Satan worship, underage drinking, drug use, animal abuse—”</p>

<p>The list ran on and on.</p>

<p>There were a few photos, polaroids, at the bottom of the box. Several were close-ups of the Satanic symbols that had been carved and painted around the property.</p>

<p>“Satan?” Ishikana frowned. “They worshiped Humanity’s ideal of evil?”</p>

<p>“Just one of them, we’ve got a bunch,” I answered. “And it says here it wasn’t actually Satan—it was, some guy named Baphomet?”</p>

<p>A quick web on my phone search revealed Baphomet was a demon, supposedly worshiped by the Knights Templar back in the Middle Ages. From there, he was incorporated into a dozen different esoteric religions, representing forbidden knowledge and arcane perfection.</p>

<p>“And surprise, surprise, Baphomet is a goat man,” I said, showing Ishikana a picture I’d pulled up on my phone.</p>

<p>“Is there any history of Baphomet worship here in Ashling Grove?” she asked.</p>

<p>“That’s—a really fucking disturbing question,” I admitted.</p>

<p>Our town had its fair share of churches, but I’d never even heard of any kind of occultism.</p>

<p><em>Outside the Order of Stone</em>, I mentally amended, but that was just a rumor. The mayor’s office put out a notice every few years affirming the group’s presence in town and their upstanding morals.</p>

<p>We dug deeper into the boxes. Sadly, there was nothing recorded in DIK’s defense, no explanation for their behavior. Maybe they hadn’t tried to argue their case, it was impossible to say.</p>

<p>It was too late in the day to start researching Baphomet, the Library staff was already getting ready to go home for the day. Even Ishikana’s bullying wouldn’t get them to put in overtime.</p>

<p>“Come on, I want to hit the bank and then head home, I’m worried about Lorelei,” I told the Scholar.</p>

<p>“Fine, but is there a bakery that is still open?” Ishikana asked.</p>

<p>“Bakery?”</p>

<p>She gave me a slightly embarrassed look.</p>

<p>“Sure, there’s probably one still open,” I said.</p>

<p>Returning to my truck, Ishikana once again climbed into the back. Driving through town we attracted more stares. The Scholar was indifferent, but I felt uncomfortable; there was something unsettling about the whole thing. The Arachne might have been used to unfriendly looks, but I was a local and I’d never had any problems with my friends or neighbors.</p>

<p>At least, until recently.</p>

<p>“Your town seems to have an unhealthy preoccupation with the upcoming solar eclipse,” Ishikana said as we drove down main street.</p>

<p>Banners and pennants for the upcoming town festival were everywhere. Several shops and businesses had already set up decorations, including haloed suns and shadowy moons.</p>

<p>“Unhealthy?” I shrugged. “I don’t know, it's just something we do every few years, whenever there’s a solar eclipse. Trust me, we’re just a normal Pennsylvania town, and like any town, we have our weird traditions. Our state is famous because we try to predict the end of winter using a woodchuck, for god’s sake.”</p>

<p>Despite my bluster, I couldn’t help but think back to my vision where the sun was overshadowed by the moon. Was there something deeper going on, was the whole town involved?</p>

<p>The bank was just closing as I stopped to deposit Ishikana’s check. The teller was rude, but at that point, I just wanted to get home, so I ignored his rotten attitude.</p>

<p>There was still one bakery open by that point. Parking behind the building, I stayed in the truck as Ishikana skittered inside. She returned a moment later, several cardboard boxes lashed to her back and a donut in her hand.</p>

<p>“Delicious!” she said, her eyes shining as she took another bite. “I love chocolate glazed donuts!”</p>

<p>“Did you clear out their stock?” I asked, looking at the sheer number of boxes she had carried out.</p>

<p>“Hush, they were going to throw them out anyway,” she replied as she climbed into the bed of the truck.</p>

<p>I couldn't help but laugh at that.</p>

<p>Returning to Rotwood Manor, I was relieved there was no sign of Mitch, Candy, or Josh. I did notice Officer Penkle and Decker. Their squad car was parked behind some bushes on Josh’s property. The two cops gave us a deathly glare as we rolled past, but they didn’t throw on their lights and try to pull us over.</p>

<p>There were lights coming from the goat farm. The barn was lit up and people were streaming in and out of the doors. It was impossible to miss the sound of raucous activity from within.</p>

<p>“Neighborhood watch my ass.” Resisting the urge to investigate, I quickly unlocked the gate to my Lair and drove inside.</p>

<p>Parking in my customary spot, I offered to help Ishikana unload her boxes, but she had eaten the contents of several of them on the way home. Silently, I marveled how a woman who’d just eaten two cakes and a dozen donuts could look so fit and slim, at least for her human half.</p>

<p>Stepping inside, I was overjoyed when Lorelei appeared and threw her arms around my neck.</p>

<p>“Welcome back!” she cheered.</p>

<p>“It’s good to be back,” I admitted, kissing her.</p>

<p>All the unease, the pressure I’ve felt while being in town, faded as I held her.</p>

<p>Ayara wasn’t back from work yet, she had a long commute, so I decided to cook a simple dinner. I’d forgotten to hit the grocery store, but we had enough in the fridge to cook up a frugal meal, especially when Ishikana unexpectedly offered several of the loaves of bread she had purchased.</p>

<p>When the Blacksmith got home, we had a pleasant meal. Sure, we were likely under threat by a bunch of animal-sacrificing lunatics, but we were together. Avoiding the dire topics of the day, we instead chatted about Ayara’s job and her progress at her forge. Ishikana even contributed an amusing story about the Los Angeles Dungeon.</p>

<p>As we ate, the fire in the hearth burned merrily and bright, pushing back the gloom of the darkening night.</p>
