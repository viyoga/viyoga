<p>I drove several blocks, weaving left and right at each intersection, almost at random, before I finally pulled over. Taking a deep breath to steady my shaking hands, I got out my phone and texted the others through the group chat. Lorelei confirmed everything at home was fine, and Ishikana reported no issues at the library.</p>

<p>Ayara texted back, asking what was wrong. Taking another steadying breath, I replied I would explain later but that I was safe for the moment.</p>

<p><em>What was going on? </em>I knew Mrs. Johnson was a bit daft. She was always going on about UFOs, alien conspiracies, and the Order of Stone, but she never struck me as the kind of lunatic to shoot at people.</p>

<p>I was startled out of my daze when someone threw a half-filled coffee cup into my windshield.</p>

<p>Looking up, I spotted a group of teens. They were standing on a nearby corner, shouting insults my way. I was about ready to get out of my truck when I saw a cop car in my rearview. It was impossible to say if it was Penkle and Decker but given all the weirdness in town I didn’t trust any of the cops.</p>

<p>Pulling back onto the road, I decided to skip going to the local grocery store. There was every chance I’d be arrested for shoplifting or get my truck’s tires slashed.</p>

<p>Instead, I decided to run an experiment. I knew the others were safe, at least for the time being, so I decided to head to the next town over.</p>

<p>There was almost a feeling of relief as I drove past the sign that said <em>Leaving Ashling Grove</em>. It was my hometown, I’d spent almost my entire life there, but for the moment it was enemy territory.</p>

<p>Driving down a series of winding back roads, it took me about thirty minutes to reach the next town, a little podunk place somehow even smaller than Ashling Grove.</p>

<p>I rolled through both of the town’s stop lights and pulled into the only grocery store. The few pedestrians I passed didn’t even bat an eye.</p>

<p>Stepping through the store’s sliding doors, I paused for a moment, bracing for an attack, but nothing happened. Blinking, I grabbed my phone and pulled up the shopping list Ayara and I had written up. Pulling a cart from a nearby line, I slowly wound through the store, knocking things off the list.</p>

<p>I passed a few other shoppers, but nobody was paying attention to me. After enduring the hateful stares from the friends and neighbors I had grown up with, it was a relief to bask in the indifference of strangers.</p>

<p>The only people who talked to me were the deli counter clerk and the woman manning the checkout line. Loading my bags, I headed out to the parking lot. For a moment I just stood by my tailgate, taking one deep breath after another.</p>

<p>But I knew I couldn’t just stand there. The women I loved were still in danger. Hell,<em> I</em> was still in danger; there was no way I was going to up and abandon my home. Texting the others, I confirmed I had finished shopping and I’d be dropping the groceries off at Rotwood Manor before going back to Ashling State to pick up Ishikana later in the day. I warned the Arachne to stay in the study room until I texted her that I was outside.</p>

<p>Driving back to town, I felt the familiar tension build up. A small headache was forming behind my brow when I passed the sign that said <em>Welcome to Ashling Grove. </em>Below the sign, someone had hung a banner. <em>Solar Eclipse Festival – October 14th!</em></p>

<p>For some reason, I knew without a shadow of a doubt that the town festival would mark the end of our tribulations, one way or another.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It seemed like Herb was hosting another party by the time I got home. Several cars were parked in front of his house, and more were in the pasture in front of his barn. I noted that Penkle and Decker’s squad car was amongst their numbers.</p>

<p>Unlocking and relocking the gate, I allowed my Lair’s mana to balm my weary soul, the powerful, familiar current filling me with renewed strength.</p>

<p>Lorelei greeted me as soon as I stepped out of the truck, materializing to wrap me in a tight hug.</p>

<p>“Welcome home,” she whispered.</p>

<p>I kissed her. “It’s good to be back, even if it’s just for a few hours.” I sighed and ran my hand through my hair. “Come on, help me out, I’ve got ice cream in the back of the truck, and I don’t want it to melt.”</p>

<p>“Ice cream!” She gave me a high five when I confirmed I’d bought strawberry, her favorite flavor.</p>

<p>As we carried everything inside, I gave her an abridged version of what had happened in town. She already had an inkling something had occurred, given the bond between us, but she still couldn’t believe Mrs. Johnson had taken a shot at us.</p>

<p>“She was so nice!” She sighed. “The last time we were over, to fix their sump pump, she offered us cookies. Sure, they were shaped like aliens, but they smelled so good!”</p>

<p>I shook my head. “I don’t get it either. Maybe they’re being brainwashed or something? But I couldn’t see the Johnsons going to Herb’s barn to get indoctrinated.”</p>

<p>“Maybe there’s something in the water?” Lorelei suggested.</p>

<p>“Makes me glad we’re on well water then,” I replied.</p>

<p>Texting Ayara, I asked her to message us when she left work. Normally, I had no interest in being so overbearing with my lover’s lives, but at that point, I believed we all needed to be hyper-vigilant.</p>

<p>I spent the rest of the afternoon practicing out in the backyard, getting used to the new rapier Ayara had given me. I wasn’t much of a swordsman, but Lorelei found several tutorial videos online. She propped up her tablet and walked me through each exercise, checking my form and execution.</p>

<p>“I think you need to put more weight on your front foot,” she said, rewinding the footage and replaying it for me.</p>

<p>I tried to copy the instructor. There was no way in hell I’d pick up a Proficiency with just one day’s worth of practice, but the sword at least felt less awkward in my grip. After we finished, I decided to ask Ayara for some follow-up lessons. The Blacksmith’s Proficiencies were for two-handed swords, maces, and shields, but I was sure she could give me some good tips.</p>

<p>Ishikana texted me, indicating she was wrapping things up at the library. I once again asked Lorelei to defend the homestead while I went out. That time, I decided to bring my sword with me, resting it on the passenger seat as I returned to town.</p>

<p>The ominous, crushing feeling returned in full force as I drove toward the campus. I took side roads, going around the town’s main street, but I could still sense the hostility in the air.</p>

<p>Reaching the library, I saw Ishikana through the lobby’s glass doors. She seemed to be in a heated argument with the head librarian. Even though she towered over the woman, the librarian was yelling at the Scholar, her face red with rage.</p>

<p>Ishikana gave one last shrill hiss at the woman, then marched out of the building. Without saying a word, she climbed into the bed of my truck. As soon as she was secure, I took off, unwilling to idle and make us targets.</p>

<p>“Fools, damn fools,” the Scholar cursed.</p>

<p>“Everything okay?” I asked.</p>

<p>“Ashling State has banned us from their campus,” the Arachne responded. “They claim we’re a disturbance—an unwholesome<em> element! </em>Those bastards, don’t they know who I am? I’ll bring the whole weight of the Dire Archive down upon their misbegotten heads!”</p>

<p>“Not to push your buttons, but what is the Dire Archive?” I asked.</p>

<p>Ishikana settled back in the bed of the truck, resting her bulk between the ladder racks. She was still fuming, but it seemed my question had the intended effect of distracting her.</p>

<p>“I guess I never explained my station, did I? Apologies,” she said. “My homeland is a technocracy, a nation ruled by scholars and so-called experts. These factions are aligned with various libraries, all jostling for power by accumulating knowledge and secrets.”</p>

<p>I thought about that for a moment. “So, you’re with the Dire Archive?”</p>

<p>“Yes, we specialize in what my countrymen consider <em>deep lore</em>, specifically about the System and how it works,” Ishikana answered, her tone becoming calmer and more professional. “We’re not a major player in government, we merely exist as part of it in support of our quest to learn more about the nature of our reality.”</p>

<p>Chuckling, I replied, “I’m glad Rotwood Manor hasn’t become some kind of chip in a high-stakes game of government power.”</p>

<p>“Oh, I wouldn’t say that,” Ishikana replied. “Even if we play little role in government, we still jockey for power like everyone else. I fully intend to show those snotty Goblins in the Chasm Archive that our knowledge is utterly superior to their pathetic insights!”</p>

<p>The party at Herb’s farm was in full swing as we reached Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>Disturbingly, I noticed Herb’s herd of goats seemed to be thinning out. Unless he was keeping them elsewhere, it appeared as if he had fewer animals than he had just a few weeks before.</p>

<p>After we parked and got out of the truck, Ishikana reached into her spandex garment and pulled a roll of papers out of a hidden pocket.</p>

<p>“I wasn’t able to get much done today, but I did manage to sneak out some truly interesting documents.”</p>

<p>Lorelei appeared. “Oh, show me, show me!”</p>

<p>Sitting on the porch steps, I unrolled the papers, revealing a series of photocopied documents and a handful of photos.</p>

<p>My eyes instantly went to the largest of pictures, which looked to be some kind of group photo snapped during a frat party. It had been taken within Rotwood Manor, in the main hall. The walls were covered in graffiti, and the floor was littered with beer cans and cigarette butts.</p>

<p>A crooked banner hanging behind them proclaimed <em>DIK New Year’s 2005!</em></p>

<p>Much of the print was water-damaged, but it was easy to recognize Larry Gnotts in his Delta Iota Kappa shirt. The picture from his obituary had been cropped from the larger photo.</p>

<p>To his left stood Herb, his arm around Larry’s shoulder. In the lower right corner of the picture was Josh, sprawled out on the floor unconscious. Mitch and Candy were standing to the far left. Candy was wearing a shirt that proudly proclaimed she was a <em>Bitch for DIK!</em></p>

<p>There were a couple of other people in the photo, but I didn’t recognize them. Most of the photo was a blurred, ruined mess, likely damaged by the same leaky pipe that had ruined the other DIK documents we had seen.</p>

<p>“Michael Easten, died March 19th, 2007—alcohol poisoning,” Ishikan said, pointing to a blurry section of the photo. “Next to him is Elton Linkon—September 22, 2006, same cause of death.”</p>

<p>I couldn’t help but shiver as I put the picture down.</p>

<p>The rest of the bundle was documents from their expulsion trial. It confirmed Herb and Josh had all been members of DIK; Mitch had been the president. They had been seniors in 2008 when they had been expelled and the frat forcefully disbanded by the college.</p>

<p>Ishikana ran down the list, pointing out more casualties. Of the forty-plus members of DIK, only fifteen were still alive. Everyone else had died, either of drug or alcohol abuse, accidents, or <em>mysterious circumstances</em>.</p>

<p>“I wasn’t able to fully dig up what happened to them, but I imagine they were bad ends,” the Scholar added in an apologetic tone. “I started noticing a funny thing though.”</p>

<p>She pulled another sheet of paper from her hidden pocket. “A number of them died on the same day as your town festival—the day of a solar eclipse.”</p>

<p>My blood turned to ice. I thought of all the bloody halos we’d seen around my Lair, scrawled and scratched into walls and onto the boulders around the bonfire pit.</p>

<p>Ishikana shook her head. “Critical Conjecture tells me that if we look back at the history of Rotwood Manor, and the deaths that happened around your property, a number of them happened on the same day as a solar eclipse.”</p>

<p>Lorelei looked at her in horror. “What does that mean?”</p>

<p>The Scholar frowned. “It tells me we better prepare, because there’s another eclipse happening soon, and I’m positive the survivors of DIK will come after us.”</p>
