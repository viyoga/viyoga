<p>“Somehow, the biggest letdown of the day was discovering our Lair has a freaking Dungeon,” Lorelei sighed.</p>

<p>“We already knew there was a Dungeon,” Ayara reminded her. “We just didn’t know how to access it.”</p>

<p>Returning to the basement, we discovered no matter how many times we closed and opened the door under the stairs, it always led to the Oubliette of the Forgotten. Disturbingly, we realized it changed location each time, opening into a different part of the labyrinth. Lorelei peeked in and confirmed each change put us in a different, unfamiliar region she hadn’t mapped with her Proficiency yet.</p>

<p>I checked my cell phone after returning to the main hall. I had one new message, a voicemail from Professor Scott Brown. His response was brief, just an address and a time, the next morning at 10am.</p>

<p>“Well, that’s his home address,” Lorelei confirmed. “He doesn’t have any presence on social media, and it doesn’t look like he’s teaching anywhere else. It looks like he just up and quit one day, moving out into the middle of nowhere.”</p>

<p>I glanced at the clay shards, which I had spread out on the dinner table. They were ancient, little more than brown dust. It appeared there had been an illustration painted on the side, but it was impossible to say what it had depicted based on the shattered remains.</p>

<p>Tired, injured, and frustrated, all I wanted to do was get some sleep. Lorelei kissed me good night and then vanished, dissolving into a cloud of mana. Ayara helped me redress my wound; she was clearly still worried about me. I told her I’d be fine. Ishikana eventually convinced her to head to bed, giving me some time to myself.</p>

<p>Retreating to my room, I tried calling my parents again. Someone picked up, but instantly slammed the phone back down. Feeling uneasy, I decided to check on them the next morning.</p>

<p>In my dreams, I was back in the woods. Like before, there were cloaked figures chasing after me. But unlike before, I could see their faces. I recognized the younger versions of Mitch, Josh, and Herb as they screamed for my blood.</p>

<p>I ran as hard as I could, but eventually I tripped and fell. Even though it was a dream, I swore I could feel their knives as they stabbed into my back.</p>

<p>With a yelp, I awoke. Lorelei appeared, rushing to my side, the bond between us thrumming with fear. Gulping for air, I told her about my dream.</p>

<p>I thought of the missing person’s report the cops had confronted me with. <em>Was Ishikana right, had DIK killed them? Just how many people had Mitch and DIK sacrificed?</em></p>

<p>Dawn was still an hour away, based on my phone, but I couldn’t fall back asleep.</p>

<p>Rolling out of bed, I apologized to Lorelei, but the Hearth Spirit was more worried about me than anything else. She insisted on checking my ankle and putting on a fresh bandage before I headed to the kitchen. I had a decent Resilience stat, but not enough to just heal overnight.</p>

<p>As Lorelei rewrapped my ankle, I checked the text I had sent Randy the day before. I had asked if he’d be willing to barter for some Healing Potions. He had replied, indicating he was more than happy to trade.</p>

<p>There was no question in my mind we’d be returning the Dungeon. I wanted to be fully prepared.</p>

<p>Limping into the kitchen, I saw that Ayara was already awake.</p>

<p>She looked a bit rumpled. “I called out of work again. And no, don’t worry about it. I’m going with you to meet this professor.”</p>

<p>“As am I,” Ishikana agreed as she scuttled into the room along the exposed ceiling beams.</p>

<p>“Guess none of us could sleep,” I grumbled.</p>

<p>I rubbed my face, unable to think of an argument against their insistences. Grabbing the Drow’s offered cup of coffee, I gulped it down along with a granola bar. Afterward, I went back to my room to get dressed.</p>

<p>We’d have to get a move on if we were going to meet Professor Brown in time, especially if I was going to check on my parents first.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“I can’t believe I’m doing this,” I grumbled.</p>

<p>“It’s the fastest, most circuitous method of reaching your parents’ house,” Ishikana replied.</p>

<p>I was bound in a silk harness, hanging from her back as we bound from trees and rooftops, leaping over Ashling Grove during the predawn hours. Below I could only see a few people, mostly dog-owners taking their pets out for early morning walks. Nobody looked up as we silently dashed past.</p>

<p>“You should feel honored,” Ishikana grunted as she leapt atop a nearby tree. “Arachne rarely offer to carry other people, let alone allow them to ride upon our backs. We are no pack animals, and our dignity would never allow people to view us as such.”</p>

<p>It was incredibly weird, feeling her spider body ripple, tense, and release as she jumped and crawled along the rooftops. Her human half felt so warm, so natural, as I held onto her shoulders, but everything else seemed so alien.</p>

<p>“There.” I pointed. “You see that maple tree? That’s my parents’ backyard.”</p>

<p>We had left Lorelei and Ayara with the truck, parked behind the Order of Stone’s meeting hall. I figured nobody would look for them there, though the Drow Blacksmith found the place very eerie.</p>

<p>“Hurry back,” she had whispered.</p>

<p>After everything that had happened, it seemed too dangerous to just drive to my folk’s place. At first, I had thought about sneaking on foot, but Ishikana had insisted that she had a better idea.</p>

<p>Closing my eyes, I buried my face in her hair as she made the largest jump yet, crossing several rooftops as she leapt for the maple tree. I hung on tight, my face pressed against her back.</p>

<p>Despite the gut-lurching terror of the jump, I couldn’t help but breathe in the Arachne’s scent. She smelled like aged paper, cinnamon, and vanilla.</p>

<p>The world lurched as we landed. The tree creaked and groaned; dying leaves were shaking loose, but it seemed like nobody had spotted us.</p>

<p>Raising my head, blinking, I saw Ishikana had turned her head to give me a coy look. “Are you alright, Brent?”</p>

<p>“Just dandy,” I said as I loosened the harness and carefully slipped loose.</p>

<p>Ishikana skittered onto the main trunk, giving me room to climb down the tree. I had clambered up and down its branches as a kid, but it felt very, very different to climb down as an adult.</p>

<p>Suppressing a wince as I landed, I glanced toward the house. There was a light on in the kitchen.</p>

<p>My parents were early birds. My dad woke up at five AM every day to make breakfast, have a cup of coffee, and read the news before heading to work.</p>

<p>Staying low, I crossed the lawn and ducked into a bush by the kitchen window. Carefully, I peeked above the windowsill.</p>

<p>Dad was sitting in the kitchen, just like I expected him to. He’d given up the local newspaper for a tablet years ago, but it was still reminiscent to see him sitting there, catching up on the day’s events.</p>

<p>Mom came into the room, still dressed in a robe. She came behind my dad and gave him a quick hug before heading to the kitchen counter to get a cup of coffee. I hid there for a moment, just watching their quiet, domestic moment, before heading back to the maple tree.</p>

<p>“You’re not going to talk to them?” Ishikana asked incredulously.</p>

<p>“No,” I answered as I climbed up to join her.</p>

<p>They were safe, that was all that mattered. At least, that’s what I told myself. In truth, I couldn’t bear the thought of them turning against me, hurling curses and threats. That would break something inside of me, I was sure of it.</p>

<p>As I got back into the harness, I silently vowed to kill Mitch and the others for what they had done.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It took us an hour to drive out of town, avoiding the cops and locals, and two more to reach Professor Brown’s house. Ishikana insisted we stop along the way to get <em>a real breakfast</em>. When I argued that Ayara and I had already eaten, the Scholar pointed out that she needed far more calories than either of us.</p>

<p>The Drow just thought she wanted to get fresh donuts.</p>

<p>Using my GPS we found a little mom-and-pop bakery along the way. Watching the owners’ reaction as a giant spider walked into the store and nearly cleared off their shelves was priceless.</p>

<p>We then sat out in the parking lot. Ayara and I shared a few plain donuts while the Arachne joyfully scarfed down a box of bear claws.</p>

<p>I suggested we save a box of pastries for our visit. I didn’t know what kind of reception the professor had for us, but my mom had raised me to be the kind of man who brought gifts when visiting others.</p>

<p>“What were their Classes and Levels?” Ishikana asked me.</p>

<p>I glanced back at the store, using Identify.</p>

<p>“They’re both Crafters, level four and level six,” I replied.</p>

<p>“Good,” she nodded, pausing her feast. “Keep practicing. At level one, you should be able to identify people five levels above you. At level two it increases to ten, and so on.”</p>

<p>Ishikana had insisted that Ayara and I begin using Identify at every opportunity. She was disappointed the Blacksmith hadn’t leveled the Skill further, and she felt I needed to push myself harder as well.</p>

<p>If nothing else, I was <em>really </em>damn curious what Mitch’s Classes were. From some Internet sleuthing Lorelei had done, she had found that the guy came from a well-off family. He owned a share in several car dealerships, probably making him one of the wealthiest men in town.</p>

<p>I kept the AM news station on as we drove, but there was nothing new about events in Ashling Grove. Outside of hating my guts and trying to shoot me, people were acting just like normal. Ashling Grove was gearing up for the town festival, which promised to be the biggest in modern history since we were due for a full solar eclipse.</p>

<p>The rest of the news was a repeat of things I had heard before.</p>

<p>“Oh, Humanity finally discovered how to unlock the Ranger,” Ishikana commented. “Interesting, I wonder how your use of firearms will influence the growth of ranged combat classes?”</p>

<p>“Do you have guns back where you’re from?” I asked.</p>

<p>“Indeed, but they’re magic-based, rather than the chemical propellant Humans use,” the Arachne answered.</p>

<p>“Magical gunmen?” Lorelei’s eyes shimmered. “That’s so cool!”</p>

<p>Professor Brown lived way, way, out in the middle of nowhere. The only other houses I saw in his area were hunting cabins and vacation spots for the rich folks who liked to go into the mountains during the weekends and holidays.</p>

<p>I had to double-check my GPS as we navigated a private road. My truck’s suspension squealed and protested as we drove up the dirt lane. Trees ran uninterrupted to our left and right. Fall was setting in, and the ground was covered under a blanket of brown, orange, and yellow leaves. It would have been a beautifully scenic trip if we were in the right mindset to appreciate it.</p>

<p>Finally, a cabin came into view. It was rustic but comfortable looking. I could imagine some college professor retiring there to write his memoirs or a slew of textbooks.</p>

<p>As we pulled to a stop, a figure stepped around the corner of the building.</p>

<p>Professor Scott Brown wasn’t who I had expected. I had been picturing a wizened, old man, gray and scholarly. Instead, there was a feverish vitality about the man, like a candle that had burned down too quickly. His hair and close-cropped beard were iron-gray, and his skin was a faded shade of brown, like well-worn leather. Like me, he was dressed in flannel and jeans, along with a pair of stained boots.</p>

<p>An ax hung in his hand.</p>

<p>Slowly climbing out of the truck, we regarded him across his lawn.</p>

<p>”This is more of an—eclectic group than I was expecting.” His voice was thin and scratchy. “So, you’re the new owner of Rotwood Manor?”</p>

<p>I nodded.</p>

<p>He sighed. “Shit. Well, I can guess why you contacted me. Come around back, we need to talk.”</p>
