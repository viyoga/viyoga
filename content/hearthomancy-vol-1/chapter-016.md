<p>Before I was going to invite anyone over to visit, I was determined to finish cleaning out the house and clearing the grounds. There were still rooms in the residential and atrium wing that needed to be cleaned out and both the front and back lawns were overgrown tangles. It didn’t matter how Ayara felt about me, I doubted she’d be impressed seeing Rotwood Manor in such a messy state.</p>

<p>“New game plan!” I declared boldly. “We’re going to haul out every beer bottle and broken piece of furniture. We’ll rip out every chunk of damaged plaster and old carpeting. This place is going to be spick and span!”</p>

<p>Lorelei celebrated my new directive with a high five.</p>

<p>We began in the residential wing, clearing it out room by room. Using Hearth Restoration, the spirit whirled garbage out of windows, tossing it into the dumpster waiting below. I ripped out chunks of damaged walling, saving what I could, and trashing the rest.</p>

<p>Someday, I wanted to replace all the wainscoting and wood paneling, the carved features, and the handcrafted balusters and railings. Rotwood Manor would be a work of art, a piece of supreme craftsmanship.</p>

<p>It was a bit spooky clearing out the room where we had seen the surge of Darkness mana, but nothing eerie happened. Just to be sure, I took the door off the hinges and stacked it with the rest of the salvage.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei called from the front of the house.</p>

<p>Rushing to the foyer, I saw she was standing by the front doorway, staring out at the driveway. “Someone is coming.”</p>

<p>I suddenly wished I still had a wand. I shouldn’t have expected trouble, especially in broad daylight, but I still had a premonition of unease.</p>

<p>Mitch and a woman, dressed in some gaudy house dress, walked down the driveway. The woman was carrying some kind of large dish wrapped in tinfoil.</p>

<p>“Howdy, neighbor!” Mitched waved, flashing us a huge smile.</p>

<p>“Afternoon,” I said, not budging from my spot at the top of the damaged porch.</p>

<p>The pair walked around the drive, avoiding the overgrown and junk-filled lawn.</p>

<p>Mitch looked at the dumpster. “Wow, you guys have been busy!”</p>

<p>“Very,” I answered.</p>

<p>He gave a speculative look at Lorelei. If he, or the woman, who I assumed was his wife, were frightened by her spectral appearance, they didn’t show it.</p>

<p>“I’m Lorelei!” The Hearth Spirit said, floating closer. “I’m Brent’s familiar!”</p>

<p>“Familiar?” The woman grinned. “Wow, Mitch, we’ve got a Wizard for a neighbor!”</p>

<p>“Is that right?” Mitch laughed. “Wow, that’s amazing!”</p>

<p>He turned to the woman, giving her a showy introduction. “This is the missus, Candy!”</p>

<p>Candy held up the dish she was carrying. “When Mitch told me we had new neighbors, I knew we had to bring over a welcome-to-the-neighborhood casserole!”</p>

<p>I took the dish, and Lorelei peeled back the foil.</p>

<p>“It’s goat and asparagus!” Candy said proudly.</p>

<p>I resisted the urge to recoil. “Goat and asparagus?”</p>

<p>“Yup, fresh goat, from Herb’s farm!” Mitch jerked a thumb toward the opposite side of my property from his house. “You’ve met Herb, right? He’s a bit on the quiet side, but his farm is second to none!”</p>

<p>“Yum yum!” Candy grinned.</p>

<p>I handed the dish to Lorelei, who held the dish at arm’s length like it was roadkill.</p>

<p>“Say, Brent.” Mitched came a bit closer, giving me a conspiratorial look. “Have you had any more issues?”</p>

<p>“Issues?” I repeated.</p>

<p>He nodded sagely. “Intruders, trespassers, that sort of thing? The missus and I used to spot people coming onto your property, folks coming to party or go <em>ghost </em>hunting.”</p>

<p>Candy gave Lorelei a big wink.</p>

<p>“No, nothing,” I said.</p>

<p>“Well, how about this, just in case!” He pulled a folded sheet of paper out of his pocket, handing it to me.</p>

<p>It was some kind of flier. <b>JOIN THE NEIGHBORHOOD WATCH, KEEP ASHLING GROVE SAFE! </b>it said at the top.</p>

<p>“Neighborhood watch?” I repeated.</p>

<p>He nodded. “Yup, I think I mentioned it before. Herb, and Josh—you know Josh, right?” Mitch pointed down the driveway, likely toward the rancher house across the street from Rotwood Manor. “We run the group.”</p>

<p>“We actually use Herb’s barn as our headquarters,” Candy added.</p>

<p>“It’s not much, we just go on patrols, check the area, and call for help if we see anything weird!” Mitch said.</p>

<p>“Keeping our homes safe!” Candy chirped.</p>

<p>“Herb, Josh, and I can help you keep an eye on your property,” Mitch said. “Just let us know, we’re happy to help. We could even patrol the grounds and make sure nobody is sneaking in.”</p>

<p>I felt a chill run up my spine. I wasn’t sure why, but the last thing I wanted was Mitch, Herb, or that Josh guy trampling through my Lair.</p>

<p>“Let me think about it,” I answered, sticking the flier in my pocket. “I’ll be honest, I’m not too keen to think of a bunch of strangers roaming around outside my house.”</p>

<p>“But we’re not strangers, we’re friends!” Candy insisted.</p>

<p>“Brent.” Lorelei tapped me on the shoulder. “We need to go check on—the thing? You know—the thing inside?”</p>

<p>“Right, the thing,” I echoed, glad for her intervention. I waved to the couple. “We’ve got something to take care of, but thanks for the casserole.”</p>

<p>Mitch opened his mouth.</p>

<p>“I’ll think about it,” I said more forcefully as I headed toward the door.</p>

<p>“Don’t forget to bring back the dish when you’re done!” Candy called.</p>

<p>I slammed the door shut. Ducking, Lorelei and I hid by the nearest window, peeking outside.</p>

<p>Mitch and Candy just stood there for a moment, like a pair of exceedingly creepy statues. I had to resist the urge to try and summon Hearth Flame and blast them. Eventually, though, they turned around and began walking up the driveway. They were whispering to each other, but it was impossible to hear what they were saying.</p>

<p>“That was fucking weird,” Lorelei said, looking at the dish she was holding. “What should we do with this?”</p>

<p>“I’d be tempted to just throw it in the fireplace, but I’m afraid it’d do something weird to our hearth,” I admitted, peeling back the foil again to look at the freakish concoction. “Let’s just toss it in the backyard.”</p>

<p>“I have been aiming to start a compost heap,” the Hearth Spirit said doubtfully.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It felt good, watching as the final dumpster was hauled away.</p>

<p>Lorelei and I had worked for several days straight, but we’d finally finished the demo work, tearing out the last of the manor’s walls and hauling away the junk that had littered its halls. We’d still need to clean the yard, but I felt like we deserved a break after all our hard work.</p>

<p>Besides, I had a date to prepare for.</p>

<p>Ayara had texted me the day after our Dungeon delve to make sure Lorelei and I were okay. We had messaged back and forth over the following days, just idle chit-chat for the most part.</p>

<p>I had learned that the Drow Blacksmith worked at a steel mill, one of the few left in Pennsylvania. Working there gave her a small boost to her Class progress, but she was already planning on another Dungeon delve to try and level up even further.</p>

<p>I was tempted to invite her to visit, to make the most of Crafter’s Boon, but I wanted the setup to be perfect.</p>

<p>“Don’t let her dangle for too long,” the Hearth Spirit had warned me. “A girl can lose interest no matter how incredibly fuckable her would-be paramour is.”</p>

<p>Taking that to heart, I decided to take Ayara up on her offer to meet for a beer. I wasn’t ready to introduce her to my home, but I did want to see her. Lorelei was right, if I had any chance with her, romantically or in business, I had to take the first step.</p>

<p>Not that I had any idea how Ayara would take my relationship with the Hearth Spirit. I wasn’t planning on keeping that a secret.</p>

<p>“You’ll never know if you never ask,” I reminded myself firmly.</p>

<p>Lorelei hovered in front of me as I dressed. “Looking good! Not <em>William Shatner</em> good, but still manly and sexy!”</p>

<p>“I’ll take that as high praise.” I chuckled. “You sure you’re going to be okay here, by yourself?”</p>

<p>“Yup, I’m going to go look for Stinky!” the Hearth Spirit confirmed.</p>

<p>Stinky was the name she’d given to the big, orange tabby cat that was living somewhere on my property. I had spotted him a couple of times, and Lorelei had started leaving out scraps of food. She was convinced she could domesticate the animal and make him a house pet.</p>

<p>Having a house cat wasn’t actually a bad idea. During our cleaning, I had found several mouse nests and plenty of droppings. Having a cat in the house to deter any further vermin infestations would be a lifesaver.</p>

<p>I just wished Lorelei had picked a better name for the cat, or just found a less smelly animal to adopt. Stinky smelled like old gym shoes, but the Hearth Spirit didn’t seem to mind.</p>

<p>Double-checking myself in the bathroom mirror, I waved goodbye to the Hearth Spirit and headed out the door.</p>
