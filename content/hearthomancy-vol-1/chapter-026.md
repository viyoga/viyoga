<p>I didn’t say anything to my folks, but Lorelei could sense my unease through the bond we shared.</p>

<p>Quietly, we finished up dessert and then headed out. My mother extracted a promise for us to come back again soon.</p>

<p>“It was lovely meeting you,” she said, hugging Ayara.</p>

<p>The larger woman stood there as if poleaxed.</p>

<p>“The honor was mine,” she repeated woodenly, unsure of how else to respond.</p>

<p>Waving goodbye, we got in my truck. Lorelei settled in the middle, floating over the truck’s console. Only once we pulled away and hit the road did Ayara realize something was wrong.</p>

<p>I quickly explained to her about the drunk we’d found trying to break into our house and his association with our neighbor, the goat farmer.</p>

<p>“And if he knew the goat farmer, I bet he knew Mitch, Candy, and Josh too,” I concluded, my fingers tight against the steering wheel. “They’re all in the neighborhood watch together.”</p>

<p>Lorelei looked pensive. “Should we call the cops or something?”</p>

<p>I shook my head. “You remember Officer Penkle and Decker, right? They already accused us of hosting <em>pagan rituals</em>. Besides, it’s not like we have any proof against them.”</p>

<p>Mitch had already established a paper trail against us. I was sure the neighborhood watch would weaponize the complaints they’d made, playing us up as the bad guys, if we went to the cops.</p>

<p>Ayara rapped her fingers against the dash. “Mitch and his wife are creepy. I see them sometimes when driving home from work in the evening.”</p>

<p>“Have they tried anything?” I asked.</p>

<p>She shook her head. “No, but they stare, like they’re jealous or something.”</p>

<p>As we reached the foothills surrounding our home, I couldn’t help but stare out the windows, wary of threats.</p>

<p>We drove by Mitch and Candy’s home. The two of them were out front, sitting on their porch. Mitch smiled and waved, but his eyes were ice cold.</p>

<p>Reaching our driveway, I got out to open the gate and locked it again as soon as I’d driven the truck through. We were under attack, that was clear, but I had no idea why.</p>

<p>“We need to figure out what’s going on,” I said as I parked in front of Rotwood Manor. “Do they have something against us, or against the house?”</p>

<p>I thought of the vision I’d had in the bonfire circle and the strange quotes attributed to Eustace Phelps. The house’s past was checkered with death and misery, but as Lorelei had said, something <em>bad </em>had happened there.</p>

<p>If we weren’t careful, I feared another tragedy would occur.</p>

<p>Ayara got out of the car. “I texted Ishikana earlier, asking her to hurry.” She gave me a playful smile. “I might have mentioned we found another strange relic to tempt her to finish her business and get here sooner. She’s an addict for secrets and mysteries.”</p>

<p>Her phone dinged.</p>

<p>The Drow looked at it and murmured in surprise. “She said she’s dropping everything, and she’ll be here by tomorrow afternoon!”</p>

<p>Ayara quickly texted her boss at the steel mill, letting her know she’d be taking off work Monday due to a <em>personal emergency</em>. I double-checked my calendar, ensuring I didn’t have any jobs booked.</p>

<p>“You won’t get in trouble?” Lorelei asked the Blacksmith.</p>

<p>Ayara shook her head. “No. My shift boss, a nice lady, loves me. It’s not like they can hire another Blacksmith. If I tell her I have an issue and I’ll need a day or two, she’ll be cool. Besides, you really want me here to help welcome Ishikana. She’s a bit—of an oddball, as Humans like to say.”</p>

<p>“Powerful, brilliant, crazy?” the Hearth spirit guessed.</p>

<p>“Something like that,” Ayara agreed as she finished her text. “Despite how haughty she can act, she has a mischievous side too. She loves to surprise people.”</p>

<p>I wasn’t sure about having a random guest on the grounds, but if the Scholar was half as insightful and powerful as Ayara claimed, it’d be worth it.</p>

<p>Just the same, I decided to inspect the property line before going inside. Ayara rushed to her forge, then came back with the rapier she had been smithing for me.</p>

<p>“It still needs a bit of work and some polish. I wanted to add some embellishments to the hilt and pommel, but it should work as your spell focus and as a weapon,” she said.</p>

<p>I carefully took the blade. The rapier was less than a meter long. It was a narrow, single-edged blade with a basket guard wrapped around the hilt. Quartz crystal insets gleamed along the back of the blade.</p>

<p>Looking at it with Mana Manipulation, I could sense red mana ripple along the edge. The weapon seemed incredibly light in my hands.</p>

<p><b>Mana-Enhanced Rapier (Normal Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A rapier forged in an area of high mana density, improving its durability. Fire can be seen along its razor-sharp edge.</em></p>

<p>Calling upon Hearth Flame, I sent a spark of fire shooting from the blade’s tip. It slammed into the nearby rock, charring the granite. Next, I focused the flames on the blade itself, the arcane fire wrapping itself around the rapier like a second edge.</p>

<p>“It’s fantastic,” I said.</p>

<p>Cupping Ayara’s cheek, I kissed her deeply. She leaned down, bracing her hand on my shoulder as her lips mashed against mine.</p>

<p>Breaking apart, she rested her forehead against mine.</p>

<p>I kissed her again, on the cheek, before stepping back. Reaching in my truck, I grabbed my backpack, stuffing it with the spare enchanted stakes we kept in a box under the porch.</p>

<p>“Lorelei, keep an eye on things. If you sense anything weird at all, come find me,” I said.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit nodded, a look of fierce determination on her pale face.</p>

<p>I belted the rapier to my side, then set out into the woods.</p>

<p>Using Mana Manipulation, I pulled on the Darkness mana lurking between the trees. I hadn’t practiced as much as I had wanted with my new Skill, but I knew how to summon my Lair’s mana to enhance my stats or give myself temporary buffs.</p>

<p>I wove the Darkness like a cloak of shadows. The last thing I wanted was to run into Mitch or Josh. If they really were after me, or the house, and if they had murdered someone—</p>

<p>None of it made any sense. If there was something about the house or the property, they could have just bought the place or snuck in and taken it. Rotwood Manor had sat vacant on and off for years.</p>

<p>Reaching the property line, I began checking the stakes. The ones by the road were fine, nobody had messed with them. Pulling the shadows tighter, I stalked along the property line I shared with Mitch and Candy</p>

<p>They were no longer on the porch. The blinds were drawn, and I didn’t see any lights on. I sat there, silently watching their place for a moment, hoping to witness some kind of nefarious activity, but it appeared they weren’t home.</p>

<p><em>Maybe I was just being paranoid?</em></p>

<p>Still, I checked all the stakes, confirming they were fully charged.</p>

<p>Reaching the back of the property, I followed the gorge. We’d set stakes there as well, just in case someone tried to fell another tree to use as a bridge or if they scaled the gap. It’d be a nasty trip, but not an impossible one.</p>

<p>One of the stakes had fallen over, but I didn’t see any signs it had been discharged. I could almost see Ayara’s mana, like a tiny lightning bolt, dance over the metal cap. Carefully, I knelt and stuck it back in place.</p>

<p>As I rose, the mana around me rippled and whirled. My cloak of shadows was stripped away, the magical energy carried off by a surge of magical energy.</p>

<p>The mana flowed down into the gorge, following a cliff path so narrow that I hadn’t even noticed it before. The mana poured into a narrow opening in the cliff face, vanishing underground.</p>

<p>More mana was siphoned off the area, cascading down into the narrow opening. Through the shifting mana, I could make out ghostly phantoms advancing down the cliff and slipping into the crevice.</p>

<p>I debated going back to the house to get reinforcements, but I was afraid by the time we’d return we’d lose our chance to follow the strange procession. Instead, I pulled out my phone and sent a brief text to Ayara.</p>

<p>Unsheathing my sword, I followed after the phantoms.</p>

<p>Edging along the cliffside trail, I felt the figures brush against me, their touch as ephemeral and cold as a winter’s breeze. It had only been the start of fall, but I felt chilled to the bone.</p>

<p>Reaching the opening, I was shocked to see a deep passage burrowing down into the earth. It was narrow; I’d have to turn sideways to follow after the ghastly procession. I took off my backpack and laid it by the entrance. Turning on my phone’s flashlight, I pushed into the crevice and began inching forward.</p>

<p>The phantoms swirled past me, a thousand indistinct whispers echoing along the passage. I had to stop every few feet to suck in my breath and push further down the passage. Somewhere, in the back of my mind, I feared what would happen if I got stuck.</p>

<p>I was just squeezing past another obstacle when a new System notification popped up.</p>

<p><b>You Have Received a New Lair Quest! – Witness Eustace’s Regrets</b><br><b>Rewards: Lair Progress</b></p>

<p>Before I could even process what that meant, I felt the passage widen. Squirming forward, I tripped into some kind of cavern.</p>

<p>It was about a dozen meters across, shaped like an uneven dome. Mana flowed around the chamber, forming a vortex of Fire, Darkness, and Death. The motes glowed so bright that I didn’t need my flashlight. I turned it off and tucked away my phone.</p>

<p>The mana whirled like a hurricane, buffeting against me. In the eye of the storm, I could see a terrible bonfire burning.</p>

<p>The phantoms were gathered around the flames. Their forms blurred as they danced, bowed, and worshiped. I spotted colonial settlers, revolutionary soldiers, peasant farmers, and frat boys amongst the ghostly crowd.</p>

<p>As I stepped closer, another mirage formed around us. The walls of the cavern were painted with phantasmal mana, making it a copy of the bonfire circle. But unlike the scene above, the rocks and boulders were free of DIK graffiti and spray paint. The older marks though, the ancient etchings, bloody halos, and eldritch symbols remained.</p>

<p>Looking up, I was stunned to see the trail of a hundred different sunrises and sunsets arch across an illusionary sky. Day and night blurred together, impossible to tell apart.</p>

<p>Every few thousand iterations, though, the sun would pause at its zenith, a shadow crossing its path. Everything was cast into darkness as I witnessed the recreation of a solar eclipse.</p>

<p>Each time, the figures cried out. Their voices were little more than hoarse echoes.</p>

<p>Diabolic flames leap up from the bonfire circle. I wasn’t sure if I was seeing a haunting or some kind of horrifying ritual dredged up from the past. Gripping my newly forged sword, I called upon Hearth Flame, coating the rapier in vivid fire.</p>

<p>Blades appeared in the ghost’s hands: knives, daggers, axes, and farming tools. They fell upon their neighbors. Struggling shapes were cut, stabbed, and flayed. Death mana flooded the area, running in rivulets along the ground, all seeping toward the base of the flames.</p>

<p>Some of the horrors came toward me, weapons raised. I slashed the nearest phantom. It burst into flames; its insubstantial form consumed by my hearth’s burning mana. The other figures backed up.</p>

<p>Death mana, like clotted blood, seeped across the floor of the cave.</p>

<p>It took constant effort to keep the Skill up, but the second I let my effort wane, the figures surged forward. They pressed in from all sides, driving me closer to the center of the cave.</p>

<p>The sacrificial offerings reached the edge of the bonfire.</p>

<p>The fire erupted, becoming a pillar of flame. Something terrible moved at the heart of the conflagration.</p>

<p>I reached a small clearing around the edge of the bonfire. The phantoms hung back, fearful of my weapon and the bonfire’s raging flames. The light from the fire was blinding, but I felt no heat, just a bitter chill that sapped my strength.</p>

<p>A phantom, more solid than the others, emerged from the crowd. Though little more than a black, inky silhouette, I recognized the hammer-wielding figure from my previous vision. He still held onto his weapon, but in his other hand, he carried a large clay pot.</p>

<p>“You lied to me!” the phantom roared, pointing his hammer at the flames.</p>

<p>A voice, like nails against obsidian, screeched back. I couldn’t understand the words, but the hate, the anger and the malice were unmistakable.</p>

<p>“You know what this is and what I had to do to get it!” The giant raised the jug. “Honor your word. Bring her back!”</p>

<p>The flames crackled again, like chittering laughter.</p>

<p>“Then we are all doomed!” the man said, swinging his hammer toward the side of the jug. “With this, I bind you, and I damn you for all eternity!”</p>

<p>The illusion abruptly shifted. One moment I was standing aside from the scene, witnessing some vision of the past, the next the hammer-wielding phantom was standing before me. Instead of aiming his hammer at the jug, he was swinging it at my head.</p>

<p>I barely managed to bring my blade up to block. The flames along my rapier sparked violently. I nearly lost my sword, my fingers struck numb from deflecting the blow.</p>

<p>“You mustn’t be here!” the phantom yelled as he wound his hammer back for another strike.</p>

<p>I slashed out with my rapier. The blade itself did little damage to the phantom, but I could see Hearth Flame burning his shadowy substance, weakening him with each hit.</p>

<p>“Get out, flee!” the giant shouted, punctuating each word with a wild swing of his hammer. “The darkness will consume you as it has consumed me!”</p>

<p>“Fuck you!” I snapped back. “This is my land, my house! I’m not going anywhere!”</p>

<p>Overhead, the stars, suns, and moons spun as another solar eclipse came into alignment. The pillar of flame burned even brighter.</p>

<p>“I’ve lost it all and God will surely punish me!” the phantom roared, his voice echoing off the cavern walls. “You will be damned as well!”</p>

<p>I lunged, calling upon every ounce of my magic. My rapier burned brightly as it pierced the phantom’s heart.</p>

<p>He cried out in rage as he vanished.</p>

<p>But the illusion was not over. The eclipse, the haloed sun, hung overhead, glowing with unholy light. The bonfire reached upward; the eclipse somehow glowing even brighter.</p>

<p>The horror contained in the flames cried out, its voice like the death throes of the universe.</p>

<p>Raising my blade, I called upon Rotwood Manor and my connection with its hearth. My rapier glowed like the noonday sun as I stabbed it into the heart of the bonfire.</p>

<p>I was knocked off my feet as the entire illusion exploded, waves of mana blasting outward. Darkness and shadows flashed across my vision as I felt myself tumbling. The phantoms gibbered madly as I fell through their midst.</p>

<p>Everything went black.</p>

<p>“Brent?” Lorelei shouted. “Brent!”</p>

<p>I heard Ayara shouting my name as well.</p>

<p>With a pained grunt, I hit the ground.</p>

<p>Blinking, I looked up and saw the evening sky through the canopy of the trees. My entire body ached fiercely as I slowly flexed my fingers and toes.</p>

<p>Lorelei shouted again. “Brent?”</p>

<p>“Over here,” I croaked.</p>

<p>I felt strong hands leveraging me off my back as Ayara came to my side. Her eyes went wide as she stared at my rapier, which was still clutched in my hand.</p>

<p>The rapier was scorched, and the blade was partially melted.</p>

<p>Lorelei put her hands up to her mouth. “Brent, what happened?”</p>

<p>“I ran into a bit of a detour,” I answered with a gruff laugh. “Can you two help me back to the house? I’m feeling a bit winded.”</p>
