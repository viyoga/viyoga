<p>We had to resist the urge to sigh in relief as we crossed the boundary for my Lair. The manor’s mana washed over us as a warm rush of power. Lorelei practically melted in her seat, her form sinking through the upholstery.</p>

<p>Stopping the truck, I got out and pulled a stake of scrap wood from the back. I walked to the boundary, feeling it via the flow of mana. Planting the stake, I wrapped the top in bright blue electrical tape.</p>

<p>I’d have to probably mark out the boundaries of my Lair eventually, but it was good to know where I’d be crossing over when I entered and exited my property.</p>

<p>Glancing about, I took a look at my neighbors. I had researched Rotwood Manor, but I knew next to nothing about the neighborhood I was moving into.</p>

<p>There was another house, a rambling rancher set deep into the woods, across the street from me. It looked to be in rough shape, with an overgrown lawn and a beat-up station wagon parked in front of the peeling garage door.</p>

<p>Further down, to my left, was some kind of goat farm. The main house sat in front of several acres of pasture. There was a barn further out back. I couldn’t even begin to count the number of goats, but the smell was eye-watering. Thankfully, the woods between their property and mine helped filter out the stench.</p>

<p>To my right was some kind of custom-built McMansion. An expensive SUV was parked in the driveway, and the lawn was landscaped to a degree of eerie perfection.</p>

<p>All in all, it was kind of an eclectic mix, especially with Rotwood Manor sitting in between all of them, but it wasn’t unheard of in rural Pennsylvania. Rich people loved to build their homes out in the middle of nowhere, and land was dirt cheap out on the far edge of Ashling Grove.</p>

<p>My mother had raised me to be polite to my neighbors. I made a note to try and stop by all three houses and introduce myself later.</p>

<p>Returning to the truck, I saw Lorelei tapping her fingers against the dash. She was slowly mastering her form, controlling when she was solid versus intangible.</p>

<p>“Lorelei was the name of the woman who lived here,” she said as we drove down the driveway, the house just coming into view.</p>

<p>“Lorelei Ganlts.” The spirit closed her eyes. “She was the daughter of Patrick Ganlts, the local lumber baron. He was a businessman who owned a dozen sawmills and other businesses around Ashling Grove. Rootwood Manor was built using timber from his mills. It was the finest house outside of Philadelphia, or at least that’s what people around here used to claim.”</p>

<p>I assumed she was talking about the early 1900s, around the turn of that century.</p>

<p>“I must have inherited her memories, her figure, her face when I was summoned,” the spirit continued as we came to a stop in front of the house. “I know I’m not her, I’m something new, but her love for this house, the place she spent her entire life, still gives me form.”</p>

<p>Parking in front of the house, I watched as Lorelei drifted through the car door and floated in front of the manor. I then climbed out of the truck.</p>

<p>Spreading her arms, I felt the mana around us swell.</p>

<p>With a resounding <em>crack</em>, like thunder, the area shook. All the dirt, grime, and the dead vines that had been clinging to the front of the house fell away, revealing the ancient, clapboard siding.</p>

<p>Lorelei turned to me and smiled. “I am Lorelei, Hearth Spirit of Rotwood Manor. Together, with my master, we will see this house restored. We’ll make it shine once again!”</p>

<p>“Yes, yes we will.” I reached into the back of my truck and pulled out a large, wooden crate.</p>

<p>Its contents <em>clinked</em> loudly as I set it on the ground. “But first things first, we need to make some money.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“So, Strength and Finesse are kind of self-explanatory,” I said. “Strength is how physically strong you are, while Finesse is tied to your speed and dexterity. Fortitude is how good you are at taking a hit, while Resilience is how fast you recover and your overall stamina.”</p>

<p>“Okay, I get that,” Lorelei said. “And Wisdom is—how smart you are?”</p>

<p>I shook my head. “Nope, there’s no stat for intelligence. Trust me, there’s a whole debate about that. Anyway, Wisdom is your ability to recall and apply knowledge, things you’ve learned, while Discipline is your ability to focus, to apply yourself to a task.”</p>

<p>Lorelei rubbed her brow. “If I had enough Resilience, would I still have this headache?”</p>

<p>I just laughed. “Trust me, it becomes second nature over time, especially when it comes to applying the free stat points you get when you level up. I’ve dumped all of mine into Wisdom and Discipline.”</p>

<p>We walked deeper into the woods.</p>

<p>“So, you’ve never tried Bottling Mana before?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>“Nope, but I watched plenty of videos on YouTouch,” I answered as I pushed through the woods, following the trail of Water mana that the Hearth Spirit had detected. “Besides, everyone has got to start somewhere. This will be a good chance to earn my initial Proficiency.”</p>

<p>As we walked, I made notes, creating a rough map of the property. There were many different elements present, each one a potential revenue stream. Still, I’d start with Water, all the tutorials said it was the easiest to bottle.</p>

<p>It took another few minutes of trudging through the undergrowth, but we found a small, rocky creek tumbling through the woods. I could feel the mana around us, meaning we were still on the property.</p>

<p>Briefly, I wondered if the borders of my Lair matched the actual property lines recorded in the county assessor's office. Hearth Magic wasn’t particularly popular, most mystical practitioners preferred more explosive, splashy magic like Fire or Lightning. The bits and pieces I had picked up had been from a dozen different sources, and none of them had been particularly reputable.</p>

<p>Still, I had bound myself to the manor, so my research had paid off. I just hoped the next part worked.</p>

<p>Finding a clear spot by the side of the creek, I opened the crate and pulled out a collection bottle.</p>

<p>It wasn’t much to look at, just a clear glass container formed into a cylinder with a metal screw-on lid, kind of like a Mason jar. I explained that each container would hold a few thaums of mana, enough to brew a potion or power an enchantment.</p>

<p>“Thaums?” Lorelei repeated.</p>

<p>I pulled out my grimoire and flipped to the pages I needed. “It’s the equivalent to about one hundred MP of mana, the cost of most basic Skills. Think of it as a measurement of potential, how much you can do with that much raw mana.”</p>

<p>Bottled Mana was always in high demand. It was used by the Crafters for a variety of purposes, like brewing potions, enchanting gear, making ammunition, or fueling rituals.</p>

<p>Wizards were supposed to excel at Bottling Mana, and I hoped my new Class would give me an even bigger bonus. <em>It had to be an aligned Proficiency, right?</em></p>

<p>Lorelei leaned over my shoulder as I reviewed my notes. I hadn’t spent much time communing with Rotwood Manor’s mana, but I could feel it all around me. Water mana, cold and sleek, flowed through the creek. All I had to do was reach out, extract the raw mana, and place it into the jar.</p>

<p>I ducked one of my hands into the water while I held the container with the other. I couldn’t help but shiver; the water was icy cold.</p>

<p>I’d spent weeks researching how Bottling Mana worked, watching tutorial videos and reading guides, hoping to jumpstart my Proficiency. Taking a deep breath, I tried to focus. Reaching out to the chilly feeling running up and down my arm, I tried to follow my notes and allow the sensation to flow through me and into the container.</p>

<p>Nothing happened.</p>

<p>“Right, Rome wasn’t built in a day,” I grunted.</p>

<p>I must have sat there for two hours, constantly trying to grasp ahold of the mana. But each time I thought I had it, I felt it slip through my fingers, trickling back into the creek.</p>

<p>Lorelei floated around me, watching, not saying a word.</p>

<p>Finally, frustrated, I pulled my hand out of the water. I rubbed it against the hem of my shirt, trying to get feeling back in my numbed fingers.</p>

<p><b>Skill Mana Perception has reached Lvl 3!</b></p>

<p>“You were close,” the Hearth Spirit suddenly said. “I could almost see it, the mana flowing through your hand, up your arms, and into the jar.”</p>

<p>She drifted closer. I could feel my skin prickle as she touched my still-chilled fingers. “Can I try and help you this time?”</p>

<p>Together, we dipped our fingers into the creek.</p>

<p>This time, I could feel the mana readily respond to me, to us. It flowed up my arm, through my chest, and down my opposing arm. Slowly, the jar filled with bright, blue essence.</p>

<p><b>Proficiency Bottling Mana Unlocked!</b></p>

<p><em>Allows users to extract and preserve ambient mana for later use. The element and quality of the mana depend on the location the mana is extracted from.</em></p>

<p><em>Higher levels of this Proficiency allow greater amounts of mana to be extracted and at higher quality.</em></p>

<p>I was blown away by our rapid progress. <em>Was that the power of a Lair?</em> I quickly screwed the lid on the jar. The last thing I wanted was to spill the results of our hard-won victory.</p>

<p>“That was amazing!” Lorelei cheered as she hugged me.</p>

<p>I couldn’t help but grin, wrapping one arm around her back while I hoisted my first jar of Bottled Mana overhead. “Here’s to the future of Rotwood Manor!”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It was nearly dark by the time we walked out of the woods. The crate I was carrying held five Bottled Mana jars. They glowed a soft shade of blue.</p>

<p>“Master, are you sure you need to leave?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>I nodded. “It’s Brent, and yeah, I need to sell these bottles ASAP. They start to lose potency if they sit for too long.”</p>

<p>A guy I had gone to high school with, Randy, was a Crafter. He liked to experiment and brew potions on the side. I had reached out to him a few weeks ago when the idea of purchasing Rotwood Manor had first entered my mind. He had promised to buy everything I produced for a decent price.</p>

<p>I imagined his head would explode when I showed him what I had.</p>

<p><b>Bottled Mana (Superior Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A jar containing four Thaums of Water mana.</em></p>

<p>Superior Grade, the second highest rating I knew of, was an insane result, especially for our first try. I was sure it was due to the density and purity of mana within my Lair. There’d be no issue selling the entire box. The money I raised via Bottling Mana would go right back into Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>As crass as it sounded, making as much money as quickly as possible was my major concern. It’d take a fortune to fix up Rotwood Manor. And while I had a few contractor jobs lined up, I had been serious when I told Vince I didn’t want to spend the rest of my life fixing up other people’s bathrooms or redoing their kitchens.</p>

<p>Lorelei looked upset that I was leaving, but I promised her I’d be back first thing in the morning, with the intention of staying. The Crafter lived nearly an hour away, and I wasn’t sure how the Hearth Spirit would handle beings separated from my Lair for so long, especially with our newly forged link.</p>

<p>After selling the Bottled Mana I’d go back to my folk’s house, pack up, and come back in the morning with the necessities I needed to stay at the manor.</p>

<p>“I’ll be here,” Lorelei said mournfully as I packed up my truck.</p>

<p>It broke my heart. I had barely known the spirit for more than a day, but she was a part of me, just as I was a part of her. Though still newly forged, my magic had created a bond between us.</p>

<p>Acting on impulse, I reached out and took her hand, giving it a quick squeeze.</p>

<p>“I will never abandon you, or this house,” I promised her.</p>

<p>Her pale cheeks flushed pink. “I’ll be waiting.”</p>
