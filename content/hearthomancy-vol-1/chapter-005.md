<p>Randy was a Crafter. By day he worked for a pharmaceutical company, trying to adapt the System to modern, industrialized medicine. By night he brewed up potions, pushing the System to discover new recipes and formulas.</p>

<p>When I texted I had a crate of Bottled Mana, he had told me to come over ASAP. He was ecstatic.</p>

<p>Driving off my property, I once again felt my power ebb. It was an uncomfortable sensation, going from something that felt akin to omnipotence back to my everyday self. I resolved to get Rotwood Manor fully habitable as soon as possible.</p>

<p><em>Permits first</em>, I reminded myself. I’d do things by the book.</p>

<p>Lorelei’s sad face as I had driven away kept floating through my head. I ended up turning on the radio, putting on some random AM news station as I hit the highway, trying to distract myself.</p>

<p>The news in the post-System world wasn’t all that different than before. Wars, strife, and the stock market still existed, changed but ultimately persistent.</p>

<p>There was some kind of rebellion in some Southeast Asian country I’d never heard of. A group of Clerics, aligned with Ephis, Goddess of Reason, had overthrown the military junta. Elsewhere, a group of researchers believed they had discovered the method to unlock a new Evolution for Warriors, the Ranger Class.</p>

<p>I listened more intently when a new report from Damascus hit the wire. A group of adventurers, all level fifty-plus, had broken through a new section of the Dungeon, discovering another reality. There was no word if they had encountered another intelligent species, but Drow consultants thought it was a possibility.</p>

<p><em>Maybe we’ll meet some sexy Goblins or something</em>, I snorted to myself.</p>

<p>The Drow had supposedly made contact with dozens of other species during their exodus, but Humanity had only encountered a handful of them. Most were distrustful or outright xenophobic, which I couldn’t blame them for. Humanity’s history of first contact, even between different cultures within our own species, was fraught with tragedy. I doubted they had done any better.</p>

<p>I drove past the sign that sat at the town’s borders, <em>Leaving Ashling Grove. </em>Beyond were darkened foothills and winding roads.</p>

<p>Randy lived in a large house, built from river stone and oak. I had to admit, I had been a bit jealous when I had discovered how well the Crafter had done for himself after The Reckoning. He had parlayed his love of chemistry into a lucrative career.</p>

<p>Still, I reasoned, Rotwood Manor would one day eclipse any other house. Together, Lorelei and I would make it into something spectacular.</p>

<p>Randy was out in the driveway, practically dancing with excitement as I parked the truck.</p>

<p>“Dude, dude!” He ran over, giving me a huge handshake. “Were you serious when you texted me or were you just fucking with me?”</p>

<p>Grinning, I pointed to the crate sitting in my truck bed. “You’re the Crafter, you tell me?”</p>

<p>Randy’s eyes went wide. “Holy shit!”</p>

<p>He quickly guided me into his garage, which doubled as his home workshop.</p>

<p>Randy was a year younger than me. He looked like the stereotypical nerd, with thick glasses and a bushy beard. Despite his dweeby appearance, I knew he had played rugby throughout high school and college. I’d be surprised if his secondary Class wasn’t Warrior.</p>

<p>Handing him the crate, he quickly checked each jar. Crafters had a variety of Skills, depending on their specialization, including ones that allowed them to assess the quality and value of System-marketed objects.</p>

<p>“Seventy-five bucks each,” he said without preamble.</p>

<p>Resisting the urge to jump for joy at getting over two hundred bucks for my first day’s efforts, I kept up a poker face. “One hundred.”</p>

<p>We haggled for a bit, but neither of us were Merchants. We agreed to eighty-five apiece for that first batch.</p>

<p>“You think you can get me other elements?” Randy asked excitedly.</p>

<p>I nodded. Obviously, I didn’t want to give everything away, but I was sure I could easily get Earth, Water, Fire, and probably Darkness and Death with a bit of practice. The manor’s grounds teemed with all kinds of mana, maybe even elements we hadn’t discovered or named yet.</p>

<p>Skills and certain items came with various elemental attributes, which dictated their nature. They instantly made sense to anyone who’d played video games or read fantasy books as a kid. Fire, Earth, Wind, and Water were the initial four the System had unlocked. Over time Humanity had discovered Lightning, Light, Darkness, and a few other oddities. Scientists treated it like discovering new entries for the Periodic Table.</p>

<p>“I can. Is there something specific you’re looking for?” I asked vaguely.</p>

<p>Randy began pouring the Bottled Mana into some kind of decanter. The garage was crowded with work benches covered in the kind of glassware you expected to see in a high school science lab.</p>

<p>“I’m trying to unlock a new Class,” Randy admitted as he squinted at the flask. “A couple of friends of mine think we’re close to unlocking Alchemist Evolution for Crafters.”</p>

<p>I whistled in appreciation.</p>

<p>From what the Drow had explained, most newly integrated realities started with just the starting Classes like Laborer, Crafter, Merchant, Medic, Mentor, etc., along with the combat classes like Warrior, Wizard, Cleric, and Jack. My new class, Hearthomancer, was likely what would be considered a tier-two Class, an Evolution unlocked as Humanity began discovering just what the System could offer.</p>

<p>“How close are you?” I asked.</p>

<p>He shrugged. “It’s impossible to say, the System makes it very hard to qualify progress, as you know. But I swear, when I’m working on a really complicated formula, or trying to brew something new, I can feel it itching at the back of my skull. I just need that first Inspiration!”</p>

<p>“Inspiration?” I asked.</p>

<p>“A spark, an idea, a bit of System-assisted brilliance,” he said. “Think of it like a guided breakthrough.”</p>

<p>“And you think working with a wide variety of ingredients will help?” I guessed.</p>

<p>“Higher quality too,” he agreed, pulling out his phone.</p>

<p>Mine <em>dinged</em> a moment later, indicating I was suddenly four hundred and twenty-five dollars richer. It felt fucking incredible.</p>

<p>“You keep bringing me Bottled Mana; I’ll keep paying,” the Crafter pledged.</p>

<p>I shook his hand. “I think this is the beginning of a long, beautiful friendship.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It was late by the time I got back to my parent’s house, but they were still up, watching the news.</p>

<p>My mom had been kind enough to save me a plate from dinner. As it warmed up, I gave them an abridged version of the last few days. I informed them I’d be spending most of my time at the manor. My dad was impressed by the money I had made, but my mom worried that I was doing something illegal.</p>

<p>“No, Mom,” I reassured her. “Selling Bottled Mana is not like selling drugs. It’s legal, here, I can show you on my phone!”</p>

<p>My parents were on the older side, both well into their fifties. Their generation hadn’t taken well to The Reckoning. Most older folks went out of their way to ignore the System, rarely if ever looking at their stats or Skills. To them, it was something alien and strange, an oddity best avoided.</p>

<p>They didn’t understand why I had been so adamant about becoming a Wizard or purchasing Rotwood Manor, but they still supported me. And I loved them for that.</p>

<p><em>I have no idea how I’m going to introduce them to Lorelei though, </em>I thought.</p>

<p>Wishing them good night, I headed to the suite I had thrown together in the basement when I had first moved back in. Grabbing a shower, I sank into my bed, already coming up with a list of things I needed to pack and take with me in the morning. I also started formulating a business plan.</p>

<p>My dad had insisted that I go to Ashling State, but I had little interest in earning a four-year degree and going into some stable, traditional line of work. We had compromised, and I had gone to a nearby community college for a two-year degree.</p>

<p>Loath as I was to admit it, the degree had been useful, especially when I had set up my own little one-man contractor business. For years I had saved up money, renovating kitchens, installing mudrooms, and replacing windows. I had become the go-to handyman for most of the town.</p>

<p>I pinched every penny I could, dreaming of buying my own home and turning it into a proper Wizard’s Lair.</p>

<p>I imagined my business acumen would only help me in managing Rotwood Manor. I had big plans for turning it into a money-making venture. Bottling Mana was just the start.</p>

<p>Thinking of future dreams, and everything I needed to do to get to that point, I quickly drifted off to sleep.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“You’re back!”</p>

<p>Lorelei tried to hug me by throwing her arms around my neck, but she only managed to pass through me instead. The odd chill I experienced as she did, sent an icy, but not unpleasant, prickle across my skin.</p>

<p>“I promised I would, didn’t I?” I said as I popped open the bed of my truck. “Were you okay here by yourself?”</p>

<p>“I guess?” The Hearth Spirit shrugged. “I just kind of—faded.”</p>

<p>I paused. “Faded?”</p>

<p>She looked perplexed. “I don’t know how else to explain it. I just sort of vanished after you left. It was like I was asleep, or something, but dreaming? I don’t know.”</p>

<p>I pulled the camp bed I had borrowed from my folks out of the truck. “What did you dream about?”</p>

<p>“The house, the past,” she answered. “I drifted through Lorelei’s memories, remembering the house back in its glory days.”</p>

<p>Rotwood Manor only looked marginally better, even with all the vines and dirt gone. While I was glad to see that my repair work had held, it still looked like a ragged shell of a building.</p>

<p>I began piling up all the stuff I had brought from my parents’ house. Raiding their old camping supplies, I had grabbed everything I would need to stay at the manor. I’d still need to work, and I wanted to pop back to visit my folks occasionally, but otherwise, I planned on spending every free moment I had fixing up my new home.</p>

<p>“Come on,” I called to Lorelei. “It’s early, but we have a full day ahead of us. There’s a bunch of paperwork I need to do before we can begin fixing up the house, and after that, I want to start mapping.”</p>

<p>I filled the Hearth Spirit in my plans for the day, listing all the projects I wanted to get done. She smiled brightly, knowing each one was a step in ultimately bringing our home back to life.</p>
