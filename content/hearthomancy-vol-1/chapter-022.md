<p>When Lorelei warned me that a car was coming down the driveway the next morning, the last thing I expected was a police vehicle.</p>

<p>We’d barely pulled ourselves out of bed when we both sensed someone crossing the boundary of my Lair. Hurriedly dressing, we were just stepping outside when the cop car parked, and two officers got out. The first cop was older and skinny as a rail, while his partner was younger and heavier.</p>

<p>“Good morning, Mr. Owens,” the first cop said. “I’m Officer Penkle, this is my partner, Officer Decker.”</p>

<p>“Good morning,” Lorelei responded warily.</p>

<p>“And who might you be?” the officer asked.</p>

<p>“Lorelei, Mr. Owen’s familiar,” she answered tartly.</p>

<p>She held out her arm, the limb fading to mist before she conjured it back together. If either cop was impressed, they didn’t show it.</p>

<p>The younger officer walked around their vehicle and posted up against the bumper. He gave our house a passing inspection, snorting loudly.</p>

<p>I felt myself bristle, but before I could respond the older cop spoke up. “Do you know why we’re here today, Mr. Owens?”</p>

<p>“No idea,” I answered with complete honesty.</p>

<p>Officer Penkle crossed his arms and gave me a hard glare like he expected me to wither and confess to unspecified crimes. I leaned against the top post of the porch stairs and waited him out.</p>

<p>He sighed. “There have been some reports—complaints, if you will, about activity on your property.”</p>

<p>“What, the noise from construction?” I had always worked during reasonable hours, and with the woods surrounding my house, I couldn’t imagine anyone hearing my power tools or equipment.</p>

<p>“No,” the second cop, Decker, spoke up. “Music, screaming, shouting. We’ve also received reports of unruly gatherings taking place on your property.”</p>

<p>“Someone suggested you were holding <em>pagan rituals</em>,” Penkle added.</p>

<p>“Pagan rituals?” Lorelei gazed about like she was expecting a bunch of cloaked cultists to pop out of the woods.</p>

<p>I looked at the two cops like they were nuts. “And how come I haven’t seen you two before? You just decided <em>now </em>to stop by and investigate?”</p>

<p>“We’re the ones asking questions here,” Decker insisted.</p>

<p>Lorelei shook her head. “You’re not asking questions; you’re just making wild accusations! Brent and I have been too busy working and fixing this place to worship Satan or whatever.”</p>

<p>“More pointedly, I’d be very interested in hearing where these <em>reports</em> are coming from,” I said. “Was it the so-called neighborhood watch?”</p>

<p>Penkle and Decker shared a quick look, answering my question well enough.</p>

<p>“Rotwood Manor is <em>the</em> town’s blackspot,” Decker replied. “Do you even know how many times the cops have been called out here over the years, or how many times the coroner's office has been here? This place is cursed.”</p>

<p>“That’s your opinion.” Lorelei put her hands on her hips. “We think it’s a lovely house!”</p>

<p>The cop snorted again.</p>

<p>“There haven’t been any gatherings, and I’m certainly not hosting a bunch of Satanists,” I told the two cops. “Now, is there something I can actually do for you, officers?”</p>

<p>Decker pushed off the bumper and pointed at me. “Right now? Just know we’re watching you. My bet? We’ll be scooping your remains off the lawn or bringing cadaver dogs to search the woods.”</p>

<p>I felt my blood chill. “Is that a threat?”</p>

<p>“Of course not,” Penkle said, pushing his partner back toward the passenger seat. “This is all just a friendly visit, at least for now. Have a nice day, Mr. Owens. Mind your behavior and hopefully, we’ll never have to drop by again.”</p>

<p>Lorelei and I stood there for a while, even after they departed.</p>

<p>“What do we do, Brent?” she whispered.</p>

<p>“We keep doing what we’ve been doing,” I answered. “We level up, we fix up this house, and we stand firm. I won’t let the fucking <em>neighborhood watch</em> or anyone else run us off. This is my home, and I’m not going anywhere.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>The rest of the week passed uneasily. Lorelei and I went out to deliver more Bottled Mana to Randy and to do some handyman jobs around town. Each time we left the property we kept checking in the truck’s rearview mirrors, concerned that we were being followed by the cops.</p>

<p>It was a relief when Saturday rolled around. Shortly after lunchtime, Ayara appeared, all her worldly goods stuffed into the back of a moving truck.</p>

<p>Ayara and I worked together to move everything onto the front lawn, where Lorelei organized the boxes and furniture.</p>

<p>“Oh, look, we’ll finally have a full set of pots and pans,” she said excitedly as she used a marker to label another cardboard box.</p>

<p>“Damn, this is heavy, what’s in it?” I grunted as I lifted the final container off the truck.</p>

<p>“Books,” Ayara answered as she helped me set it on the ground.</p>

<p>Stacking it with the others, I took a moment to wipe my brow. Summer was gradually coming to an end, but it was still unpleasantly muggy outside.</p>

<p>The lawn was crowded with all the Blacksmith’s furniture and property. She had way more stuff than I had expected, including a rather nice sectional sofa that Lorelei had already earmarked for the main hall.</p>

<p>“Oh, she’s got a TV too, a big screen!” the Hearth Spirit said. “We can watch movies on it!”</p>

<p>“Sure, though we’ll need to find somewhere to mount it,” the Drow woman said.</p>

<p>Lorelei was ecstatic. “We should totally watch Buckaroo Banzai tonight! It’s a blast!”</p>

<p>Both of us gave a twitch as we felt the flow of mana around the manor recoil.</p>

<p>“We’ve got visitors.” Lorelei sighed.</p>

<p>Stepping around the truck, I was unsurprised to see Mitch waving as he walked down the driveway. Another man, someone I didn’t immediately recognize, walked behind him.</p>

<p>“Shit, I <em>really</em> need to build a gate or fence or something,” I muttered.</p>

<p>“What about a moat?” Lorelei suggested as she drifted over to my side.</p>

<p>“Only if we can fill it with sharks,” I answered.</p>

<p>Mitch looked around the yard. “Hey, Brent! Is everything okay? We saw the cops show up the other day, and then a moving truck today. Candy and I were worried that you were in trouble or moving out.”</p>

<p>His words were sympathetic, but I felt a malicious edge to them. He had expected me to give up and run, just like the owners before me.</p>

<p>I crossed my arms over my chest and ignored his prodding. “Afternoon, Mitch. Who’s your friend?”</p>

<p>Mitch glanced over his shoulder. “Oh, this is Josh! You know, your neighbor from across the street!”</p>

<p>I realized he was the man Mitch and Candy had been talking to out on their lawn the night I took Lorelei out for our date.</p>

<p>Josh looked to be about the same age as Mitch. But where Mitch was clean-cut and wholesome, Josh looked beat down and seedy. His old Ashling State T-shirt had holes in it, and his beer gut stuck out over his sweatpants. The bushy beard, untamed hair, and glassy eyes completed the look of someone who spent his every waking moment stoned off his ass.</p>

<p>He stared at the manor. “The temple has been defiled!”</p>

<p>Lorelei frowned. “What?”</p>

<p>Mitch roughly patted the other man on the shoulder. “Just a joke, I’m sure. Josh is quite the jokester!”</p>

<p>“Yes, a joke,” Josh said, giving us a yellow-toothed grin. “The temple remains pure!”</p>

<p>Ayara stepped around the truck, glaring at the two men.</p>

<p>“Oh, who’s this!” Mitch pointed to the moving truck. “You taking on roommates?”</p>

<p>“I don’t think that’s any of your business,” I answered, in no mood to play whatever fucking games Mitch was up to.</p>

<p>He held up his hands. “Sorry, sorry! As the head of the neighborhood watch, I have to be vigilant!”</p>

<p>“Like you calling the cops on me with a bunch of bogus complaints?” I snarled.</p>

<p>He took a step back, faking innocence. “Whoa, bud, I have no idea what you’re talking about! I’d never do that, would you, Josh?”</p>

<p>“I’m not exactly a fan of the cops,” the stoner replied.</p>

<p>Mitch shook his head. “See, this is all the more reason for you to join our little organization, to stop <em>misunderstandings</em> and <em>fake reports</em>, like that! This is a nice neighborhood, but it’s not always safe!”</p>

<p>“What makes you say that?” I challenged.</p>

<p>“Would you believe Herb, the goat farmer, spotted some guy on his land the other evening?” Mitch whispered conspiratorially.</p>

<p>“Oh, who was it?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>“Nobody knows,” Mitch replied. “But it always pays to be on the lookout!”</p>

<p>Lorelei and I hadn’t sensed any more intruders in my Lair. While out at night, Bottling Mana, we had spotted figures moving through the goat pastures that bordered our property. It was hard to tell, but they seemed to be heading in and out of the barn.</p>

<p>At the time, I had figured it was Mitch and the rest of the neighborhood watch. They had claimed to use the goat farmer’s barn as their headquarters.</p>

<p>“The sacred grounds must be kept secure. The Lord demands nothing less,” Josh intoned, spreading his arms wide like a man quoting scripture.</p>

<p>“Just how high are you?” I demanded.</p>

<p>“I totally ate a pound of shrooms last night,” Josh freely admitted.</p>

<p>“Right, so anyway, sorry for being nosy!” Mitch grabbed Josh and hauled him back. “But remember what I said about joining the neighborhood watch! We’ve all got to take care of each other!”</p>

<p>Josh nodded fervently. “The light wanes, the hunger grows!”</p>

<p>“Okay, you’ve got to go,” I said sharply. “Mitch, the next time you want to bring Josh for a visit—don’t. In fact, I want all of you to stay the fuck off my land.”</p>

<p>For a moment, an ugly, angry look flashed across his face. It almost vanished before I could even register it.</p>

<p>“Yeah, sorry! I totally get you!” Mitch nodded to Ayara. “It was nice to meet you. Welcome to the neighborhood!”</p>

<p>We watched them go.</p>

<p>Ayara frowned. “So, those were your neighbors? Anything else I should know about before I move in?”</p>

<p>I scratched my chin. “Well, the house <em>might </em>be haunted.”</p>

<p>We moved Ayara’s stuff inside and began sorting it into the various rooms. Her dining room set was pretty simple; it looked tiny compared to the vastness of the main hall, but it was nice to have a proper place to sit and share our meals. We set up the sofa and chairs by the hearth, and I promised Lorelei to mount the TV on a neighboring wall.</p>

<p>Most of the remaining boxes went into Ayara’s new room. As she had warned me, most of them contained books. She had several bookshelves, which she crammed with texts on weapons, blacksmithing, and magic.</p>

<p>“<em>Dictionary of Dungeon Runes Fifth Edition</em>.” Lorelei read the spine of one of the weightier tomes.</p>

<p>“Dungeon Runes are basically the alphabet for enchantments,” Ayara explained. “Think of them like a universal language for magic. Even if we can’t read all the runes or grasp their full meaning, they are immensely useful for Crafters.”</p>

<p>Ayara had also packed several armor and weapon racks to show off her favorite pieces, the ones she had chosen not to sell. The tiny room was crowded, but she looked immensely proud of how she managed to arrange everything.</p>

<p>“My first sword,” she said, pointing to a blunt shortsword hanging above her bed. “It’s a piece of junk, but it’s <em>my</em> piece of junk. I see it as a reminder of where I started and the heights I want to ascend.”</p>

<p>She chuckled. “I had to sneak into my house’s forge to make it. I was punished with a week’s worth of guard duty when I got caught, but it was all worth it!”</p>

<p>We put away the other stuff, the pots and pans, the towels and sheets. It was late in the afternoon by the time we finished.</p>

<p>The Drow Blacksmith was still itching to get outside and fire up her forge.</p>

<p>Ayara had already carried all her tools and raw materials out to her makeshift workshop. A coal bin had been set up too, and a few shovelfuls had been loaded into the forge. I had set up a heavy-duty extension cord so she could plug in her tools.</p>

<p>She was already halfway out the back door before I called for her to wait.</p>

<p>Activating my new Mana Manipulation Skill, I reached into Rotwood Manor’s hearth.</p>

<p><b>Mana Manipulation</b><br><b>MP Cost: </b>50</p>

<p><em>It can be said that a Hearthomancer and his Lair are one being, linked by the mana flowing through them. He can summon this power forth, freely utilizing the mana of his Lair to empower or aid him.</em></p>

<p><em>“Fire is the blood in my veins, and my bones are made of the Earth. Wind flows in my lungs, and Darkness fills my hands. You think you’re fighting a man? You’re fighting a land enraged!”—Pyter, the Unyeilding</em></p>

<p>Flames licked my fingers. Rather than being burned though, I felt the Fire mana swirl down my arm, filling me with strength.</p>

<p>Concentrating, I focused the mana into my fingertips, forcing it back out till I held a small mote of pure hearth flame.</p>

<p>Hurrying outside, I placed it in Ayara’s forge. The coal she had loaded burst into bright flames, the fire dancing with all the colors of my hearth.</p>

<p>Ayara’s eyes went wide. “Praise the End.”</p>

<p>She got out a pair of bellows and built up the flames till they roared inside her forge.</p>

<p>“Here, let me try something,” the Hearth Spirit said.</p>

<p>She conjured a gust of wind using Mana Manipulation, directing it into the forge. The coals grew even hotter as the Blacksmith watched in wide-eyed amazement.</p>

<p>“The End be praised,” she uttered.</p>

<p>Lorelei and I stepped back as the Drow put on gloves, an apron, and goggles. She grabbed a piece of steel out of her materials pile.</p>

<p>We watched in rapt attention as Ayara placed the metal in the forge, the steel quickly growing white-hot with heat. Placing it on the anvil with a pair of forge tongs, the Blacksmith grabbed a hammer from a nearby toolbox and got to work.</p>

<p>The air rang with her efforts, the mana around us shivering from each impact.</p>

<p>Fire, Darkness, and Death mana coiled up from The Blacksmith’s Regret, joining Ayara’s efforts. She timed her strikes so that each hammer blow was overlaid with the surging mana.</p>

<p>Again and again, she placed the steel back in the forge. Sweat slicked down her brow as she gradually beat the metal into the shape of a longsword.</p>

<p>Ayara held the blade out, inspecting it for flaws or defects. “Perfect.”</p>

<p>She quenched the blade in a container of heated oil. The blade sizzled and flames leapt.</p>

<p>“It still needs to be sharpened and tempered,” she explained once she pulled the cooled blade free from the trough.</p>

<p>The Drow Blacksmith sharpened the blade using an electric grinder. She checked the forge’s temperature and had Lorelei adjust the air flow. Once she was satisfied, she placed the blade back into the flames.</p>

<p>She drew it forth and put it back into the forge several times till she was satisfied. Finally, she laid the steel against her anvil.</p>

<p>It still needed further sharpening, but the sword’s blade was fully forged. I could see mana rippling through the steel.</p>

<p>“It’s beautiful,” Lorelei said with a reverent note in her voice.</p>

<p>“I’ll have to finish tomorrow,” Ayara said wearily.</p>

<p>I hadn’t noticed it, but it was almost fully dark out; we had been standing there for hours.</p>

<p>Congratulating our exhausted Blacksmith for her hard work, we all sat in the backyard to relax and cool down. I went inside and pulled some beers from the fridge before joining my lovers out in the grass.</p>

<p>A cool breeze blew out of the woods. Crickets and other small insects chirped quietly. We spotted Stinky for a second before he vanished back into the brush.</p>

<p>Lorelei took my hand, and I reached out and put my arm around Ayara’s shoulder. Both women leaned into me. Together, we watched the stars slowly spin overhead.</p>
