<p>“So, you forgot to mention your friend, the System Scholar, was a <em>giant freaking spider</em>!” Lorelei hissed accusingly.</p>

<p>Ayara shrugged uneasily. “She told me not to say anything in her letter. Ishikana thought it would be <em>funny.</em>”</p>

<p>The Scholar, walking ahead of us, turned her head and grinned over her shoulder. “And it was! Trust me, your expressions were priceless!”</p>

<p>“I’d thought you were a Drow,” I said stubbornly.</p>

<p>“Never make assumptions,” Ishikana replied in a lecture-like tone. “I am an Arachne. Up until now, Humanity has not formally met my species.”</p>

<p>“Guess I should feel honored?” I deadpanned.</p>

<p>“Nonsense, news of Humanity meeting the Arachne should already be on the news,” Ishikana replied. “That was part of my business in New York City, meeting with the UN and the US government to formalize my species’ relationship with yours.”</p>

<p>Lorelei paused. “Really?”</p>

<p>“Of course,” the Scholar replied. “How else am I to study your Lair? The last thing I want is for a local adventurer to come after me because he thinks I’m some kind of Monster! If I’m going to work here, I’m going to need to access local resources, including Ashling State’s library!”</p>

<p>“That and you’d never be able to sneak out for cake or baked goods if people thought you were a Monster,” Ayara added.</p>

<p>“Hush,” the Arachne chided her.</p>

<p>The van had already departed. The driver had quickly unloaded the vehicle, pulling multiple suitcases and trunks from the cargo area. The Arachne had quickly bundled her luggage in silk and loaded it onto her back before I formally welcomed her onto the property.</p>

<p>I had been sure to secure the gate, locking it before hurrying up after the others.</p>

<p>Watching the Scholar walk was a fascinating thing. Her eight spider legs rippled forward, her gait smooth and utterly self-assured. Her head was constantly twisting to the left and right as she inspected my Lair.</p>

<p>“Amazing, I’ve never seen such a plethora of mana types in a single location,” she noted.</p>

<p>To the side, an eddy of mana, Water, Wind, and Earth, whirled together. Leaves danced like they were being carried by an unseen current.</p>

<p>Ayara nodded toward me. “Most Drow houses are focused on the dominant mana type of the Dungeon they're situated inside, but I’ve seen mana I can’t even identify on the grounds of Brent’s Lair.”</p>

<p>Ishikana squinted, the six orb-like eyes on her brow furrowing as she stared at the whirlpool. “I’m seeing traces of Life, Death, and Rot, along with Regrowth.”</p>

<p>“Rot and Regrowth?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>Ishikana straightened and began walking toward the manor, which was peeking through the trees ahead. “Rot and Regrowth are hybrid elements formed by an excess of Life and Death mana in one location.”</p>

<p>“We do have the start of a very nice compost pile,” the Hearth Spirit said proudly.</p>

<p>The Arachne scanned the grounds. “I wouldn’t be surprised to discover other types of hybrid mana, like Gloom, Brimstone, or Tempest.”</p>

<p>I’d never even heard of those elements before, they hadn’t been discovered by Humanity yet.</p>

<p>Ishikana paused again at the edge of the driveway. She just stood, staring at the hulking form of Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>“By the Black Abyss’s Breath,” she swore.</p>

<p>Lorelei floated up beside her. “Pretty impressive, huh?”</p>

<p>Slowly, the Arachne spun around to face me. She crouched down so we were eye to eye. Despite her alien appearance, I had to admit she was gorgeous, her six extra eyes dotting her brow like shimmering, onyx gems.</p>

<p>“Brent Owens, you might just own the most singularly wonderful home in this reality,” she whispered.</p>

<p>I couldn’t help but grin. “You haven’t even received the full tour yet!”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>I was worried that Ishikana wouldn’t be able to get inside; she appeared to be far larger than the manor’s front door. But all she did was unload her luggage, tuck her legs against her side, and then easily ducked inside. The Arachne yanked her suitcases and trunks behind her on a silken cord.</p>

<p>“Learning to navigate narrow passages is a critical skill if one is going to dwell within a Dungeon,” she said as we followed her inside.</p>

<p>She took off before we could stop her, crawling up one of the walls and disappearing through a third-story doorway in the residential wing.</p>

<p>“Do you ever get used to that?” I asked Ayara.</p>

<p>“You mean watching an Arachne, who weighs more than a small car, skittering up a wall and ducking through a door faster than you can blink?” She chuckled. “Never.”</p>

<p>Stinky came racing out of a nearby doorway, his claws <em>clicking</em> against the floor as he bolted outside. Ishikana appeared a moment later, leaping from a second-story room before landing gracefully in the middle of the foyer.</p>

<p>“I must apologize, but I seemed to have startled your cat!” she said.</p>

<p>“Serves the little ruffian right,” I grumbled.</p>

<p>Lorelei sighed. “Poor Stinky.”</p>

<p>The Arachne beamed. “Please, show me the hearth!”</p>

<p>We walked to the back of the house. Lorelei preened proudly as she showed off Rotwood Manor’s fireplace. The flames leapt high, countless different hues of mana shimmering from the fireplace.</p>

<p>“Unbelievable,” the Arachne repeated. “Mr. Owens—”</p>

<p>“Please, call me Brent,” I said.</p>

<p>She nodded. “Can you bring up your Lair’s menu for me?”</p>

<p>Focusing on the flames, I quickly called up Rotwood Manor’s stats.</p>

<p><b>Rotwood Manor</b><br><b></b><b>Hearthomancer Lair Lvl 3</b><br><b>Hearth – 100%</b></p>

<p><b>Boons: </b>Crafter’s Boon<br><b>Banes: </b>Invader’s Bane</p>

<p><b>1 Lair Quest In Progress</b></p>

<p>We finally got a look at the new Boons and Banes being offered.</p>

<p><b>Truth Seeker’s Boon</b></p>

<p><em>Your hearth burns with insightful flames! When investigating events within your Lair, your hearth’s mana will assist you, bringing forth phantoms of the past. Secrets and sealed paths will be easier to discover as well.</em></p>

<p><em>“Your first mistake was double-crossing me. Your second? Thinking you’d get away with it.”—Vicxtric, the Rightfully Paranoid</em></p>

<p><b>Curse Reach’s Bane</b></p>

<p><em>Your hearth burns with purifying flames! Residents and guests of your Lair receive buffs to their spiritual fortitude, making them more resilient against spiritual attack Skills like Hex, Curse, or Warp Perception. The foes casting these Skills will suffer severe backlash.</em></p>

<p><em>“Get out! Get out! Get out!”—Words Found Scrawled on the Walls of Netherwail Prison</em></p>

<p>“I’ve never heard of a Lair being offered Banes before.” Ishikana pulled a phone from her pocket as she began taking notes. “Typically, Lairs offer a small selection of Boons as they level, granting bonuses to their masters or certain Crafters onsite. Your Crafter’s Boon would be considered a top-tier upgrade for most Lairs, only available once they’ve been established for years, sometimes decades.”</p>

<p>“Really?” Ayara frowned.</p>

<p>Ishikana <em>tsked</em>. “Unless you’re part of a Master of Sacred Flames’ harem, don’t expect to know all of a Lair’s secrets. Typically, specifics like levels, mana capacity, and the details of the various Boons are highly guarded.”</p>

<p>The Drow blushed, realizing she was very much a part of my harem and thus privy to my secrets.</p>

<p>“Curse Reach’s Bane seems an awful lot like Vile Will’s Bane,” I noted, pulling up the other option. “The descriptions are nearly the same, except one deals with mental attacks while the other protects against spiritual ones.”</p>

<p>“Both sound pretty cool,” Lorelei contributed.</p>

<p>“I don’t even know where to begin,” Ishikana said, turning back to me. “There are simply too many questions and very few answers.”</p>

<p>All I could do was grumble in agreement.</p>

<p>“Do you know what Lairs are?” Ishikana asked me.</p>

<p>That caught me flatfooted. “They’re places of power, owned by Wizards?”</p>

<p>“A base definition, but not a bad one,” she admitted. “In truth, Lairs are a poorly understood subject, even amongst my peers. They are loci of power, places so steeped in mana that they take on a life of their own. Some argue they are related to Dungeons. And like all living things, Lairs grow and change, becoming more powerful under the guidance of their masters.”</p>

<p>That actually fit with some of the things Lorelei had said.</p>

<p>Ishikana grinned. “I feel, though, that Rotwood Manor might throw that definition out of the window.”</p>

<p>“I just wish I knew what made my home so unique,” I replied.</p>

<p>“As do I,” the Arachne responded. “I did some preliminary research on Rootwood Manor and Ashling Grove before coming here, but found scant details online. I couldn’t find anything about this <em>Eustace Phelps</em> person you mentioned, nor anything profound about the town’s history.”</p>

<p>“Well, there’s been some updates,” I admitted, waving for everyone to take a seat.</p>

<p>While we had a few more pieces of furniture, thanks to Ayara, none of them would support Ishikana’s weight. The Drow Blacksmith and I took a seat on the sofa while the Arachne lashed a web to the ceiling. She quickly wove herself a hammock to hang from.</p>

<p>I focused on the house’s mana, particularly the energy running through the ceiling beams, but they showed no sign of strain from holding the Arachne aloft.</p>

<p>The Scholar curled into her seat, occasionally flexing her legs. “I got rather stiff, crouching in the back of that van. Traffic driving out of New York City was abominable. But don’t mind me, please, tell me your latest news!”</p>

<p>Slowly, I reiterated the events of the day before and the strange occurrence in the cave. Ayara went out to her forge to retrieve the rapier I had melted. Ishikana inspected it closely and then asked me to use Hearth Flame.</p>

<p>I carefully coated the ruined blade with my Skill, tamping down the power so I didn’t accidentally melt it further.</p>

<p>“Hearth Flame is a known Skill for Masters of the Sacred Flames, but they typically don’t unlock it till much later,” she explained. “You said you received it as the result of an Evolution from Mana Bolt?”</p>

<p>“That’s not how it normally goes?” I asked, dampening the flames.</p>

<p>“No, but I’m starting to believe nothing here is normal,” she said faintly. “Can you show me this cave you mentioned?”</p>

<p>Heading out back, we took a moment by Ayara’s forge so Ishikana could inspect the anvil. She once again seemed marveled by the strange relic. She checked it with a high-level perception Skill, but it shed no further details for her.</p>

<p>“Are there other relics like that on Earth, from before the System?” I asked as we walked through the woods.</p>

<p>Ishikana nimbly jumped from tree to tree overhead. “Not that I know of. I’ve read documents about similar objects in other realities, but typically those worlds had traditions of magic before the introduction of the System.”</p>

<p>As we reached the back of the property, I explained some of the issues we’d run into and the security we’ve put in place.</p>

<p>“You don’t have a defensive Skill for your hearth?” Ishikana asked Lorelei.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit held out her hands. “Nope?”</p>

<p>“Strange, that should have been your first Skill.” The Scholar rubbed her chin.</p>

<p>“Maybe we should focus on doing another Dungeon delve,” I told the Hearth Spirit. “We’re still relatively low-level, we might not get another unlock or Evolution until level ten.”</p>

<p>Still, Ishikana seemed impressed by the enchanted spikes created by Ayara, complimenting the Drow for her hard work.</p>

<p>We reached the back of the property. But no matter how far we walked along the edge of the gorge, we never spotted the cave entrance.</p>

<p>My backpack was sitting at the base of a tree, not even close to where I thought I had left it. For a moment I wondered if I had dreamt the whole event.</p>

<p>“It’s likely your Lair is far larger on the inside than the outside, full of hidden realms and concealed paths,” the Arachne told me.</p>

<p>“Bigger on the inside?” Lorelei echoed.</p>

<p>The Scholar nodded. “It isn’t unheard of when it comes to powerful Lairs with ample mana. Reality warps and changes as mana density rises. Many Drow houses feature hidden chambers and entire realms only accessible to approved members of the household. Likely, the cave Brent found was summoned by a spike in mana density.”</p>

<p>I gazed down the gorge. “Do you think we can find it again?”</p>

<p>She nodded. “As you level up your Class you should find more mastery over your Lair. You mentioned your Mana Manipulation Skill allows you to pull on the mana around you. I imagine once it becomes more powerful, you’ll be able to shift the entire Lair, summoning rooms and passages as needed.”</p>

<p>“That’s so cool!” Lorelei cheered, giving the surprised Arachne a high five.</p>

<p>I still wanted to go over our new Boons and Banes with Ishikana, but I could tell her strength was starting to flag. Touring the house and the grounds had taken most of the day; already the sun was starting to set.</p>

<p>We showed her to the basement room we had prepared.</p>

<p>“It’s perfect,” she said, looking around the emptied wine cellar.</p>

<p>Crouching down again, she took my hands. I noticed her fingers were covered in hard bits of chitin, the same black color as the rest of her shell.</p>

<p>“I’m not much for emotional theatrics, but I just wanted to thank you for putting your trust in me,” she said, regarding me with all eight eyes. “I swear to keep your Lairs secrets as my own unless given permission to inform the wider world. This is an amazing opportunity, and I will not squander it.”</p>

<p>Ayara chuckled, clearly thinking of her own words the night before.</p>

<p>I nodded, releasing the Arachne’s hold. “Get some rest, we’ve got a lot of work to do. First thing tomorrow, we’re going to start solving some of the mysteries about this place.”</p>
