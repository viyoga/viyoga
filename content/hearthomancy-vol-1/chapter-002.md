<p>There were still a few more documents for me to sign, so we retreated outside and used the hood of Vince's car to sort through them. The muscle-bound realtor looked upset that we hadn't encountered any Monsters inside the old house.</p>

<p>There was a Dungeon below Ashling State and it wasn't unheard of for Monsters to spontaneously appear in the hills surrounding the town. Nothing in my research had indicated, though, that my home was a nest for hostile critters.</p>

<p>“Good luck.” He shook my hand one last time as he looked up at the building doubtfully. “Don’t forget, the first mortgage payment—”</p>

<p>“Is due at the start of August,” I interjected. “Yup, don’t worry.”</p>

<p>Vince’s company had arranged the mortgage through a regional bank. It wasn’t too onerous. The land was worth more than the structure that sat upon it, but I was determined to pay down the loan as quickly as possible.</p>

<p>I had scheduled a few minor jobs over the next few weeks, mostly fixing up people’s kitchens or installing new showers. Even if my plans to make money from Rotwood Manor fell through, those small jobs would pay enough to help cover my first mortgage payment.</p>

<p>I waved as Vince pulled away.</p>

<p>Resisting the temptation to just run back inside and investigate the hearth, I instead went back to work. I still needed to tarp up the roof.</p>

<p>Returning to my tools, I fired up my Laborer-specific Skill.</p>

<p><b>Second Wind</b><br><b>MP Cost: </b>100</p>

<p><em>The Laborer digs deep into his reserves, finding a burst of stamina to finish the day’s work. This effect cannot be invoked again until the Laborer has time to rest and recuperate. </em></p>

<p>I worked like a man possessed for the rest of the day. Several of my Proficiencies nearly leveled up, particularly Roofing, as I quickly placed and secured the tarps over the roof.</p>

<p>Securing the atrium was the hardest part. Much of the glass had fractured inside the rusty, sagging frames, making for truly perilous work. Still, I persevered, not stopping till the entire roofline of Rotwood Manor was secure.</p>

<p>I walked around the outside of the building, making sure there weren’t any unsecured doors or broken windows I had missed. By the time I had finished, night was falling, and the woods around my new home had grown quiet and dim. Only a few birds softly sang in the gloom.</p>

<p>I had worked all day and burned through Second Wind, but instead of being tired, I felt reinvigorated. I wasn’t sure if it was from the pride of being a first-time homeowner or the second-hand effects of all the mana in the air, but I felt great.</p>

<p>Logically, I knew I should have packed things up and headed home. I had spent the last few years living with my parents, saving up the money to buy my first house. Living in their basement hadn't been ideal, but it had all been worth it.</p>

<p>If nothing else, I had practiced and leveled up my Hearth Magic Proficiency using their fireplace.</p>

<p>Pulling out my phone, I texted my parents, letting them know I’d be staying at the manor a bit longer, trying to get some more work done. My mom texted back, reminding me to be safe.</p>

<p>Grinning, I tucked my phone away. Reaching into my truck’s glove compartment, I grabbed my grimoire.</p>

<p>All good Wizards had a grimoire, a tome that contained all their accumulated arcane knowledge. Most big-time legit adventuring Wizards had fancy, leather-bound books with brass fixtures and embellished spines. My grimoire was a marble composition notebook, the kind sold at the dollar store.</p>

<p>And while it wasn’t as fancy as other spellbooks, I knew the contents were worth their weight in gold. There were few Hearth Magic practitioners in the world, it was an obscure Proficiency, rarely pursued by those in the mystic arts.</p>

<p>Hearth Magic adepts focused on cultivating local mana to empower their homes. And while they lost a great deal of power when stepping beyond their property, they more than made up for it with the sheer power and flexibility they possessed within their domains, which were referred to as Lairs.</p>

<p>I hadn’t been kidding when I told Vince I wouldn’t need to worry about doing construction work ever again if I successfully bonded with the manor and its mana.</p>

<p>Flipping through my notes, I found the binding ritual I had created using bits and pieces of research and conjecture I’d gathered. The rite would bind the manor and its energies to me, becoming my Lair. Unlike the hearth in my parents’ home, I would be establishing a permanent link with Rotwood Manor. It would likely cripple my growth for life if I ever lost my home.</p>

<p>Everything I needed for the ritual was packed in the truck. I just needed the willpower to see it through.</p>

<p>I glanced over my shoulder at the dark, looming house.</p>

<p>“Fuck it.” I snapped the book shut. “I’m doing it.”</p>

<p>Reaching into the back of the truck, I retrieved the old fishing box I used to hold my ritual magic components. Inside were containers full of Herbs, Pulverized Minerals, and Bottled Mana. Everything a budding Hearth Magic practitioner, like me, needed to conduct a bonding spell.</p>

<p>Hurrying through the house, I returned to the main hall. In my senses, unearthly lights swam around the fireplace.</p>

<p>Grabbing the lantern and my carving knife from my kit, I quickly cleared a spot in front of the fireplace. I kicked away all the accumulated garbage and dirt until I could see the floor.</p>

<p>I felt bad, scratching up the floor, but I knew it would be worth it if I could bind myself to the Lair. Silently, I promised to fix the damage as soon as I could afford to.</p>

<p>Setting up the light, I quickly got to work, using my notebook as reference as I carved the ritual circle. It wasn’t a pentagram or anything. Despite what some of the TV preachers might have said, magic had nothing to do with Satanism. Instead, I carved a series of concentric circles, each one separated by a set of runes. I rubbed Herbs and Minerals such as Wolfsbane, Blessed Salt, and Iron Filings into each one.</p>

<p>The items had cost me a pretty penny since I had to use ones recognized by the System rather than the mundane ingredients you could buy at any old store. They had all come from Crafters I knew.</p>

<p>The circles weren’t to protect me or the house, but rather the connection I was trying to establish between us. The circles would block outside interference, ensuring a smooth binding.</p>

<p>It took me nearly an hour to complete the circles. Double-checking my notes, cribbed from a dozen different websites and forums, I ran through the rite again in my head. Some of it I had made up, but it felt right. I could sense the house’s mana brushing against me.</p>

<p>Rotwood Manor was lonely. It longed for a master, someone to take care of it and bring it back to glory.</p>

<p>Once the circles were ready, I went to the fireplace and cleaned out the ash and rot that had accumulated inside. Underneath all the garbage I found an ancient fireplace grate. The metal was rusty and brittle with age. The second my fingers brushed against it, I felt a surge of power, of connection.</p>

<p>Setting it up, I ran back outside and grabbed all the scrap wood from my earlier labors.</p>

<p>Piling it into the fireplace on top of the grate, I added a handful of Herbs. They had been gathered from Rotwood’s grounds the first day I had come to tour the manor. The Crafters had told me that ingredients gathered from the ritual site contained the greatest potency.</p>

<p>Retrieving a lighter, I lit the wood, which caught instantly. Red hued fire burst to life, forcing me to jump back. Catching myself on the mantle, I narrowly avoided stepping on the protection circles and ruining all my work.</p>

<p>Taking a moment to steady my breath, I ran through the spell one more time as I carefully took a seat in the middle of the rings. I called upon my magic, allowing it to fill and empower the circles. Vivid light flowed through the carvings.</p>

<p>Once they were fully charged, I began the ritual.</p>

<p>I thought I would need to coax Rotwood Manor’s mana to me. Instead, the second I began to cast the rite, I felt it rush into me. It hit me in the chest like a sledgehammer. My vision whited out as arcane energy snapped through me like bolts of lightning.</p>

<p>I continued to chant, working each syllable through numb lips. System notifications popped up in my vision.</p>

<p><b>Skill Hearth Magic has reached Lvl 3!</b><br><b>Skill Hearth Magic has reached Lvl 4!</b></p>

<p><b>Class Wizard has reached Lvl 4!</b><br><b>Class Wizard has reached Lvl 5!</b></p>

<p><b>New Class Available!</b></p>

<p><b>Evolve Class from Wizard to Hearthomancer?</b></p>

<p>I accepted without even reading the new Class’s description. By the name alone it was everything I wanted.</p>

<p>More notifications rolled by, but I lost track as I focused everything I had on completing the rite.</p>

<p>Finally, I uttered the last verse. Just as I blacked out, a new message appeared.</p>

<p><b>Set Rotwood Manor as your Lair?</b></p>

<p>“Yes!” I shouted.</p>

<p>Another bolt ripped through me, and my thoughts were torn away.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Master? Master, are you alright?”</p>

<p>Blinking my eyes, I looked up and saw a ghost hovering above me.</p>

<p>She gave me a small grin. “Thank goodness, you’re all right!”</p>

<p>Groaning, I sat up. Wan sunlight was breaking through the windows; I had apparently passed out and spent the night in front of Rotwood Manor’s hearth.</p>

<p>I wasn’t sure what I was more dazed by, the ghost floating above me or the roiling flame that burned in the manor’s fireplace. It burned seemingly without fuel or substance, a blazing inferno of pure mana.</p>

<p><b>Rotwood Manor</b><br><b></b><b>Hearthomancer Lair</b><br><b>Hearth – 100%</b></p>

<p>“Damn, even the System calls it Rotwood Manor,” I muttered, annoyed.</p>

<p>“Master?”</p>

<p>I turned back and inspected the apparition.</p>

<p>She looked like a beautiful woman spun from smoke and mana. She floated several feet above the floor, her obsidian-black hair floating around her head like an ebon halo. A white lace nightgown and matching shawl barely covered her sultry form. The fabric wicked away to ephemeral wisps around the edges.</p>

<p>“Who are you?” I asked.</p>

<p>The spirit drifted back, tapping her finger against her lips. “That’s a really good question!”</p>

<p>Her face was sculpted perfection, with a narrow nose, tapered chin, and high cheekbones. Her eyes, a ghostly shade of bluish-gray, glowed with arcane energy.</p>

<p>There were a bunch of System notifications demanding my attention, but I shut them down. Instead, I focused on the power flowing through me.</p>

<p>Reaching out, I couldn’t sense my MP reserve anymore. It had been subsumed by the mana of Rotwood Manor. Having that much power at my fingertips was a heady sensation. It erased any pain or discomfort brought about by sleeping on a hard, dirty floor.</p>

<p>I had succeeded, binding myself, body and soul, to my new Lair.</p>

<p>“Master?”</p>

<p>I focused on the spirit floating above me.</p>

<p>“Just call me Brent.” I got up, dusting the worst of the dirt and the grime off my clothes.</p>

<p>“Okay then, Brent, why are you sleeping on my floor?”</p>

<p>I frowned. “Your floor? For someone calling me <em>master</em>, that’s a pretty bold claim.”</p>

<p>The spirit looked puzzled. “It is, isn’t it? But I know it’s true, this is my house. I can feel it around me, I know it intimately, like it was my own body. If nothing else, I can remember its hall and rooms.”</p>

<p>She glanced around, her expression growing morose. “Especially back in its better days.”</p>

<p>The spirit drifted up, her eyes roving across the filthy floor and broken walls.</p>

<p>Taking the moment, I looked through the sprawl of notifications poking at the edge of my consciousness.</p>

<p><b>Class Changed from Wizard to Hearthomancer!</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiency Hearth Magic has reached Lvl 5!</b></p>

<p>My heart skipped a beat as I read my new Class description.</p>

<p><b>Hearthomancer</b></p>

<p><em>A specialized Wizard subclass that focused on binding the practitioner’s spirit to a given location, the Wizard’s Lair. A Hearthomancer enjoys enormous strength within his Lair, freely using its mana as his own to fuel his Skills.</em></p>

<p><em>Hearthomancers gain three points of Wisdom and three points of Discipline per level, with two free stat points to allocate as they choose.</em></p>

<p><em>The Hearthomancer gains bonus Skill and Proficiency advancement within his Lair.</em><br><em>The Hearthomancer’s Spell-casting potential outside his Lair is greatly diminished.</em><br><em>The Hearthomancer’s Spell-casting potential within his Lair is greatly amplified.</em></p>

<p>I nearly passed out again when I checked my status.</p>

<p>“Twenty thousand?” I choked, rereading my available MP.</p>

<p>“Brent?” The spirit floated back down. “Are you okay?”</p>

<p><b>Skill Hearth Spirit Unlocked!</b></p>

<p><em>The Hearthomancer may summon a friendly Hearth Spirit, which personifies his Lair. The familiar can amplify their master’s Skills and act as a guardian for his Lair.</em></p>

<p><em>Hearth Spirits gain two points of Fortitude, two points of Resilience, two points of Wisdom, and two points of Discipline per level, with four free stat points to allocate as the Hearth Spirit chooses. Hearth Spirits do not gain a secondary Class.</em></p>

<p><em>The familiar’s personalities and appearances are typically drawn from the history of the Lair.</em><br><em>Hearth Spirits inherit lesser versions of their master’s Skills and Proficiencies.</em></p>

<p>Glancing at her, a new stat window appeared.</p>

<p><b>Lorelei </b><br><b></b><b>Race: </b>Spirit<br><b>MP: </b>20000/20000</p>

<p><b>Ability Scores</b><br><b></b><b>Strength: </b>5<br><b>Finesse: </b>10<b> </b><br><b>Fortitude: </b>17<br><b>Resilience: </b>18<br><b>Wisdom: </b>20<br><b>Discipline: </b>20</p>

<p><b>Classes</b><br><b></b><b>Hearth Spirit Lvl 5</b></p>

<p><b>Skills</b><br><b></b><b>Hearth Restoration Lvl 1</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiencies</b></p>

<p>The spirit, Lorelei, frowned and waved her hands in front of her face. “Gah, what is this?”</p>

<p>“You don’t know what a stat screen is?” I asked, closing the System.</p>

<p>“No!” she answered, clearly relieved when the data disappeared.</p>

<p>I chuckled. “It's a bit too early in the morning to talk about the System, at least not without some coffee. Follow me, and I’ll explain the screwed-up nature of our world.”</p>
