<p>We retreated to the main hall to talk business. I was frank with Ayara, explaining our challenging long-term financial situation. I was making decent money Bottling Mana, and I still had my home contractor and handyman work, but all my finances were dedicated toward fixing up my Lair.</p>

<p>For the time being, Rotwood Manor was a money pit.</p>

<p>Before we agreed to anything, she insisted on sharing her stats with me. Ayara showed me what she had to offer:</p>

<p><b>Drax’Ayarellum of House Druxsellaic</b><br><b></b><b>Race: </b>Drow<br><b>MP: </b>600/600</p>

<p><b>Ability Scores</b><br><b></b><b>Strength: </b>20<br><b>Finesse: </b>15<br><b>Fortitude: </b>20<br><b>Resilience: </b>16<br><b>Wisdom: </b>10<br><b>Discipline: </b>10</p>

<p><b>Classes</b><br><b></b><b>Blacksmith Lvl 6</b><br><b>Enchanter Lvl 5</b></p>

<p><b>Skills</b><br><b></b><b>Eyes of Discernment Lvl 2</b><br><b>Identify Lvl 1</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiencies</b><br><b></b><b>Weaponsmithing Lvl 6</b><br><b>Armorsmithing Lvl 4</b><br><b>Smelting Lvl 3</b><br><b>Etching Lvl 5</b><br><b>Runic Knowledge Lvl 2</b><br><b>Heavy Armor Lvl 3</b><br><b>One-Handed Blunt Weapons Lvl 3</b><br><b>Shields Lvl 3</b><br><b>Two-Handed Swords Lvl 2</b></p>

<p>“I mostly focus on making weapons, but I’m good at forging armor as well,” she explained. “Runic Knowledge and Etching ties into my secondary Class. I can etch runes into items to buff them. Right now, the buffs are pretty limited, but If I continue to level those two Proficiencies, I’ll eventually be able to create my own magical items.”</p>

<p>“Like a Magical Blacksmith?” I guessed, remembering what she had said in the Dungeon.</p>

<p>“Exactly!” She ducked her head. “I love the idea of crafting magical weapons, the challenge of it. I can’t do that yet, but I will, eventually.”</p>

<p>“Sadly, I think I’ll have to continue working at the steel mill, at least for a bit longer,” she said. “Mundane swords and weapons don’t go for a lot, I mostly break even on costs. You might be better off offering a spot to—”</p>

<p>“Nope.” I cut her off. “I know you, I like you, and I trust you. I already decided I don’t want a bunch of random assholes trampling all over my property. Consider this a long-term proposition, I’m happy to help you level up to the point we can be profitable.”</p>

<p>Ayara wanted to pay rent, but I turned that down. I couldn’t take her money for such rough accommodations. The Drow insisted we’d at least share the utilities and set up a food budget.</p>

<p>We agreed to a sixty-forty split, after costs, for anything Ayara crafted and sold while working in my Lair. As she said, it would be a negligible amount to start, but once she had a chance to level up, and hopefully get her Fusion, she predicted she would be able to earn a substantial amount.</p>

<p>“Trust me, forty percent for me is more than fair,” the Drow argued when I suggested making it an even split. “Don’t discount the benefits I’ll receive from Crafter’s Boon and the ambient mana of your Lair.”</p>

<p>I left the window open to negotiate later once she had leveled up her Classes or I unlocked new Boons.</p>

<p>“We’ll need a forge,” she said excitedly as we sealed our deal. “Do you know a place around here with good Fire and Earth mana? That’d be an optimal location!”</p>

<p>Scouting the grounds, we found a spot just off the backyard. Currents of Fire and Earth mana flowed around the small clearing. The Blacksmith instantly began sketching out ideas in the dirt.</p>

<p>“We can put the forge here—the anvil here—and maybe set up some work benches over here by this tree.”</p>

<p>I found out Ayara didn’t have a forge of her own.</p>

<p>“I live in a rinky-dink apartment,” she sighed. “The landlord wouldn’t let me set up a smelter or forge in the backyard. Normally, I have to borrow workspace from other Crafters I know. It’s a pain in the ass and it’s killing my progress in my Class.”</p>

<p>She glanced around the clearing. “We can build a decent enough forge for cheap, but I’ll need an anvil. Damn, that’s probably going to cost me a few hundred bucks.”</p>

<p>“Oh, I just remembered!” Lorelei’s eyes went wide. “There's an anvil on the property you can use!”</p>

<p>“Really, we have an anvil?” I asked, surprised.</p>

<p>“Sure.” The Hearth Spirit drifted toward the woods. “Follow me!”</p>

<p>We dashed to keep up with Lorelei, who drifted through the trees like a ghost. Her form was white against the murk. Though it was a sunny day, we soon found ourselves nearly lost in darkness and shadow. I had to help Ayara, who was only wearing sneakers, as we scrambled across roots and over rocks.</p>

<p>I swore I could hear voices whispering from the trees, but Ayara said she didn’t hear anything.</p>

<p>The mana around us changed, becoming darker, heavier. We clambered down narrow paths. Darkness and Death mana permeated the woods. There were even darker, unknown elements mixed into the flow. The air stank of rot and garbage, and I felt extremely uneasy, like there was something watching us.</p>

<p>“Lorelei, where are we?” I called.</p>

<p>“I don’t remember,” she admitted, coming to a stop. “Something—bad? It feels bad.”</p>

<p>She looked around, a slightly bewildered look on her face. “I totally forgot this place existed. But when Ayara mentioned she needed an anvil, I suddenly remembered. Someone—left one here?”</p>

<p>She seemed unsure. I shared a worried look with the Drow Blacksmith, but she shrugged.</p>

<p>“I’m willing to take a risk and keep going if I can save myself some money,” she answered.</p>

<p>I pulled out my map, planning on marking the location, but I realized that I didn’t know where we were. We were still within the bounds of my Lair; I was sure of that.</p>

<p>Putting my map back, I resolved to ask Lorelei to help me sketch out a route later. If nothing else, the spot would be amazing for Bottling Mana.</p>

<p>We reached another line of rocks. I realized they were part of some ancient wall, long since tumbled down and covered in dirt and moss. Huge trees, ancient and gnarled, loomed above us, completely blocking out the sun.</p>

<p>Lorelei had floated down to the bottom of the vale. More tumbled rock walls lined the area, the ruins of some long-forgotten house or structure. Garbage filled the remainder of the depression.</p>

<p>I spotted a potbelly stove, a pile of rotted box springs, and even a stack of tires. There were a few more modern pieces of refuse, including a mold-eaten sofa, but most of the trash looked to be decades old.</p>

<p>“At least there’s no pentagrams,” I muttered to myself.</p>

<p>Lorelei drifted above a large heap of rusted junk. “Here.”</p>

<p>I stumbled over a half-rotted recliner. “Here?”</p>

<p>She nodded, a faraway look in her eye. “There should be an anvil underneath all this. It was—abandoned here? I forget why, but I’m sure this is the spot.”</p>

<p>I offered to go get tools, but Ayara was too excited at the idea of a free anvil. Pulling apart the junk pile, she tossed the stuff aside like it weighed nothing.</p>

<p>“Blacksmiths get two points into Strength per level, and I’ve tossed a few of my free stat points into it as well,” she said with a grunt as she tossed away a concrete sewer pipe.</p>

<p>I helped out, refusing to just stand around with my thumb up my ass. We both got filthy in the process, but soon we saw the glint of metal under all the junk.</p>

<p>“Praise the End,” Lorelei said softly as she hurled the last bit of the crud away.</p>

<p>Half buried in the mud was an anvil. It was huge and must have weighed a ton.</p>

<p>Somehow, there wasn’t a fleck of rust on it. Like the manor house, mana coiled around it, fighting off corrosion and rust, preserving it against the ravages of nature and time.</p>

<p>“I’ve never seen anything like it,” Ayara admitted, gently stroking her fingers along the exposed surfaces.</p>

<p>The thing practically radiated Fire mana, like it was a burning coal. Darkness and Death mana churned off it like smoke.</p>

<p>I had no clue how it had gotten there, but there was little doubt we were looking at a powerful relic.</p>

<p><b>The Blacksmith’s Regret (Perfect Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>An ancient anvil, long abandoned on the grounds of Rotwood Manor. It has absorbed an astounding amount of Darkness, Death, and Fire mana from its surroundings, granting it unusual properties.</em></p>

<p><em>Items forged using this anvil will receive bonuses to quality.</em></p>

<p><em>Items forged using this anvil may receive random Darkness, Death, and/or Fire traits depending on the Proficiency level of the Crafter.</em></p>

<p><em>“I’m so sorry—please, forgive me.”—Eustace Phelps</em></p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It took the rest of the day to excavate the anvil and begin dragging it out of the dump. The Blacksmith was thrilled beyond words at our discovery and was eager to begin constructing her workshop right away.</p>

<p>“I’ve never even heard of a mundane object from before The System absorbing mana and becoming a graded item,” she said as she helped me heft the chunk of steel over a set or roots. “Almost every graded item is either man-made or sprung up after the System’s integration.”</p>

<p>I was glad to see her so excited and enthusiastic, but I was a bit concerned.</p>

<p>The anvil was old, it might have been older than Rotwood Manor itself. Patrick Ganlts, the lumber baron, had originally owned the property. <em>Was the anvil some kind of artifact from an earlier, unrecorded occupant? What else had happened on the grounds of our home, how much of that influenced what Rotwood Manor had become?</em></p>

<p>I had no idea who Eustace Phelps was or why his regrets had infused the anvil with so much power. <em>Shouldn’t the anvil have rusted away to nothing before The Reckoning? How did it survive?</em></p>

<p>That led to all kinds of uncomfortable questions, not just about the history of Rotwood Manor, but of the nature of mana and the System itself.</p>

<p>Lorelei guided us back toward the house. She just shrugged when I asked her about the history behind the mysterious anvil.</p>

<p>“Sorry, I don’t remember,” she said with a frown. “If there are memories from before Lorelei Ganlts’s time, I don’t have access to them.”</p>

<p>“Just as long as there aren’t any other ghosts on the grounds,” I grunted.</p>

<p>She laughed and hugged me. “Oh, are you jealous?”</p>

<p>“More like—curious,” I admitted.</p>

<p>It was dusk by the time I forced Ayara to call it quits for the day. The strange whispers had died down, but I still felt like there was something in the woods watching us.</p>

<p>Even the Drow’s seemingly endless stamina had begun to flag by the time it got dark, and I was afraid we’d end up injuring ourselves if we kept working. I promised her we’d finish retrieving the anvil first thing in the morning.</p>

<p>I gave her first run at the bathroom while I grabbed my phone. Digging online, I tried to search up more about the history of Rotwood Manor or Eustace Phelps but came back with nothing. There also wasn’t any mention of other relics like the anvil, which made me even more curious.</p>

<p><em>Just how unique was my Lair?</em></p>

<p>I’ll freely admit I did a double take when Ayara tapped my shoulder.</p>

<p>She had changed into a fresh set of clothes, another set of workout gear similar to what I’d seen her wearing after leaving the Dungeon. The stretchy material looked tantalizing, especially against her still damp, lilac-shaded skin. Her hair was bound up in a towel; drops of water were still running down her neck.</p>

<p>“Damn,” I muttered.</p>

<p>Her eyes met mine, a small smile creeping across her lips as she gauged my reaction.</p>

<p>Coming closer, she pressed herself against me, embracing me in a tight hug.</p>

<p>“You are just so damn <em>cute</em>,” she whispered.</p>

<p>“Cute!” Lorelei burst into laughter.</p>

<p>The Drow Blacksmith stepped back, blushing furiously. “I’m sorry, I can’t help it! When I see his cute little round ears—”</p>

<p>She trailed off, her face growing red.</p>

<p>Lorelei was floating on her back, her feet kicking in the air as she laughed herself hoarse.</p>

<p>I took a deep breath. In truth, I wanted nothing more than to bury my face back in Ayara’s tits, but I owed the two of them an honest conversation before allowing myself to get any further involved with the Drow.</p>

<p>“Lorelei,” I called.</p>

<p>Upon hearing the serious tone of my voice, she instantly stopped cackling and laughing. “Brent?”</p>

<p>Reaching up, I took her hand. “I don’t know exactly where to begin with this, but I love you. You know that right?”</p>

<p>“Of course!” She floated closer. “I can feel your love, your dedication, through the bond we share, remember! I know just how you feel.”</p>

<p>I glanced at Ayara, who was staring back at us.</p>

<p>“So, you know how I feel about Ayara then too, right?”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit’s hand squeezed mine. “I already said I was alright with it, didn’t I? I know you’re not just lusting after her frankly ridiculous sexy body, though I wouldn’t blame you.”</p>

<p>Ayara stared at us, her hands going involuntarily to her ears. “Sexy?”</p>

<p>“Please don’t think you’re some Plain Jane, especially wearing that much spandex,” the Hearth Spirit chuckled.</p>

<p>The Drow looked down at her outfit. “I have a hard time finding non-work clothes that fit me. I’m too big and too broad shouldered for what most Human women wear. Drow tailored clothes are expensive and have to be custom ordered.”</p>

<p>“I think Drow beauty standards are just different,” I told Lorelei before turning back to the Blacksmith. “But by Human standards, Ayara, you are a pure knockout.”</p>

<p>She ducked her head and mumbled, “Thank you.”</p>

<p>“But Brent wants you for more than just your body, your looks,” Lorelei told her. “He admires you. He appreciates what you can bring to Rotwood Manor with your crafting skills, and he trusts you. If I were in your shoes, I’d be head over heels for this guy!”</p>

<p>“Lorelei!” I chided the Hearth Spirit.</p>

<p>She vanished before reappearing near the ceiling. “Sorry, not sorry!”</p>

<p>I chuckled before turning back to Ayara. “Anyway, she isn’t wrong. I know we already talked a bit about how—the Drow handle these sorts of things, but I wanted to get it all out in the open, with the three of us.”</p>

<p>My heart was hammering a mile a minute, but I did my best to play it cool.</p>

<p>Ayara took a deep breath, then reached for my hand.</p>

<p>“I don’t know much about relationships either, to be honest, but I like you—I think.” Ayara’s hand engulfed mine.</p>

<p>“Let’s just take our time then,” I said, feeling on solid ground. “We’ve got a lot of work to do, let’s take that as an opportunity to really get to know each other, to see if this thing is going to work out, professionally and romantically.”</p>

<p>“I think I would like that a lot,” she said faintly.</p>

<p>For a moment, I swore the hearth glowed an unearthly, pure shade of white.</p>
