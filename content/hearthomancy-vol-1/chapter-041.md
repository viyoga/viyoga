<p>The day of the eclipse started quietly. I woke up early, lingering in bed with Ayara. Despite the pressure of the day, we refused to be rushed. We enjoyed ourselves immensely.</p>

<p>There weren’t any more post-nut Inspirations, but I felt strangely at ease as we finally dressed and readied our arms and armor.</p>

<p>Ayara had given up on going into work, burning her remaining vacation days to grind the Dungeon and work at her forge. She hadn’t unlocked Magical Blacksmith yet, but she felt close.</p>

<p>We had stored some of the spare weapons she had forged behind the barricades we had built along the driveway. My property definitely looked ready for war.</p>

<p>I had managed to visit Randy a few more times, sneaking out in the dead of night to trade Bottled Mana for supplies. Ishikana had me pick up a few alchemic components as well, which she had used to set up more traps. She promised some truly explosive results.</p>

<p>The eclipse was set to happen just after noon, but I was sure Mitch and the rest of DIK would act before then. Ishikana’s Critical Conjecture marked that Mitch needed to make his sacrifice within the Oubliette of the Forgotten at the height of the eclipse.</p>

<p>Cars had been driving down the road to Herb’s farm since sunrise. It appeared Mitch was really putting Candy to work, using her brainwashing Skills to summon everyone they could for the assault. We wouldn’t be facing just DIK, but possibly the entire town.</p>

<p>Our plan was straightforward. We’d lure DIK and as many townsfolk as possible onto the property before springing our traps. That would hopefully rob DIK of most of their strength. From there, we’d fall back toward the house.</p>

<p>As long as we kept Mitch and DIK from entering the Dungeon and making their sacrifice, we’d win.</p>

<p>The old axiom, <em>No Plan Survives First Contact</em>, roared in my head as we double-checked our defenses. I’d strung orange, plastic safety netting, the kind of stuff you’d see around construction sites, along the side of the driveway. It wouldn’t stop anyone, but it would at least slow them down if they tried to come at us through the woods.</p>

<p>The overall goal was to funnel the attackers down the driveway and toward the barricades we had built using scrap wood and sheet metal. Our emergency supplies and spare weapons were stowed behind these fallback positions where we’d try to hold off the Cultists and buy as much time as possible.</p>

<p>We’d planted enchanted stakes along the mouth of the driveway, ensuring anyone who came at the gate would get zapped.</p>

<p>Finally, as the last of the morning burned away, we realized there was nothing else we could do. All our preparations were in place. Calling the others, I took a seat on the porch steps and pulled out lunch.</p>

<p>It wasn’t much; we were running low on food again. All we had were some peanut butter sandwiches and some stale baked goods that Ishikana had snuck out to buy a few days before. I handed out a few bottles of water; most of them had been refilled from the tap.</p>

<p>“Any sign of Stinky?” Lorelei asked me, clearly concerned for the cat.</p>

<p>I shook my head.</p>

<p>She pressed her hands to her chest. “I hope he’s alright!”</p>

<p>“I’m sure he’s just hiding,” I told her. “He’s a survivor. He’ll show up once he thinks we’re in the clear.”</p>

<p>Finally, noon rolled around. The alarm I’d set on my phone went off, warning us we only had half an hour until the eclipse began.</p>

<p>Getting up, I gave Lorelei and Ayara quick kisses.</p>

<p>“Let’s get ready to greet our guests,” I said, heading down the driveway.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>There were hundreds of cars parked at Herb’s farm. Some folks had abandoned their vehicles along the side of the road. They’d left the doors open and the engines running in their rush to join the sermon at the farm.</p>

<p>The goat farm’s pasture was crowded with hundreds of people.</p>

<p>We were too far away to hear anything, but I spotted Mitch standing in front of the crowd. Josh and Herb were behind him holding the DIK banners. It seemed like everyone in town was there, all of them wearing black clothes.</p>

<p>They stood in rapt attention as Mitch spoke, Cindy always by his side.</p>

<p>“Mind Leech is a rare Class, one adept at mental manipulation and brainwashing,” Ishikana reminded us. “It’s likely the sacrifices conducted by DIK over the past few weeks were meant to boost her powers.”</p>

<p>“Creepy,” Lorelei shivered.</p>

<p>“I don’t know what lies and visions she’s feeding the residents of Ashling Grove, but she’s the key to all this. Once we take her out, her victims should return to normal,” the Arachne explained.</p>

<p>The townsfolk were armed, and not just with kitchen knives, table legs, and pitchforks. Many of them were carrying firearms like shotguns and rifles. We were in Pennsylvania after all, and hunters and home defense enthusiasts were abundant.</p>

<p>Penkle, Decker, and the rest of Ashling Grove’s police department were present as well. They were in full riot gear, with truncheons, shields, and body armor.</p>

<p>I hoped none of them were packing enchanted ammunition. We were lucky in at least one regard, none of the high-level adventurers employed by Ashling State, like Victor the Dungeon guide, were present.</p>

<p>“And my parents aren’t here either,” I noted with relief.</p>

<p>Ishikana had sworn her traps to be mostly non-lethal, but I couldn’t imagine being forced to fight my mother and father. It was bad enough as it was; I recognized schoolmates, drinking buddies, and old co-workers in the mix. Merle was there, an ax in hand, along with the library staff from Ashling State.</p>

<p>“I wouldn’t mind bloodying her nose,” Ishikana said, pointing to the head librarian.</p>

<p>There were more people than we had anticipated, but I still trusted our plan. “Lorelei, watch our flanks. If they come at us from multiple sides, we’re depending on you to slow them down as we fall back toward the manor.”</p>

<p>“You can count on me!” she responded.</p>

<p>A shadow began creeping across the sun. The eclipse was beginning. I could feel the ground beneath me shake as something ancient and hungry struggled against its prison.</p>

<p>The sermon on Herb’s farm broke off. Most of the townsfolk followed after Mitch and Candy, but smaller groups broke off across the pasture. I just prayed the enchanted spikes and traps along the sides of the property and around the gorge would be enough to keep us from being swarmed.</p>

<p>A wave of black-clad figures streamed down the road toward our front gate. They walked in lockstep, sleepwalkers trapped in murderous nightmares. The cops and hunters were at the forefront. The cops raised their shields, forming a phalanx, while the townsfolk formed rank behind them.</p>

<p>They lined up in front of the gate. I couldn’t see Mitch and the others yet, but I knew they were there, pulling the puppet strings.</p>

<p>The sky grew dark. The moon began to form a halo over the sun, the shadow far darker than logically possible. I felt the ground heave again, the mana revolting against my control. Distantly, I swore I heard something cry out in impossible hunger.</p>

<p>Ayara slammed her mace against her shield.</p>

<p>“For the Houses, for the End!” she roared. “May Umbralis grant you mercy, for you shall find none with me. When you die, I pray She lays you down to dark, endless dreams!”</p>

<p>The cops’ shield wall separated. Mitch stepped forward, leering like a demented jack-o’-lantern. He burned from within, dark power radiating out from him like a raging bonfire.</p>

<p>“Baphomet’s day has come!” he yelled.</p>

<p>The cultists behind him cheered, while the townsfolk remained eerily silent.</p>

<p>“You’ll never take this place,” Ishikana goaded him. “But don’t worry, I’ll be sure to add your name to the history books. Maybe as some minor footnote?”</p>

<p>He didn’t say another word. Mitch just pointed straight at us. The shield wall closed again as the cops began to advance. The hunters lined up behind them, rifles raised and ready to fire.</p>

<p>Gripping my rapier, I ignited the blade. It burned brighter than the waning sun.</p>

<p>“Come get some, motherfuckers!”</p>
