<p>Ayara and I decided to meet at a bar not far from her job. There were still steel mill towns dotted here and there across the state. Technology and automation had made the job safer and cut down on the required workforce, but the industry still needed people to man the controls and manage the forges.</p>

<p>It took me a little less than an hour to drive to our meeting point.</p>

<p>The mill’s smokestacks cast long shadows over the landscape as I drove into the town. There was a quiet, desperate air about the shops and businesses that lined the main street. I imagined if the mill shut down, the entire place would fade away, becoming just another ghost town.</p>

<p>The bar we’d agreed to meet at wasn’t much, just your typical, middle-of-nowhere establishment. Neon signs hung in the window along with faded cardboard signs, advertising name-brand beer as well as a few local brews.</p>

<p>Stepping inside, nobody gave me a second look. I was dressed in jeans and a decent shirt. My footwear might have been magical, but they didn’t appear different from typical workmen’s boots.</p>

<p>If they had been pointed or dyed bright red, I might not have ever worn them.</p>

<p>The wooden bar along the back of the room was scuffed but well-maintained, much like the tables that crowded the space. Music was playing, the playlist probably picked by the middle-aged woman manning the bar.</p>

<p>There were a few patrons here and there, likely employees from the steel mill based on their garb. The barroom was dim and smelled faintly of hops and floor cleaner.</p>

<p>I ordered a beer, then grabbed a seat at a corner table.</p>

<p>Ayara arrived a few minutes later. She was still dressed for work, wearing a heavy workman’s button-down, a pair of stained jeans, and well-worn boots. I could tell she’d stopped at home to clean up because I caught a hint of lavender soap as she sat down.</p>

<p>“Hey.” She blushed faintly. “Thanks for coming all this way.”</p>

<p>“It really wasn’t all that far, only an hour,” I answered before raising my glass. “The beer here is pretty good.”</p>

<p>“A couple of the local Crafters are trying to unlock the Brewer Class,” she explained. She went to the bar, got a glass, and came back. “So, how’s it going?”</p>

<p>“Good,” I said, before giving her a brief rundown of all the work Lorelei and I had put in over the past few days.</p>

<p>“Wow, you sound busy,” she said, masking her awkwardness with a sip of her drink. She gave me a speculative look, then asked, “So, just to get it out of the way, what’s your relationship with Lorelei? Are you together?”</p>

<p>I nearly spit out my drink at how direct she was.</p>

<p>But I had resolved to not lie to her. “Yeah, it’s still kind of new, but—we’re bonded. There’s no other way to explain it.”</p>

<p>Ayara snickered. “And yet you still decided to ask me out on a date. Very bold of you.”</p>

<p>Then it was my turn to feel embarrassed.</p>

<p>She reached over and put a hand against my wrist. “I like bold men. You Humans, you’re so cute, but so <em>conservative </em>when it comes to your relationships. It’s mind-boggling!”</p>

<p>“Cute?” I sputtered.</p>

<p>She ducked her head. “It’s—your ears.”</p>

<p>My hand went to the side of my head as she blushed even deeper. “Ears?”</p>

<p>She gave an embarrassed chuckle. “In Drow society, short ears are considered attractive. My siblings and cousins used to make fun of me for how long and pointy my ears are.”</p>

<p>I choked down the compliment I nearly shot out. There was no need to make things even more awkward.</p>

<p>“I’ve never been called <em>cute </em>before,” I said instead.</p>

<p>She looked up. “You are very <em>cute</em>.”</p>

<p>We both quietly drank our beer out of mutual mortification.</p>

<p>“Anyway.” Ayara cleared her throat. “I know Human culture tends to be more reserved, but where I come from, it’s not uncommon for important and powerful members of a household to have many spouses.”</p>

<p>From what I knew, Drow houses were huge, extensive things, composed of many families. They were more like clans than anything else, usually with hundreds of individuals and numerous families related by blood and marriage.</p>

<p>“The Master of the Sacred Flames for House Druxsellaic has six wives,” she continued, “including his familiar. Such arrangements are common. My great-great-grandmother, who was leader of our house, had a harem of over twenty.”</p>

<p>“Must have been hell to juggle,” I said.</p>

<p>Ayara laughed. “She said the same thing.”</p>

<p>Reaching out, it was my turn to take her hand. “I’m not going to tell you I’m some kind of lothario with twenty wives. I don’t know what the future will hold. All I know is that I want you as part of it.”</p>

<p>“Bold words.” She smiled.</p>

<p>We fell into easy conversation after that. I told Ayara about my life and growing up in Ashling Grove. She in turn told me a bit about her history. I finally learned that the faint, sophisticated accent she had was British. House Druxsellaic was based out of a Dungeon that connected to London, England. When she had learned English, she had learned from a British tutor her house had hired.</p>

<p>Ayara was the youngest of six siblings, all sisters.</p>

<p>“I always felt like the odd one out,” she confessed quietly. “My family are all Warriors, some of great renown, but I didn’t feel that was my calling. I was bigger and stronger than my sisters, but I was happier sneaking off to the forge rather than sparring in the practice grounds.”</p>

<p>Escaping the crushing pressure of her house as soon as she could, Ayara had immigrated to America.</p>

<p>“My family has connections with House<em> </em>Fazon, which is based out of New York City’s Dungeon,” she explained. “From there, I went to school, then got a job at the steel mill. I hoped working with metal hands-on would allow me to grow my Class, but the progress has been painfully slow. I’ve been Dungeon delving when I can afford to, hoping that I can level up enough to be able to go full-time with Blacksmithing.”</p>

<p>She only had limited access to a forge. Ayara produced a few items a month, mostly for friends back in NYC, but was proud of her work. She had pictures of the knives, swords, and bits of armor she had produced on her phone. I could tell she had zeal and dedication toward her dream.</p>

<p>That made me even more sure she was the perfect person to be Rotwood Manor’s first Crafter. Her eyes went wide in shock when I quietly mentioned the power of the ambient mana around the house and the Lair Upgrade I had unlocked, Crafter’s Boon.</p>

<p>“Such upgrades are uncommon, even in well-established Drow houses with powerful Masters of the Sacred Flames.” She shook her head, speaking in a whisper. “You haven’t told anyone else yet, have you?”</p>

<p>“Just you,” I confirmed.</p>

<p>She blushed, understanding the level of trust I had in her.</p>

<p>“Good, keep it to yourself,” she confirmed. “If any of the Crafters I knew even heard a hint of what you just mentioned you’d probably be dealing with intruders and squatters all over your property, just hoping to leech the bonuses by proximity.”</p>

<p>“Still,” she sat back in her seat. “Your Lair must be sitting on a huge wellspring of mana to offer such options at your first upgrade. House Druxsellaic and<em> </em>Fazon both possess similar Boons, but they’re venerable houses that have existed since the exodus. Both houses jealously guard the fact they contain such Boons, and they strictly limit the crafters on grounds to avoid depleting the mana more quickly than it can be regenerated.”</p>

<p>“Lair mana can be depleted?” I asked, once again realizing that there was so much I didn’t know.</p>

<p>“Supposedly, but I’ve never seen it myself,” the Drow admitted. “It could just be a fib, a way of controlling access to their Lairs for those they truly trust or can profit off of. Such access is extremely lucrative for both sides.”</p>

<p>I asked if she could reach out to her friends in NYC or anyone she was still in contact with back home, to see if they were willing to talk about how Drow Hearth Magic or how their Lairs worked. Ayara said she’d try, but she couldn’t make promises.</p>

<p>“There are Scholars—that’s an advanced version of the Mentor Class—who study such subjects. I’m good friends with one. She’s a bit <em>eccentric</em>, but I’ve never met someone who knows more about the System than her. I can contact her if you want.”</p>

<p>“Please,” I nodded, before pausing. “Do you trust her?”</p>

<p>I was loath to reveal secrets, but I knew I’d have to open up a bit to <em>someone</em> if I ultimately wanted to get answers about my Class and my Lair.</p>

<p>“With my life,” Ayara answered. She typed out an email on her phone. “I can’t promise anything, Ishikana tends to be very busy, but if I hear anything back, I’ll let you know.”</p>

<p>The bar was closing up by the time we called it a night; Ayara still had work in the morning.</p>

<p>I escorted her out to her car. We had already made plans for the Drow Blacksmith to come visit Rotwood Manor the following weekend.</p>

<p>“I can’t wait,” she said, before leaning down and planting a chaste kiss on the corner of my lips.</p>

<p>Driving home, I felt like I was drifting on cloud nine. Everything was coming together; I couldn’t wait to show off my home to my new paramour.</p>
