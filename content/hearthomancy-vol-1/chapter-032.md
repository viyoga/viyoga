<p>We went to bed early. Ayara had work in the morning, and Ishikana and I wanted to return to the library to dig deeper into Rotwood Manor, DIK, and the demon Baphomet. Lorelei said she’d stay home and work on cleaning around the house. The lawn was starting to get overgrown again, and she thought she might be able to use Hearth Restoration to trim it back.</p>

<p>“It’s worked on every other chore so far,” she argued.</p>

<p>Lying in bed, I was pleased to have Ayara asleep against my side. She was so tall that her feet nearly hung off the bottom of the mattress, but she still managed to curl up against me. For a while, I just let my fingers play through her alabaster hair.</p>

<p>I was just falling asleep when I felt a jolt. A <em>zap</em>, like a bolt of lightning, played across my senses. I shot up in bed, startling Ayara awake.</p>

<p>“What’s wrong?” she asked, her hand already fumbling for a weapon.</p>

<p>Lorelei drifted through the wall. “Brent, more intruders!”</p>

<p>I could almost feel them, like ticks burrowing into my skin, a phantom irritation. There were three of them, crossing over from the goat farm onto my land. I couldn’t tell who exactly it was, but I had my guesses.</p>

<p>Quickly dressing, Ayara and I headed toward the main hall. The hearth’s flames were roaring, the fire shot through with strands of Darkness and Fire mana. The Drow Blacksmith rushed to her room and came back a moment later, strapping armor over her chest. She had a new broadsword sheathed on her hip.</p>

<p>“Here,” she said, handing me a bundle. “I feel bad. I keep handing you half-finished weapons, but—”</p>

<p>“We might need it,” I finished. “Thank you.”</p>

<p>The new rapier was shorter than the first one; the blade was thicker and heavier. Fire mana rippled dangerously along its edge.</p>

<p>“What’s the commotion?”</p>

<p>Ishikana entered the room. Her hair was done up in a bun, and an oversized sleep mask covered her forehead. She was wearing a black silk robe and little else. Without her usual attire, I noticed her spider body was covered with fine, copper-brown hairs, like fluffy down. They ran along her back and over the ends of her legs.</p>

<p>Another jolt to my senses broke my daze before I could begin to leer.</p>

<p>“Three of them, that way!” Lorelei said, pointing.</p>

<p>A growl rose in my throat as I sensed the three people bumbling onto my property. One of them was hurt, I guessed they had tripped one of Ayara’s spikes, leading to the initial zap that had woken me.</p>

<p>I glanced at Ishikana; I was sure the town was abuzz about her arrival, but I didn’t know if Mitch and the neighborhood watch were aware of her presence yet. If there was going to be trouble, having the Scholar as our ace in the hole could save our lives.</p>

<p>As we headed toward the front door, I explained my reasoning to the Scholar, asking her to remain behind.</p>

<p>“That makes sense,” she agreed as she stopped in the foyer. “I’ll stay here and secure the house. Just be sure to send me a signal before you come back inside.” She gave me a sharp grin. “I would hate for you to be entangled in one of my webs.”</p>

<p>The exterior lights were on, shining spotlights onto the yard. I grabbed two flashlights from my truck, handing one to Ayara and keeping one for myself. Lorelei floated ahead of us, glowing dimly.</p>

<p>Even in the dark, I somehow knew exactly where to put my feet as we tromped into the woods. I used my flashlight to guide Ayara, who stumbled over the root-filled, uneven ground. Ahead, we could hear the intruders fumbling through the undergrowth.</p>

<p>I could sense Invader's Bane eating away at their stamina, corroding their strength. A few flashlights snapped on as we got closer. The intruders were in a clearing, arguing and coughing, short on breath from the effect of the Bane.</p>

<p>Stepping out of the trees, I raised my rapier. “Mitch. You better have a damn good reason for trespassing onto my property.”</p>

<p>Mitch stood there, a furious look on his face. A mild burn, likely from Ayara’s inscribed stake, covered his forearm.</p>

<p>Next to him was Josh. The stoner was bent over, hand on his knees, as he struggled to breathe. Herb, the goat farmer, was wheezing as he leaned against a tree. All three men were messily dressed, like they’d just thrown on their shirts and pants before crossing the property line. They reeked of smoke.</p>

<p>“Brent?” Mitch’s expression swiftly changed as he gave me a fake smile “Hey, neighbor!”</p>

<p>“Don’t give me that shit,” I snarled. “What the hell are you doing here?”</p>

<p>He gestured toward Herb. “We were over at Herb’s—neighborhood watch meeting—and one of his goats ran away. It ran into the woods, and we went after it.”</p>

<p>I stole a look at Lorelei, but she just shrugged. “I didn’t sense it, but I normally don’t pick up animals crossing back and forth through the woods.”</p>

<p>Ayara’s stakes were calibrated to ignore them too, so they could be telling the truth.</p>

<p>Of course, I believed that just as much as I believed rocks could fly and pigs could sing. The three men looked suspicious as fuck, red-eyed and shifty even without the Bane.</p>

<p>“You need to get out of here,” I told them.</p>

<p>“The offering—” Josh coughed, unable to even start his rambling.</p>

<p>Mitch put his hand on his shoulder, silencing him.</p>

<p>“But what about the goat?” Herb asked.</p>

<p>I gritted my teeth. “I’ll find it and kick it back onto your property.”</p>

<p>Herb eyed my sword. I saw he had a knife strapped to his waist. Unlike something I’d expect a farmer to carry, it was rather fancy, with an elaborate hilt and a long, stiletto-style blade.</p>

<p>Ayara unsheathed her sword, sinking its point into the dirt with her hands on the hilt. “You heard the man. Get out. If we find your goat, you’ll get it back.”</p>

<p>Josh looked ready to utter some more nonsense, but Mitch tugged him roughly, silencing him.</p>

<p>We escorted all three men to the property line. Mitch warily looked at the discharged stake, his hand going to the burn along his arm.</p>

<p>“Just put some salve on it, you’ll be fine,” Ayara told him.</p>

<p>“Next time we’re overcharging them,” I told the Drow loudly. “I want intruders to fry like they went and pissed on an electric fence.”</p>

<p>Mitch glared at me, unable to hide his animosity behind his usually placid mask. Still, he was in no position to fight back.</p>

<p>I watched and made sure all three crossed back over onto the goat farm. As they did, I felt the irritation, the sense of violation that had been prickling at me, fade.</p>

<p>Despite the late hour, there was a crowd gathered in front of the barn. I couldn’t pick out individual faces, but I could tell they were all watching us. My fingers clenched around the hilt of my new rapier.</p>

<p>Mitch straightened, while Herb and Josh struggled to recover. They might have known about the stakes, but clearly, they had been unprepared for the effect of the Invader’s Bane.</p>

<p>“What’s the goat look like?” I demanded.</p>

<p>Josh and Herb stared at each other.</p>

<p>“Brown, black, spotted? Horns?” I demanded.</p>

<p>“Black, with horns,” Herb confirmed.</p>

<p>I sighed and rubbed my face. “If I find it, I’ll bring it back here.”</p>

<p>We stalked back into the woods before any of them could say another word.</p>

<p>“There’s no way in hell they just <em>lost a goat</em>,” Lorelei whispered.</p>

<p>“No kidding,” I agreed. “Either way, if one of that guy’s animals is on my land, I want to find it and get it the fuck out of here. I don’t even trust his goats at this point.”</p>

<p>Ayara frowned. Reaching for the damaged stake, she plucked it and tossed it aside. “I don’t know if I can make them as powerful as you requested, but I can try.”</p>

<p>I gave a weak chuckle. “I was mostly saying that for their benefit, but I’d appreciate the effort just the same.” I turned back to Lorelei. “Can you check and see if the cops are still parked across the street? I’d love for them to hear our side of the story before Mitch and the<em> neighborhood watch</em> fill their heads with more bullshit.”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit flittered off through the woods, like a ghost in the gloom. She returned a moment later, shaking her head. “They’re gone.”</p>

<p>“Shoot,” I rubbed the back of my neck. “Any idea where our errant farm animal has run off to?”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit closed her eyes and focused, then pointed. “That way—I think?”</p>

<p>As we walked, Ayara texted Ishikana, explaining the situation. The Arachne promised to come join us.</p>

<p>I was about to ask how she would find us, but the Scholar came bounding through the trees a few minutes later.</p>

<p>“It was easy to find you,” she scoffed when I asked. “You cause more vibrations than a Monster stampede.”</p>

<p>The Arachne had changed clothes, putting on a more practical sweater and her elastic shell covering. She was carrying a large throwing knife, which was strung to a length of silk bundled around her hips.</p>

<p>“Trust me, she’s deadly with that thing,” Ayara swore as I gazed at the odd weapon.</p>

<p>“Never doubted it,” I muttered, before addressing the Arachne. “Is the house safe?”</p>

<p>“Perfectly intact,” she answered. “Just let me step through the door first when we return.”</p>

<p>Lorelei led us deeper into the woods, and soon I found myself lost. Nothing felt familiar, even the mana around me seemed alien and strange. Twisted trees loomed out of the darkness, and mana hung like fog across the landscape.</p>

<p>“Death, Darkness, Gloom, even Oblivion Mana,” Ishikana said with rapt interest. “I’ve never even seen the Oblivion element myself. I’ve only read descriptions of it in esoteric texts.”</p>

<p>“Yay us,” I deadpanned, my flashlight moving back and forth. “Do I even want to know what Oblivion mana is?”</p>

<p>“A heavy condensation of Darkness bordering on pure Void energy, the power of unmaking,” the Arachne answered.</p>

<p>“I bet I could make an amazing sword with that kind of mana,” Ayara said.</p>

<p>I wanted to chide her for the idea, but honestly, I was wondering how much Randy would pay me for Bottled Oblivion Mana.</p>

<p>The path curved down between ancient granite boulders. We spotted the occasional hoofprint in the muddy ground as the panicked animal fled deeper into the woods.</p>

<p>“I bet you five bucks Mitch, Josh, and Herb were trying to sacrifice that goat,” I said darkly.</p>

<p>Ayara snorted. “No bet. I’ve never seen three more guilty-looking Humans. The better question is, were they trying to sacrifice it on Herb’s farm or within your Lair.”</p>

<p>“And why were they sacrificing it?” Ishikana added. “I don’t imagine Humans go around murdering goats for no reason. Was it meant as a sacrifice to Baphomet?”</p>

<p>I shivered. “If we start suggesting the existence of demons, I’m going to be really upset.” I paused. “Wait, there <em>really </em>aren’t demons, are there?”</p>

<p>Ishikana shrugged. “Not that I know, but some Gods and Goddess have been demeaned as demons before.”</p>

<p>An unhappy expression flitted across Ayara’s face.</p>

<p>The path slopped even further, and soon a cave mouth came into view. It was formed from scarred bedrock, the opening surprisingly square and regular—as if it was intentionally crafted. Within it hung a strangely familiar door frame.</p>

<p>The door was ajar, and the prints led inside.</p>

<p>“Is it worth chasing a goat this far?” Ayara asked.</p>

<p>“Probably not, but now I want to know why there’s a fucking door here,” I answered. “Is this all part of Truth Seeker’s Boon?”</p>

<p>I couldn’t help thinking of the vision of the past I had experienced by the gorge. <em>Was my Lair granting me further insight thanks to my latest upgrade?</em></p>

<p>Channeling Hearth Flame, I ignited my blade. It glowed like a burning brand.</p>

<p><b>Class Hearthomancer has reached Lvl 7!</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiency Hearthomancy has reached Lvl 6!</b></p>

<p><b>Skill Hearth Flame has reached Lvl 6!</b></p>

<p>The triple notification threw me off balance. Lorelei’s eyes went wide in shock.</p>

<p>“The System responds strongly to personal resolve and decisive actions,” Ishikana said once we explained our surprise. “It also tends to interject before key moments.”</p>

<p>“Guess we’re going after that goat then,” Ayara sighed, before brightening. “Maybe I’ll get some level-ups too!”</p>

<p>My blade was far brighter than my flashlight, so I stowed it, freeing up my hand. Advancing first, Lorelei and I entered the cave, Ayara and Ishikana ducking in behind us. The misty mana flowed around us, the tide pulling it deeper into the cave.</p>

<p>The door frame was plain wood, identical to a dozen others in my house. I couldn’t place where I had seen it before.</p>

<p>Ahead, we could hear the pleading <em>bleat</em> of a goat. I stepped forward, trying to tell how far ahead the animal was. When I looked back, the doorway was gone, and a cave passage stretched off behind us.</p>

<p>“Guess we’re not going back that way,” Lorelei whispered.</p>

<p>With no other options, we followed the passage deeper. The rough bedrock was soon replaced by smooth stone. The floor flattened out as the passage widened. Small orbs of fire, like torches, began to appear in regular intervals along the walls.</p>

<p>We passed through another opening and found ourselves in a large chamber. A wave of disorientation hit us. Ayara and Ishikana recovered quickly, but it took Lorelei and me a moment to get over it.</p>

<p>When we did, I noticed that there were runes everywhere, carved in the floors, ceilings, and along the walls. The huge doorway at the far end of the room was covered with them, and they glowed an ominous shade of red like they’d been painted in blood.</p>

<p>I took a deep breath. “Okay, can anyone explain to me why there’s a Dungeon in my Lair?”</p>
