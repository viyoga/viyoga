<p>Lorelei drifted behind me, <em>tsking </em>and <em>tutting</em> at the dilapidated state of Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>Returning outside, I quickly cleaned up my tools and equipment, feeling guilty that I had left them sitting out overnight.</p>

<p>A workman was only as good as his tools. I resolved to treat mine a little better.</p>

<p>Lorelei kept glancing around, looking at the moldering exterior of the mansion and my piecemeal efforts to shore the place up. She was distressed by the boarded-up windows and the tarps stretched along the roof.</p>

<p>“This is just a start, don’t worry,” I assured her as I packed the last of my gear into the back of my truck. “It’ll take a while, but I’m going to fix this place up. Trust me.”</p>

<p>She gave me a small smile.</p>

<p>I was grateful that she seemed to know what a truck was. Rather than climbing inside though, she just drifted through the door and sat in the passenger seat. Her clothing changed, morphing from the skimpy nightgown and shawl to a more respectable, if old fashioned, dress. If she was aware of the change, Lorelei didn’t show it.</p>

<p>It felt weird to leave my new Lair so soon, but there wasn’t anything to eat, and I wasn’t one hundred percent sure about the tap water if I was being honest. Thankfully, there was a gas station at the edge of town. We’d pop in, grab some food and drinks, and then head back to Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>I explained all of this to Lorelei. She didn’t say anything, but she seemed relieved we would only be gone for a short while.</p>

<p>We rolled down the driveway slowly. The gravel path was pitted and rutted, making for a bumpy ride. The trees scraped at the sides of my truck. Rotwood Manor’s woods had grown wild and unruly, their growth likely boosted by the ambient mana.</p>

<p>I took a moment just to feel the different types of mana flowing through the property. Earth and Water seemed the strongest away from the manor. With my new connection I could also sense Wind, Fire, and Darkness as well, joined with currents of Life and Death.</p>

<p>I was sure if I fully explored and mapped the property, I’d find all the other elements represented. My plans for raising the funds necessary to fix the house suddenly seemed much closer within reach.</p>

<p>We both gave a small gasp as we reached the end of the driveway. The manor’s ambient mana, the fuel for my magic and Lorelei’s existence, suddenly dimmed. I could feel it trailing after us, connecting us to Rotwood Manor like an umbilical cord for an astronaut.</p>

<p>Still, I felt noticeably diminished. Sneaking a peek at my stats, I saw my MP was back to its normal levels.</p>

<p>That explained the information I’d seen regarding my Class description. Within their Lair, Hearthomancers were the undisputed kings and masters. But outside their territories, they lost much of their power.</p>

<p>I turned toward Lorelei. “Are you alright?”</p>

<p>The spirit nodded. “I think so? Everything is just—so strange. Ever since I saw that odd screen, the System, all this weird information has just been flowing through my head. I know stuff without knowing it,”</p>

<p>She sank into her seat. “How do I even know it’s called the System?”</p>

<p>It was a daunting task to explain everything to her. <em>Hell, I barely understood half the changes that had overtaken the world since The Reckoning.</em></p>

<p>“Let’s start with what you do know,” I suggested, driving slowly. “Before you saw your stats, you said you remembered the manor in better days, right?”</p>

<p>Lorelei’s brow creased. “I can. I remember when it used to be a grand manor. The floors were polished until they shone. The walls were decorated with portraits and paintings, and crystal chandeliers hung from the ceilings. My father was…”</p>

<p>She trailed off. “But he wasn’t my father, not really? I don’t know, it’s like I’m remembering another person’s life, seeing their memories, but I know they’re not my own?”</p>

<p>The Skill description had said the familiar’s personality was drawn from the Lair's history. <em>Maybe Lorelei was drawing on a previous resident or owner before the place fell into ruin?</em></p>

<p>“Do you know how long ago this was?” I asked.</p>

<p>“No,” she said faintly. “Though I do remember when the house was wired with electricity! The lights were amazing. They made everything so bright and with none of the soot stains caused by oil lamps!”</p>

<p>“Okay, so pretty far back,” I confirmed.</p>

<p>We came to an intersection, stopping at a red light.</p>

<p>Another truck rolled by. In the bed was a giant beast. It looked like a mountain lion, only about five times larger than any I had seen before. It was prickled with countless arrows.</p>

<p>Lorelei gawked in amazement. “What is that?”</p>

<p>“Some kind of Monster,” I guessed. “They sometimes spawn close to Dungeons. There’s one nearby, underneath Ashling State.”</p>

<p>The spirit looked at me quizzically.</p>

<p>“Okay, so let’s back up,” I said as the light changed. “Let’s start with The Reckoning.”</p>

<p>Nobody knew why The Reckoning had happened; some scientists argued it was caused by an unexpected shift in reality, our existence accidentally coming into alignment with another universe with conflicting rules. Religious leaders argued that it was a sign from god, though <em>which </em>god was another hotly debated subject.</p>

<p>Others claimed existence was just a simulation, and we’d been patched with an upgraded version. The Drow had their own explanation.</p>

<p>What couldn’t be argued with was the System, the overarching RPG rules that came into existence overnight, and how it redefined reality. Suddenly, everyone had stats that quantified their mental and physical abilities, and they had Skills, which seemed like magic.</p>

<p>“Proficiencies were a bit weird,” I said as we coasted down the road. “At first, they were all combat-related. Soldiers, athletes, historical recreationists, people who fought with their hands, suddenly saw all their experiences detailed and assigned level values.”</p>

<p>Lorelei frowned. “Like?”</p>

<p>I shrugged. “A martial artist would look at their stats and see a Proficiency for Unarmed Combat, a soldier would see entries for various firearms or any Survival training they had received. At first, all the Proficiencies were combat and survival related.”</p>

<p>But over time, new, non-combat entries had appeared, like the ones I had related to construction. There were Proficiencies for Singing, Dancing, Painting, Acting, Cooking, just about any activity or craft that took dedication and practice.</p>

<p>“The Drow explained that the System is adaptive. It grows as the civilizations it affects grows, creating new Proficiencies, then Classes and Skills,” I explained. “It starts combat-focused but eventually branches out.”</p>

<p>Lorelei quirked her brows. “Drow?”</p>

<p>“Dark elves,” I explained. “They’re a group of nomads Humanity first met when we started exploring the Dungeons.”</p>

<p>Dungeons were wormholes, links to other realities, other universes touched by the System. Like their name, they took the appearance of towers, underground labyrinths, or elaborate mazes, divided up into floors. Beyond Monsters, they held treasures and secrets, deadly perils, and tantalizing rewards.</p>

<p>“Some Dungeons, like the college's, are small; they don’t connect to anything,” I explained to the Hearth Spirit. “But the big ones? If you brave the depths and find an exit, you can find yourself in an all-new reality.”</p>

<p>That’s how Humanity had first met the Drow. They were religious refugees who’d fled their home reality centuries ago. They had drifted through the various Dungeons, trying to find a new sanctuary. The Drow were more than happy to share their knowledge of the System and Dungeons in turn for somewhere safe to call home. Almost every major city with a Dungeon featured a Drow enclave.</p>

<p>“You only tend to find Dungeons inside of big cities, a place with a lot of people, a lot of history, and a lot of mana,” I told the spirit.</p>

<p>Ashling State was kind of the exception to the rule. Nobody knew why the Dungeon had formed there, but the locals were more than happy to take advantage of the economic benefits that came from it.</p>

<p>“For example, the one inside Philadelphia is massive. It goes down at least fifty floors,” I explained.</p>

<p>Dungeons were fueled by mana, the ambient energy created by all of existence. It was another point of contention on <em>why</em>, but mana clustered around places of significance, places where important or dramatic events had occurred.</p>

<p>The largest Dungeon on Earth was in Damascus in Syria, the oldest city in the world. Adventurers were still trying to map its depths, but it was said to go beyond two hundred floors.</p>

<p>We reached the gas station. It was little more than a waystation, a place for people heading in or out of town to get gas and a snack.</p>

<p>Hopping out of my truck, I sent a quick text to my folks to let them know I was fine.</p>

<p>I had been staying in their basement, which had its own entrance. It wasn’t uncommon for me to come and go while they were still asleep. They rarely worried if they didn’t see me for a day or two, especially if I was working.</p>

<p>It was very tempting to tell them I was moving out, but I held off. There were things I needed to do first.</p>

<p>Only a handful of people were inside the store besides the clerk. A few glanced at Lorelei, but if they thought it was odd that I was being followed by a ghost, nobody commented. They were used to adventurers coming into town to delve into the Dungeon.</p>

<p>The spirit looked up and down the store’s narrow aisles. “For everything you told me, this just feels way too… normal. There are Monsters and Dungeons now, people can do magic!”</p>

<p>For a while, things<em> had</em> gone insane. I was in high school when The Reckoning had hit. I remember staying home, glued in front of the TV, as my family watched the news with bated breath.</p>

<p>I took a sip of the store’s coffee. It tasted like crap, but it had enough caffeine to help start my day.</p>

<p>“People still need to work, to make money,” I said with a shrug as I hit the shelves and grabbed something to eat. “Do you want anything?”</p>

<p>“No.” She shook her head.</p>

<p>I wasn’t even sure if she could eat or drink, honestly. I didn’t know a ton about familiars. None of the sources I had read had mentioned anything about them, but I was bonded to a Lair far more powerful than the average practitioner.</p>

<p><em>Hell, I’d never even heard of a Hearthomancer, my new Class, before.</em> I idly wondered if I had somehow unlocked a new Class. It wasn’t unheard of. Typically, folks who managed to unlock a new aspect of the System kept the news to themselves, only revealing what they’ve learned once they were sure of their new powers.</p>

<p>Identification Skills necessary to read another person’s Class were uncommon, at least on Earth. More often, people had perception Skills tied directly to their Class, like how I’d received Mana Perception from being a Wizard.</p>

<p>Paying for my purchases, I stopped by the corkboard by the door. There were a few notices, including a note that the town festival was set to be held that fall, just before Halloween. There were a few construction jobs, but I ignored them.</p>

<p>We got back into my truck and hit the road. It seemed like a perfectly normal, sedate weekday morning.</p>

<p>“But yeah, things did go mad for a while, but people are adaptable.” I drank my coffee as we headed back to the manor. “We still need bankers and bakers, or in my case, home contractors with an interest in Hearth Magic.”</p>

<p>In truth, once things settled down, most people had gone on and continued with their lives. Their day-to-day existence after The Reckoning was not all that much different than before. The world kept spinning.</p>

<p>Some though, specifically the younger generations, had taken full advantage of the powerful changes that had taken place.</p>

<p>“Instead of growing up to be bankers, or bakers, or whatever, some people become adventurers instead,” I continued. “They delve into Dungeons or hunt Monsters. Others became Crafters or Mentors, exploring the System itself, trying to piece together what it can really offer us.”</p>

<p>The System gave everyone two Classes. Most people used their first for their careers, while the secondary Classes were their hobbies or passions. Switching Classes was pretty straightforward. You just had to focus on a different field or project long enough to pick up the aligned Proficiencies. Eventually, a System notification would pop up, asking if you wanted to change one of your Classes.</p>

<p>“For example, if you worked as a contractor long enough, you’d be offered the chance to change your Class to Laborer. Once you unlock a Class, you can start learning Skills associated with it and you’ll get bonuses for your aligned Proficiencies growth.”</p>

<p>Nobody knew the exact reason why, but certain Classes were more closely related to certain Proficiencies than others. Wizards got magical expertise, while Laborers got practical job skills. You could earn Proficiencies outside your alignment, but they grew slower than ones that were aligned with your Class.</p>

<p>“I could drop everything and become a chef. I’d have to earn aligned Proficiencies, like Baking or Cooking, before being offered by the System to change one of my Classes to Crafter. After that, I’d start learning and growing at an accelerated rate.”</p>

<p>“The System just dumps that knowledge in your head?” Lorelei asked. “Like you suddenly know how to bake bread or sear a perfect steak?”</p>

<p>“Sort of,” I answered, unsure. “It just organizes things you already know and makes them easier to access. For example, a chef can easily recall a recipe, even if they only saw it at a glance, or remember a trick they heard about once to improve their cake. The higher their Proficiencies, the lower the chance of making a mistake or failing, depending on how much of a challenge they’re facing.”</p>

<p>With a thought I could easily recall every trick and tip I knew about Construction. Any wall I framed would be straight and plumb, and any Roofing I did would help ensure against leaks or wind damage.</p>

<p>“Everything has gone nuts,” the spirit lamented.</p>

<p>I grinned as we reached the driveway to Rotwood Manor. “Don’t get overwhelmed yet, this is just the beginning.”</p>
