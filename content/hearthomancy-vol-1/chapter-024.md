<p>Ayara insisted on helping me install the new gate when it arrived.</p>

<p>It was the kind of thing farmers used to corral their livestock, made from reinforced steel and supported by concrete-sunk posts. We bought a few extra sections of fencing to go along the sides, ensuring nobody could drive around it.</p>

<p>It would cost a fortune, but I was strongly considering fencing my entire property line by that point.</p>

<p>“I can put an enchantment on this,” the Drow said as we muscled the gate into place, settling it on its hinges. “I’ll have to come out and recharge it every day or so, but if anyone tries to jump over it or walk around it, they’ll get a zap.”</p>

<p>“Lethal?” Lorelei asked, a dangerous gleam in her eye.</p>

<p>“No, but it’ll make them think twice about trying to sneak down your driveway.” The Drow laughed. “It was one of the first enchantments I learned. I put it on my bedroom door when I was a kid. It would shock anyone who tried to sneak into my room.”</p>

<p>Stinky made one of his rare appearances. He brushed against the gate posts a few times, then sauntered off, disappearing back into the woods. We heard something, a mouse or a squirrel, give a shrill cry of surprise a moment later, followed by the sound of pursuit.</p>

<p>“I’ll never get used to your world’s cats,” Ayara said with exasperation.</p>

<p>“The Drow have cats?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>The Blacksmith wiped her brow. “Yeah, but they’re the size of tractor-trailers and eat intruders. The creatures you keep as pets seem far more insidious and dangerous.”</p>

<p>I noticed movement from the other side of the street. Glancing over my shoulder, I saw the curtains on Josh’s front windows twitch. I resisted the urge to flip the asshole off.</p>

<p>It had been two weeks since Merle’s visit. There hadn’t been any new attempts at harassment, but we 'd seen the cops drive by multiple times. I was sure Mitch, and the neighborhood watch were keeping an eye on us as well, just waiting for another opportunity to lodge a complaint. Before installing the gate, I had made sure to get all the appropriate permits.</p>

<p>“We could leave bags of flaming Griffin shit on their doorsteps,” Ayara offered, giving Josh’s house an evil glare.</p>

<p>“Where would we even get Griffin shit from?” Lorelei challenged.</p>

<p>Ayara laughed. “Trust me, I have my connections. Oh, I managed to sell that sword, by the way.”</p>

<p>“Already?” I was surprised, she had only finished it the day before.</p>

<p>She nodded. “An adventurer guild out of Philadelphia picked it up. They said if they like it, they’ll order more.”</p>

<p>It turns out, there was a website similar to Chad’s List just for equipment and Dungeon exploration gear. Ayara had sold a few pieces through the site before, and she had put up her new sword almost as soon as it was done.</p>

<p><b>Mana-Enhanced Longsword (Normal Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A longsword forged in an area of high mana density, improving its durability. Darkness can be seen along its razor-sharp edge.</em></p>

<p>“Mana-enhanced weapons aren’t common, at least not on Earth.” Lorelei double-checked the hinges on the gate, ensuring they met her standards. “They’ll be in high demand. If I can keep churning them out, not only will I level up faster, but I might be turning a profit faster than expected.”</p>

<p>“All good things,” Lorelei agreed.</p>

<p>“I’m kind of surprised you didn’t enchant it first,” I said.</p>

<p>“I’ve tried that before, but I’ve found most adventurers prefer to put their own enchantments on weapons,” she answered. “Remember, I’m still a novice, I can’t do permanent buffs yet. If I put a charge-based enchantment on an item, they’d have to find someone to refill it for them.”</p>

<p>I paused, glancing at the line of property stakes running alongside the roadway. “Can you put enchantments on anything?”</p>

<p>“As long as I can carve or etch a rune, sure. Metal is best, but wood or leather will work in a pinch.” She tilted her head. “What are you thinking, Brent?”</p>

<p>“I’m thinking I’m about to get that fence I wanted after all,” I chuckled darkly.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It had cost me several days' worth of Bottling Mana revenue, but the gate was a nice addition to my Lair’s security. I’d mounted the mailbox next to it, along with a doorbell camera so any potential guests would need to announce themselves before entering.</p>

<p>A new, hand-painted sign that stated <em>Intruders Will be Zapped and Debuffed</em> hung proudly across the gate.</p>

<p>The enchanted property line spikes took a few days, with a lot of trial and error. Ayara was more than happy to help, though, since she was making progress in both her Classes and Proficiencies.</p>

<p>It turned out wood wouldn’t work for my plan. The stakes held the zapping enchantment just fine, but when they discharged, the wood tended to explode. Eventually, we found a workaround, creating a metal cap to hold the enchantment. We also had to set a trigger threshold, since I didn’t want to zap any wildlife that crossed my property line, like deer or squirrels.</p>

<p>Lorelei was especially worried for Stinky’s safety, but Ayara confirmed the cat should be able to come and go safely.</p>

<p>Ayara gave me a pass rune, a little metal disk which I attached to my keychain. She made one for herself. As long as we held onto them, we wouldn’t get zapped crossing the stake line.</p>

<p>“I leveled up Runic Knowledge thinking of that one,” she said with a grin. “I think I’m close to leveling up my Enchanter Class too.”</p>

<p>“Then you’ll get the Fusion for Magical Blacksmith?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>“I certainly hope so,” Ayara replied. “The System likes to offer Evolutions and Fusions at level five, ten, and so on, but you can force them early. Ishikana helped me get Blacksmith and Enchanter when I was only a level three Crafter. With Crafter’s Boon, it’s entirely possible I’ll get offered Magical Blacksmith before I hit level ten in either of my Classes.”</p>

<p>Lorelei joined me as I went around the edge of the property. We pulled out the old stakes and replaced them with the new ones.</p>

<p>Lightning played around the enchanted stakes. Ayara warned us they had come out a bit more powerful than the enchantments she had created as a kid. They wouldn’t kill anyone, but they’d certainly leave an impression.</p>

<p>After we planted them, we could hear them <em>humming</em>, like an electrified fence.</p>

<p>Walking along t<em>h</em>e perimeter of my Lair, I could sense the mana on my property was changing, becoming thicker, and denser. There was a hint of wariness, of malice in the mana, like it was just waiting for an intruder to debuff.</p>

<p>I had to admit, I approved of the change. I didn’t know why Mitch and my neighbors were harassing me, but I wouldn’t put up with their shit. No one was going to force me out of my home or off my land.</p>

<p>As we went around the edge of my property, I kept an eye out for the junkyard. Based on my reckoning, it shouldn’t have been far from the property line adjacent to Mitch’s house, but I never spotted it. I made a note to have Lorelei lead me back there so I could map an accurate route and chart its location.</p>

<p>Thankfully, I didn’t hear any ominous whispers, and the overbearing presence I had sensed before was gone.</p>

<p>As we went along the back of the property, I stopped to show Lorelei where the tree-trunk bridge had been and the bonfire circle I had found. Double-checking, I was relieved to see nobody had used the fire pit recently. The ashes were old.</p>

<p>Still, I wasn’t too happy with all the Satanic crap all over my property.</p>

<p>Lorelei pointed at one of the rocks. “Hearth Restoration!”</p>

<p>Magic scoured against the stone like a hundred scrub brushes. The graffiti faded, but even after a half-dozen castings, I could still make out the pentagrams, bloody halos, and Greek characters for DIK.</p>

<p>“Bunch of assholes, wrecking up the place,” she muttered, running a finger over the boulder.</p>

<p>“Any idea what they were doing back here?” I asked.</p>

<p>“Maybe?” She closed her eyes and concentrated.</p>

<p>I heard Lorelei gasp as mana suddenly swelled around us. Darkness, Fire, and Death energy welled up, forming ghostly figures. We were surrounded by phantoms, by images from the past.</p>

<p>Day turned to night as the dead ashes burst to life.</p>

<p>The ghostly bonfire raged, the flames reaching high into the sky. Hundreds of figures drank, danced, fought, and fucked around us, little more than indistinct shadows. Mana flared around the rocks as pentagrams and halos were spray painted or etched into their side, the entire scene rippling and distorting around the desecration.</p>

<p>“Something is wrong,” Lorelei said, her face straining. “Brent, I can’t control it!”</p>

<p>The figures came to a stop, then began moving backward, rewinding through time. I caught glimpses of DIK frat boys and party girls, their clothing changing as fashion flowed backward in time. I was amazed to see the sheer amount of history, for how long people had been coming to the bonfire pit. Jeans and t-shirts gave way to dresses and collared shirts, to rustic frontier garb. They all worshiped around the bonfire. In the swirling mists, I caught sight of animals being dragged before the flames.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei yelled.</p>

<p>The mana became a vortex, a swirling storm sucking us deeper and deeper into the past. I saw one figure, a large man, kneeling before the flames. In one hand he held a hammer, in the other, a struggling animal.</p>

<p>“Stop, stop!” the Hearth Spirit screamed.</p>

<p>I used Mana Manipulation to grab the riptide of mana, trying to slow its maddening pace. Slowly, the scenes came to a stop, everything freezing in a singular moment.</p>

<p>The kneeling man remained in front of the bonfire, his hammer laying by his side. Something horrible, a darkness beyond all description, hung within the flames.</p>

<p>And then the scene vanished, fading back into the past. The mana receded, leaving us staggered and confused.</p>

<p>“Lorelei, are you alright?” I called.</p>

<p>She looked pale, even for a spirit.</p>

<p>“I think so? What was that?” She gazed at the burnt remains of the bonfire. “You saw that too, right?”</p>

<p>I nodded. “I think we need to do some further digging on the history of Rotwood Manor, from before your time. Who lived here—and what the fuck did they do?”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>We were both still feeling ill at ease by the time we finished staking the property line.</p>

<p>While passing the goat farm I spotted the farmer out in his pasture. A familiar figure, the drunk who had tried to break into my house, was standing next to him. The two had apparently been deep in a heated argument before we’d happened upon them.</p>

<p>I didn’t think they were talking about neighborhood watch business. They broke off their fight to stare at us, their gazes full of disturbing intensity.</p>

<p>I decided to ask Ayara to fast-track my new weapon.</p>

<p>Returning to the manor, I was surprised to see that a package had arrived for us. It was large, the sides festooned with shipping labels and <em>handle with care</em> stickers.</p>

<p>“From London,” Lorelei said, reading the shipping labels.</p>

<p>“It must be from Ishikana,” Ayara explained as we brought the box into the main hall. “In her last email she mentioned she had visited Great Britain—some kind of business trip.”</p>

<p>Opening it up, we found it was loaded with all kinds of weird, European sweets, cookies, and candy.</p>

<p>“Oh, she remembered my favorite crisps!” Ayara said, her accent suddenly sounding stronger than ever.</p>

<p>“Wow, there’s a ton of snacks in here!” The Hearth Spirit said, taking a deep sniff. “I’m almost jealous that I can’t eat!”</p>

<p>Stacking the box’s contents, I saw there was a small, thin package at the bottom. There was a letter addressed to Ayara taped on top, but the package itself had my name on it.</p>

<p>“For Brent Owens.” The handwriting was neat and elegant.</p>

<p>Ayara took the envelope. “Ishikana sent you something?”</p>

<p>I ripped the top of the package open. “I guess so?”</p>

<p>Inside was a thin booklet, some kind of manual. I could feel mana, condensed and constrained, trapped within the booklet’s pages.</p>

<p>Ayara’s eyes went wide. “It’s a Skill Book!”</p>

<p><b>Skill Book: Identify (Superior Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A Skill Book that contains the Skill Identify. Once used, this book will crumble to dust.</em></p>

<p>I carefully flipped through the booklet. It only contained a few pages, each one covered in the same, neat script that had been used to address the envelope. The last page contained a complex diagram, a flowing set of circular lines with rows upon rows of runes written between each one. The mana glowed from the page.</p>

<p>“So, wait, this thing can just <em>teach</em> someone a Skill?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>Ayara nodded. “Yes, the first few pages contain instructions and applications for the Skill, but it’s the diagram at the back that essentially imprints the Skill in the reader’s mind. Once that happens, the book will disintegrate.”</p>

<p>I looked at the front cover of the book, but it was blank. “How come I’ve never heard of one of these things before?”</p>

<p>Ayara ducked her head, looking embarrassed. “There’s kind of an—embargo on these things, at least for worlds only recently integrated into the System.”</p>

<p>“You’ve been holding out on us,” Lorelei realized.</p>

<p>“Not me! I wish I could make Skill Books, even ones for common Skills like Identify go for a premium amount,” the Blacksmith swore, before clearing her throat. “There aren’t many Crafters who can make Skill Books. These Crafters have been <em>discouraged</em> from trading them to Humanity. We’ve discovered in the past that giving newly integrated worlds Skill Books can have—adverse effects.”</p>

<p>I warily put the book down. “Define adverse effects.”</p>

<p>“Nothing bad will happen to you!” Ayara promised. “It’s just that using Skill Books can damage the overall growth and unlock speed for newly integrated species. Rather than pushing themselves to unlock new Skills, people end up relying on Skill Books too much. And if they’re not pushing themselves—”</p>

<p>“They are not unlocking new Classes, Skills, or Proficiencies either,” I said, finishing her sentence.</p>

<p>“Basically,” she agreed. “They become hooked on Skill Books, trying to take the easy way out. The supply can never keep up with demand, leading to violence in some rare situations.”</p>

<p>I could see people killing for Skill Books. The ability to shortcut the System and gain abilities faster than normal, or totally outside one’s Class alignment, was hugely tempting.</p>

<p>Ayara glanced at the book in my hands. “In this case, I don’t think there will be an issue. You’re already sufficiently driven and motivated to master your Class. Using a Skill Book to unlock a common Skill like Identify should be okay.”</p>

<p>I remembered her stat sheet. “Is that how you got Identify?”</p>

<p>She shook her head. “I learned it as a kid. It was one of the first Skills I got, but I haven’t put the effort into leveling it. I usually rely on Eyes of Discernment instead, since I spend most of my time checking raw materials or finished goods.”</p>

<p>“So, this Ishikana lady just gave us a book worth its weight in gold?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>“Ishikana’s secondary Class is Scribe,” the Drow explained. “She can create Skill Books for any Skill she knows. Her works are in high demand in many Drow households because of how useful they are.”</p>

<p>“Brent, you should use the book!” Lorelei said excitedly.</p>

<p>Ayara opened the envelope addressed to her and began reading the letter.</p>

<p>“I still want to know why she sent a Skill Book in the first place,” I said stubbornly.</p>

<p>“When she saw your stats, she was appalled by the fact that you didn’t have Identify,” Ayara said, scanning the first page of the letter. “She feels your notes and observations are lacking because you are missing what she considers a <em>key Skill</em>.”</p>

<p>“Geez, how nice of her,” Lorelei snarked. “And why did she send a letter? I thought most modern people only used emails.”</p>

<p>“Ishikana is older than she admits, but typically she only likes to send letters when she’s about to play a trick on someone…” Ayara began reading the second page of the letter. Her face went pale.</p>

<p>“Well, shit,” she whispered.</p>

<p>I looked at her. “What’s wrong?”</p>

<p>Ayara lowered the letter. “Ishikana said she’s coming for a visit!”</p>
