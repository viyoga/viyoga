<p>Ayara was just as dismayed by Ishikana’s findings as we were when she got home. Gathering in the main hall, we got busy on our phones, comparing the known deaths surrounding Rotwood Manor against a list of solar eclipses we’d found on Wikiplace.</p>

<p>It wasn’t a hundred-percent match, but it was close enough to confirm our worst fears.</p>

<p>“I say we strike first!” the Blacksmith argued. “We burn those freaks out, torch their homes.”</p>

<p>Ishikana shook her head. “If we do that, they’ll have every justification for sending law enforcement after us. I wouldn’t be surprised if they’re taunting us with the gatherings at the goat farm, trying to get us to attack them.”</p>

<p>We discussed calling in the FBI, or leaning on Ishikana’s contacts in the government, but I ultimately decided against it. There was no guarantee that anyone who came to town would be resistant to the malaise affecting Ashling Grove. I did not want the FBI joining the siege.</p>

<p>“I still can’t believe the whole town is supporting them,” Lorelei argued. “The town festival isn’t just a cover for some crazy Baphomet worshippers, I’m positive!”</p>

<p>Ishikana had done further digging but hadn’t found any connection between the town and devil worship outside DIK. There wasn’t a hint of occultism support amongst the townsfolk. Even the Order of Stone made regular appearances at the town’s one Episcopalian church, much to the other partitioner’s discomfort.</p>

<p>It was doubly confusing when I mentioned my trip out of town. Ishikana made me describe the uncomfortable, unwanted sensation I had experienced when returning to Ashling Grove.</p>

<p>“They’re likely using magic against us,” she concluded. “Some kind of curse or mind control Skill, warping the townsfolk’s perception of us, making them view us as enemies or Monsters. They could even be forcing the townsfolk to support their Baphomet worship.”</p>

<p>She scowled. “Such Skills come with a cost though, usually paid in blood. I fear the missing persons the cops asked about this morning are already dead.”</p>

<p>That made me wish we could grab the next upgrade for my Lair, I’d grab one of the offered Banes. It was just a matter of which one.</p>

<p>“I did discover one more thing,” Ishikana said, pulling out her phone.</p>

<p>She pulled out an emergency contact form.</p>

<p>“They refused to dig up a local history expert for me, but I managed to get a peek at some files I wasn’t supposed to see,” she said with a grin. “He retired and moved out of town four years ago, but Professor Scott Brown was listed as Ashling State’s foremost expert on local history and folklore.”</p>

<p>“Where’s he living now?” I asked, noticing the form was several years out of date.</p>

<p>“Here’s his address, according to Gaggle,” Lorelei said after hammering away on her tablet. “Damn, I don’t know how we survived without the Internet, it’s amazing!”</p>

<p>Professor Brown lived a few hours out of town. I tried calling the phone number Lorelei had found but only got a voicemail. Being as succinct as possible, I left a message asking Brown to call me back as soon as possible.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Good, come at me again!” Ayara challenged.</p>

<p>Wiping the sweat from my brow, I adjusted my grip on the practice sword and took another swing at her. The Drow easily deflected the blow and lunged forward, swatting her blade against my neck.</p>

<p>“Better,” she said.</p>

<p>“Doesn’t feel better,” I groused, rubbing the welt.</p>

<p>Drow martial practice was much rougher and more hands-on than the gyms Vince and other wannabe Human Warriors used. As soon as I had said I wanted to practice swordcraft, Ayara had taken me out into the backyard and crafted two practice swords from bits of spare wood.</p>

<p>Even as the house’s exterior lights came on, she pushed me to keep practicing. With all the frustration and anger I was feeling, and the weight of inaction hanging over me, I was more than happy to throw myself into sparring.</p>

<p>Ishikana was prowling the grounds with Lorelei. The Arachne had offered to increase my Lair’s defenses with her traps and snares, which I had gladly accepted. I hoped that would complete my last remaining Lair Quest.</p>

<p>Ayara and I were crossing swords once again when an orange blur shot out of the bushes and streaked between us. I barely dodged as Stinky rushed past us and into the house.</p>

<p>“Stinky, come back!”</p>

<p>Lorelei emerged from the trees, Ishikana right behind her.</p>

<p>“What happened?” I asked.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit glared at the Scholar. “Someone tried to catch Stinky in a web!”</p>

<p>Ishikana shrugged. Her palps, the front limbs that I had first mistaken for legs, crossed and then switched back. “It was an accident, I swear. When I heard that bush rattling, my predator instincts just went off.”</p>

<p>“Meanie.” Lorelei drifted toward the house. “Stinky? Stinky! Come on out, we’ll feed you tuna!”</p>

<p>I had no clue if we had any tuna, but I followed the Hearth Spirit back into the house. She floated around a bit, calling the cat’s name, before heading downstairs. Ayara and Ishikana tailed after us.</p>

<p>After Ishikana had moved in, I had expected my basement to become a warren of webs and threads, but the Scholar was far more tidy than I had anticipated. Passing her room, I saw there was only a single web in the corner, which she likely used as a bed. Her trunks and luggage were arranged around the room, revealing the countless outfits and articles of clothing she had packed.</p>

<p>“A woman is only as powerful as her wardrobe,” she replied as she leaned over me. “Clothing is just another weapon.”</p>

<p>Ayara snorted.</p>

<p>We didn’t find the cat, but we didn’t find any jars full of cursed water or any other strange relics either, which was a bit of relief. I thought we had enough on our plate as it was.</p>

<p>Returning to the base of the stairs, Lorelei was distraught. “I was sure he came down here!”</p>

<p>Glancing at the door under the stairs, I suddenly felt a wave of déjà vu. Like a man in a dream, I reached out and grabbed the brass doorknob.</p>

<p>Mana surged around me, snapping through the air like a gust of wind. The others reeled back, shocked by the sudden burst of power.</p>

<p>Turning the knob, the door swung open and Stinky rushed out, running back upstairs.</p>

<p>Ahead of me stretched the Dungeon.</p>

<p>“Grab your gear,” I said, propping the door open. “We’re going in.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“This feels incredibly reckless,” Ishikana complained.</p>

<p>“But right,” I replied, my hand tight around the hilt of my sword.</p>

<p>The moment I had opened the door, I felt called to the depths of the Oubliette of the Forgotten. Lorelei sensed the same compulsion. She drifted behind me, fists clenched.</p>

<p>Ayara and Ishikana brought up the rear. Despite her complaints, the Scholar was fully armed and ready to help us drive off any Shadow Dregs or other Monsters. Ayara looked eager to have something she could hit.</p>

<p>I shared the sentiment.</p>

<p>The Dungeon seemed different than before. The rune-carved walls spanned around us, but the twists and turns had changed, the layout shifting in our absence.</p>

<p>The ever-present cry, the inhuman call of hunger we had heard before, was gone as well. The only sound was the echo of our footsteps and the rasp of our breathing. It was utterly unnerving, but we pressed on.</p>

<p>Emerging into a large chamber, I saw the floor was littered with bleached bones. Not a scrap of flesh or blood remained across the sacrificial remains.</p>

<p>“Goats, dogs, cats—no Humans, at least as far as I can see,” Ishikana said, giving a bone pile a critical eye.</p>

<p>“Thank goodness we managed to rescue Stinky,” Lorelei replied.</p>

<p>Ayara raised her mace. “To arms!”</p>

<p>Writhing Shadow Dregs pushed up through the bones, rising like a foul miasma. They burst forth from rib cages and skulls, soundless screaming for more blood, more sacrifices.</p>

<p>My blade was sheathed in bright flames. All the lessons and tutorials on sword fighting flew right out of my head as I hacked and slashed at the Monsters. They crashed into us like a dark tide, all claws and shadowy tendrils.</p>

<p>Ayara, once again armed with a shield and mace, pushed to the fore to act as our vanguard. Lorelei and I fought by her side as Ishikana leapt onto the ceiling. Skittering above us, she rained down Skills as we were fighting below.</p>

<p>It was a bloody melee. I felt cuts and bites rip across my arms and chest, but I kept on fighting. Every blow Lorelei took was echoed across our bond. Even if the Hearth Spirit could not be truly wounded, she could still feel pain. Still, for every wound she took, she dealt two back.</p>

<p>Ayara fought like a titan. Shadow Dregs exploded into mist with each strike of her mace, and her shield was scored with countless claw marks. She activated a rune on her weapon, and the mace began to glow with a silvery light.</p>

<p>Ayara kept up a litany of battle cries, invoking Umbralis and the honor of the Drow houses.</p>

<p>A Shadow Dreg lashed a tendril around my leg and prepared to take a chunk out of my thigh. I tried to use Mana Manipulation to blast it away with a burst of raw mana, but the arcane energy fizzled away. I just couldn’t concentrate.</p>

<p>Instead, I smashed the Shadow Dreg with the pommel of my blade, its body roiling like jelly. Kicking hard, I heaved it away. Ayara finished a moment later, stomping it under her work boots.</p>

<p>Another wave of Dregs was destroyed by a cascade of frost as Ishikana unleashed a torrent of Ice Bolts. She weaved back and forth as she dodged impossibly warped limbs and maws. The creatures rolled over the floors and walls, stretching and distorting against the glare of Hearth Flame.</p>

<p>I slashed my blade through a hulking Dreg, the cut leaving a burning trail in my vision. As I blinked my eyes clear, I realized there were still more Monsters coming, called to life by the bones around us.</p>

<p>“Lorelei!” I called.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit joined me. Drawing the power of our connection and the fire raging in Rotwood Manor’s hearth, we sent a boiling wave of Hearth Flame across the floor. The bones were charred and reduced to ash, then to nothing. The scouring fire washed through the chamber, utterly annihilating the sacrificial offerings.</p>

<p>The flames burned without smoke. The Dregs cried out and vanished, leaving nothing but fire-cleansed stone.</p>

<p>“Everyone alright?” I asked.</p>

<p>Ishikana jumped down and pointed toward my foot; blood was pooling around the sole of my boot. Something had wrapped its claws around my ankle, ripping my skin and soaking my sock in blood.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei cried.</p>

<p>Feeling the first pangs of pain, I sat against the wall and pulled out the first aid kit I had packed.</p>

<p>“We really need to recruit a Medic,” I joked weakly as I pulled down my shredded sock.</p>

<p>The injury was bleeding freely, but it wasn’t as deep as I had first feared. I quickly wrapped it up, staunching the wound. Using Second Wind, I heaved to my feet.</p>

<p>I hadn’t received any level-ups during the brawl, but Ayara confirmed she had picked up advances in her combat Proficiencies.</p>

<p>“Sadly, these Monsters are too weak for me to level,” Ishikana said. “I theorize the System has safeguards put in place to prevent the strong from victimizing the weak, even Monsters. Generally, you need to fight Monster at or above your level to advance, and there is no reward for killing other intelligent beings unless that’s part of your Class.”</p>

<p>“There are classes for killers?” Lorelei gasped.</p>

<p>“Cultists are a common, problematic Class. They can advance by <em>sacrificing</em> living beings to their Gods or masters,” the Scholar responded. “I didn’t believe the Class was available on Earth yet, but recent events are making me reconsider my position.”</p>

<p>“You’ve never actually seen Mitch, Josh, or Herb in person, have you?” I mused. “If you get a chance, can you use Identify on them?”</p>

<p>“Yes. I have an evolved version, Piercing Insight, which should instantly reveal their Class and even their Skills,” she answered.</p>

<p>Ayara hadn’t been able to read their Classes during our earlier confrontations. If nothing else, that clearly indicated they were more powerful than they let on.</p>

<p>The discussion helped distract me from the throbbing pain running through my leg. Ayara helped me walk. Despite the battle, I was still being called deeper into the depths.</p>

<p>We found another doorway at the far end of the chamber. Limping further, we traveled down the spiraling path. Runes ran along the walls, but Ayara didn’t recognize them. Several times she paused us to take photos with her phone.</p>

<p>“Shit, I just leveled up Runic Knowledge,” she muttered.</p>

<p>We reached another doorway. The passage we had been walking down had steadily widened, leading to an opening that was so large that Ishikana could step through without ducking.</p>

<p>My Hearth Flame barely illuminated the massive room. Like the vision in the cave, the chamber was dome-shaped, the ceiling rising far, far overhead. But unlike the cave, there was nothing in the center of the room. A few meters ahead, the floor fell away, leaving a huge, circular pit that seemed to drop away to infinity.</p>

<p>“So, the real estate agent failed to mention the bottomless pit,” I snarked as I limped to the edge and peered down.</p>

<p>“Guess we don’t have to worry about garbage service anymore,” Ayara joked, trying to add some levity to the moment.</p>

<p>“I’m still keeping my compost pile,” Lorelei said insistently.</p>

<p>Ishikana crept up to the edge of the pit, but no further.</p>

<p>Looking down, I saw the pit’s walls were perfectly smooth, without a crack or seam. There were spots of ash and dust, but that was it. I couldn’t even feel wind or air coming up from below, and there was no mana either. It was a perfectly vacant space, a hole to nowhere.</p>

<p>“Have you ever seen anything like this before?” I asked the Scholar.</p>

<p>She shook her head. “Brent, do you know what an oubliette is?”</p>

<p>“No,” I answered.</p>

<p>“It’s a Human word, an old one,” she replied. “It’s a type of dungeon or prison, formed from a deep pit or shaft. The only escape route is to climb out from below.”</p>

<p>I gazed toward the bottom of the pit. All I could see were endless shadows.</p>

<p>It felt rather anticlimactic, fighting through a room of Monsters to reach an empty room and a seemingly bottomless pit, but there were no further rooms or passages off the chamber. Lorelei closed her eyes and confirmed with Dungeon Cartography that we were at a dead end.</p>

<p>The feeling drawing me into the Dungeon faded, leaving me exhausted. After two battles in two days, I was a bundle of aches.</p>

<p>Shaking my head, I suggested we all turn around and head home. There seemed to be no answers, no epiphanies in the Dungeon, at least that day.</p>

<p>As Ayara helped me out of the room, I noticed a bit of litter pushed against the wall. Stopping her, I painfully crouched down and saw it was bits of broken, clay pottery. Nothing came up when I used Identify, but I felt an echo of mana around them just the same.</p>

<p>Sweeping them up, I carried them with me as we returned to the manor.</p>
