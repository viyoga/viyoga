<p>I groaned as I found myself lost in some strange dream.</p>

<p>It wasn’t the usual nightmare, where Mitch and the DIK frat chased me through the woods with their sacrificial knives.</p>

<p>Instead, I found myself in a jungle of impossibly vast trees. Huge ferns, thorny vines, and tropical flowers surrounded me. They were dyed a riot of colors, greens, blues, reds, and yellows, all somehow brighter, more vivid than anything I’d seen before.</p>

<p>Somewhere, in the distance, I heard the mournful howl of a beast. The scenery blurred like a wet oil painting as I turned my head, but it appeared I was alone.</p>

<p>The dream drove me to push through the underbrush, the plants around me heavy with early morning dew. The landscape continued to melt and meld around me, shifting with uncanny speed.</p>

<p>Finally, I drew upon a huge structure, some kind of forgotten temple or palace. I rose from a clearing, the pillared tiers climbing toward a purple, cloudless sky. A blood-red sun was just rising overhead.</p>

<p>Vines hung along its exterior, and moss clung to its walls. Time had ravaged the strange, alien carvings along its sides, but I swore I recognized some of them.</p>

<p>“It’s not time for this,” a faint voice whispered in my ear. “Not yet!”</p>

<p>Before I could turn to see who was speaking, the dream popped like a bubble. Everything vanished, and I found myself in my bed. Still weary from the effects of my nightmares, it felt as though the attack from DIK had just happened yesterday. But as I fully awoke to the birds chirping outside, I recalled that weeks had already passed.</p>

<p>“Morning,” Lorelei said, materializing above me.</p>

<p>I grinned, the odd dream already fading from my thoughts. “Morning.”</p>

<p>“I’d ask if you were feeling better, but I know you're sick of that question,” the Hearth Spirit said. “Feel up to a few small jobs around the house?”</p>

<p>I sat up in bed. “I think I can manage.”</p>

<p>Lorelei sighed. “Honestly, I’d love to just spend the day snuggling under the blankets with you, but I know you’ll—”</p>

<p>She gave an <em>eep</em> of surprise as I pulled her onto the bed. Rolling over, I pressed her into the mattress.</p>

<p>“There’s a lot I want to get done, but I think we can spare a few minutes,” I told her firmly.</p>

<p>She grinned and then pulled me in for a kiss.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Even Lorelei’s delightful distraction couldn’t keep me in bed all day. I was itching to get back to work. There was so much to do.</p>

<p>Entering the main hall, I could hear Ayara already out at her forge. Her hammer rose and fell with a consistent, strangely comforting clamor.</p>

<p>The System hadn’t been super clear with all the rewards we had received for defeating Mitch. We hadn’t earned a Lair level up or any new Boons or Banes, but we had all received a ton of Class and Skill advancement. Even Ishikana had gained a Class level. Ayara had been offered her Class Fusion, allowing her to become a Magical Blacksmith.</p>

<p>Since then, she had been dedicated to leveling her new Class. When she wasn’t with me or at work, she was at her forge. She’d even gone part-time at the steel mill, sure that her new offering of weapons and armor would make up for any shortfall from her reduced paycheck.</p>

<p>Her mana-infused swords were already a hit with her customers; she had just received an order for twenty more the day before. Ayara was sure she’d be able to earn even more once she figured out how to engrave long-term enchantments on them.</p>

<p>The manor’s main hall was a bit of a mess since it had become our headquarters in fixing all the damage from the Cultists' attack. Supplies were stacked all over the place, and our poor blackboard was a mess of to-do lists and schedules for when work teams were coming in.</p>

<p>Most of the exterior of the house was covered in tarps and scaffolding, including the giant hole Mitch had blown through the front of the manor. Glancing at the schedules, I was glad to see we were on track to have everything patched by late November.</p>

<p>Going through a Pennsylvania winter with only three walls was a bit too daunting, even for me.</p>

<p>The immediate aftermath of DIK’s assault had been pure chaos. The town elders had been forced to call in emergency responders from across the county. Local hospitals had been flooded by patients suffering from exhaustion, minor burns, broken bones, and more.</p>

<p>It took us nearly a full day to cut everyone free from Ishikana’s webs or to pull them from the traps. A few folks had even gotten lost on the Lair’s grounds, lured into dead ends by the phantoms.</p>

<p>I made sure to revoke the invitation I had extended the townsfolk. It had been necessary to ensure that Vile Will’s Bane would destroy Candy’s mind control, but by that point I was more than happy to kick everyone out.</p>

<p>At first, the cops had wanted to arrest me. But I had stood my ground, especially when Merle and half the town came to my defense. They didn’t fully understand what had happened, but they knew they were only alive thanks to our intervention.</p>

<p>Things had only become more complicated when the cops learned their body cams had been running the entire time. Mitch and Candy hadn’t realized they were standard issue with riot gear, so the entire event had been recorded.</p>

<p>When some of the footage was leaked onto the Internet, Ashling Grove’s elders had gone into full damage control mode. The town festival was already ruined; the last thing they wanted was videos of their police officers opening fire on an innocent homeowner defending himself from insane Cultists.</p>

<p>It took a few weeks to hash out the details, but we eventually came to a deal. In return for keeping my mouth shut, and not suing the fuck out of the town, I got a hefty settlement.</p>

<p>I’d need the money to fix the house. Outside repairing the hole, I was going to redo the siding and replace the windows. If I had any money left over afterward, I figured I’d deal with my mortgage.</p>

<p>Merle would still come by for inspections, but he promised to go easy on us.</p>

<p>The bodies of Mitch, Josh, Herb, and the other dead Cultists were never recovered. The few survivors of DIK were in jail, charged with thousands of different crimes: kidnapping, assault, attempted murder, and murder.</p>

<p>I was sure they’d be hit with even more charges once the cops finished excavating the graves hidden below Herb’s barn. Even the FBI had shown up, investigating a decade and a half of missing persons cases.</p>

<p>Candy was in a mental institution. The backlash from Vile Will’s Bane had left her non-responsive; the authorities were unsure she’d ever be well enough to stand trial.</p>

<p>“Brent, remember, you’re supposed to take it easy, so pick some <em>small jobs</em>,” Lorelei said.</p>

<p>I snapped away from my recollections; I had too much work to do to dwell on what had happened.</p>

<p>“Right, I’m sure if I hurt myself again, I’ll never hear the end of it.” I chuckled. “Let’s start with some coffee first, then we can see what needs to be done.”</p>

<p>Stinky wandered into the main hall and flopped down in the bed Lorelei had built for him. He had shown up the day after the siege, acting like nothing had happened.</p>

<p>At least the Hearth Spirit had been able to give him a much needed bath. He still smelled like old sneakers, but the stench wasn’t nearly as eye-watering as it had been before.</p>

<p>Yawning, he sprawled out on his back and watched as I drank my morning coffee. Lorelei stayed to play with him afterward as I went to check on Ishikana.</p>

<p>The Scholar had taken over a series of rooms across the main hall from the servant’s quarters. She had transformed them into her office, lining their walls with bookshelves and whiteboards. Webbing had been used to organize notes and photographs along the ceilings and walls.</p>

<p>Ishikana was behind her desk, typing on a laptop while she sorted papers with her spidery limbs.</p>

<p>“Good morning,” I called.</p>

<p>She looked up from her work and gave me an easy smile. “Good morning, Brent. How are you feeling?”</p>

<p>“Better,” I admitted.</p>

<p>It had taken me some time to shake the wounds I had received, even with my mom coming by twice a day to check in on me. My condition had been complicated by the nasty case of backlash I’d received from ingesting too many Healing Potions. I had been eager to get out of bed and get back to work, but the women in my life pushed me to rest and recover.</p>

<p>“Wonderful!” Ishikana skittered around the desk. “I once again wanted to thank you for agreeing to my proposition. Are you sure I can’t pay you?”</p>

<p>I shook my head. “Nope. You risked your life to help us. I’d never be able to look at myself in the mirror again if I took any more of your money.”</p>

<p>“Outside the grocery bill.” The Arachne’s palps twitched as she grinned. “But in all honesty, thank you. I promise to repay the trust you’ve put in me.”</p>

<p>Ishikana had officially become Rotwood Manor’s Scholar in residence. She’d never publish my name, but Ishikana was allowed to share any findings about the System she made based upon her research.</p>

<p>Apparently, her most recent thesis was already causing quite a stir in the Archives back in her home reality. The Goblins were incensed, much to her delight.</p>

<p>Ishikana crouched to eye-level and took my hands. Her grip was hard but warm.</p>

<p>“Brent. I can’t think of all the words necessary to express my gratitude.” She blinked at me; a look of admiration clear on her inhuman face.</p>

<p>My brain flatlined. Ever since the battle, Ishikana had been surprisingly earnest with me, apparently seeing me in a new, more impressive light. I had no idea how to deal with our evolving relationship. Part of me was definitely attracted to her; from the waist up, she was a gorgeous woman. But part of me was still dealing with the fact that she had the butt of a giant, freaking spider.</p>

<p>The life of a Hearthomancer was infinitely complex and challenging.</p>

<p>Ishikana was continuing her research into the history of Ashling Grove and the horror under the house, but we had no clue what kind of entity Eustace Phelps had summoned and then sealed. The Arachne wasn’t even sure if he had called the being into our reality, or merely bound an already present nightmare by the rules and rituals he knew.</p>

<p>Either way, I would keep my oath. There was no way in hell that thing was ever getting loose while I still drew breath.</p>

<p>Bidding the Scholar farewell, I popped outside to check on Ayara. She was intensely focused on her work, so I decided not to disturb her. Instead, I sent her a text, reminding her to find me when she took a break.</p>

<p><em>Maybe we could have lunch together?</em></p>

<p>I spent the rest of the morning doing a few odd tasks around the house. Lorelei reappeared in order to supervise, making sure I didn’t overexert myself. I cleaned up the last of the junk left from the siege, then worked on a task I had long ignored, repairing the floor in front of the hearth. The ritual circles I had carved in the wood still needed to be fixed.</p>

<p>“There,” I huffed as I inspected my work an hour later. “A bit more sanding, and you won’t even know they were there!”</p>

<p>“Don’t worry, I’ll always remember,” Lorelei said fondly.</p>

<p>After that, I decided to try and map out the property a bit more. We had discovered several formally hidden areas while searching for lost townsfolk. I wanted to try and add them to my already deeply convoluted map.</p>

<p>We brought jars for Bottling Mana in case we found new gathering spots. Truth Seeker’s Boon might become our secret money maker, depending on what kind of gathering spots we found.</p>

<p>“I swear, when I bought this place, it was only twenty acres, but I’m sure it’s way more than that now,” I said as I pulled out my map.</p>

<p>I still hadn’t found the cave where I had experienced the vision of Eustace Phelps, but I was determined to locate it. If nothing else, I wanted to offer his phantom my thanks.</p>

<p>We slipped easily through the woods, the land practically bending to ease our passage. My map now covered several pages; I had been forced to tape on additions as it kept expanding. Ishikana promised me a <em>reward</em> if I ever managed to fully chart my domain, but it seemed like a losing bet every time I tried.</p>

<p>Still, I suspected the Arachne would give me my <em>reward</em> either way.</p>

<p>“Brent, I think we just found another Gloom mana source!” Lorelei floated toward a rocky crevice to our right.</p>

<p>Following the Hearth Spirit, I scrambled down the rocks, once again glad for my boots. Such an appropriate piece of equipment as a Dungeon reward made me wonder how aware the System was. <em>Was it playing favorites? Was that why I became the Master of Rotwood Manor while Mitch and DIK were blocked from purchasing it time after time?</em></p>

<p>At the bottom of the crevice was a small creek, one I hadn’t been aware of. Grumbling that I’d need to revise my map again, I used Mana Manipulation. Darkness and Water mana swirled together, forming a small torrent of Gloom I could bottle.</p>

<p>Randy would be thrilled. He had been asking for more unique elements in his quest for the Alchemist Class. At this point, I was wondering how close he <em>really </em>was to unlocking the Class, but as long as he was paying, I was selling. The settlement from the town’s elders was nice, but I knew Rotwood Manor would still be a money pit even after the latest round of repairs.</p>

<p>Grabbing a jar, I worked with Lorelei to harvest the mana. It was tricky at first. Gloom, by definition, was immaterial and temporary, a combination of Darkness and Water mana that was defined by chilling mist.</p>

<p>“Brent?” Lorelei tapped my shoulder. “Look!”</p>

<p>Raising my head, I saw a familiar, wood panel door set into the opposite side of the crevice. Dried blood was crusted over the brass knob.</p>

<p>Carefully, I climbed over the creek and put my hand on the doorknob. A small shock, like static electricity, ran over my fingers.</p>

<p>Lorelei hovered worriedly behind me as I opened the door. On the other side was a wintery landscape, a forest made from frost and snow. Flowers, formed from blue and white rime, covered the ground, while trees like pillars of ice rose into the sky. In the distance, I could make out the crumbling ruins of a castle.</p>

<p>“Well—shit,” I muttered, staring at the impossible vista.</p>
