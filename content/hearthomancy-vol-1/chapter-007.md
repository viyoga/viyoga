<p>“Close your eyes and make a wish,” Frank said.</p>

<p>I heard electricity snap and crackle as the electrician turned on the main breaker. The lights strung along the basement ceiling flickered to life.</p>

<p>“We did it!” Lorelei cheered, pumping her fists into the air. “High Five!”</p>

<p>I slapped the spirit’s outstretched palm. Frank did the same a moment later.</p>

<p>She had discovered High Fives after watching some schlocky 80s movie, and it had become her go-to celebratory move. It was, frankly, cute as fuck, so I was more than happy to indulge her.</p>

<p>“I didn’t think that would work,” Frank admitted as he slumped to sit on the floor. “Would you believe I managed to level up my Class and two of my Proficiencies with that?”</p>

<p>Frank was an older guy, well into his fifties. He was one of the fewer older folks I knew who had jumped at the opportunities the System had provided.</p>

<p>A lifelong electrician, he had selected Crafter as his main Class and Wizard as his secondary Class. He specialized in Lightning Magic and had picked up a slew of related Skills and Proficiencies to complement his profession. Over the years, Frank had become the go-to wildcat electrician when dealing with odd-ball installations and old homes.</p>

<p>I reached out and shook his hand. “I can’t thank you enough.”</p>

<p>“Don’t worry, the money helps.” He laughed. “But yeah, this has been fun. You’ve got the bones of a beautiful house here.”</p>

<p>“Thank you.” Lorelei grinned happily at the compliment.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit and I had spent nearly two weeks cleaning out and gutting the house. We had filled nearly ten dumpsters by the time we were done, stripping much of the house to the frame and floorboards. She had leveled up her Skill twice, becoming a veritable hurricane of cleanliness.</p>

<p>She had also learned to condense her form, appearing almost completely Human when she wasn’t floating about. Only the wispy end of her clothing gave away the fact that her body was composed of mana.</p>

<p>Her personality had bloomed as well. I didn’t know what kind of person Lorelei Ganlts had been, but the spirit that carried on her name and memories was a bright, bubbly woman. She was a constant well of enthusiasm, always happy to lend a hand to fix up our home.</p>

<p>When I wasn’t working or gutting the manor, I was Bottling Mana. We had yet to map out the full property, but Lorelei had found areas rich with Earth, Fire, and Darkness mana. Bottling different elements, and capturing more thaums, had become easier as my Proficiency leveled. Randy had eagerly bought them all.</p>

<p>Once we had cleared out the worst of the garbage, and I was sure that I had enough money, I had reached out to Frank. Like me, he had heard the stories about Rotwood Manor, and he was fascinated by the chance to finally work on the legendary haunted house.</p>

<p>There hadn’t been any further odd occurrences since the incident with the slamming door. Several times, though, I swore I had heard whispered snippets of conversations, echoes of the house’s past somehow preserved in its mana.</p>

<p>It was spooky, but unlike Lorelei, I didn’t believe in ghosts.</p>

<p>With Frank’s help, we replaced the old electrical service panel and then double-checked the house’s wiring. There had been a handful of short circuits, and some standard DIY rewiring that had to be fixed, but the house was in startling decent shape.</p>

<p>“I figured someone would have snuck in to steal the copper years ago,” he had said.</p>

<p>Confirming everything looked good, we cleaned up and returned to the foyer. There weren’t many lights on, most of the old fixtures had been smashed or broken. I had strung utility lights here and there, which glowed cheerfully as we surveyed the results of our handy work.</p>

<p>“I’ll probably need to come out two or three more times before you wrap up, but I think you’re in a pretty good spot,” Frank concluded, looking mighty proud of himself. “When Merle comes out, tell that old goat who helped you out! I’m sure he’ll get a kick out of it!”</p>

<p>I laughed as I shook his hand. “Don’t worry, I promise.”</p>

<p>Now that the power was back on, I invited Frank to have a beer, but it was late, and he wanted to get home. Escorting him out of the house, Lorelei and I waved goodbye as he hopped into his van and took off, his taillights vanishing down the driveway.</p>

<p>“One job down, dozens to go.” I laughed, closing and locking the door.</p>

<p>We returned to the main hall. My cot remained next to the fireplace, but the room had grown crowded as I had piled up salvage from around the house. Doors, wainscoting, wood paneling, and bits of cabinetry sat in neat piles. I had even swept up the broken bits of the chandelier, wondering if we could use magic to eventually fix it.</p>

<p>There wasn’t nearly enough salvage to restore the house, but I wanted to save everything I could.</p>

<p>There were tools and supplies here and there as well. An ancient chalkboard, salvaged from one of the old supply rooms, leaned next to the hearth. Lorelei and I used it to keep track of all the things we needed to fix or replace.</p>

<p>It was a very, very long list.</p>

<p>To the side of the board, I had written my own, personal to-do list:</p>

<p><b>1- Find More Ways to Make Money</b><br><b>2- Keep Up With Mortgage Payments!</b><br><b>3- Level Up!</b></p>

<p>I already had plans for the first item.</p>

<p>From talking to Randy, I learned that Crafters were always looking for two things: ingredients and areas with a high density of mana.</p>

<p>Crafters received passive bonuses from working in areas with a high density of mana. Normally they flocked to workshops near Dungeons, leeching off the mana that leaked out of them. Ashling State rented out space to local Crafters, leasing unused classroom buildings during the summer.</p>

<p>But I imagined Rotwood Manor would make them look like pauper accommodations with the sheer density of mana present. Randy had pestered me a few times about visiting to gauge out my new home, but I’d pushed him off, stating that I wasn’t ready for guests.</p>

<p>He was a nice guy, but I knew he was working with a team of other Crafters to try and unlock the Alchemist Class. If I invited him to work on my property, I’d have a hundred Crafters clamoring all over my Lair.</p>

<p>Shedding my dirty clothes, I braced myself for another cold shower.</p>

<p>As I quickly washed off, I decided I wanted to invite a small, elite team of Crafters, the best of the best. People I could trust to keep word of Rotwood Manor’s bounty to themselves.</p>

<p>I still had a few more renovation jobs on the docket, but I wasn’t particularly eager for them. I was tired of working for other folks, doing small-time jobs. Working as a handyman had been a means to an end, not the end itself.</p>

<p>Dressing, I returned to the main hall. Lorelei was on her tablet, totally absorbed in the movie she was watching. I could almost feel her amusement as the hero fought against some kind of giant, mutant insect.</p>

<p>The bond between us was still growing. She had started joining me when I went out for contractor jobs, acting as my assistant. As my familiar, she had inherited lesser versions of all my Proficiencies. With her help, I was able to complete jobs at nearly twice the speed.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit Skill was leveling slowly. Each time it did, it reduced the strain on my mana when we were away from Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>After the first few days, I started taking Lorelei on dates around my hometown. She couldn’t eat or drink, so restaurants and cafes were out. But there were plenty of other places to show the Hearth Spirit.</p>

<p>We visited various spots around Ashling Grove, like the central library and the hall used by The Order of Stone. We’d stop and check out the smaller landmarks as well.</p>

<p>Lorelei remembered some of the events the weather-worn monuments were dedicated to. There were a surprising number of statues and plaques around town. Most were tied to historic disasters, like the fire that had taken out the old garment factory or the landslide that had destroyed the town’s first church.</p>

<p>“Ashling Grove has had more than its fair share of tragedy,” Lorelei had noted wryly.</p>

<p>We had even made a quick stop at my parent’s house so I could introduce them to Lorelei. They had found her <em>odd but charming</em> according to my mother. We had stayed for dinner, enjoying a surprisingly nice evening with my family.</p>

<p>Lorelei’s movie reached some kind of dramatic finale. She giggled and laughed as the hero burned the monstrous bugs’ nest. The way the light shone through her semi-translucent form was hypnotizing.</p>

<p>Shortly after moving in, I borrowed a mobile hotspot from the local library. I had introduced her to the Internet, gifting her an old tablet of mine. The Internet connection wasn’t great, but the Hearth Spirit was entranced by the modern age.</p>

<p>When we weren’t busy, she'd watch tutorial videos on YouTouch, learning about home renovation and maintenance Skills. There were also plenty of System-specific tutorials online. Lorelei was a quick study, determined to put her borrowed knowledge to good use.</p>

<p>She’d also watched a slew of movies. After she had gotten used to <em>the talkies</em>, she found herself falling in love with the crappiest, cheesiest movies imaginable.</p>

<p>“William Shatner is a national treasure,” she said as the end credits rolled.</p>

<p>Laughing, I threw my dirty clothes in the laundry bag. The power had been restored, but it wasn’t like I had a washing machine or any appliances yet. There was a fridge from my folk’s place I planned on bringing over, but I’d still need a stove, microwave, and a ton of other things to begin outfitting the house.</p>

<p>At least I no longer needed to go back to my folk’s place every few days to recharge the power block we’d been using for Lorelei’s tablet and my phone.</p>

<p>“Who won, the giant bugs or William Shatner?” I asked as I watched the Hearth Spirit plug in her device to recharge.</p>

<p>“William Shatner, of course,” she answered. Lorelei paused. “It’s so weird. I have all this knowledge, but it’s all mixed up and without context.”</p>

<p>She tapped the surface of the tablet. “Like, I know what this is, I know how to use it, but I don’t have any clue how we got from radio, to TV, and now the Internet. It’s like I’m half-remembering a textbook I read years ago, or I skimmed through a Wikiplace entry and only picked up one word in ten.”</p>

<p>“The fact that you’re referencing Wikiplace is pretty insane.” I laughed.</p>

<p>She watched as I turned out the lights and then crawled into the tent. Lorelei’s form began to fade.</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit didn’t sleep, at least not like Humans did. She claimed she just dissolved back into the house’s mana, becoming one with it. While in communion with Rotwood Manor, she saw snippets of its past, memories trapped in time.</p>

<p>It made me think of the strange incident with the empty room and the slamming door. I hadn’t had any further nightmares since then, but the odd whispers continued. I had told Lorelei I didn’t believe in ghosts, but whatever was happening in my home certainly seemed supernatural.</p>

<p>“Good night, Brent,” Lorelei whispered as she turned into mist.</p>

<p>I closed my eyes, but my mind kept roving over the list of things we needed to do. Bottling Mana was all well and good, but I knew that was just the beginning. Maybe we could start gathering Herbs as well?</p>

<p>Researching online, I hadn’t learned anything new about being a Hearthomancer. But I was sure that if I leveled up my Class, I’d discover all new ways of improving myself, Lorelei, and Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>And the easiest way to level up was to hit a Dungeon.</p>
