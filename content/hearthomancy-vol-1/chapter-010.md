<p>It was a sunny, summer morning as we packed up the truck and drove toward Ashling State. Leaving the property, I double-checked with Lorelei to make sure she was ready.</p>

<p>“Trust me, it’ll be fine!” The Hearth Spirit held up her hand, cocking it like a gun. “I can’t wait to show you how all that target practice has paid off!”</p>

<p>We had been lucky and got into a Saturday delving event after only two weeks. Normally people lined up for months, but there had been an unexpected cancellation. I had jumped at the chance when they had called the night before, asking if I could make it.</p>

<p>The month had been stressful. Between my day job and working on the manor, I had been running myself ragged. Bottling Mana had leveled up again, reaching level three. Every penny I earned either went into the fund I’d set up for mortgage payments or into paying for the Dungeon trip.</p>

<p>Lorelei and I had done a ton of little handyman jobs too; rehanging doors, fixing leaky roofs, and replacing broken drains. I had a small base of loyal customers who called me out as soon as something broke. Most were older folks, happy to pass my recommendation by word of mouth.</p>

<p>I might not have loved my job, but I did it well. We ran around all over Ashling Grove, picking up small paychecks here and there.</p>

<p>But I was sure the Dungeon delve would be worth it. Leveling up would not only help me defend my home, but it could even unlock new ways of making money.</p>

<p>There hadn’t been any further intruders on the property. As I had hoped, mapping the property line had increased Lorelei’s awareness. When door-to-door salesmen or the postman came, she could feel them crossing over the boundary.</p>

<p>Still, I’d seen the drunk around town. He seemed to loiter around the high school or around the edge of Ashling State’s campus, hanging out with kids too dumb to avoid his company.</p>

<p>I’d also spotted Mitch a couple of times. He still creeped me out, but I didn’t know why. I avoided him, just politely waving from the distance before beating a strategic retreat.</p>

<p>Maybe once I was rich, I could put a fence and gate around my property. Preferably one covered in spikes and topped by magical turrets or flaming skulls or something.</p>

<p>Shaking off these thoughts, I tried to focus on the day’s coming events.</p>

<p>Thankfully, there wasn’t a ton of traffic or pedestrians on a Saturday morning. We rolled through Ashling Grove. The town was quiet; the coffee shops and brunch places were busy, but most other businesses hadn’t even opened for the day.</p>

<p>Ashling Grove was the quintessential sleepy college town. Plopped down in the middle of nowhere, it still managed to sport a handful of bars, restaurants, and more than five stoplights. Settled in a deep valley, the hills around the central drag were lined with residential streets and an eclectic mix of houses.</p>

<p>Ashling State was located on almost the exact opposite side of town from Rotwood Manor, which made me wonder why DIK had chosen it for their frat house. There were a few off-campus frats here and there, but none of them matched the legendary debauchery of the boys of Delta Iota Kappa.</p>

<p>Even over fifteen years after their expulsion, I still occasionally heard new stories about their wild days.</p>

<p>Heading up onto campus, we passed row upon row of new buildings, all constructed in the last few years. After the discovery of the Dungeon, the school had exploded in size. It offered courses not only in adventuring but crafting as well.</p>

<p>Lorelei pressed her face against the passenger-side window. “Wow, this place is huge! Is there really a Dungeon here?”</p>

<p>I nodded. “It opened under the old bursar’s office. It’s relatively small, only a few floors, but it’s a good way to get an introduction to adventuring. The college has a couple of courses with pro-adventurers as professors. They take their students down to show them the ropes and give them a jump start on leveling.”</p>

<p>The college opened the Dungeon to the public on weekends—for a fee of course. It was expensive, but thankfully I didn’t need to pay for two tickets. Lorelei was my familiar, so she got in on mine.</p>

<p>Driving through the campus, I parked in a lot not far from the central commons. There were a few students about, but I guessed most of them were still asleep, nursing hangovers from the night before. Glass and steel buildings butted up against older structures constructed from lumber and stone. Trees lined the campus’s wide boulevards, giving everything a peaceful, collegiate atmosphere.</p>

<p>Lorelei got a few curious looks as we headed into a nearby classroom building. Most of the people heading inside seemed to be like us, low-level individuals looking to get a leg up.</p>

<p>I noticed many of them were carrying weapons: swords, maces, and short spears. Their armor was mostly black, tactical gear, the kind of cheap, second-hand stuff you saw online or purchased from an Army-Navy store.</p>

<p>Lorelei gazed at me, noting I was only carrying my wand and backpack. “Brent?”</p>

<p>“Don’t worry,” I told her. “Part of the course cost includes armor rental. It’s not the best stuff, but I’ve read it’ll provide decent protection against the Monsters on the first few floors of the Dungeon.”</p>

<p>In truth, the rental had cost extra, but it was cheaper than just buying gear. I felt a bit guilty because that money could have gone toward repairing the house, but I knew leveling up would ultimately serve us, and Rotwood Manor, better.</p>

<p>I was eager to see what my Class could offer.</p>

<p>Heading into the building, I was surprised to see a few people had set up booths and tables. Most seemed to be advertising local adventuring guilds.</p>

<p>“The Mountain Lion Guild was rated the top local guide in 2022!” a woman shouted next to her booth. “Benefits include in-guild Crafters and discounts on Dungeon delves!”</p>

<p>“Think of them like local, intramural sports clubs,” I whispered to Lorelei, who gazed at the woman with interest. “Rather than joining a gym or something, people exercise and level up by going on delves or hunting down rogue Monsters.”</p>

<p>“Monsters?” she gasped.</p>

<p>“Like that creature we saw in the back of that hunter’s truck the day we met,” I answered.</p>

<p>Monsters were a new phenomenon of The Reckoning. They were like Lorelei, beings of pure mana, usually generated by Dungeons. Unlike her, though, they weren’t sentient, thinking beings, but rather creatures of raw, mindless aggression.</p>

<p>“Monsters generally don’t leave their Dungeons, but sometimes they can be born in the wild, popping up in places with high mana density,” I explained.</p>

<p>“Like our home?” Lorelei asked worriedly.</p>

<p>“Maybe in the past,” I admitted, “but I doubt that’ll happen with you and me around.”</p>

<p>Before buying the property, I had checked the history of Rotwood Manor. There were hundreds of local ghost stories and more than its fair share of deaths on the property, but nothing about wild Monsters popping up. Generally, they only appeared in the hills and mountains around town, created by mana leaking from the college’s Dungeon.</p>

<p>Following the signs posted on the wall, we entered a large classroom. Other would-be adventurers filed in, till the space was packed. Lorelei and I grabbed a spot by the back wall.</p>

<p>A man wearing a blue, collared shirt and slacks entered the room. “Good morning, everyone. My name is Stan, I’m an employee here at the college. I know you’re all eager to get started, so I’ll keep this brief.”</p>

<p>He began reading from a tablet, doing a brief roll call before going through all the rules and a bunch of legalese. I had read everything before, so I kind of just let my attention drift.</p>

<p>There were twenty-three people signed up. The college would break us up into teams of four or five with a guide, a high-level adventurer, to escort us. It’d kind of be like a safari, we’d all head into the Dungeon and the guide would essentially mark targets for us. As long as we got a chance to deal some damage to the Monster, we’d get credit when the thing was killed.</p>

<p>If we kept a move on, and didn’t run into too many issues, we’d at least be able to clear the first floor.</p>

<p>“The Dungeon is five floors in total,” the man said dully, still reading from his screen. “The Monsters start around level one or two but can reach level ten or more on the lower floors. We <em>strongly</em> recommend you don’t go too deep past your level. As you agreed in your waivers, the college is not—”</p>

<p>I scanned the room. Most of the adventurers appeared to be Warriors, but I spotted a woman who was likely a Jack based on the bracer of throwing knives she carried. There were a couple of Wizards as well, all armed with staves or wands. A few even had familiars, but they were little more than motes of light or small creatures like mice or birds.</p>

<p>What really caught my eye, though, was the lone Drow in attendance.</p>

<p>I’d never actually got a good look at a dark elf up close and in person. Her skin was a dusky shade of purple, which was perfectly matched by her pearl-white hair. Long, pointy ears offset her sharp, strict-looking features; she was not a delicate-looking woman, but certainly striking.</p>

<p>I was also impressed by the heavy armor she was wearing. Unlike everyone else, she was wearing full plate mail, complete with breastplate, gorget, gauntlets, and greaves. An open-faced helmet, with slots for her ears, was tucked under her arm. A heavy shield and mace were resting by her feet.</p>

<p>“See something you like?” Lorelei teased, whispering into my ear.</p>

<p>I snapped to attention. “What?”</p>

<p>“Nothing.” She giggled quietly. “Don’t worry, I won’t get jealous.”</p>

<p>Before I could say anything further, the meeting ended. I was surprised when the Drow woman stood.</p>

<p>I always assumed the dark elves were the small, delicate type. The armored woman nearly had to duck her head as she strode out of the room. I put her well over six feet tall, with muscles galore based on how easily she moved in her heavy equipment.</p>

<p>“Damn, I hope she’s in my group,” I muttered to myself.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>It seems my wish was granted. Following the directions given by the man in the collared shirt, I was ushered into another, smaller classroom. The armored Drow woman, along with the Jack and two men dressed like Warriors, were crowded inside.</p>

<p>“What the heck is that?” the Jack asked, staring at Lorelei.</p>

<p>“I’m not a <em>that</em>,” the Hearth Spirit replied peevishly.</p>

<p>“She’s my familiar,” I answered, glaring at the woman. “And she’s right, she’s not a <em>that</em>.”</p>

<p>The Drow snorted, while the Jack held up her hands.</p>

<p>“Sorry, sorry!” she replied sheepishly. “I’m just kind of new at this! I’ve never seen a familiar before.”</p>

<p>“Great,” one of the Warriors, a heavyset Asian man grumbled. Based on his gear, he looked like a somewhat seasoned adventurer. “I was really hoping to clear the second floor today, but instead—”</p>

<p>Ignoring his grumbling, I noticed that the other Warrior was clearly a rookie. His gear was brand new, likely just off the shelf. He pointed toward the Jack and was about to make some kind of joke when the door slammed open.</p>

<p>“Stow the chatter.”</p>

<p>A middle-aged man wearing battered leather armor strode into the classroom. He was armed with a broadsword and a pistol was holstered by his waist.</p>

<p>“My name is Victor, I am a level thirty-six Warrior,” he said without preamble. “I’m your guide for today. You will follow me, you will do as I say, or so help me God I will let the Monsters eat you. I don’t give a shit about the waivers you signed, if you die, I won’t feel an ounce of pity.”</p>

<p>Lorelei grinned. “Oh, I like him!”</p>

<p>He turned a steady eye toward us. Victor had a forceful presence; he was the highest-level person I’d ever met face to face. Still, I refused to be intimidated. My skin prickled as he used some kind of Skill.</p>

<p>“Blacksmith, Jack, Warrior, Warrior—and a Wizard,” he said, his eyes resting on me and Lorelei. “Good, nobody was dumb enough to sign up with fake information.”</p>

<p>As part of signing up, I had been forced to reveal that I was a Hearthomancer. The college didn’t want people signing up and incorrectly listing their Classes. As part of the sign-up, though, both sides had signed NDAs, guaranteeing my privacy. I specifically asked to keep my exact Class a secret from any group I was a part of.</p>

<p>Someone from the school’s Wizard division had reached out, asking if they could interview me about my Class unlock and how I had obtained Hearthomancer. I had politely turned them down.</p>

<p>“Right,” Victor called, “follow me! We’ll pick up any gear you’ve rented and then head into the Dungeon.”</p>

<p>Victor led us to a neighboring room, which had been converted into an armory. An attendant quickly took my measurements and then disappeared into the racks to fetch a chestguard and gloves. The two Warriors, who were friends I assumed, grumbled as they waited, but the Drow woman carefully inspected the gear hanging on the racks.</p>

<p>“It’s all well made.” She spoke with a mild, elegant accent. I couldn’t quite place it.</p>

<p>Victor nodded. “I’m glad to hear it. The school doesn’t go cheap on the gear, even the rental stuff. Everything here was made by students enrolled in the Crafter courses.”</p>

<p>He slapped a hand against his chest. “My gear was made by a graduate, an alumnus working in hopes of unlocking Leatherworker.”</p>

<p>The Drow woman inspected his gear, then nodded. “They might be close to unlocking their next Class.”</p>

<p>Victor grinned at the news. There were more Crafters out there than just Randy and his friends looking to unlock advanced Classes.</p>

<p>The attendant asked, but Lorelei couldn’t use any of their gear. I’d researched familiars after summoning the Hearth Spirit, and it seemed likely that she’d eventually earn a Skill that would allow her to materialize her own arms and armor. Until then, I had to help keep her safe.</p>

<p>The delve came with a Healing Potion. It was Low Grade, probably the result of some students' coursework, but I wouldn’t turn down free healing.</p>

<p><b>Healing Potion (Low Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A simple Healing Potion, capable of healing small injuries and wounds.</em></p>

<p>“Don’t drink more than one of those,” Victor warned us. “Just like any medicine, Healing Potions can be dangerous if overused. Drinking even two in a single day can poison you.”</p>

<p>I stuck the potion in my belt. Double-checking, I confirmed I had my grimoire, food, and water stowed in my bag along with a small first-aid kit.</p>

<p>The delve was only supposed to last a few hours, but the last thing I wanted was to get stuck somewhere without anything to eat or drink. And my mom would never forgive me if I didn’t pack bandages and antiseptic.</p>

<p>Armed and ready, Victor directed us to the building next door. It was a small stone structure, a humble reminder of the college’s old days, before it had blown up and devoured most of the local real estate. There was a brief queue, as the various groups from that day headed in, but eventually, our turn came up.</p>

<p>“Raise your hand if you’ve done a Quest before,” Victor asked.</p>

<p>The Drow and the one Warrior, the veteran, raised their hands.</p>

<p>Victor turned to the three of us. “When you enter the Dungeon, you’ll get your first Quest, to reach the end of the first floor. Essentially, the System will integrate a bit of new data into your brain, so you might feel dizzy or sick. Do not, under any circumstances, puke on my boots.”</p>

<p>The one Warrior chuckled at his friend while the Jack looked worried. Shaking my head, I followed Victor.</p>

<p>Rather than going through the main entrance, we went through an external basement door, marching down single-file. Below was a low-ceilinged space. The Drow had to stoop to avoid hitting her head.</p>

<p>The basement had likely originally been used for storage. But with the advent of the System, another reality had breached the building’s foundation.</p>

<p>The Dungeon entrance was shaped like a crude, stone gate half-melded with the building’s walls. Incomprehensible runes were carved into the entrance. Using Mana Perception, I could see mystical energy flowing in and out of the gate.</p>

<p>It swirled around like a riptide, pulling us toward the Dungeon.</p>

<p>“Blacksmith in the front with me,” Victor instructed. “You two Warriors, watch our flanks. Jack and Wizard, stick to the middle. Don’t get separated, don’t get lost.”</p>

<p>Without further preamble, he led us through the gate.</p>

<p>I felt a sickening twist as I stepped over the threshold. I was forced to shut down Mana Perception as the world warped, the mana frothing like white-water rapids.</p>

<p>Stumbling into the Dungeon, a sharp pain speared me between my eyes. Lorelei nearly dissolved as she struggled to hold her form together.</p>

<p><b>You Have Received a New Quest! – Clear the First Floor of the Dungeon!</b><br><b>Rewards: Class Progress</b></p>

<p>“What the fuck?” the rookie Warrior mumbled.</p>

<p>The Jack leaned against the wall, doing her best to keep from vomiting. Victor, the veteran Warrior, and the Drow stood aside. The veteran was sniggering at his friend, but the Blacksmith occupied herself with putting her helmet on.</p>

<p>“Brent?” Lorelei put her hand on my shoulder. Her touch felt cold but comforting.</p>

<p>“I’m okay,” I said as the pain and disorientation faded. “How about you?”</p>

<p>She nodded. “I’m okay. I felt our bond—falter there for a moment, as if it had been overwhelmed by the Dungeon's mana, but then it stabilized.”</p>

<p>Looking at our stats, I was surprised to see the Hearth Spirit Skill had leveled up again, reaching level five. Closing my eyes, I could feel the bond between me and Lorelei thrumming. More distantly, I could even sense Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>“Crazy.” I grinned. <em>If we could level up Skills and grow even stronger by just stepping foot into a Dungeon, what could we do when we got to Monster slaying?</em></p>

<p>“Let’s go,” Victor said, his words echoing my sentiments.</p>

<p>Following him, we ventured into the Dungeon.</p>
