<p>“Wow, this place is huge!” Lorelei craned her neck to stare up at the library.</p>

<p>At ten stories, it was one of the tallest buildings on campus. Built using steel and glass, it stood out starkly against the older, river stone buildings that sat by its feet.</p>

<p>“Supposedly some rich steel magnet donated all the money for its construction after his kid went to school here in the 1970s,” I explained. “You can find a plaque with his name out front.”</p>

<p>Ishikana deftly jumped out of the truck and landed beside us. A few people in the parking lot paused, staring at the Arachne, but she ignored them.</p>

<p>“Please, Mr. Owens, lead the way,” she said.</p>

<p>I began walking toward the entrance. “Like I said, just call me Brent.”</p>

<p>“As you say,” she agreed.</p>

<p>“I don’t remember you attending Ashling State, do I?” Lorelei asked me.</p>

<p>I shook my head. “Nah, I didn’t have an interest in going to some fancy school, I already had my dream by then. I went to a community college two towns over and got a two-year degree.”</p>

<p>I’d always treated Ashling State like some fancy castle up on the hill. The antics, the parties, and the stories of collegiate hijinks never interested me. They weren’t magic, not like the System was offering.</p>

<p>Reaching the front of the building, I held the glass doors open as Ishikana squeezed inside. We got a few more concerned looks from the early morning bookworms, but for the most part, the sleek, retro-modern lobby was empty.</p>

<p>“So cool,” Lorelei whispered as she gazed about.</p>

<p>Most of the tower was hollow. The upper stories were arranged in overlapping balconies and terraces. They were loaded with bookshelves, reading lounges, and study rooms, all laid out like the 1970s idea of the future.</p>

<p>The air smelled like paper and coffee, and the murmur of hushed voices was joined by the rustle of pages being turned.</p>

<p>“I always assumed your Human libraries were filled with nothing but computers,” the Arache said in a hushed tone.</p>

<p>Shrugging, I spotted the reception desk and started walking that way. “Colleges tend to pride themselves on their book collections; it’s a mark of prestige. Besides, there are still plenty of people here on their laptops, tablets, and phones, taking advantage of the comfortable seats and free Wi-Fi.”</p>

<p>A woman nearby looked up from her tablet and shushed me. I resisted the urge to flip her off.</p>

<p>The people at the reception desk were already panicking with Ishikana’s approach. I realized she hadn’t been kidding when she’d said she brought down the hammer via her contacts in the Department of Education. I tried to contact the college a few times, researching Rotwood Manor, but they’d always stonewalled me before.</p>

<p><em>Maybe having her around wouldn’t be so bad after all?</em></p>

<p>A middle-aged woman ran around the desk to stand before us. “Good morning, Lady Ishikana!”</p>

<p>The Arachne’s tone was frosty. “Are you the head librarian?”</p>

<p>“Yes!” The woman was already sweating, her entire figure radiating fear and repulsion.</p>

<p>“I have research to be done, you’ll be assisting me.” Ishikana pointed to me. “This is Brent Owens. He is my valuable partner in this project. Treat him and his familiar as you would me.”</p>

<p>“Of course, just let us know what you need!” the head librarian said.</p>

<p>The others behind the desk rapidly nodded in agreement.</p>

<p>“We’ll need a study room, the biggest one you have,” the Scholar demanded.</p>

<p>“Please, follow me!” The head librarian scurried between two nearby shelves. The other staff members hurried after her.</p>

<p>“What the hell do you have over them?” I asked Ishikana.</p>

<p>She gave a small grin. “Nothing much. I just promised the Department of Education a few tricks in advancing the Mentor Class, maybe a tip on how to unlock the Teacher Evolution. They were more than happy to put a little bit of weight on the college. Maybe they threatened Ashling State’s scholar money?”</p>

<p>“Knowledge is power,” Lorelei said solemnly.</p>

<p>The Scholar nodded. “Exactly. Some fools think the world is ruled by raw strength, but access and knowledge are still the ultimate tools of authority.”</p>

<p>The head librarian was forced to kick a few unhappy students out of the study room. Ishikana quickly took over the space, spinning a web and suspending herself from the ceiling at the head of the table.</p>

<p>I realized the outfit covering her sternum and abdomen was useful for more than just protecting her modesty. It held countless pockets; she took out a laptop, several notebooks, and an army of pens and highlighters.</p>

<p>“Water,” she ordered one of the junior librarians. “Cold, no ice.”</p>

<p>He frowned. “I’m not—”</p>

<p>“Go,” the head librarian growled.</p>

<p>The Arachne quickly set out another set of orders, demanding all the documents the college had on Rotwood Manor, the Ganlts family, and everything it had on the town’s history.</p>

<p>The head librarian explained that none of the older material was digitized, by order of the town elders.</p>

<p>“Town elders?” she asked.</p>

<p>“Yeah, you know, like the mayor's office, the town council, the historical society,” I replied.</p>

<p>“Don’t forget The Order of Stone!” Lorelei contributed.</p>

<p>Ishikana looked at us blankly. “The Order of Stone?”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit nodded. “Sure. They’re kind of like the Knights of Columbus or the Masons, just—creepier?”</p>

<p>I’d only encountered the cowled and cloaked members of The Order once or twice in my life. I shivered at the memory.</p>

<p>“Whatever,” the Scholar sighed, waving for the head librarian to fetch the materials.</p>

<p>Soon the room was stacked with boxes and carts full of old books. Ishikana grabbed the oldest and began reading.</p>

<p>“We’re assuming the Water of Styx is from the same time period as the newspaper it was entombed with?” I asked.</p>

<p>Ishikana nodded, tapping the side of her head. “Critical Conjecture is a Scholar Skill that allows me to sift through a huge pile of incomplete, even contrasting data, to come to a potential conclusion. It’s not always accurate, but even if it’s wrong it gives me a direction toward the truth. We’ll likely have to spend several days sorting through the data, but I’m confident we’ll walk away with <em>something</em> useful.”</p>

<p>I’d never been much for studying, but I was excited for the work ahead. The mysteries around my home were overwhelming, filling me with dark curiosity. Who was Eustace Phelps, and what kind of dark rituals had taken place on my land? Who, or what were those phantoms we kept seeing?</p>

<p>I wanted to know the truth.</p>

<p>Lorelei, infected by my excitement, gave another cheer as we delved into our research.</p>

<p>“Please be careful,” the head librarian reminded us, as she handed us special gloves to wear. “These books are some of the oldest in our collection. They’ve never been digitized, so these are the only copies.”</p>

<p>Ishikana skimmed a finger along the cover of the book she was reading. “You must have local history experts too, correct? Professors that studied the lore of this town?”</p>

<p>“Maybe?” the head librarian answered evasively.</p>

<p>Ishikana flicked her fingers toward the door. “Find them for me.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>We quickly fell into a rhythm. We’d scan through books for anything relevant to our research, putting aside the ones that needed further study and stacking the rest to be reshelved. I didn’t have any specific Skills or Proficiencies that made the task easier, like Ishikana’s Unbreakable Focus, but Lorelei and I still managed to tear through our fair share of books, journals, and documents.</p>

<p>Occasionally, I caught Lorelei staring at a particular picture or article, her expression distant. Likely, she was reliving old memories, the phantom thoughts and feelings she had inherited from Lorelei Ganlts.</p>

<p>“Here.” She carefully held up an old photo, the ink faded to faint shades of sepia brown.</p>

<p>It showed a middle-aged man, richly dressed. He was grinning as he held a shovel, point down. All around him, fallen trees and hewn stumps.</p>

<p>“Patrick Ganlts,” the Hearth Spirit said.</p>

<p>We all gathered around the image. There wasn’t anything written on the photo, but the accompanying documents indicated it was from 1903 or 1904, years before he’d built Rotwood Manor.</p>

<p>“He wasn’t a local, he moved to Ashling Grove just after the turn of the century. Over the years he slowly squeezed out his local rivals, sometimes using underhanded tricks and violence to get what he wanted,” Lorelei told us, her voice empty, cold. “Under his watch, Ashling Grove went from little more than a rugged outpost into a true town. Shops and businesses sprung up along its streets, and workers came to fill his lumber mills, charcoal burners, mines, and workshops.”</p>

<p>“I don’t remember reading about any mines,” I said.</p>

<p>Lorelei nodded. “He struck a few of them, but they never produced much, just a little bit of coal or iron. There were actually a few on what would eventually become our property, but they never panned out.”</p>

<p>“Do you remember if there was anything there before he sank his mines?” I asked.</p>

<p>Lorelei furrowed her brow. “Maybe? There had been something there, but by the time Patrick Ganlts purchased the land it was reduced to ruins, overgrown and forgotten.”</p>

<p>She shook her head. “I just remember the rumors at the time. The land was haunted, cursed, but Patrick didn’t care. He was making money hand over fist. Even if the mines didn’t pan out, he’d show everyone just how damn—”</p>

<p>She trailed off. “Sorry, mind if I take a minute?”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit vanished, fading through the wall before I could stop her. Through our shared link I felt a deep, misplaced melancholy, a sadness not entirely her own.</p>

<p>“I’m going after her,” I declared, putting down the book I had been studying.</p>

<p>Heading back out to the library lobby, I endured a few more dirty looks from irritated librarians and college kids. I guessed our visit was causing a bigger commotion than anticipated. Several students got up and left, grumbling darkly.</p>

<p>Trusting the bond between us, I followed Lorelei, trailing her outside.</p>

<p>School had already started up, so the campus was crowded. There was a buzz of excitement in the air, the town festival was only a few weeks away.</p>

<p>Walking across the campus a short distance, I found Lorelei in a park between two of the modern classroom buildings. A few students were drifting back and forth, but they were giving the ghastly woman on the park bench a wide berth.</p>

<p>She wasn’t crying, which was a good sign. Taking a seat next to her, I just took her hand. It felt cold and only slightly more substantial than morning mist.</p>

<p>“Sorry,” she finally uttered.</p>

<p>“There’s nothing to feel sorry for,” I told her firmly.</p>

<p>She shook her head, her ebon locks wicking away into motes of darkness. “I know these memories aren’t mine, but—Patrick Ganlts—my father—her father, loved her so much! He did all these terrible things and ruined people's lives, but he just <em>doted</em> on her. I can remember how happy she was on her sixteenth birthday or the day he bought her a pony, but I can also remember the wretched heartbreak when he suddenly died.”</p>

<p>Her shoulders heaved, but she refused to shed a tear for a life she’d never led.</p>

<p>“I know Lorelei Ganlts died a lonely, miserable woman, hounded by the mistakes her father had made, the misfortune that haunts Rotwood Manor.” Her voice was barely above a whisper. “I keep repeating, over and over, that I’m not her, that I’m just starting my own life. But when we look back through pictures and those stories—”</p>

<p>I pulled her into a hug, holding her against my chest.</p>

<p>“I’m the one who should be sorry, I didn’t even think how all this would affect you,” I said.</p>

<p>She shook her head, her hair brushing against my collar. “No, I shouldn’t have let it affect me like this, it’s silly.”</p>

<p>“There’s nothing silly about it,” I put my hands on her shoulders and raised her till we were back to eye level. “These are still your memories. You said it was impossible to distinguish between you and the manor, that you were one in the same, right? Well, it’s never comfortable digging into your own past, acknowledging the pain and heartbreak you endured.”</p>

<p>“Brent!” She wrapped her arms around me.</p>

<p>Then the tears came. Holding her tightly, I comforted her till she could weep no more.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Where’s your familiar?”</p>

<p>“Lorelei went home,” I answered as I stepped back inside the study room and closed the door.</p>

<p>Once she felt settled, the Hearth Spirit had kissed me goodbye before fading away, allowing the link to Rotwood Manor to pull her back home.</p>

<p>“A shame, but understandable,” the Arachne answered as she turned the page of some massive, leather-bound book. “A familiar is intimately bound not just to their master, but to their Lair as well. And even only scratching the surface of Rotwood Manor’s past, it is a story filled with tragedy.”</p>

<p>She pointed to a small pile of books she’d saved. “Each and every one of them records some death or disaster that took place on your property. The mine Lorelei mentioned? It collapsed, killing five, in April of 1902. There were several logging accidents, with multiple fatalities over the following years. The deaths continue into the modern era. As far as I can see, the only group <em>not </em>struck by tragedy was the frat that occupied the house. During their stay, there were only two recorded deaths, both from alcohol poisoning.”</p>

<p>“There have been another six related deaths since the frat was evicted, including the last owner of Rotwood Manor,” she continued. “He fell off the roof and broke his neck. The owner before him committed suicide by throwing himself into the gorge.”</p>

<p>I’d been aware that Rotwood Manor had a blood-soaked past, but I was shaken by the sheer numbers Ishikana had so calmly recounted. No way in hell such a body count was normal regardless of the timespan involved.</p>

<p>The Scholar leaned forward; her eyes locked on me. “Brent, I believe you’re at the center of a delightful mystery. I just hope you survive long enough that we can discover the source of this carnage.”</p>
