<p>“To your left!” I shouted.</p>

<p>Ayara brought her mace down, smashing the Shadow Dreg that had been creeping along the wall.</p>

<p>I had been holding back the horde with Guardian’s Stormwall. As soon as Lorelei signaled she was ready, I released the Skill, disorienting the Monsters with a burst of thunder. They fell back into a confused heap.</p>

<p>Lorelei swooped in and ignited them with a burst of flames.</p>

<p><b>Skill Guardian’s Stormwall has reached Lvl 3!</b><br><b>Skill Hearth Flame has reached Lvl 7!</b><br><b>Skill Hearth Spirit has reached Lvl 8!</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiency Hearthomancy has reached Lvl 8!</b></p>

<p><b>Class Hearthomancer has reached Lvl 8!</b></p>

<p>“Good work,” I panted, my head aching from the sudden, massive expenditure of MP.</p>

<p>Ayara, still vigilant, glared up and down the passage before lowering her guard. “I think we cleared this section of the Dungeon. Did you level up again?”</p>

<p>“Level eight, along with a slew of Skill upgrades” I confirmed.</p>

<p>My Hearthomancy Proficiency had been growing by leaps and bounds thanks to my new Skill. Doubling my spell list had given me a lot more tools for grinding progress.</p>

<p>Lorelei struck a pose and flexed her arms. “I’m more powerful than ever!”</p>

<p>“Good, but I think I need a break,” the Drow said. “We should go back and check and see how Ishikana is doing.”</p>

<p>Agreeing, we allowed the Hearth Spirit to guide us through the winding corridors of the Oubliette of the Forgotten. We had spent nearly a week straight grinding the Dungeon, exterminating Shadow Dregs and the more advanced Shadow Remnants.</p>

<p>We hadn’t encountered any more terrifying epiphanies but that wasn’t the point. We just wanted power.</p>

<p>Ishikana had joined us for the first few days, helping us grind faster with her superior levels. Eventually though, I had asked her to see to the house’s defenses directly while Ayara, Lorelei, and I continued to push our levels.</p>

<p>If we were going to defeat Mitch and the DIK, we needed every edge we could get.</p>

<p>Emerging from the door under the basement stairs, I was hit by another notification.</p>

<p><b>Lair Quest Complete – Harden your Lair’s Defenses!</b><br><b>Lair Rotwood Manor has Reached Lvl 4!</b><br><b>New Lair Boons and Banes Are Available!</b></p>

<p>“I’d almost forgotten about that quest,” Ayara admitted as we told her the news. “I wonder why it triggered now though?”</p>

<p>“Maybe from leveling up my Class and Skills?” I answered with a shrug. “Either way, I’m not complaining. There are only two upgrades I’m interested in at the moment, it’s just a matter of picking which one.”</p>

<p>Returning upstairs, I double checked the doorbell camera. Someone had dumped a fresh load of garbage by the gate, but at least they hadn’t shot up my <em>Private Property</em> signs again.</p>

<p>The cops sitting outside didn’t seem to care. Penkle and Decker hadn’t lived up to their threat to get a search warrant, but there was almost always a squad car parked along the street, watching us.</p>

<p>The eclipse was only days away, and the attacks against us had stepped up in intensity. it seemed like the entire town was infected with a vehement anger against us. So far, they hadn’t marched up to Rotwood Manor with torches in hand, but I imagined that's where things were going.</p>

<p>We’d be ready for them.</p>

<p>After cleaning up, Ayara, Lorelei and I walked outside. The woods around the manor looked completely different than before. The trees were covered in spidery silk and webbing, making me think that my home had been transported to some haunted, fantasy realm. Gloom mana lay heavy across the landscape, deepening the shadows and leaving a lingering feeling of dread.</p>

<p>“Perfect,” I muttered.</p>

<p>Ishikana, sensing our presence, leapt out of a nearby tree, landing clear of the tall grass we had allowed to reconquer the lawn.</p>

<p>She inspected us, then smiled. “Congratulations, it appears you’ve leveled up again!”</p>

<p><b>Ability Scores</b><br><b></b><b>Strength: </b>12<br><b>Finesse: </b>14<br><b>Fortitude:</b> 19<br><b>Resilience:</b> 19<br><b>Wisdom: </b>35<br><b>Discipline: </b>39</p>

<p><b>Classes</b><br><b></b><b>Hearthomancer Lvl 8</b><br><b>Laborer Lvl 7</b></p>

<p><b>Skills</b><br><b></b><b>Hearth Spirit Lvl 8</b><br><b>Hearth Flame Lvl 7</b><br><b>Guardian’s Stormwall Lvl 3</b><br><b>Mana Manipulation Lvl 6</b><br><b>Identify Lvl 2</b><br><b>Second Wind Lvl 3</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiencies</b><br><b></b><b>Mana Bottling Lvl 4</b><br><b>Hearthomancy Lvl 8</b><br><b>Carpentry Lvl 6</b><br><b>Construction Lvl 7</b><br><b>Plumbing Lvl 3</b><br><b>Roofing Lvl 4</b><br><b>Electrical Wiring Lvl 3</b><br><b>One-Handed Swords Lvl 1</b></p>

<p>I had finally picked up One-Handed Swords. Taking the Scholar’s advice, I had put some of my free stat points into Finesse. It improved my dexterity and gave me a better chance of landing my blows or parrying strikes.</p>

<p>Ishikana shook her head. “Your progress has been simply astounding, far beyond what you should be capable of on Earth. At this rate, I’m going to have to completely rewrite what we know about the System.”</p>

<p>“And I bet you couldn’t be happier.” Ayara laughed.</p>

<p>I inspected the woods around my house. “It looks like you’ve been busy too.”</p>

<p>The webbing wasn’t just for show. The Scholar had hidden countless traps and snares within her silken strands, and only some of them were of her design.</p>

<p>After watching <em>Home Alone</em>, Lorelei had insisted that we step up our game. Ayara and I had ended up adding a few surprises of our own.</p>

<p>The house had been fortified too. I’d installed a bar across the front and back doors, and I’d put chicken wire in front of the intact windows. Inside, I’d barred every path I could, ensuring if someone got into the house, they couldn’t just make a beeline to the basement or find a hiding spot in the residential or atrium wing.</p>

<p>I wasn’t exactly happy with all the impromptu changes to Rotwood Manor. It was meant to be my sanctum, not a fortress, but there was no way in hell I’d let Mitch or the others just march in and take it from me.</p>

<p>The mana around us became tempestuous. Darkness roiled across the landscape.</p>

<p>Checking my phone, I saw there was a group of people standing in front of my driveway.</p>

<p>Mitch and the other members of DIK weren’t hiding what they were anymore. Each was wearing a black cloak, with long, gleaming knives belted at their waists.</p>

<p>“I knew this was coming,” Ayara growled as I showed everyone the image. “The bastards are going to offer <em>parley</em>.”</p>

<p>“They’re going to get my boot up their asses,” I answered, double checking my sword and armor. “Come on, let’s go see what’s on their minds. If we leave them out there, they’re likely to throw a flaming bag of shit or something.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Ayara had insisted that I wear armor. She didn’t have the time or resources to make a new set for me, but she had been able to resize one of her older breastplates. It still hung a little loose around the collar, but it was far better than even the rental gear I had borrowed from Ashling State.</p>

<p>We were carrying a few Healing Potions as well. Sneaking out past the cops had been tough, but Randy had been more than happy to trade. He had been especially excited when I mentioned that I could get him some more uncommon elements.</p>

<p>The Drow Blacksmith walked at my side, and Lorelei drifted behind us as we advanced up the driveway. Ishikana stuck to the trees and her webs, ready to ambush the neighborhood watch if given a chance.</p>

<p>Mitch stood in the middle of the road. Candy stood by his side, while Herb and Josh had taken up positions to his sides. Both men were carrying banners with the Greek characters for DIK. The frat letters were framed by pentagrams and satanic script.</p>

<p>There were more people behind them, all hooded and cloaked. I didn’t know any of them, but I recognized them from the photo Professor Brown had shown us. They were older, but clearly recognizable as the remaining members of DIK.</p>

<p>Stopping a few meters from the gate, I put my hand on the hilt of my sword.</p>

<p>Mitch, grinning like a madman, stepped forward. “Afternoon, Brent!”</p>

<p>I gestured toward his cloak with my free hand. “You decided to give up the pretenses? I know we’re getting close to Halloween, but I think your costumes are a bit much.”</p>

<p>“Black is great for hiding blood!” Candy chirped.</p>

<p>“Well, we found out about the research you and your—spider friend were doing at Ashling State, so we figured it’d be rude to show up in less than our Sunday finest,” Mitch answered. “She can come out, if she wants.”</p>

<p>Ishikana landed behind us. Rising to her full height, her legs rattled.</p>

<p>“Cultist scum,” she hissed at the crowd.</p>

<p>Using Identify, I was grateful to see my hard work had paid off. I could only read their primary Classes, but Mitch, Josh, and Herb all came back as Cultists, as did most of the crowd. Mitch was the highest level, at sixteen.</p>

<p>Cindy came back as a level fourteen Mind Leech.</p>

<p>Mitch laughed. “We are the true inheritors of the land you stand on, its rightful guardians!”</p>

<p>“Really?” I growled. “See, as far as I can tell, you’re standing over there, and I’m standing over here. And I fucking <em>dare </em>you to step closer.”</p>

<p>Herb went for the knife at his waist, but another Cultist stopped him.</p>

<p>“Now’s not the time!” Mitch snapped at the farmer. He turned back to regard me. “Our Lord’s Holy Day draws close, Brent. You can feel it, can’t you? Baphomet's power is welling up from below, demanding blood and obedience.”</p>

<p>“We haven’t missed a sacrifice yet,” Herb said sharply. “Each kill we’ve offered Our Lord has granted us more power! Soon, we’ll be gods, masters of this earth, this reality!”</p>

<p>“You’re a fucking goat farmer, know your place,” Ayara shot back.</p>

<p>Mitch laughed. “When the moon shrouds the sun, all will be ours! Now is the time for the final sacrifice!”</p>

<p>The mana around me surged. I could almost feel the hunger, the bloody <em>want</em>. Grabbing a hold of the energy with Mana Manipulation I forced it down.</p>

<p>I was the Hearthomancer, I was the master of the Lair, not some godforsaken <em>thing</em> that happened to be sealed on the grounds.</p>

<p>If Mitch saw the struggle for power, he didn’t comment. Instead, he stepped back and took on a more reasonable, friendly tone.</p>

<p>“It’s not too late,” he insisted. “Just walk away. If you flee fast enough, far enough, maybe Our Lord will forget about you. He isn’t demanding your blood on his altar—yet.”</p>

<p>“You can tell Baphomet, or whatever, to kiss my ass,” I retorted, the power of my Lair at my full command again.</p>

<p>Pulling on it, I brought a wave of Death and Gloom mana to the fore, lacing it with motes of Fire. The woods around us grew darker, more menacing. Fear, clotted and thick, rose in the air as bright, illusionary eyes appeared from the shadows.</p>

<p>Several of the Cultists screamed in terror. A handful broke and ran, scrambling toward the perceived safety of Josh and Mitch’s houses.</p>

<p>“Unlike your offer, mine isn’t so generous,” I said, addressing Mitch. “Fuck off or die. If you step one foot on my land, I’ll make sure your bones will spend the rest of eternity rotting in the ground with that stupid goat god of yours.”</p>

<p>Mitch didn’t say another word. He just turned and walked away, heading back toward his house. Cindy stared at me hard. A strange, buzzing sensation reached out toward me, but it couldn’t quite take hold. With a surge of mana, I disrupted the attack.</p>

<p>Giving a final <em>harumph</em>, she turned and followed after her husband.</p>

<p>I slowly eased my hold on the mana around us, and the woods returned to normal.</p>

<p>“Come on,” I said, turning to head back down the driveway. “We know Mitch and the rest of DIK aren’t just going to give up. We still have a few days to prepare, and I know exactly what upgrade I want to choose next for our home.”</p>
