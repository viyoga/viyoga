<p>My ears were ringing. Staggering to my feet, I instinctually pulled on the Fire mana around me. My vision cleared as arcane energy filled my shaking limbs.</p>

<p>The front door of Rotwood Manor was gone, along with a good chunk of the wall. Bits of burning wood, plaster, and brick littered the lawn. Small fires burned here and there.</p>

<p>Groaning, I helped Ayara to her feet. Her armor was singed and melted, but otherwise, she seemed unharmed.</p>

<p>“Brent!”</p>

<p>Ishikana jumped down from the roof. Her front legs had been scorched, but her sky-high stats had likely prevented serious injury.</p>

<p>Looking around, we could only see a sea of wounded, confused, and terrified people. There was no sign of Mitch, Herb, or the other Cultists. Josh’s corpse was already disintegrating, turning to dust. Whatever Mitch had done, he had drained the man dry, using his life force to power his attack.</p>

<p>The eclipse hung overhead, paused in time. Phantoms danced in the shadows, doomed to repeat the same merciless tasks over and over again.</p>

<p>“Fucking cheaters,” I grumbled, looking at the frozen sky.</p>

<p>Lorelei appeared, gripping me in terror. “Brent! Mitch is inside the house; he’s heading into the Oubliette!”</p>

<p>I looked toward Ayara and Ishikana; they both nodded sharply.</p>

<p>There wasn’t any time to deal with the mess across my property. The cops had received the brunt of the damage since Mitch had forced them to lead the assault. I saw Officer Penkle leaning against a tree, puking his guts up.</p>

<p>“Penkle!” I shouted.</p>

<p>He wiped his mouth and looked at me.</p>

<p>“You’re in charge. Get the EMTs, the fire department, whoever you need to take care of these folks, but don’t set foot inside the fucking house!”</p>

<p>I thought for a second he was going to argue, but he wearily nodded instead.</p>

<p>Pausing only to make sure Merle had escaped the blast, I scrambled through the hole blown through my house. I then helped Ayara climb up behind me.</p>

<p>The foyer was covered in burning debris. Mitch’s attack had torn apart the room and collapsed the grand staircase. It broke my heart to see the damage to the manor, but I knew we couldn’t afford to delay.</p>

<p>Bloody sunlight poured in from the remaining windows. Reaching the main hall, I saw the hearth was glowing with black and red flames, the fire dampened to little more than ephemeral coals.</p>

<p>A mob of footprints led right to the basement stairs. Someone had torn the door off its hinges. I dashed down the stairs and to the Dungeon door. A bloody handprint was wrapped around the brass knob.</p>

<p>“Brent, wait!”</p>

<p>Lorelei vanished down the hall toward the boiler room, then came back a moment later with The Water of Styx clutched against her chest.</p>

<p>“We’ll need this.” She spoke with utter conviction.</p>

<p>I tucked the jar into a pouch at my waist, then opened the door. Beyond yawned a long, dark passage. It seemed familiar and utterly alien to me at the same time, my memory and the memories of the manor blurring together.</p>

<p>Phantoms raced ahead of us; black-clad Cultists, peasant worshippers, doomed fools all locked in time. Amongst their number, I saw Eustice Phelps, his face locked in an expression of rage and anguish.</p>

<p>We all twitched as we heard some anguished scream echo from below.</p>

<p>“Guess the Monsters aren’t on the Cultists’ side,” Ayara said faintly.</p>

<p>I tightened my grip on my rapier. “Come on. We have no idea how long Mitch can freeze the eclipse, but I bet it’s just long enough for him to make his sacrifice.”</p>

<p>Lorelei led the way, the rest of us right behind her. We couldn’t spare a word to speak, but I felt so proud of our little group. I had no idea of the true stakes involved, what would happen if Mitch communed with the horror below Rotwood Manor, but we persevered. The whole world might have been in danger, but for us—we were fighting for each other, for the love we shared and the lives we had built.</p>

<p>The corridor wound downward. Here and there we saw sights of fighting and splattered blood.</p>

<p>We reached a small room. There was another door on the opposite wall. The runes around it glowed red, casting sinister illumination over the two bodies lying on the floor.</p>

<p>The Cultists had been torn to ribbons. There was no sign of what had killed them, but I imagined their savage wounds had come from Shadow Dregs. I silently thanked the Monsters for buying us time.</p>

<p>Pushing on, we could hear the sounds of combat from below. Phantoms, ghosts of the past, ran beside us. We were all heading to the same place, the pit at the heart of the Oubliette of the Forgotten.</p>

<p>I popped Second Wind. It wasn’t a combat Skill, and I knew whatever strength it gave me was fleeting. We couldn’t afford a drawn-out fight.</p>

<p>We nearly tripped as the Dungeon shifted around us. The walls ground against one another as the floor shook. The hallway ahead of us vanished, replaced by a new doorway.</p>

<p>“Here!” Lorelei flew forward.</p>

<p>Following her, we reached the pit. Somehow, the chamber was even larger than before, stretching to become a massive vault. The domed ceiling was painted with mana, creating a false copy of the eclipse.</p>

<p>Mitch and the Cultists were at the edge of the pit, fighting off a wave of Shadow Remnants. Bodies, clawed and shredded, lay along the ground.</p>

<p>Their blood dripped into the pit, feeding the unearthly pillar of flame that roared up from below.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei’s eyes went wide as she pointed.</p>

<p>In the heart of the flames was a gigantic column, risen from the heart of the pit. Atop it was some kind of stone vault.</p>

<p><b>Prison of ??? (Unknown Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>SEALED. FORGOTTEN. DAMNED.</em></p>

<p>Carvings squirmed across its surface, forming eldritch, maddening patterns. It was a black hole of mana, an absolute point of nihility. I dared not use Mana Manipulation, sure that the sight would strike me blind.</p>

<p>Mitch was at the edge of the pit, hands in the air as he screamed ecstatic prayers. A blooded knife was in one hand, the DIK banner Josh had been carrying in the other.</p>

<p>“For the Lord, for the days of glory to come!” The remaining Cultists cut down the last of the Shadow Remnants and then formed up in front of Mitch.</p>

<p>We readied our ragged battle line.</p>

<p>The Oubliette shook. The phantoms moved around us, pantomiming Mitch as they offered rich sacrifices to the horror trapped within the prison. The stony tomb shook as whatever was trapped inside roared in malignant rage.</p>

<p>The Cultists rushed us.</p>

<p>Casting Hearth Flame, I covered my blade in a sheath of burning embers. Everything hurt, I didn’t even know how I was still standing, but I fought like a madman. A Cultist launched herself at me, knife in hand.</p>

<p>I battered aside her lunge as I stepped forward, closing the distance between us. The woman screamed into my face. Her eyes went wide in surprise a moment later as I plunged my rapier into her gut.</p>

<p>I didn’t pause to think of what I had just done. I twisted and tossed her aside. Another Cultist hit me across the face with a makeshift club. Blood flowed from a gash across my brow; everything spun.</p>

<p>It was nearly impossible to tell the living from the dead. Phantoms fought, died, and lived again in stuttering loops. The pillar of flame illuminated the chamber in hellish light, but it only seemed to steal heat rather than shed it outward.</p>

<p>Ishikana reared over the club Cultist and grabbed him with her two front legs. She hurled him away like he was a rag doll. Someone charged and stabbed her in the side. She knocked him down, and I pounced. Pinning him to the floor, I smashed him in the face with the pommel of my sword till he stopped moving.</p>

<p>Ayara was mobbed by several Cultists. Lorelei burned them with her magic, but they fought on, gouging and stabbing at the Blacksmith with murderous desperation.</p>

<p>I created a snap Guardian’s Stormwall, channeling the Skill for just a split second before releasing it. The Cultists were stunned by the thunder, giving the Drow a moment to punch, kick, and smash her way free.</p>

<p>“You’re too late!” Mitch jeered.</p>

<p>Shoving another Cultist aside, I reached the edge of the pit. Mitch was before me, Herb bowed in prayer at his feet. Hundreds of phantoms clustered around them, killers and victims alike all repeating the same moment.</p>

<p>I watched in horror as Mitch plunged his knife into the goat farmer’s neck. Blood spurted, flying like a crimson banner.</p>

<p>The pillar shivered, the flames hungrily consuming Herb’s offering. The prison shook, then cracked. The roar that followed nearly deafened me.</p>

<p>“It’s done!” Mitch screamed. “Come, Lord Baphomet, the prison is broken! This world is yours, reward us as your justly crowned kings!”</p>

<p>The vault rocked on its plinth. Something so hideous, so otherworldly that my conscious mind rejected it, squirmed out of the cracks.</p>

<p>“Brent!” Lorelei was suddenly by my side.</p>

<p>I felt her power, the power of Rotwood Manor, of my home, well up inside of me.</p>

<p>My vision was overlaid with visions of the past. Eustace Phelps stood beside me, his hammer in one hand, the clay jar in the other. Mimicking his actions, I pulled the Water of Styx from my pouch.</p>

<p>“Then we are all doomed!” Eustace shouted, swinging his hammer at the jug he held. “With this, I bind you, and I damn you for all eternity!”</p>

<p>In front of me, Mitch cackled madly. “I did it! I did it!”</p>

<p>Calling on my last ounce of strength, I smashed the jar into his face. The Water of Styx bubbled instantly turning to steam as the cult leader’s whoops of joy turned into screams of agony. He stabbed at me with the sacrificial knife. The blade punched through my armor and dug into my chest, but I would not relent.</p>

<p>Together, with Lorelei, we cast him into the pit.</p>

<p>“<em>With this, I bind you, and I damn you for all eternity!</em>” I shouted, echoing the words of Eustace Phelps.</p>

<p>Mitch screamed as he fell into the pillar of fire. The flames were dyed an impossible shade of black as he was consumed, body and soul.</p>

<p>Falling to my knees, I wobbled at the edge of the pit. Mitch’s knife was sticking out of my chest. My vision was blacking out, but I held on for just one more moment. From my cracked lips, I uttered an oath.</p>

<p>“This is my damn house. I don’t know what you are, or what you want, but as long as I live here, you’ll never see the light of day.”</p>

<p>The thing sealed in the tomb raged and roared as the cracks vanished. Slowly, the pillar began to sink, dragging the horror away, back to its prison.</p>

<p>Strong hands grabbed me and hauled me back from the brink as I passed out.</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Brent?”</p>

<p>I coughed as sweet but bitter medicine was forced down my throat.</p>

<p>Swallowing hard, I allowed the uneasy warmth to spread through my body before I cracked open my eyes.</p>

<p>“Ayara,” I gasped. “Seriously, you’ve got to stop waking me up like this. I love you, but there’s—”</p>

<p>I didn’t get to finish my joke. The Drow leaned down and frantically kissed me. I could feel her trembling. Her grip on my shoulders was far too tight.</p>

<p>When she finally let go, I saw we were in a storage room just off the basement stairs. Nearby were several Cultists. They were alive, but barely. There was no sign of Mitch, Herb, or the ghastly pit.</p>

<p>Ishikana leaned against the wall, an empty Healing Potion clutched in her fingers. Another spent bottle was on the floor next to Ayara.</p>

<p>“We all made it?” I asked weakly.</p>

<p>“Barely,” the Scholar said. “I have no idea what happened, or what you did, but we suddenly found ourselves here. The Oubliette of the Forgotten has vanished.”</p>

<p>She pointed to the stairs. As she had said, the door to the Dungeon was gone, replaced by a discolored section of ancient plaster.</p>

<p>“Shit.” With Ayara’s help, I sat up.</p>

<p>I felt sick. Too many Healing Potions in a short period had left me jittery and nauseous. I couldn’t help but think of Victor’s warning of using too many in too short a period of time.</p>

<p>But I was alive, that’s what counted. My body burned with a hundred sharp aches, but the wound in my chest was already starting to scab over.</p>

<p>Looking around, I spotted Lorelei hovering nearby.</p>

<p>“Are you okay?” I asked.</p>

<p>She turned to look at me, her expression going slack for a moment before the familiar warmth flooded her face.</p>

<p>Coming over, she gave me a long, lingering kiss.</p>

<p>“Yes, Brent,” she responded. “I can’t give you the details, I don’t even think I understand them really, but we will be just fine.”</p>

<p>“That’s great, because I can barely move,” I muttered. “Can someone help me up?”</p>

<p>Ishikana ended up helping me wobble up the stairs, Ayara and Lorelei behind us. We left the wounded Cultists below, bound by the Scholar’s silk.</p>

<p>Stepping into the main hall, I was happy to see normal, afternoon sunlight streaming through the windows. The flames in the hearth were still muted, but they glowed with pure shades of mana.</p>

<p>Outside, I could hear sirens and the sound of a confused commotion.</p>

<p>Sighing, I had Ishikana help me toward the front of the house. “We’ll need to invite the emergency responders inside, otherwise Intruder’s Bane is just going to start eating at them.”</p>

<p>I still had no idea what had happened, or what we had done in the depths of the Dungeon, but that didn’t matter at that moment. There would be time later to sort things out.</p>

<p>I had a property full of wounded people to deal with and a half-blown-up house to fix. If I was lucky, I’d find time for a nap.</p>
