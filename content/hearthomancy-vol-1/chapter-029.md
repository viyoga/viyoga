<p>Lorelei stayed with me that night, her spectral form chill against my side. Thankfully I didn’t notice any jealousy or possessiveness in her actions. Instead, she excitedly talked about all the options before us. I fell asleep listening to her wonderful plans for our future.</p>

<p>Waking up early, I found Ayara and Ishikana in the main hall, looking through the chest we had found the jar of Styx water inside. I hadn’t been able to divine anything about its origins from the chest’s contents. Afraid of damaging the materials further, I had tucked it away until the Scholar’s arrival.</p>

<p>Carefully, the Arachne extracted each piece of paper using a specialized Skill from her Class.</p>

<p><b>Archaeologist's Retrieval</b><br><b>MP Cost: </b>150</p>

<p><em>Scholars search the past for lost wisdom and forgotten knowledge. A deft touch is critical, especially when dealing with ancient artifacts and deteriorating documents.</em></p>

<p>Weaving her fingers above the steamer trunk, she pulled out the newspaper clippings one sheet at a time. I could see strings of her mana plucking out faded and crumbled bits, restoring them to the whole.</p>

<p>“Good morning!” Ayara called once she realized we were watching them.</p>

<p>Ishikana nodded absently, still focused on retrieving the front page of some long-forgotten newspaper.</p>

<p>I kissed the Drow, then headed to the kitchen. Despite my promise to jumpstart my exploration of Rotwood Manor’s mysteries, I wanted to Bottle Mana. Our fridge was empty, and I feared my bank account was heading the same direction unless I got some work done.</p>

<p>“Nonsense,” Ishikana said as we returned to the main hall and explained our need to go shopping. “Who else is going to act as my escort to the college library?”</p>

<p>She was dressed once again in her sexy librarian outfit, a black blazer and matching skirt. I noticed she’d covered her lower abdomen and sternum with a garment made from spandex, which I assumed covered her more feminine features. Despite her monstrous nature, it was hard not to see how attractive her human half was.</p>

<p>Lorelei giggled, clearly amused by my confused attraction. If Ayara noticed, she didn’t comment.</p>

<p>Ishikana skittered off to the basement. As she did, I bent down to inspect the newspaper clippings she had restored.</p>

<p>Most seemed to be inconsequential articles about happenings in Ashling Grove. A lot of the clippings were news articles, but there were weather reports and horoscopes predicting the movement of the moon and the stars as well.</p>

<p><em>Was it all tied to the vision I had, the solar eclipse?</em></p>

<p>“I’ve never even heard of the Ashling Gazette,” I said, picking out the name of the paper.</p>

<p>“Oh, I remember that!” Lorelei perked up. “My father—I mean Patrick Ganlts—owned it!”</p>

<p>I stood back up. “Really?”</p>

<p>She nodded. “Yeah, he bought out the paper when it started publishing nasty stories about him. It was around for a long time before that though.”</p>

<p>“Must have gone out of business sometime after that,” I murmured.</p>

<p>The only paper I knew of was the county one published the next town over. When researching Rotwood Manor I had contacted them, asking if they had anything in their archives, but the only stories they had concerned DIK. They’d run several stories about the frat’s antics, including the climactic party that had eventually seen them all expelled from school.</p>

<p>Ishikana returned. “Here.”</p>

<p>I grabbed the check from her hands, remembering she had promised to pay for the right to study Rotwood Manor. My eyes practically rolled when I realized she had over quadrupled her initial offer.</p>

<p>“Wait, this is way too much!” I protested.</p>

<p>“Posh,” she waved her hand. “Don’t consider it charity, but an investment on my part. You are at the nexus of several extremely rare circumstances. Outside your fascinating Lair, you are likely the first Human to unlock a previously unknown Class variant.”</p>

<p>A predatory grin sprung across her face. “The other System Scholars will be positively green with jealousy, especially the Goblins. Oh, I wish I could see their reaction! Even if I never publish a word about Rootwood Manor or its master, the secrets and insights I will uncover will be utterly priceless.”</p>

<p>I still didn’t like the thought of taking so much of the Arachne’s money, but I already knew that check could not only pay down a good chunk of my mortgage, but it could cover some critical work on the house.</p>

<p>“Fine, I’ll keep it,” I said begrudgingly.</p>

<p>Secretly, I had already earmarked a chunk of money to restore a larger bathroom, probably in the residential wing. I imagined Ishikana could squeeze into the bathroom we currently had, but it wouldn’t be a comfortable fit.</p>

<p>I could fix up a few rooms as well for Ayara and myself. The servant’s quarters were comfortable enough and close to the main hall, but I wanted more. I doubted we’d ever have servants, but we could turn the rooms into offices and parlors, maybe workshops for future crafters.</p>

<p>My shared dream with Lorelei, of Rootwood Manor’s past, would be restored to reality if I had any say about it.</p>

<p>Looking back at the recovered newspapers, I noted they were nearly a hundred and fifty years old, dating from the 1880s. Most seemed to deal with the budding growth of Ashling Grove, which at that point was little more than a lumber town at the edge of the Pennsylvania wilderness.</p>

<p>Rootwood Manor wouldn’t be built until 1909. Patrick Ganlts had been one of the richest men in the state at the time. His wealth had only exploded with the start of World War One, when he began selling wood, charcoal, and other materials to the Allies.</p>

<p>It was hard to believe that it would all be gone in a scant few decades. He died in 1929 under <em>unusual circumstances</em>. The following years saw his empire and family brought to ruins.</p>

<p>I glanced at Lorelei. I hadn’t talked to the Hearth Spirit much about the details of her mortal life. She had made it clear there was a distinction between the mortal Lorelei and herself, that the woman I knew was the spirit of the house, not just some ghostly fragment.</p>

<p>I had done more digging into the life of the mortal Lorelei Ganlts. She had lived a life of luxury and misfortune. Penniless and alone, she died in 1937.</p>

<p>I did not want that fate for the Hearth Spirit.</p>

<p>Shaking my head, I went back to reading through the papers. The news articles seemed to be fairly tame, reiterating events from the larger world. The most sensational stories appeared to be about the assassination of President James Garfield.</p>

<p>The local stories mostly covered accidents, deaths, and tragedies. I knew working conditions at the turn of the century had been dangerous, but it appeared there was another fatal accident every week in the town’s mills, mines, or industries. Many of them were owned by Patrick Ganlts.</p>

<p>Reading the articles, the size of the town’s graveyards, and the number of ancient, crumbling tombstones suddenly made a lot more sense.</p>

<p>Inspecting the trunk, I saw that the Arachne had only recovered a small portion of the materials. It would take her hours, if not days, to restore the rest.</p>

<p>Clearing my throat, I called the others. “We should pick our next Boon or Bane for the Lair. We won’t be able to level up or improve it further until we do.”</p>

<p>“Awesome!” Lorelei gave me a high five. “Which one are you thinking of picking?”</p>

<p>“Truth Seeker’s Boon seems to be the natural choice,” I answered. “We’ve already been given a peek at the past thanks to the manor’s mana, imagine what we could learn under the effect of the Boon.”</p>

<p>“It could be dangerous too,” Ayara pointed out, reminding us of my battle in the cave.</p>

<p>“In this case, it might be fair for me to share my stats,” Ishikana said, rearing up to her full height.</p>

<p><b>Ishikana of the Dire Archive</b><br><b></b><b>Race: </b>Arachne<br><b>MP: </b>13550/13550</p>

<p><b>Ability Scores</b><br><b></b><b>Strength: </b>40<br><b>Finesse: </b>108<br><b>Fortitude: </b>75<br><b>Resilience: </b>77<br><b>Wisdom: </b>335<br><b>Discipline: </b>360</p>

<p><b>Classes</b><br><b></b><b>Scholar of Endless Webs Lvl 62</b><br><b>Scribe of Lost Secrets Lvl 43</b></p>

<p><b>Skills</b></p>

<p><b>Archeologist’s Retrieval Lvl 18</b><br><b>Danger Sense Lvl 15</b><br><b>Piercing Insight Lvl 21</b><br><b>Fire Bolt Lvl 5</b><br><b>Fire Blast Lvl 5</b><br><b>Ice Bolt Lvl 3</b><br><b>Ice Blast Lvl 2</b><br><b>Lightning Bolt Lvl 3</b><br><b>…</b></p>

<p><b>Proficiencies</b><br><b></b><b>Critical Conjecture Lvl 24</b><br><b>Archeology Lvl 22</b><br><b>Unbreakable Focus Lvl 17</b><br><b>Trap Making Lvl 14</b><br><b>Trap Detection Lvl 13</b><br><b>Thrown Weapons Lvl 15</b><br><b>Unarmed Combat Lvl 6</b><br><b>Scribe Tome Lvl 17</b><br><b>Copy Tome Lvl 15</b><br><b>…</b></p>

<p>Her Skill list scrolled on for pages. Most of the Skills listed were low-level, but her range was staggering. Her Proficiencies were equally long, but most seemed aimed toward her work as a Scholar or her secondary Class.</p>

<p>“Both Scholar of Lost Secrets and Scribe of Endless Webs are Master Classes, Evolutions of my original Mentor and Crafter classes,” the Arachne explained in a haughty tone. “The Scribe Class gives me minor aptitude across a wide spectrum of elemental magic Skills while Scholar of Endless Webs grants me additional Proficiencies in making and laying traps.”</p>

<p>“Very thematic,” Lorelei said, gazing at her spider body.</p>

<p>“Thank you,” Ishikana replied with a sharp smile. “You’d be surprised how dangerous the life of a Scholar can be, especially with rivals always trying to stab you in the back or steal your latest discovery. It was difficult to unlock the Evolution. It had many requirements, including my Arachne species, but it was well worth it.”.</p>

<p>“Anyway, the two Banes being offered, Vile Will’s and Curse Reach’s, are both incredibly powerful, but I’m not sure how applicable they’ll be here on Earth yet,” the Scholar told us. “Typically, you only see mental or spiritual attacks coming from specialized Classes like Mentalists, Witches, or Cultists. I’d recommend picking them up eventually, but for now—”</p>

<p>“Truth Seeker’s Boon!” Lorelei cheered.</p>

<p>I picked it from the list. I felt the entire house groan and shift. The entire property stretched and flexed like some earthbound and buried giant was stirring from long, dark dreams. The mana around my Lair shifted, falling into new patterns, or more likely following paths long lost and forgotten.</p>

<p>Nothing seemed different to the naked eye, but I knew we had just crossed a threshold, taking a path we could not retreat from.</p>

<p>“Right.” I steadied myself with a deep breath. “I need to go to the bank I guess, then the grocery store.”</p>

<p>“But before that, we have an appointment at Ashling State,” Ishikana reminded me. “I’ve already taken the liberty of contacting the college’s dean via a contact at the US Department of Education. They’ll be expecting us. We can use their library to research the history of your Lair and what was the genesis of its wonderful, unique nature.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>Ayara got ready to head to work while Lorelei, Ishikana, and I loaded into my pickup truck. The Arachne rode in the back, curling her legs around the side of the bed.</p>

<p>“Oh, wait, before you go, you’ll need this!” the Drow Blacksmith said as she ran over.</p>

<p>She handed Ishikana a gate pass, just like the one I carried on my keychain.</p>

<p>“I’ll get you a set of keys made as well,” I added.</p>

<p>The Scholar nodded. “Thank you. I don’t expect to leave the property often unless I’m off to do research—”</p>

<p>“Or sneak off to the nearest donut shop or bakery.” the Drow chuckled.</p>

<p>“But I appreciate the gesture,” the Arachne finished gravely.</p>

<p>I gave Ayara a quick kiss, then let her pull out of the driveway first, my truck right behind her car. The Blacksmith opened the gate and then locked it as we drove past. Giving her one last wave, we started driving into town.</p>

<p>Ishikana crouched low in the truck’s bed. I opened the back window so we could talk.</p>

<p>“So, you and Ayara are together? Good.” Ishikana said, sounding like a proud mother hen. “You’re a good man and a good match for her. Treat her well.”</p>

<p>“Of course, I will,” I answered a bit too hotly.</p>

<p>She giggled. “Don’t worry, I didn’t mean that as an insult. I’m just rather protective of Ayara. I consider her a dear student of mine, and I want only the best for her.”</p>

<p>“Brent is the best!” Lorelei insisted from the passenger seat.</p>

<p>“I’m sure he is,” Ishikana agreed.</p>

<p>The drive into town was uneventful. There wasn’t such a thing as <em>rush hour</em> in Ashling Grove. At most, a few of the coffee shops would get crowded as locals and college kids rushed to get their morning shot of caffeine before the day started.</p>

<p>Still, I couldn’t help but feel everyone’s stares as we drove down the road, the Scholar perched on the back of my truck. Listening to the AM news radio station, the major story was the announcement that Humanity had made contact with another intelligent species, the spider-like Arachne. Hailing from an unknown world, they were pledged allies to the Drow and were more than happy to offer their hands in friendship to Humanity.</p>

<p>“Really, you did all this just so you could study Rotwood Manor?” I muttered.</p>

<p>Ishikana chuckled. “In truth, we’ve been in contact with your leaders for a while now. We’ve just been waiting for a good time to introduce ourselves to your species at large. As you’ve undoubtedly noticed, people unfamiliar with our kind tend to view us with fear or hostility, as Ashling Grove is aptly demonstrating.”</p>

<p>She wasn’t kidding. As we got closer to the college campus, we were getting even more looks, and not all of them were friendly. Even stranger, I didn’t think they were all directed at Ishikana. As we stopped at a light at the edge of the college campus, I watched as a group of students gave me and Lorelei dirty looks before murmuring amongst themselves and stalking away.</p>

<p><em>What was that all about</em>? <em>Was the neighborhood watch spreading nasty rumors about us or something?</em></p>

<p>The light changed, so I pulled through the intersection and followed the posted signs till we reached Ashling State’s library.</p>
