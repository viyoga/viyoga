<p>“Druxsellaic Endures!” the Drow Blacksmith shouted, bringing her mace down on the Brackish Lizard’s head.</p>

<p>The creature’s skull shattered, and it tripped backward, already dead on its feet.</p>

<p>Lorelei and I fired a barrage of Mana Bolts, striking the two remaining Monsters that had been flanking the Drow. One of the creatures, already wounded, faltered, and then died.</p>

<p>The last Monster charged toward me. I twisted away from the blow. The lizard skidded on its hind feet, unable to find purchase on the wet stone.</p>

<p>Still, it managed to scrape its claws against my chestguard. Out of MP, I opted to kick the Monster, driving my boot into its side.</p>

<p>It gave out a pained hiss. I stomped on its ribs, knocking it completely prone. Ayara finished it off a second later, shattering its spine with an overhead strike.</p>

<p>Panting, I rested my hands on my knees, breathing heavily. “Holy shit, is everyone alright?”</p>

<p>“I’m fine,” Lorelei said, her form flickering. “I think I just need a moment.”</p>

<p>“Brent, what about you?” Ayara insisted.</p>

<p>I checked my armor. The Monster’s claws had scratched the metal, but the chestguard was still intact.</p>

<p>“I’ll have to give my compliments to the college’s armory when we get back,” I said, straightening. “Let’s take a quick break. That last fight blew through all my MP.”</p>

<p>Thankfully, even without my access to Rotwood Manor’s bottomless mana, my reduced MP still refilled at a startling rate. From the back-of-napkin math I had done, my MP recharged at a set rate based on my maximum MP when I was within my Lair. I didn’t have nearly as much MP when I was away from home, but what I did have, refilled at a frankly insane rate.</p>

<p>Which was good. The Annex was full of Monsters, and we needed every advantage we could get.</p>

<p>The Pond Lizards and Giant Roaches we had encountered had all been level two or three according to Ayara. The Brackish Lizards we had just fought had been level six, higher than me and the same level as Ayara. Their backs were covered in rows of reed-like spikes, making them far more dangerous than their lower-level counterparts.</p>

<p>Checking my notifications, I saw Mana Bolt had leveled again. Focusing on my stats, I also sensed that Hearth Magic was close to leveling, likely from Lorelei’s contributions to our fights.</p>

<p>Sadly, it was impossible to tell if we were making any sort of progress toward the exit. Unlike the first floor of the Dungeon, the Annex was truly a maze. Lorelei had taken to mapping our route through the Dungeon using the spare paper and pens I had tucked into my bag.</p>

<p>“I think we need to go that way,” the Hearth Spirit said, pointing to a nearby passage. “That leads to an area we haven’t been to yet.”</p>

<p>Ayara grunted and began to rise, but I waved for her to sit. “Relax, I still need another moment to fully recharge.”</p>

<p>She shook her head. “Sorry, I’m just—"</p>

<p>The Drow Blacksmith trailed off. I could tell she had been uneasy since we’d fallen into the trap. I took a seat next to her.</p>

<p>“Anything you want to talk about?” I asked gently.</p>

<p>Slowly, she took off her helmet. “House Druxsellaic is known for its Warriors. Our annals are full of heroes. We are regarded as a House of protectors and defenders. My mother and father both pushed me to carry on our family’s legacy, especially when they saw how quickly I was growing. But I never felt myself called to the battlefield. Instead, my heart always belonged to the forge and the art of crafting weapons.”</p>

<p>Lorelei floated nearby, but we both remained silent.</p>

<p>“But now? I wonder what would have happened if I had become a Warrior? Maybe you wouldn’t have been hurt saving me. Maybe we wouldn’t even be—”</p>

<p>“That’s bullshit.”</p>

<p>The Drow’s eyes went wide at my comment.</p>

<p>“It is,” I insisted. “I got hurt doing what I felt was right, there’s no shame in that. And while I’ve got a hell of a headache, I survived.”</p>

<p>I sighed. “Listen, people should be free to be who they want to be. I mean, I got plenty of shit from people because I became a Wizard instead of something more useful. Nobody here, including me, really knows what Hearth Magic can ultimately do. But I’ll be damned if I’m going to give up on my dreams just because I’m not living up to others’ expectations.”</p>

<p>“What got you into Hearth Magic in the first place?” Lorelei asked, hovering over me.</p>

<p>“It’s silly,” I admitted, feeling sheepish.</p>

<p>“Please.” Ayara tentatively reached out, putting a hand on my wrist.</p>

<p>She drew her hand back quickly, but I felt bolstered by her touch.</p>

<p>“There was an old movie when I was a kid, an animated one.” I tried to recall the memory. “This wizard moved into a beaten, old tower. It was full of junk and there were holes in the walls and ceiling. But the old guy just rolled up his sleeves, pulled out his wand, and began fixing the place up.”</p>

<p>I could remember the technicolor flames leaping into the fireplace as the wizard began animating the junk around the room, turning them into simple servants. They swept the floors, patched the holes, and carried in his supplies as he orchestrated them with his magic. It was a silly, comical scene, but it stuck with me.</p>

<p>“After the Reckoning, I learned there was a whole school of magic dedicated to that kind of stuff. I was hooked,” I finished.</p>

<p>Plenty of people had made fun of me for my choice. Worse, some people had thought I had made a mistake, some kind of life-destroying, sub-optimal choice. They thought that life was a game that had to be <em>Min/Max’ed</em> to be considered <em>victory</em>.</p>

<p>I was surprised when Ayara smiled at me. “That’s a good dream, a noble goal.”</p>

<p>“Yeah, it’s so cool!” Lorelei agreed. “I don’t remember that movie. We’ll have to watch it together when we get home!”</p>

<p>“Sure thing,” I promised her.</p>

<p>Confirming that my MP was full, I got up. Offering my hand to Ayara, I helped the Blacksmith back to her feet.</p>

<p>“When we get out of here, if you still feel guilty, you can buy the first round of beers,” I told her.</p>

<p>She blushed, the skin along her cheeks going a darker shade of purple. “I’d like that.”</p>

<p class="scene-break">***</p>

<p>“Huh, that’s odd.”</p>

<p><b>Hearth Spirit Has Unlocked New Proficiency!</b><br><b>Proficiency Dungeon Cartography Unlocked!</b></p>

<p>I blinked at the notification. “I didn’t realize you could gain Proficiencies of your own, I thought you just piggybacked off mine.”</p>

<p>“It’s cool though!” Lorelei said, holding up the map she had been charting. “I already know how I could do a better job the next time we go delving!”</p>

<p>I smiled at her. “Congratulations!”</p>

<p>She held up the sheet, focusing. “Hmmm, I don’t know why, but I think we’re near the exit!”</p>

<p>“Dungeon Cartography is a common Proficiency for adventurers,” Ayara explained. “With Dungeon layouts always shifting, they gain a knack for picking out major landmarks, like entrances and exits, or stairs leading up or down. I wouldn’t be surprised if Victor and the other guides all had it too.”</p>

<p>Still, I wondered why the Hearth Spirit had picked up the Proficiency so quickly. Normally it took days or even weeks of research and effort to unlock a Proficiency, especially for one so far out of alignment with her Class.</p>

<p>Still, I wasn’t going to complain. “Which way?”</p>

<p>We followed the drifting spirit down a wide hallway. The briny smell in the air grew worse like we were walking through a salt marsh. Reeds and grass pushed up through the stones, growing along the walls.</p>

<p>Ayara walked first, shield raised, while I followed right behind her, another Mana Bolt ready to go. With our cut-down group, the Blacksmith had been an admirable tank while Lorelei and I focused on dealing as much damage as possible.</p>

<p>“Up ahead,” Lorelei warned us.</p>

<p>Peeking through the door frame, we spotted a huge lizard. The Monster was easily the size of a compact car. Its moldy, gray scales were covered with layers of moss and bracken. Yawning, it revealed a maw full of razor-sharp teeth.</p>

<p>“Level eight Marsh King,” Ayara said, squinting. “It’s huge, it must be some kind of mini-boss.”</p>

<p>Typically, the exits for Dungeons or the stairs down, deeper into the depths, were protected by a powerful Monster. It seemed the Annex wasn’t any different.</p>

<p>Behind the slumbering beast, there was a wooden door. A rune was carved in the door frame above it.</p>

<p>“That’s the exit,” Ayara said, pointing to the symbol. “It should take us back to the entrance, based on what I know.”</p>

<p>“Right, can we just sneak past that ugly lizard?” Lorelei asked.</p>

<p>The Drow shook her head. “No, we’ll have to defeat it first.”</p>

<p>The Hearth Spirit looked worriedly at the oversized Monster while I scanned the room. The chamber was about ten meters across. Outside the puddles and reeds along the floor, the area was relatively clear, with only minimal debris.</p>

<p>“I have an idea,” I said, quickly sketching out the battle plan I’d come up with. “The lizards we have fought so far have been fast, faster than the roaches at least, but they can’t turn well. If we can kite it around the room, Lorelei and I can whittle it down with spells. Once it’s weakened, Ayara, you can go in and finish it.”</p>

<p>“It’s a good plan,” the Blacksmith admitted.</p>

<p>“Let’s do this!” the Hearth Spirit said, raising her hands.</p>

<p>Ayara looked confused. With a sigh, I gave Lorelei a high five. The Drow copied me a moment later.</p>

<p>“Once more into the breach,” I muttered.</p>

<p>I crept into the room, going to the right, while Lorelei snuck into position on the left. As we did that, Ayara marched right into the middle of the room, banging her mace against the face of her shield.</p>

<p>The Marsh King’s head snapped up, a growl rattling up its throat. Standing, I saw it was even taller than we had suspected, the top of its head reaching Ayara’s collarbone. Still, the Drow Blacksmith wasn’t intimidated.</p>

<p>She might not have been a Warrior, but she had the heart of a champion.</p>

<p>Rearing its head back, I was surprised when the Marsh King spat a glob of water at Ayara. She raised her shield and blocked. The room rang with the impact, and the Blacksmith was driven back by the force of the blow.</p>

<p>The mini-boss charged while Ayara was still off balance.</p>

<p>“Now!” I shouted.</p>

<p>Lorelei and I fired our Mana Bolts at almost the same time from opposite sides of the room. My spell hit the creature in the side, while Lorelei struck it in the neck. The Marsh King <em>squawked</em> in pain and surprise. It skidded along the floor, battering into the Drow Blacksmith. She deflected the hit with her shield as the lizard came to an uneven stop.</p>

<p>“My turn!” I yelled, firing another Mana Bolt. “Over here, you stupid lizard!”</p>

<p>I burned through my mana, firing as many shots as I could. The tip of my wand glowed as I cast spell after spell. The Marsh King ducked its head and drew up its reedy spikes, shielding itself from the salvo.</p>

<p>Hissing, it unwound its bulk and slunk low, charging my position. I threw myself to the side, landing roughly as the creature crashed past me. It nearly hit the wall. Recovering quickly, the Monster spat another water bullet.</p>

<p>I grunted in pain as the attack hit me in the shoulder, nearly throwing me off my feet.</p>

<p>“Don’t you dare!” Lorelei fired her own attack from across the room.</p>

<p>The Marsh King hissed angrily but kept its focus on me. Our plan began to fall apart as the Monster completely ignored the Hearth Spirit’s attacks.</p>

<p>Snapping its teeth, it tried to take a bite out of my arm. I barely avoided its snapping jaws, but I managed to hit it point-blank with a Mana Bolt.</p>

<p>Shrieking, the creature fell back. My spell had struck it in the eye, nearly blinding it.</p>

<p>“For the houses, for the End!”</p>

<p>Ayara crashed into the mini-boss like a runaway freight train. Her mace smashed the lizard across the face, hitting it on its blind side. The creature reeled from the stunning blow.</p>

<p>The Blacksmith rammed the Marsh King into the wall; the two of them crashed onto the floor with the Drow on top.</p>

<p>The Marsh King thrashed and kicked at her, its claws gouging against her armor. Ayara fought back, slamming her mace into its side. But the mini-boss refused to give up. The Marsh King was far stronger and more resilient than we had given it credit for.</p>

<p>The lizard bit her on the shoulder, its teeth tearing into her armored collar and pauldron. The Marsh King twisted and threw her off, pinning Ayara to the floor. It knocked her mace from her hand with its tail. Lorelei kept pelting it with spells, but like me, she was almost out of MP.</p>

<p>Gripping my wand like a dagger, I did the only thing I could think of. Rushing forward, I wrapped my arm around the lizard’s neck and jabbed the wand toward its face.</p>

<p>Mana flared along the length of my wand. For a split second, the weapon was coated in bright, white flames. Power surged through me like I was momentarily connected to Rotwood Manor and its endless mana.</p>

<p>Screaming, I stabbed the Marsh King in its remaining eye with the spell-charged wand.</p>

<p>The mini-boss shrieked. Flames leapt from its ruined eye socket as it convulsed.</p>

<p>It released its bite on Ayara. She heaved her legs back, throwing it off her. I continued to hold onto its neck, twisting my wand deeper into its skull.</p>

<p>The Marsh King placed its claw against my chest and pushed. I could feel the claws punching through my armor and digging into my chest, but I refused to let go.</p>

<p>I wrapped my legs around its neck, pinning it in place. “Hurry!”</p>

<p>Ayara grabbed up her mace and raised it in a two-handed grip. She brought it down against the Marsh King’s exposed ribs. She smashed down, again and again. The Drow Blacksmith didn’t stop until the creature gave a final spasm, then went deathly still.</p>

<p>Rolling free, I laid on my back. Adrenaline surged through my veins; my entire body rang with a buzzing numbness.</p>

<p>Lorelei rushed over to me. “Brent!”</p>

<p>“I’m okay,” I insisted as she helped me sit up.</p>

<p>Ayara was bloodied and her armor was rended and breached in a dozen spots. I insisted she drink our remaining Healing Potion.</p>

<p>“I’ll be black and blue tomorrow, but I’ll live,” I insisted, checking the scratches across my chest and the bruises forming along my shoulder. “Either way, we did it!”</p>

<p>Lorelei passed on normal formalities, allowing for more sedate Low Fives as we celebrated our rough victory. I checked the Marsh King, confirming it was truly dead.</p>

<p>I tried to pull my wand free, but only the handle came loose. The metal had melted during the brawl. Just the same, I tucked the broken handle into a pocket.</p>

<p>It wasn’t exactly a badass sword, but it would make a nice trophy to hang above our mantle.</p>

<p>A chest had appeared in front of the Annex’s exit.</p>

<p>“Our reward?” Lorelei wondered.</p>

<p>Opening it, we found a small box and a pair of boots inside.</p>

<p><b>Enchantment Tools (Medium Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A set of tools used to craft enchantments, allowing Crafters to carve or etch enchantments into objects.</em></p>

<p>“Oh!” Ayara’s eyes lit up as she opened the tool kit and spotted the delicate blades, rasps, and carving tools inside.</p>

<p>“What is all that stuff?” the Hearth Spirit asked as she looked quizzically at the box’s contents.</p>

<p>“Humanity hasn’t unlocked Enchanter yet, but it’s an Evolution to the Crafter Class,” Ayara explained. “It allows you to enhance items, granting them magical effects, like the speed boost I put on my boots.”</p>

<p>She showed us a pair of runes etched into the leather of her boots. The marks appeared to be fading; as we watched, they slowly vanished. Ayara explained that she had expended its charge to ram the Marsh King during the brawl.</p>

<p>“I’m still low level, I can only do temporary or charge-based enchantments. Eventually, I want to fuse it with my Blacksmith Class to become a Magical Blacksmith.” She ran a hand over the expended runes. “It seems I still have a ways to go.”</p>

<p>I snorted. “You did amazing, trust me.”</p>

<p>The boots were apparently for me.</p>

<p><b>Hearth Strider’s Boots (Medium Grade)</b></p>

<p><em>A Hearthomancer’s duty will often take him far away from his hearth. These enchanted boots will never wear out and will provide excellent footing even in the most inhospitable of conditions.</em></p>
