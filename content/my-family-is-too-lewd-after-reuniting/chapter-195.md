<p>“Hello, Committee Member.”</p><p>“Yes, hello.”</p><p>As usual, I exchanged awkward greetings with the staff before entering my office.</p><p>Since I had to pass through the office where ten Association members worked to reach my private office, these awkward greetings had become routine.</p><p>My subordinates probably knew that I wasn’t a standing committee member with real authority or goals—I was just Han Ji-hyeok’s planted lackey, a parachuted figurehead to make it easier for him to push his agenda.</p><p>‘It’s unreasonable to expect a standing committee member who bolts at noon to be taken seriously.’</p><p>Only Min Se-rin, my secretary, brought me documents, so I had no reason to talk to the other employees.</p><p>Today, as soon as I arrived, I loosened my tie, tossed it onto the desk, kicked off my dress shoes, slipped into slippers, pulled out my phone, and was deep into Hunter Gall when I heard voices outside my door.</p><p>“Is the standing committee member inside?”</p><p>“Yes, he is.”</p><p>“May I see him?”</p><p>“No. Please submit a formal meeting request and return once it’s approved.”</p><p>From the conversation, it sounded like someone had come to see me, and Se-rin was turning them away.</p><p>‘Why me?’</p><p>“Just in case, I should mention—I was sent by the Intelligence Bureau Director.”</p><p>“I know who you are. Aren’t you An Yoon-mun, the section chief from the Intelligence Bureau?”</p><p>“Yes, you’re well-informed. You know who I am, yet you’re still refusing?”</p><p>The man’s voice outside the door was shifting into a threatening tone.</p><p>“I’m sorry, but no. If you wish to speak with the standing committee member, fill out the request form in advance. Once approved, return at the scheduled time.”</p><p>But Se-rin didn’t flinch in the slightest—she rejected him firmly.</p><p>‘Good job, Se-rin. That’s how a secretary should be.’</p><p>I didn’t even read the documents I signed, so I had no idea why this man was here. After some thought, only one possibility came to mind.</p><p>All the approval documents that came to me were first reviewed by Han Ji-hyeok’s subordinates.</p><p>In other words, the documents I signed likely contained content that was highly advantageous to Han Ji-hyeok, even if I didn’t know the details.</p><p>He must have come because he had a complaint about one of the documents I’d approved.</p><p>Silence lingered outside the door for a while.</p><p>“Ha!”</p><p>Then came the man’s disbelieving laugh.</p><p>After laughing for a few seconds, he spoke to Se-rin again.</p><p>“You think I’m joking because I’m being polite? Get out of my way.”</p><p>“What?”</p><p>The man finally took off his mask and started speaking informally while threatening her at the same time. Min Se-rin responded in an equally heated tone.</p><p>‘Typical Information Bureau behavior.’</p><p>The Information and Investigation Bureau, an organization with unchecked power. Even back when it was split into the Information Bureau and the Investigation Bureau, it had been known as the most powerful agency. After merging, it had truly become a monstrous collective.</p><p>I’d never heard of An Yoon-moon before, but he was clearly a strong Hunter. The Information and Investigation Bureau operated on strict meritocracy, and the minimum requirement to hold the rank of section chief there was being a combat-focused A-rank Hunter with extensive field experience.</p><p>It was so bad that there were open rumors of department heads from weaker divisions bowing their heads first to section chiefs from the Information and Investigation Bureau. Their power was that absolute.</p><p>They might not have much in the way of manners, but in that section chief’s mind, he was probably thinking, *‘How dare you refuse when I, a section chief of the Information and Investigation Bureau, came all the way here and asked you politely?’*</p><p>“Should I cause a scene here?”</p><p>“The Hunter Association won’t just stand by and watch.”</p><p>“I’ll stomp you flat, take my suspension, rest up for a few days, then get reinstated as section chief. No big deal.”</p><p>The conversation was spiraling into genuinely dangerous territory. Even from behind the door, I could feel the killing intent. It was about time I stepped in.</p><p>After straightening my tie, I spoke.</p><p>“Secretary, let him in.”</p><p>“···Yes, Chairman.”</p><p>After Min Se-rin’s reply, the door opened, and Section Chief An Yoon-moon entered. He was a towering man, easily over 190 cm tall, with an enormous build.</p><p>The scars on his face and neck, along with his buzz-cut hair, made his personality obvious even without a word.</p><p>Before the door closed, Min Se-rin looked at me with concern, but I nodded to signal that I was fine.</p><p>“Chairman, my apologies for the sudden visit.”</p><p>An Yoon-moon bowed his head in greeting, then sat on the office sofa, crossing his legs.</p><p>‘What an absolute disregard for basic respect.’</p><p>From my conversations with Min Se-rin and my observations of the Hunter Association, I’d realized something.</p><p>The Information and Investigation Bureau definitely knew about Echo. In fact, they might have the most detailed information out of all the Association’s agencies.</p><p>But they were making two critical misjudgments.</p><p>First, they saw Echo and his subordinates as *‘ridiculously powerful, but if the Association poured all its resources into it, they could be subdued. However, if played right, they could be used as assets, so they’re being left alone for now.’*</p><p>That was their first mistake. Forget Echo—if Nerus alone stepped forward, the Association would have to commit everything to stop him.</p><p>And the second misjudgment is that they see me as nothing more than a “diplomat who can talk to those beings.”</p><p>In a way, this was inevitable. Among the high-ranking members of the Association, only Han Ji-hyeok knows that my relationship with them goes beyond mere communication—even to the point of requesting their help.</p><p>‘They probably think they know everything in the world… I’m not even surprised anymore.’</p><p>I stared at An Yoon-mun in silence.</p><p>“Chairman?”</p><p>As I continued to gaze at him without a word, he seemed to realize he had overstepped. He uncrossed his legs and sat up straight again.</p><p>“What brings you here?”</p><p>An Yoon-mun, without asking for permission, picked up the bottled water on my desk, opened it, and took a sip before leaning back with a relaxed smile. “I came to discuss the documents you submitted two days ago.”</p><p>“I’ve approved so many documents that I don’t even remember. Which one are you talking about?”</p><p>“This one.”</p><p>An Yoon-mun stood up, pulled a folded paper from his bag, and handed it to me.</p><p>It was filled with dense, technical language, so I skimmed through it to see what it was about, flipping to the last page.</p><p>“Hmm.”</p><p>And then I realized why this man had come to see me. The document was a request to add a clause: “National branch directors cannot simultaneously run for vice-chairman of the Association.”</p><p>Naturally, I was the one who submitted this proposal. In other words, I was telling the committee: ‘If you want to run for vice-chairman, give up the director position. Or if you want to stay as director, forget about running for vice-chairman.’</p><p>‘Fuck… I should’ve read it before signing.’</p><p>Han Ji-hyeok would’ve probably come to me first if he had any complaints. I never expected the Intelligence Bureau to barge in like this without warning.</p><p>But I couldn’t let my fluster show. No—actually, An Yoon-mun was subtly radiating killing intent, trying to intimidate me, but it didn’t feel threatening at all.</p><p>It was a dangerous thought, but if I wanted to, I could probably kill him bare-handed in three seconds.</p><p>I tossed the document onto the desk and said,</p><p>“What’s the problem with this document?”</p><p>“Ha!”</p><p>An Yoon-mun let out the same creepy laugh he had used on Se-rin before answering.</p><p>“Hey… sorry, Committee Member? If this proposal passes, Director Im, you won’t be able to run for vice-chairman, right? Isn’t that true?”</p><p>“Can’t you just resign as director and run?”</p><p>“Haa.”</p><p>An Yoon-mun clenched his fists, veins bulging in his neck as he trembled, then leaned in close and muttered,</p><p>“Listen, do you think we’re scared of some standing committee member? I already chewed out Director Min today, and now you’re pissing me off too. This isn’t a request—it’s a warning. Withdraw it.”</p><p>An Yoon-mun dropped his mask far faster than I expected and immediately launched into threats.</p><p>‘Civil Complaints Department Director…’</p><p>The director of the Civil Complaints Adjustment Department. It was the Hunter Association’s most infamous punching bag—an organization primarily staffed by non-Hunter Association members, known for handling complaints but wielding no real power.</p><p>Even the title of “director” was meaningless; the department was so powerless that its head was regularly chewed out by section chiefs from the Intelligence Bureau. And now, An Yoon-mun was trying to equate that director with me in his threats.</p><p>I stared into the void for a moment, weighing my options. Normally, I’d avoid conflict, but my time in the Association had taught me that backing down didn’t always make problems disappear. In situations like this, there was really only one effective approach.</p><p>I smiled and said to An Yoon-mun,</p><p>“Want me to kill you?”</p><p>“What? No… what?”</p><p>An Yoon-mun’s face went blank, his mouth hanging open as if his brain had short-circuited.</p><p>“Your tone’s getting on my nerves. Did you come here to beg me to kill you? Just get to the point.”</p><p>I grinned, deliberately provoking him. The veins in An Yoon-mun’s temples began to bulge.</p><p>‘There’s even a CCTV in this room. If he attacks first, that’d actually be convenient.’</p><p>An Yoon-mun wasn’t stupid enough to strike first. But he wasn’t smart enough to back down, either.</p><p>“Ah, you… fuck, this is ridiculous.”</p><p>An Yoon-mun let out a hollow laugh, genuinely at a loss for words.</p><p>“Do you know where you are, you Information Bureau thug? Get on your knees and apologize right now, and I’ll pretend this never happened.”</p><p>SLAM</p><p>When I kept laughing, An Yoon-mun slammed his hand down on the desk and snarled,</p><p>“You think being a standing committee member means I can’t touch you? You’re just some C-rank gate runner who scraped by on promotions—”</p><p>His face, marred by veins and scars, twisted in rage. He looked ready to throw a punch at any second.</p><p>I rested my chin on my hand and replied in a bored tone,</p><p>“If it bothers you that much, how about a sparring match? Let’s go down to the training hall right now and settle this.”</p><p>It was a bold statement, but if I didn’t crush him here and now, he’d keep crawling back for more.</p><p>“Sure… it’s been a while since I killed someone. Perfect timing.”</p><p>An Yoon-mun’s face lit up with a cruel smile at my response.</p><p>He probably thought he could kill me without breaking a sweat.</p><p>I stood up from my chair, pulled out a wet wipe to clean my hands, and said,</p><p>“Then let’s start by signing the waiver absolving me of responsibility if you die during sparring, and we’ll head straight to the training hall.”</p>
