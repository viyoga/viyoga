<p>“So? Did you spend a hot afternoon with Mom yesterday?”</p><p>Inside the car speeding down the road, noona keeps asking endlessly about what happened yesterday.</p><p>“Ah, yeah.”</p><p>“How many times did you do it?”</p><p>“I don’t… remember.”</p><p>“It was so good you can’t even remember?”</p><p>“Ah, come on…”</p><p>The day before yesterday was with noona, yesterday was a steamy rendezvous with Mom, and today I’m finally moving my stuff into noona’s official residence.</p><p>Noona said she’d rent a car and handle it herself, but there’s a lot to move, and there’s nothing to do at home anyway. Plus, I was planning to visit Hye-na soon, so I might as well see her while I’m at it.</p><p>I won’t be moving in right away, so for now, I’ll just take the big items. Noona said she’d bring the smaller things over whenever she visits home on weekends.</p><p>It’s been a while since I’ve driven, so I’m a little nervous, but luckily the roads aren’t busy.</p><p>“When do you start work?”</p><p>“Next month, so a little over two weeks left.”</p><p>If noona moves into the residence, then on weekdays it’ll just be me and Yu-na, who’s off work.</p><p>We always went outside to make love because we were worried about being too loud, even when no one was home. But since noona and I did it there without any complaints, it seems like the neighbors are out during the day too.</p><p>‘If Yu-na’s off, then every morning to afternoon it’ll just be the two of us… like newlyweds.’</p><p>I definitely wasn’t making any expression, but noona pinched my cheek lightly and said,</p><p>“Looks like just thinking about it feels good, huh? Yeah? Now that the nuisance is gone~.”</p><p>“W-what are you talking about… I wasn’t imagining anything!”</p><p>“Ugh, you.”</p><p>Is she a mind reader or something? As expected, I can’t handle noona.</p><p>“Come over sometimes.”</p><p>“Huh?”</p><p>“Say you have plans and come stay the night here. There are plenty of motels nearby.”</p><p>“Yeah. I will.”</p><p>I’m focused on driving, so I can’t look over, but I can sense the faint trembling in noona’s voice—like she’s trying to hide her longing.</p><p>“We should go on a family vacation before I start work.”</p><p>“Huh? Family vacation?”</p><p>“Yeah, last year Ji-hyeon was a senior in high school and Mom was too busy with work, so we couldn’t go. But this year we should be able to. Ji-hyeon might not be able to make it, so we should go before the family gets any bigger.”</p><p>‘Vacation…’</p><p>I think we went a few times when I was really young, but those memories are fuzzy, and after the divorce, we never went even once.</p><p>“Didn’t you go to the beach or on school trips at Hunter Training Academy? Did you really just train nonstop for years?”</p><p>My sister spoke in a slightly pitiful tone.</p><p>“Ah, Ba-da’s gone.”</p><p>“Right? How can someone only train all the time? Even if everyone’s in the same year, they’re all different ages. They should go out and have fun sometimes. Where’d he go?”</p><p>“Uh… it was an uninhabited island, but it wasn’t for fun. We were on a boat, then they threw us all into the sea and made us swim to the island.”</p><p>“Swim?”</p><p>“Yeah. It was a man-to-man combat training exercise on the island. But it wasn’t dangerous. If someone got too tired to keep going or got hypothermia, the instructors would grab them by the hair and pull them out. Of course, they’d get points deducted for that.”</p><p>“···.”</p><p>“I never dropped out once. I wasn’t first, but I still made it to the top ranks, built a hiding spot in the grass, and waited comfortably for the right moment to hunt.”</p><p>“···.”</p><p>My sister silently patted my shoulder.</p><p>“Let’s definitely go next time. What you did was survival training. That’s not… that’s not Ba-da, Seong-hyeon.”</p><p>“Yeah…”</p><p>Every time I talk about my trainee days, I realize how insane those trainings were—things I’d taken for granted back then now seem horrifying in hindsight.</p><p>Except for man-to-man combat, I’d never ranked outside the top in any subject.</p><p>Unlike battles against monsters, which were direct clashes of strength, man-to-man combat was pure psychological warfare.</p><p>Whoever ran out of patience first would lose. In other words, as long as the power gap between me and my opponent wasn’t insurmountable, I could win. That’s what the instructor had said.</p><p>‘Easier said than done.’</p><p>The only time I’d ever overpowered Hye-na on that uninhabited island was my one and only victory against someone stronger than me.</p><p>The next year, when we went back to the island, Hye-na had become so strong that I didn’t even dare challenge her.</p><p>‘Still, my record’s 1 win, 0 losses.’</p><p>Of course, every night, Hye-na dragged me away, beat me up until I couldn’t rest—but that was practice, not a match, so it didn’t count.</p><p>As graduation approached, I received a scouting offer from the Hunter Central Headquarters’ Intelligence Investigation Bureau, but I refused outright.</p><p>Intelligence Investigation Bureau</p><p>It was known for handling classified information and was also where black ops missions were planned.</p><p>They called it an investigation bureau, but it was basically a den of human butchers.</p><p>They primarily recruited Hunters excelling in man-to-man combat. Once hired, those Hunters would track down and arrest—or sometimes kidnap or assassinate—wanted Hunters faster than the police. Or they’d be sent abroad as operatives to carry out all sorts of dirty, underhanded work.</p><p>Even though there were other trainees with better man-to-man combat scores than me, I’d been the first to receive a scouting offer. Curious, I’d asked why.</p><p>“I’ve seen you in sparring training a few times. You’re quick and underhanded. The way you spend the whole time hunting for your opponent’s weaknesses and exploiting them left quite an impression… You’re exactly the kind of talent the Investigation Bureau needs.”</p><p>“···.”</p><p>“And I like that you don’t have family. Same as me—this line of work… is a lot easier when you’ve got nothing to lose.”</p><p>He didn’t hesitate before refusing.</p><p>Now he understood why he’d avoided the Association’s central headquarters all this time.</p><p>Coming here dredged up the memories he’d locked away on a shelf deep in his chest, memories he’d rather forget.</p><p>***</p><p>“Yes, Yoon Na-hyeon’s internship has been confirmed. You can move your things in right now.”</p><p>“Thank you!”</p><p>“So, should we move your stuff now?”</p><p>“Yeah!”</p><p>Watching his sister look around the central headquarters with wide, curious eyes filled him with joy and excitement.</p><p>The dorm was cleaner and more spacious than he’d expected.</p><p>There were two rooms in a single-occupancy dorm, complete with an air conditioner and air purifier.</p><p>‘Wow, this is nicer than my place.’</p><p>“I’ve moved all the essentials.”</p><p>“Mhm.”</p><p>Knock knock</p><p>Just then, someone knocked on the door.</p><p>‘Sis is still organizing, so I’ll get it.’</p><p>When he opened the door, an old friend stood before him.</p><p>“Hey!”</p><p>“Huh?”</p><p>This wasn’t the tomboyish figure he remembered—always in workout clothes, sword in hand, challenging people to sparring matches left and right. Instead, here was Kang Hye-na, dressed in a sharp suit, with two women who looked like aides trailing behind her. She was now a high-ranking executive in the Association.</p><p>“It’s been years, hasn’t it? Only ever hear from you when you need something.”</p><p>“You… really made it, huh.”</p><p>“Seong-hyeon, who’s this? Huh? Wha—”</p><p>His sister, having finished organizing, approached the shoe rack—only to freeze when she saw Hye-na’s face.</p><p>“You must be Seong-hyeon’s sister! You’ve had your hands full with this little brother of yours, haven’t you?”</p><p>Hye-na grinned mischievously and turned to look at him.</p><p>“Ah, hello! Director! I’m Yoon Na-hyeon, the new intern!”</p><p>His sister greeted her loudly and bowed at a perfect 90 degrees.</p><p>“Today I’m just here as Seong-hyeon’s friend, so no need for that!”</p><p>Hye-na flustered, stepped inside, and took his sister’s hands in hers.</p><p>“Yes, ma’am!”</p><p>‘Noona’s really cracked down on discipline.’</p><p>Noona was staring at me with trembling pupils, as if she wanted to say something.</p><p>“Want to grab a coffee?”</p><p>“Aren’t you busy?”</p><p>“I’ve got 30 minutes. Noona, can I borrow Seong-hyeon for 30 minutes?”</p><p>“Of course! You can take him for the whole day if you want!”</p><p>“Thank you. I’ll finish up and meet you at the first-floor café. You know where it is, right?”</p><p>“Yeah.”</p><p>Once Hye-na left, Noona tugged at my collar.</p><p>“Seong-hyeon…”</p><p>“Huh? What’s wrong, Noona? Are you hurt somewhere?”</p><p>Noona’s face had gone pale.</p><p>“Don’t tell me… you called Director Kang Hye-na about my dorm issue? You didn’t, right?”</p><p>“…”</p><p>‘Ah.’</p><p>Now that I thought about it, Hye-na and I were friends, but to Noona, she was a distant superior at work.</p><p>It probably wasn’t a huge deal, but if the two aides who had been behind us spread any rumors, Noona would definitely be labeled as a parachute appointment.</p><p>“Why… aren’t you answering?”</p><p>“I’ll explain everything at the café.”</p><p>“Promise me, okay?”</p><p>“Don’t worry, I’ll go now. Just wait here.”</p><p>“Now that I think about it… my room was way too nice…”</p><p>‘At Noona’s level, she’s not a parachute—she’s more like an air assault troop.’</p><p>I patted Noona’s shoulder and left the room.</p><p>***</p><p>When I arrived at the café, Hye-na had already ordered coffee and was staring out the window.</p><p>Even after I stood in front of her, she kept looking outside.</p><p>“What are you spacing out at?”</p><p>“Just seeing you after so long brought back old memories.”</p><p>“Old memories? From when we were trainees?”</p><p>“Yeah, those were the days.”</p><p>“You serious? Just thinking about it gives me chills.”</p><p>Hye-na let out a snort and turned to me.</p><p>“I heard you’ve been taking rookies through Gates lately. Getting ready for a full comeback?”</p><p>“Nah, I just went a few times to make some money. Comeback? I’d just be rusty deadweight by now.”</p><p>“You… I beat you once, you know. Just that one time, but still.”</p><p>“What? Finally admitting it?”</p><p>“Yeah, but now that I admit it, I’m kinda pissed. Wanna go down and spar?”</p><p>“If I spar with you right now… I’ll die.”</p><p>Talking with Kang Hye-na and reminiscing about old times was fun. Some of the memories that surfaced weren’t all bad—it wasn’t just a string of horrors.</p><p>“Oh, right. Do you know Hunter Kim Min-ah?”</p><p>“Min-ah noona? Of course. She was on my team back when I was recklessly running B-rank gates. How’s she doing?”</p><p>“Yeah, I saw her for the first time recently, and she asked if we were really friends.”</p><p>“…?”</p><p>“More precisely, she said that back when we were on the same team, you told her we were friends, but she couldn’t believe you actually had a friend. Which, fair.”</p><p>“Coming from *her* of all people, that pisses me off.”</p><p>Kim Min-ah was an A-rank Hunter. Back when we were on the same team, she was B-rank like me.</p><p>If you’re in the Hunter world, you’d know her name just from hearing it. And if you didn’t, the description “You know, that crazy bitch from the Incheon branch” would make it click for any Hunter.</p><p>‘If it weren’t for her temper, she would’ve hit A-rank way sooner.’</p><p>“Huh? I should get going soon.”</p><p>Kang Hye-na checked the time, gathered her things, and stood up.</p><p>“Yeah. Oh, right—if you run into her, don’t act like you know me. You get it? If an intern greets the director—”</p><p>“Ah, I know, I know. The two people who came with me today aren’t the type to go around blabbing about that stuff.”</p><p>“Good.”</p><p>“Yoon Seong-hyeon.”</p><p>“Yeah?”</p><p>“Come by sometimes… Everyone around me is either going civilian, retiring, or dying… I don’t have many people left. It’s lonely.”</p><p>“Yeah, I’ll stop by. Hurry up, you’re gonna be late.”</p><p>“Okay.”</p><p>As she left, the lingering regret in Kang Hye-na’s retreating figure made her seem somehow forlorn.</p><p>***</p><p>“Seriously? You’re sure you said it right?”</p><p>“I told you, it’s fine. Don’t worry about it.”</p><p>On the way home, Noona only calmed down after I told her I was okay ten times over.</p><p>“When do you have to return the car?”</p><p>“Tomorrow afternoon is fine.”</p><p>“Oh? Then… let’s go for a drive. Let’s get back before sunset.”</p><p>“Huh? Anywhere in particular?”</p><p>“Anywhere’s fine~.”</p><p>Noona seemed to be in a good mood—she rolled down the window, let the wind blow through, and smiled.</p>
