<p>The Milaris Manor was quiet.</p>
<p>Located just beyond the central market of Lustburg, it was not an ostentious establishment of luxury and decadence, but one built with purpose. It had no high towers or gemstone gates. Just clean architecture, solid enchantments, and the faint scent of silk and incense.</p>
<p>Inside her private workshop, Arachne Milaris was working with utmost focus.</p>
<p>She showed no signs of stopping, no signs of faltering with her work.</p>
<p>The enchanted threads moved as they always did— slow, precise, without wasted motion. The loom hummed, guided by her magic. Nearby, sketches floated mid-air, adjusting themselves whenever she gave a silent nod.</p>
<p>Arachne Milaris was a woman born with a supreme talent for art. A talent that had somehow dulled after all those years since she closed her heart. However, now, her heart was blazing again as she poured everything into the clothes she was preparing.</p>
<p>On the main table lay the ceremonial suit of Sol Luxuria.</p>
<p>She had been at it for days. Adjusting the fabric weight, recalibrating mana conductivity, and modifying defensive layers. She knew that Sol did not need those adjustments. Even if she were to become a King, there was no way she could create a defensive manifestation that was stronger than his own body.</p>
<p>However, she did not care.</p>
<p>Everything needed to be perfect. It was not just formal wear. It was symbolic armor— something that would represent both the King and the man himself.</p>
<p>She stopped for a moment, brushing a hand down the chestplate lining.</p>
<p>Her thoughts, uninvited, drifted.</p>
<p>Mars Luxuria.</p>
<p>Sol’s father.</p>
<p>The only man she had given her heart to. Tall. Handsome. Reliable to a fault and yet infinitely clumsy when it came to dealing with women.</p>
<p>Not charming like Sol. Not flirtatious. Not complicated.</p>
<p>Just focused.</p>
<p>He loved only one person— Blaze. And he never once gave Arachne, or any of the other women who loved him, a second look. Not out of cruelty, just honesty. In a way, it was quite admirable. After all, the women chasing him had all been of the highest pedigree. However, his love was steadfast.</p>
<p>She had accepted it long ago. Despite her obsession with him, she knew that she never had a chance.</p>
<p>Still, as she worked on Sol’s suit, those memories could not help but resurface. Not with bitterness. Just with the dull weight of something left behind but never buried. His memories lived forever in her heart.</p>
<p>“Stubborn old bastard,” she muttered, her eyes glimmering with unshed tears.</p>
<p>Mars would have never liked the suit she was making. Too flashy. Too delicate. He preferred thick armor and utilitarian function. Yet Sol was not Mars. Nor would he ever be. He needed something that balanced power with symbolism.</p>
<p>And for once, Arachne wanted to go beyond just delivering quality.</p>
<p>She wanted Sol to stand tall. To look like someone worth following. Someone even Mars would be forced to acknowledge. This was the only gift she could give him.</p>
<p>The suit was nearly done now. Just a few final stitches.</p>
<p>And maybe, once she saw Sol walk down that aisle, wearing what she made, smiling like a proper man – not as prince, nor as the Son of the Hero King – but just a man …</p>
<p>Maybe that would be enough for her to close that old chapter.</p>
<p>A knock came at the door.</p>
<p>Her butler and contracted partner stepped in, standing at attention. A nightmare crow. He had been her staunchest ally for all these years. A friend she could rely on, even as she nearly sank into madness because of a love that would never be.</p>
<p>“Mistress Milaris. His Majesty has arrived.” His gaze was gentle, and she could see the worry in his eyes.</p>
<p>She nodded. “Good. Tell the apprentices and the servants to clear out. I’ll handle this one myself.”</p>
<p>“Yes, Mistress.”</p>
<p>As the butler left, Arachne made one last check on the cuffs, brushing off invisible dust.</p>
<p>She did not love Sol. She was sure she could never love him.</p>
<p>But she respected him. And she cared for him. Not just because he was Mars' son. But because he was someone worthy of respect.</p>
<p>This was not just tailoring.</p>
<p>This was her way of saying goodbye to the past and welcoming a new future.</p>
