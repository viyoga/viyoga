<p>Watching the scene while sitting on her throne, Gabriel couldn't help the warm smile that appeared on her face.</p>
<p>Such a sight was really gratifying.</p>
<p>Though divine beasts were loyal to the goddesses, It wasn't some blind worship.</p>
<p>In fact, the goddesses never tried to act high and mighty in front of those who really knew them. This was also one of the reasons why most Holy Daughters, initially filled with staunch fervor, would become disillusioned once they became Supreme Daughters.</p>
<p>When Lady Luxuria had asked for Isis to form a contract with Sol, she had been pretty displeased though she complied.</p>
<p>Thankfully, now it seemed that Isis would willingly follow him. All that mattered now was the choice he would make.</p>
<p>'I guess he will soon leave.'</p>
<p>Thinking about this, she couldn't help but feel a little lost.</p>
<p>Those few days had been pretty fulfilling for her. Even though all she did was just watch, It had been a long time since she had so much fun.</p>
<p>The greatest enemy for people at her level was boredom. It was one thing for an Innate demi-god like her, but for mortals who reached that level, she knew that more than half of them either went crazy or killed themselves because they could not support the eternity that was opened to them.</p>
<p>A great part of the other half would pass their time sleeping and only a very small number of them were still active.</p>
<p>This was the sorrow of mortals. Even after they became demi-god, the divinity in them was usually too low to allow a complete change in cognition. As long as they kept the subjective time of mortal while living as immortal, all that would result in was sorrow.</p>
<p>For divine beasts like them, they were already born as near immortal even without becoming demigods. As such, they couldn’t avoid boredom, but it wouldn’t lead to suicidal tendencies.</p>
<p>‘I wonder if I will see him again.’</p>
<p>She didn't think she would find anything as entertaining as this any time soon. From the little she managed to decypher, Sol was an anomaly of the highest degree born from thousands of years of machination.</p>
<p>Since the Goddesses existed outside of time, they were able to observe all possible futures, past and present. But time was like a never-ending stream divided into trillions of possibilities. There was a limit to how far they could see and how many possible futures they could see.</p>
<p>If a goddess forcibly broke the limit, she would receive backlash from the law of causality set by the Supreme Mother, the Mother goddess of Order. Even for goddesses, such a price was not a small matter.</p>
<p>She could more or less guess what Lady Luxuria wished to accomplish, but this made her even more confused.</p>
<p>In the end, she sighed.</p>
<p>‘Why should I bother trying to pry in the mind of the goddesses?’</p>
<p>It wasn’t as if she could do anything even if she found the answer. In fact, she may even have more problems.</p>
<p>‘Well then, who else should I observe?’</p>
<p>Screens appeared in front of her as she pondered and in the end, she chose the one showing the discussion between Nefertiti and Nent.</p>
<p>At first, she was simply listening to them without much interest, but, when the mention of a true name came, she immediately paid her full attention.</p>
<p>Aside from anomalies like Tiamat who had both a Dimension and a Territory, or Asmodeus that could bestow power to mortals, the divine beasts could be said to be at a more or less equal level. Be it in terms of personal power or the power of their children.</p>
<p>As such, one more king would mean that Gabriel could pull ahead of them. So she was indeed happy, but</p>
<p>“What a powerful name.”</p>
<p>Some names had power, a weight, a legend behind them. By receiving those names, be it at birth or during the ascension of King level, people obtained a destiny and would be pulled by fate.</p>
<p>She did not know where those legends came from. But she could feel them and feel the weight behind them. In fact, all divine beasts could.</p>
<p>This was why they were very careful when choosing a name for their children. Since this would mean bestowing a fate to them.</p>
<p>Of course, there were always some outliers.</p>
<p>Some of them, despite possessing legendary names were able to break through the constraints of fate and follow their own road, the little monkey, Sun Wukong was such an example.</p>
<p>Some others, despite possessing powerless names, were also able to break into the flow of fate and become powerful. Blaze was such an example.</p>
<p>Those people were called Singularity. Existences that were a headache even for the goddesses since reading the future around them was basically impossible without using some trickery like reading the future of those close to them. Even then, the results were often failures.</p>
<p>But in Gabriel’s opinion, those singularities were not a problem. By breaking from fate, they made the possibility of becoming a demigod far more complicated. This was even more so since the act was done unintentionally.</p>
<p>The truly scary singularities were those who became aware of the fate binding them and manipulated the weight of their names to accelerate their growth, becoming frightening existences.</p>
<p>For such existences, even the aloof goddesses gave them some measure of respect as they did not fall short of the divine beasts and even surpassed them.</p>
<p>Thankfully, in all history, there were only three such existences.</p>
<p>The first was Anubis, the Necromancer King, and the first necromancer.</p>
<p>The second was Ambrosia, the Thousand Spells Witch, and the first witch.</p>
<p>The third and final one was Echidna, Mother and Thousand Monsters, and creator of the Chimera.</p>
<p>“Mother, can you hear me? I can feel you observing us.”</p>
<p>Gabriel was brought back from her daze by Nent’s voice.</p>
<p>Looking back, she could see Nent raising her head toward the ceiling,</p>
<p>“Mother-”</p>
<p>Nent continued a slight tremor in her voice.</p>
<p>“-I wasn’t wrong.”</p>
<p>Gabriel showed a bitter smile.</p>
<p>How could she not feel it?</p>
<p>The relief, the joy, the resentment, and so many more complex emotions that were hidden in those short words.</p>
<p>Was Nent’s choice right?</p>
<p>Gabriel did not think so. She knew very well what had happened. How could she have missed the thread of fates that went haywire the moment Sol, Nefertiti, and Isis met?</p>
<p>Even more so, she could feel that Nefertiti’s Avatar name was related to that of Isis.</p>
<p>But was her daughter wrong?</p>
<p>It was hard to say. The truth of the matter was that three hybrids with superior genes met and each of them were indeed very talented.</p>
<p>She wanted to tell the truth, inform her daughter about the reality. But, in the end, she sighed.</p>
<p>“Congratulations my dear daughter. I am proud of you.”</p>
<p>Gabriel did not know if what she was doing was right. The best would have been to say that everything was a result of fate.</p>
<p>But, watching the faint tears of happiness gathering in the corner of her daughter’s eyes, she felt no regret.</p>
