<p>Xavier had expected the conjuration lectures to take place in a classroom. Checking the schedule board, however, he discovered the session was set for the practical grounds—close to, yet distinct from, the field used for advanced swordsmanship.</p>
<p>Upon arriving, he stood beneath a nearby tree while the other students waited for the professor. A young man of the same year approached him.</p>

<p>A middle-aged man with streaks of grey in his hair strode onto the field, dressed in a simple white shirt and black trousers, a golden monocle resting over his left eye.</p>
<p>"Gather up, students. I am Professor Draven, and today we will perform magical-beast summoning," he announced, clapping his hands twice.</p>
<p>Once the class had assembled, he explained the fundamentals and rules of summoning non-bound beasts, a core branch of conjuration.</p>
<p>"For a non-bound summon, simply draw this symbol on the ground, stand in its center, and channel your mana," Draven instructed, tracing the mark with his foot. "Close your eyes. When you sense a connection forming within your consciousness, draw it toward you. After that we proceed to the binding ceremony."</p>
<p>Students began sketching the symbol; Xavier and Allen did the same.</p>
<p>"Wait patiently for the link. Once it appears, treat it gently," the professor added.</p>
<p>Xavier stood on his completed mark without yet pouring mana into it, watching as others summoned beasts one after another. A girl called forth an owl with glowing wings ; another produced a massive lizard. Many brought forth various magical birds, while the rest summoned creatures of every size and shape.</p>
<p>Beside him, Allen succeeded in summoning a majestic blue-furred wolf nearly as tall as himself.</p>
<p>Xavier simply observed, captivated by the creatures materializing from thin air.</p>
<p>"Once your beasts appear," Draven continued, "prick your finger and let a drop of blood fall onto the symbol beneath your feet. If the beast answers in kind, the contract is sealed and you may summon it thereafter without the mark."</p>
<p>Students complied. Some bonds formed successfully; others were rejected, the beasts vanishing beyond their control.</p>
<p>"Haha, don’t worry—you can always try again," Draven laughed brightly, encouraging those who had failed.</p>
