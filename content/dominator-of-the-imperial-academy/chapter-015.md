<p>Xavier changed into casual clothes in his room, then met Elena on the fourth floor where the stairs ended.</p>
<p>"Do you notice something odd here?" Elena asked.</p>
<p>Xavier scanned the area. Wall-mounted lamps lined both sides of the stairwell in pairs—except the last one on the level where they stood.</p>
<p>"This lamp?" he asked.</p>
<p>"Exactly. It stands out. There must be something here." Elena examined the lamp closely.</p>
<p>It was the only one that wasn’t working. No mana crystal sat in its holder. The lamps used mana crystals instead of bulbs; raw power made them glow with white light.</p>
<p>Elena twisted and turned the lamp. It didn’t move.</p>
<p>"Try this." Xavier pulled a mana crystal from the neighboring lamp and handed it to her.</p>
<p>She set the crystal into the empty holder. The lamp lit up. She twisted it again. This time it gave way, turning like a doorknob. The floor under their feet rose as the ceiling opened.</p>
<p>When the floor stopped, they stood on a hidden fifth floor. No rooms—just one huge hall furnished like a private chamber. Luxury sofas and wooden desks sat under a layer of dust. In the center of the room a fire burned.</p>
<p>Xavier and Elena walked closer. The flames fed on fresh wood, sharp contrast to everything old around them.</p>
<p>"There." Xavier pointed at the ceiling directly above the fire. A magical circle marked the stone.</p>
<p>"What do you think it is? A rune or an enchantment?" Elena asked.</p>
<p>"If it’s a rune, we only need to find its trigger to see what it does." Xavier looked around the room. "There have to be clues somewhere."</p>
<p>They searched thoroughly—neither of them knew exactly what they were looking for. Elena worked through the bookshelves. Xavier checked every corner, behind paintings, under the sofas.</p>
<p>"Xavier, take a look at this." Elena called him over when she couldn’t open the drawer of the main desk.</p>
<p>"Looks locked," Xavier said after trying it himself.</p>
<p>"Do we need to find a key now?" Elena asked.</p>
<p>Xavier’s patience ran out.</p>
<p>This desk doesn’t look that important, he thought, and yanked the drawer hard. The wood splintered.</p>
<p>"Such a brute," Elena said, staring at the diary resting inside.</p>
<p>She lifted it out and flipped through the pages.</p>
<p>"Xavier, this is big. It looks like the personal diary of the first emperor." Her hands trembled under the weight of the book.</p>
<p>"His handwriting is pretty shit," Xavier said, glancing over her shoulder.</p>
<p>"It might have all the answers to our questions." Elena turned more pages. The diary was thick—more than a thousand pages—covering different parts of the emperor’s life, scrambled without order.</p>
<p>"It will take time to dig out anything useful," Elena said.</p>
<p>"Let’s take it and go back for now."</p>
<p>They returned to the spot where the floor had risen. Elena turned the lamp again. The floor descended. Xavier pulled the mana crystal from the holder.</p>
<p>"With this, only we can reach the fifth floor."</p>
<p>"I’m going to read this and see if it has anything useful," Elena said, then headed to her room.</p>
<p>It was around midnight. Xavier went back to his room, dropped onto the bed, and sleep took him before he fully settled.</p>
<p>The next morning Xavier attended one of the academy’s fixed lectures. All five hundred freshmen gathered in a large hall. Elevated seats formed a semicircle around the central podium.</p>
<p>He saw Melli sitting alone on the first bench again. He greeted her with "Good morning" and sat next to her. She looked happy to see him—her only friend.</p>
<p>Professor Woodburn entered in his usual black robes.</p>
<p>"Good morning, students. Since there’s no need for introductions, let’s begin our lecture on the basics of diplomacy." He started reviewing the year’s syllabus.</p>
<p>Five minutes in, a hurried voice came from the doors.</p>
<p>"May I come in, Professor?" Elena stood there with clear dark circles under her eyes.</p>
<p>"You are late, Miss Rank 1. It doesn’t do well for first impressions." Woodburn’s face showed open disappointment. "Go on. Make sure this doesn’t become a habit."</p>
<p>"Yes, Professor. It won’t happen again." Elena apologized and sat next to Xavier.</p>
<p>"Were you reading the diary all night?" Xavier whispered.</p>
<p>"Yes. Let’s talk about it later." Elena turned her attention back to the professor.</p>
<p>The lecture was heavy—pure theory, more memorization than understanding. After four long hours it finally ended. Woodburn left a hall of exhausted students.</p>
<p>Xavier and Elena went to the cafeteria. Melli joined them.</p>
<p>"Have you heard about the secret expedition tonight?" Elena asked while they talked.</p>
<p>"What’s this about?" Xavier asked, feeling left out.</p>
<p>"A third-year senior from Thornveil is organizing a secret expedition. A small group of students will enter the main building tonight near midnight, when the hallways shift," Elena said.</p>
<p>"Once a month, on the night of the full moon, the hallways align and open a path the principal has strictly forbidden. Some students think the academy is hiding something," Melli added.</p>
<p>"Would you like to join?" Elena asked Melli.</p>
<p>"Yes. It sounds interesting."</p>
<p>"Then meet us at the roundabout at 11:30 p.m."</p>
<p>"Umm, where’s my invite?" Xavier asked.</p>
<p>"I thought you had no interest in things like this," Elena said.</p>
<p>"I don’t. But if both of you are going, I’m coming too." He invited himself.</p>
<p>After lunch they had no more lectures, so they returned to the dorms and parted from Melli at the roundabout.</p>
<p>"So, what did you find in the diary?" Xavier asked. He knew Elena had avoided the topic in front of Melli. She still didn’t trust her with it.</p>
<p>"We have a problem," Elena said. "The book is full of riddles. The only one I could make sense of says we need a sword with a fire element, then stick it into the fire to move forward."</p>
<p>"That sounds troublesome, but somehow it makes sense," Xavier said.</p>
<p>The difference between normal and elemental swords was clear: elemental swords could only be conjured. Elena had no talent for conjuration, so the work fell to him.</p>
<p>"We haven’t been taught that far yet," Xavier said.</p>
<p>"We can’t wait for lectures. Figure it out on your own time." Elena patted his shoulder. "You can do it. I trust you."</p>
<p>They reached Emberfell dorms. Elena went to her room on the third floor.</p>
<p>When Xavier opened his door, he found Fiona in his room waiting for him in her maid uniform.</p>
<p>"Good afternoon, Xavier. Lord Drakenfell has sent a letter for you." She held out a letter sealed with dragon sigils.</p>
