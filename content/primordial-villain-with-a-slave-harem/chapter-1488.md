<p>Velara spoke. Her voice was steady despite the drain.</p>
<p>"By the blood of the faithful and the will of the divine, I command the corruption beneath this ground to be unmade. Return to the void. You are not welcome in the Goddess's domain."</p>
<p>The light exploded downward.</p>
<p>It punched through the stone platform, through the cobblestones, through the foundations of the city itself. Golden radiance sank into the earth like water into sand, spreading outward in every direction, racing beneath streets and buildings and walls.</p>
<p>From below came the screeching.</p>
<p>Dozens of voices. Hundreds. Then thousands. The thin, ragged shrieks of undead minions caught in holy fire, their corrupted forms dissolving as the light tore through the tunnel networks beneath Whisperfield. The sound rose through the stone and into the square, loud enough to make children cry and soldiers flinch.</p>
<p>Then it faded.</p>
<p>Silence.</p>
<p>The light dimmed. The artifact went dark. The scripture on its surface was spent, the carvings cracked and blackened.</p>
<p>Velara swayed.</p>
<p>Aldren caught her arm and steadied her with a firm hand. "The city owes you a debt, Arch Priestess."</p>
<p>She said nothing. Her eyes stayed on the ground, on the stone beneath which the tunnels now sat purified.</p>
<p>She hated the undead. More than duty required. More than faith demanded. The scar on her face was a reminder she carried willingly.</p>
<p>Aldren turned back to the crowd.</p>
<p>The fear was still there. But it had shifted. The ground beneath their feet was clean. One threat had been removed.</p>
<p>"Thanks to the sacrifice of the frontier cities," Aldren continued, his voice rising, "information has begun to travel. The suppression artifacts are losing hold. Reinforcements are on the way."</p>
<p>He gripped the railing of the platform.</p>
<p>"The undead will no longer push through our ground. We need only hold these walls for a brief time before Duke Tharion's armies sweep in and crush the invaders where they stand."</p>
<p>His voice hardened into iron.</p>
<p>"So fight, my soldiers! Defend your homes! Defend your loved ones! Defend humanity!"</p>
<p>He drew his sword and raised it above his head.</p>
<p>"For King Alexios! For the Goddess!"</p>
<p>The square erupted.</p>
<p>The roar that came back was enormous. A hundred thousand voices crashing against the walls. Soldiers beat swords against shields. Civilians stamped their feet. The sound rolled through the streets and did not stop.</p>
<p>Women pushed forward through the crowd, pulling their sleeves up, demanding to know where the supplies needed to be carried. Elderly men hobbled toward the quartermaster stations with tools in hand, offering skills honed over lifetimes. Teenagers hauled sandbags. Children carried water.</p>
<p>The city buzzed.</p>
<p>Every street, every corner, every rooftop filled with motion. Whisperfield was going to fight with everything it had, down to the last grandmother with a kitchen knife and the last boy with a bucket of pitch.</p>
<p>Aldren watched from the platform as his city mobilized.</p>
<p>For a moment, he allowed himself to believe they could hold.</p>
<p>Then a boom shattered the sky.</p>
<p>Deep. Singular. Every head in the square snapped toward the eastern battlements.</p>
<p>"Have they begun the siege?!" a captain shouted from the wall.</p>
<p>Aldren was already moving. He reached the eastern battlement in seconds, pushing past soldiers scrambling to their positions, and looked out over the field.</p>
<p>The dwarven engines had not fired.</p>
<p>The elven lines had not advanced.</p>
<p>The bombardment had not begun.</p>
<p>Aldren squinted.</p>
<p>In the distance, above the tree line, a lone figure hovered in the sky.</p>
<p>No wings. No platform. No mount. A man standing on nothing, arms at his sides, as if the air itself had agreed to hold him there. The wind moved around him. His coat stirred. At this distance, his features were indistinct, but his silhouette was unmistakable.</p>
<p>Then the figure moved.</p>
<p>His arms spread slowly to his sides, palms open, fingers loose. For a moment, he hung there like that, suspended, a man surrendering to the sky.</p>
<p>His coat caught fire.</p>
<p>It started at the hem. Red flames licked upward along the fabric, consuming it in a crawl that accelerated as it climbed. The fire ate through his sleeves, across his shoulders, down his chest. Within seconds, every thread of his clothing was burning, and the flames were not orange or yellow.</p>
<p>They were red. Deep, arterial red. The color of blood held up to firelight.</p>
<p>Aldren's soldiers froze on the battlements.</p>
<p>The flames intensified. They wrapped around the man's body in coiling ribbons, layering over his torso, his arms, his legs, his hands. Where the fire touched, it hardened, solidified into something dense and black that swallowed the light around it.</p>
<p>Armor.</p>
<p>It formed piece by piece, plate by plate, as if the fire itself was being forged in real time around his body. Chest plate first, angular and sharp. Gauntlets next. Greaves followed, then boots, then pauldrons that rose above his shoulders in aggressive lines.</p>
<p>The flames climbed his neck.</p>
<p>They closed over his jaw. His mouth. His nose. His eyes.</p>
<p>A helmet sealed around his skull. Smooth, featureless, black as a starless night. No visor. No slits. No ornamentation. A face erased.</p>
<p>Then two points of light ignited where his eyes had been.</p>
<p>Red and wrong. They did not glow like fire or magic or any light source Aldren had ever seen. They burned inward, as if pulling the world into them rather than casting anything out. Staring into them felt like staring into a place that stared back.</p>
<p>In reality, the transformation took less than a single second, even if it felt like a whole minute had passed for those who observed from within the city's walls.</p>
<p>Where a man had floated, a thing now stood in the sky. Black armor from crown to sole, seamless, radiating a pressure that reached the city even from this distance. The red orbs fixed on the barrier.</p>
<p>Aldren's grip tightened on the battlement wall.</p>
<p>The elves were accounted for. The dwarves were accounted for. The undead, purged from below, were accounted for.</p>
<p>But the Anomaly.</p>
<p>The Primordial Villain was here, too.</p>
<p>And he was on the side of the enemy.</p>
<p>The count swallowed.</p>
<p>"Goddess help us."</p>
