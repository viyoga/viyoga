<p>Ravenshade Palace stood tall beneath a gray sky with its masterfully constructed spires rising like watchful silhouettes above the capital. The wards etched into its walls hummed with steady power, unchallenged for millennia. Inside, the atmosphere was controlled and restrained.</p>
<p>Tharion Ravenshade stood near the long table in his private strategy chamber with a thin crystal slate resting in his hand. Lines of sigils scrolled slowly across its surface as he reviewed the latest field report.</p>
<p>One of his most loyal and accomplished generals, who was trusted with 50,000 Ravenshade soldiers and ordered to help bring the end of the Vesper Consortium, had written it plainly, without flourish.</p>
<p>The army had linked up successfully with Tharion's sister, Queen Morgana, and her Elite Guards. Alongside the queen came the Scarlet Lilies, the five, well, four now due to Scar's death, Adamantite adventurers led by Lilith Ravenshade herself, Tharion's other sister. Furthermore, the Fujimori hit squad joined as well, consisting of some of the Silverwind duchy's finest, led by Lady Kaede herself.</p>
<p>Together with the Ravenshade army, they had begun the siege of Vesper Consortium strongholds, as Tharion ordered.</p>
<p>His eyes moved steadily across the text.</p>
<p>No notable resistance encountered.</p>
<p>No delays.</p>
<p>No casualties worth reporting.</p>
<p>Barriers had fallen under coordinated pressure. Morgana's brutal elemental firepower, Lilith's precision, and Kaede's unnervingly eerie strikes, still a mystery that Tharion desired to understand properly, overlapped cleanly. The report noted, almost as an aside, that not a single cannon had been fired so far, such was the might of the ladies and their allies.</p>
<p>Before they could prepare the cannons for a siege, it was already over.</p>
<p>Three fortresses had already fallen.</p>
<p>Tharion lowered the slate and allowed himself a small, contained smile.</p>
<p>"Good," he said quietly. "If my two unhinged sisters and that slit-eyed freak can all work to assist me instead of giving me constant headaches as they always do, it's time for us to bring out the champagne. I'll have to celebrate this once-in-a-lifetime occasion."</p>
<p>The duke seemed very content.</p>
<p>The Consortium had overreached. "I don't know what their plan was… I heard they went looking for allies in the Beastkin Confederation, but they're nowhere to be seen…"</p>
<p>Then his advisor spoke up. "They couldn't predict Alexios' power play to bring about this competition for the entire Greenvale Duchy. Without that, there would be no Fujimori or Ravenshade army to worry about. Furthermore, it was questionable whether Alexios would've made a move at all. There's a good chance he would've only ordered Alastair to take care of his own lands and left it at that. At least, until the syndicate began taking land. But I'm not sure if that was ever among their plans."</p>
<p>"Hmm… I suppose that's true. They drew the short end of the stick. Too many things they couldn't predict happened, turning their odds from acceptable to nightmarish," Tharion nodded.</p>
<p>But, well, it didn't matter if this current situation was the end result of dumb arrogance or sheer lack of luck; what mattered was the outcome, which was grim to say the least.</p>
<p>Then the duke's nonchalant expression turned to one of satisfaction. With this invasion, his obligation to Alexios would be fulfilled to the letter. The agreement had been clear. Support the crown against the Consortium, and Ravenshade would be rewarded.</p>
<p>There was more to be celebrating, however.</p>
<p>Alastair's incompetence had finally caught up to him. Now, the whole world knew of it, not just Tharion himself. Seeing the cost his old rival had to pay for his centuries of negligence, and that of his ancestors, filled the Ravenshade Duke with the sort of happiness no woman could ever provide him with.</p>
<p>The king's trust in that muscle-brained, arrogant mutt was gone. Alastair's standing among the other dukes had eroded. Even his image of strength before his own vassals had cracked. Once seen as an almighty ruler, who single-handedly defended the Vraven Kingdom against the might of the Beastman Confederation, now people could see him having to shamelessly fight for the mere right of being allowed to rule his own lands again.</p>
<p>Alexios had shown him the biggest disrespect a duke has ever received in the history of the kingdom, and it would not be forgotten. Tharion would make sure of that. He already hired numerous chroniclers to ensure history was recorded properly, as it should be.</p>
<p>Tharion turned the slate over in his hand once before setting it down. His expression returned to that of professional governor mode.</p>
<p>"Soon, Ravenshade will stand alone at the top. I will be the one to rule Vraven. Who knew that instead of amassing armies of overwhelming might, diplomacy and intrigue would get me so much farther in life? My ancestors were wrong in their approach."</p>
<p>He shook his head fondly as the thought settled comfortably.</p>
<p>Everything was moving as planned.</p>
<p>That was when the report arrived.</p>
<p>A courier knelt at the edge of the chamber, breath still uneven from haste. The sigil on his cloak marked him as internal intelligence.</p>
<p>The duke remained where he was, not even looking over his shoulder to look at the man as he ordered,</p>
<p>"Speak."</p>
<p>"Yes, My Lord! We lost contact with the border fortresses alongside Elvardian lands," the courier replied.</p>
<p>A pause came. Tharion finally turned around.</p>
<p>"How many?"</p>
<p>"Ten," came the answer. "Simultaneously."</p>
<p>"… Ten?"</p>
<p>The chamber did not erupt into panic or screaming.</p>
<p>Tharion walked over to his seat and leaned back.</p>
<p>At his side, the old man who was his sole trusted advisor, shifted his weight. The soft scrape of his staff against marble flooring was the only sound. His back was bent, his beard white, his eyes still clear.</p>
<p>Tharion turned his head a fraction. "What do you think?"</p>
<p>Halric took his time before answering. "The least troubling possibility is an artifact failure. Though ten synchronization arrays failing at once strains belief."</p>
<p>Tharion did not respond.</p>
<p>"Another option," Halric continued, "is internal unrest. Border towns seeking autonomy. With the resources poured into their defenses over the centuries, some may believe themselves capable of standing alone. That we would decide that it was more trouble than worth to siege our own lands."</p>
<p>Tharion lifted a hand, stopping him.</p>
<p>"I didn't ask you to invent theories you don't believe in. We've known each other too long for that. Stop skirting around like this and say what you think."</p>
<p>Halric's mouth curved. "Fair enough."</p>
<p>"This smells of an invasion," Tharion declared.</p>
<p>Halric nodded once. "You're right."</p>
<p>The old man's grip tightened slightly on his staff. "What troubles me is the silence before it. No troop movements. No supply strain. No buildup we could track. History tells us Elvardia never moves without leaving ripples. This time, there were none."</p>
<p>"Which leaves two real options," Halric went on. "The Covenant of Eternity has decided to show its hand, taking advantage of the Vesper Consortium drawing every eye elsewhere. They might've assumed it was time to use the chaos to their advantage, letting them achieve whatever unholy ambitions their dusty bones might desire."</p>
<p>"Hmm… It's possible. Damn it!" Tharion cursed. "If they turned ten whole settlements into their graveyards, breeding those filthy abominations of theirs, we'll have so many to cull."</p>
<p>"Yes, but I don't believe them to be strong enough to overtake ten strong settlements at once," Halric countered. "The most likely answer is that it's Elvardia again. Another test, like their failed push into our lands months ago."</p>
<p>Tharion nodded slowly, fingers brushing his chin as he considered it. "Maybe so. But this time, they're a lot more invested. The last invasion was a joke compared to making ten fortresses go silent."</p>
<p>"Perhaps the dwarves invented a suppressant artifact. They could be fighting a defensive siege as we speak," the advisor guessed.</p>
<p>"All this theorizing will only get us so far," Tharion decreed and stood.</p>
<p>"We have to organize a force and get to the bottom of this."</p>
