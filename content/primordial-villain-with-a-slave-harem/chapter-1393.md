<p>Quinlan took a slow breath and let his awareness spread. As he did so, he started recognizing faces.</p>
<p>Karl was the first to move closer.</p>
<p>The shapeshifter wore an immaculate black tuxedo, posture straight, shoes polished to a mirror shine. The contrast came from the head perched atop that refined body. A pig's face, pink snout twitching, small eyes sharp with intelligence. He adjusted his cufflinks, then bowed with perfect etiquette.</p>
<p>"Welcome back, son," Karl said smoothly while tipping his fedora.</p>
<p>He punctuated the greeting with a polite nod and an unmistakable oink.</p>
<p>Quinlan smiled. "Still refusing to pick a consistent form, I see."</p>
<p>Karl's tail flicked once behind him. "Consistency is for creatures who fear change."</p>
<p>Then he oinked a second time, averting Miri's and Lumi's gazes, "I only fear women. They are horrible, nasty creatures."</p>
<p>"Khm."</p>
<p>The two women holding Quinlan's hands gently coughed. Despite the delicate nature of their sound, Karl trembled and tipped his fedora a second time before stepping back behind Malakar, using the old man as a shield.</p>
<p>Quinlan chuckled amusedly before his attention shifted again.</p>
<p>A broad figure pushed through the gathering, each step heavy and grounded. The dwarf's presence felt like stone given breath. Thick arms crossed over a chest wrapped in layered leather and metal, beard braided tight with small runic clasps woven through.</p>
<p>"Björn," Quinlan greeted.</p>
<p>The primordial blacksmith grunted in response. "I hope you didn't shame my creation since I last saw you," he decreed. Indeed, the Soul Reaper was the creation of the Primordial Smith. Björn kept walking after Quinlan nodded. The dwarf was not alone.</p>
<p>Another dwarf walked beside him. Just as wide. Just as solid. The beard was longer, braided differently, and for a brief moment, Quinlan's mind stumbled.</p>
<p>Why is there another male dwarf primordial?</p>
<p>Primordials came in pairs.</p>
<p>There was only one dwarven primordial pair.</p>
<p>Then it clicked.</p>
<p>Quinlan forced a careful breath through his nose as understanding settled. The second dwarf was a woman.</p>
<p>Björn gestured to her. "This is my-"</p>
<p>He stopped.</p>
<p>Both dwarves leaned forward.</p>
<p>Their eyes narrowed in unison.</p>
<p>Their attention locked onto Quinlan's chest.</p>
<p>The red flames of Synchra rolled once beneath the armor's surface.</p>
<p>The two immediately began exchanging hushed notes between one another, whispering with a crafter's curiosity glinting in their eyes.</p>
<p>Quinlan wanted to speak more with the pair, but he never got the chance.</p>
<p>Heavy footsteps announced themselves from the far side of the clearing.</p>
<p>A woman emerged dressed in layered hides and thick cloth, colors echoing cold seas and stone cliffs. Her attire carried the shape of a barbarian's garb, practical and worn, yet it fit her aesthetic. Thick muscles lined her arms and shoulders without hiding her form. Her hair was dark and tied back.</p>
<p>Despite her rough, more masculine appearance, she was still a good-looking woman, just needing… An acquired taste to truly appreciate her beauty.</p>
<p>She stopped a short distance away.</p>
<p>Quinlan blinked once.</p>
<p>"Would you happen to be Thyra?" he asked and respectfully introduced himself. "Greetings, my name is Quinlan, the newest primordial and the son of Luminara and Mearie."</p>
<p>He remembered the stories. Malakar and Thyra. The first human pair. The foundation that was laid long before kingdoms learned to name themselves. Despite it being Mearie who stayed on the Iskaris Continent, Thyra had come far earlier.</p>
<p>The woman standing before him was the true mother of humanity. Almost all denizens of Iskaris descended from her and Malakar.</p>
<p>Thyra studied him for several heartbeats.</p>
<p>Then she stepped closer.</p>
<p>Only when she stood directly in front of him did Quinlan realize the full measure of it.</p>
<p>She was tall, extremely so.</p>
<p>Two meters (6'6") tall, to be exact, which meant she was standing eye to eye with Quinlan. She was the first woman who matched his height since his transformation with the Primordial Breeding Physique he unlocked before boning Blossom right after saving her from her slave contract.</p>
<p>Miri stepped forward before Quinlan could shift his weight.</p>
<p>She planted herself between them, shoulders squared, chin lifted as she looked up at the towering woman in front of her. Her fingers tightened around her spear, not raised, but ready. Her stance carried no hesitation.</p>
<p>"I won't let you harm him," Miri said flatly. "Not for some stupid test, and not for tradition. Quinnie is strong and perfect; you don't need to beat him up to know that. You'll take my word for it, is that understood?"</p>
<p>Thyra's gaze dropped to her.</p>
<p>Miri did not flinch. Her defiance was plain in the way her feet stayed rooted, in how her body leaned forward a fraction as if daring the taller woman to try. The size difference meant nothing to her. Quinlan could feel the protective, motherly intent rolling off her back.</p>
<p>Thyra's eyes shifted.</p>
<p>Luminara had stepped up as well, just a half step behind Miri. Her posture mirrored the same readiness. There was no bluff there either. If this turned violent, she would move without pause.</p>
<p>Just as they did when Dragnar tried to attack Quinlan for his own test, the two women were ready to throw down in order to protect their son.</p>
<p>For a long moment, Thyra said nothing.</p>
<p>Then her lips pulled into a wide grin.</p>
<p>"So," she said, voice rough, edged with gravel and wind-worn humor, "the two mother bears have finally found their cub, hm?"</p>
<p>She reached out and placed a broad hand on each of their shoulders at the same time.</p>
<p>"I'm happy for you two."</p>
<p>Miri stiffened in surprise. Luminara blinked, ears flicking back, then forward again as she processed the lack of threat.</p>
<p>Quinlan exhaled quietly. Based on how his mothers had reacted, Thyra clearly carried a reputation for being rough in both word and deed, just like Dragnar. She probably would've attacked him if not for his mothers, to measure his person or something like that. Thyra's attention returned to him.</p>
<p>"Your mothers are very important to me, child. If you ever make them cry, you'll have to deal with me."</p>
<p>Before Quinlan could respond, a sharp stomp echoed.</p>
<p>"Thyra!" Mearie snapped, foot hitting the stone path hard enough to draw attention. "Stop acting like you're my mother!"</p>
<p>Luminara scoffed beside her. "Mearie is one thing, but we're the same age. I don't need your protection."</p>
<p>Thyra looked at the elf, then grimaced. "You're so small and thin. You should put more meat on your bones."</p>
<p>Miri's scoff increased. "Are you ignoring me?!"</p>
<p>Thyra reached down without warning and patted Miri's hair, fingers brushing through it with casual familiarity. "Calm down, little one."</p>
<p>"!!" Miri's eyes went wide.</p>
<p>She slapped the hand away. "I'm not a baby anymore! I stopped being one millions of years ago!"</p>
<p>Thyra's expression softened as she looked down at her. "They grow up so fast," she muttered with a tired sigh.</p>
<p>Miri's face tightened. Her grip on the spear creaked.</p>
<p>Quinlan burst into a wide grin as he looked between them. "Wait. Don't tell me… Are you Miri's mother?"</p>
<p>Primordials were birthed by divine magic, from the governing bodies of the universe, the higher divinities. Thus, they had no biological mothers. Just like how Mearie and Luminara were his adoptive mothers, that was the form of relationship Quinlan meant.</p>
<p>Thyra turned her head toward him, expression plain and unembellished. "Miri? That's a good name, I approve. As for your question... She was a timid little girl. She would have perished without my protection. I remember her hiding behind me for years on end, clutching my clothes tightly, refusing to let go."</p>
<p>Quinlan shifted his gaze to Miri and raised an eyebrow questioningly.</p>
<p>Miri, in response, froze.</p>
