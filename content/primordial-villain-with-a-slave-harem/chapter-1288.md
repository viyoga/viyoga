<p>Eric read the politics correctly: Alastair's claim to his own duchy was fragile at best, fraudulent at worst. The king had already turned the ownership into a contest because Alastair had stopped acting like a duke and started acting like an absentee captain, willing to throw men at border wars while ignoring the rot inside his own lands. For generations, Greenvale had tolerated monsters and criminal nests within its borders because Alastair and his ancestors treated their banners as tools for the frontier, not instruments of internal order. To them, it was fine to lose some citizens. It was okay to have an entire town become a criminal den or overrun by monsters. The Sustaining Arm, consisting of the three non-frontal duchies, namely: Silverwind, Thornhollow, and Duskmere, was tasked with sending resources to help the two frontal duchies, Greenvale and Ravenshade.</p>
<p>This process was set up generations ago with the intentions of letting the kingdom function at its peak possible efficiency. The two frontal duchies did not have to worry about producing enough food to feed their own mouths, nor did they have to worry about running a bit red on their financial sheets. Even losing manpower would be okay, as the Sustaining Arm was tasked to support the two frontal duchies.</p>
<p>In exchange, the members of Sustaining Arm would enjoy the protection of the two military areas, letting them maintain smaller armies and instead focus on prosperity. They were rich lands, cultivated properly because they did not have to deal with hostile invasions; Greenvale or Ravenshade would stop them before the members of the Sustaining Arm would have to even call upon their armies.</p>
<p>In a perfect world, this setup should ensure that the kingdom functioned at peak performance, led and administered by the Central Region in the middle, acting as the glue that held it all together.</p>
<p>But the two frontier duke families had slowly realized something.</p>
<p>Their contracts stated that they were to protect the kingdom from outside threats and also help the kingdom wage its offensive wars, and in exchange, they would be supported. So then… They just had to spend their resources on creating one giant army whose sole purpose was to wage war with the other two nations on the Iskaris Continent. They were basically rewarded for being negligent.</p>
<p>Naturally, this way of thinking has led to a perversion of the grandiose vision Alexios' ancestors had for the kingdom.</p>
<p>And he has had enough of this vile, rotten way of thinking. This damned passivity. Alexios has set out to change the current status quo that has been plaguing his ancestral lands for generations.</p>
<p>He did so first by marrying Morgana Ravenshade. Through more peaceful means, he coerced Tharion Ravenshade to conduct his duties more akin to Alexios' vision. Now, Alexios was intent on either having Alastair also begin behaving properly or being replaced.</p>
<p>Naturally, the king was not naive enough to think the status quo would last, especially after his death. As such, one of his final life goals was to revise the ancient contracts between the six families.</p>
<p>That was the real reason behind the competition, which forced Alastair to move or be exposed.</p>
<p>But replacing a duke was not a button one could press without consequence.</p>
<p>First, the practical: a duchy is a functioning machine. Taxes, grain shipments, garrison rotations, border patrols, magistrates, marriage contracts, a thousand mundane threads hold a region together. Remove the hand that knows those threads, and you do not get immediate improvement. You get gaps, missed grain levies, empty watchtowers, and unpaid contractors. In the short term, that turns order into chaos. Villages that need a bailiff, ports that need a customs officer, watchtowers that need archers. Someone must keep the machine running while a new hand takes over.</p>
<p>Second, the political calculus: dukes answer to the crown but rule like petty kings at home. The court could decree Alastair unfit, which was a ship that had already sailed at this point - but the duke still commanded loyalty from (some of) his vassals, captains, moneylenders, marriage alliances, and more. If a family remains in power for tens of thousands of years, they're bound to have deep roots in their lands. Pulling those roots out was easier said than done; it risked factional splintering. A dozen families who profit from Alastair's style would not fold quietly. They would pick sides. They could turn Greenvale from a chaotic battleground into pure no man's land. That friction would spill across borders. The kingdom cannot absorb a collapsed duchy on its frontier and call it stability.</p>
<p>Third, the military reality: Greenvale's armies hold the line against the beasts. Alexios had to weigh whether the short-term cost of removing a duke was worth the long-term gain. The law of war had not changed: an undefended border invites invasion. A quick regime change that ruptured command structures would invite raids or worse.</p>
<p>Fourth, precedent and legitimacy. Crown intervention to replace a duke is a political scalpel, not a mace. The throne had to maintain an image of fairness. Having the current competition for the seat was already an unprecedented event, but if Alexios didn't even allow Alastair to fight for his own lands, then other dukes would fear arbitrary removal and either lash back or, at the very least, heavily withdraw cooperation. The monarchy's soft power depended on being the last resort, not the first mover.</p>
<p>All of that condensed into one blunt truth: the king could not simply sweep Alastair aside without paying a heavy price. Greenvale was rotten in places, yes, but it was not empty. Replace the hand too quickly, and the duchy would not peacefully improve; it would combust.</p>
<p>That was why Eric's letter mattered and why it also sucked the air from the room. It pushed the solution forward but did not erase the cost of the cure. Alexios set the parchment down and looked at his aging counsel, reflecting his own lengthy time spent living on this cruel plane of existence.</p>
<p>"This will take a long time," he muttered.</p>
<p>He picked up the next envelope from the stack.</p>
<p>Before opening it, he spoke without looking up.</p>
<p>"Get me my reading artifact."</p>
<p>As the man bowed, the king also added one more thing.</p>
<p>"And get my damned wife on the line."</p>
<p>The king already had one migraine-inducing job back when he just had to deal with the nobles. But now, he had to still deal with those people while also having the little primordial monster running through his lands, causing chaos in his wake wherever he went. Alexios was tired.</p>
<p>But he would try his damnest to ensure his nation was set up to prosper before he departed from the world of the living and greeted the Goddess.</p>
