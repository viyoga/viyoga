<p>Alexios shook his head at her, then leaned back in his chair.</p>
<p>"Joining foreign armies has its uses, Quinlan, but only later. If we were to begin there, their commanders would take one look at our soldiers’ levels and put them wherever they expect the most losses."</p>
<p>He set his empty cup on the table.</p>
<p>"Our people are veterans. They know how to fight. But out here, they’ll be treated as disposable foot soldiers, and I fear we’ll bury far too many of them before they can grow into anything else."</p>
<p>Quinlan considered the old swordsman across from him.</p>
<p>"What would you propose?"</p>
<p>Alexios remained silent for a while. His attention passed over the people around the table, lingering on his daughter before returning to Quinlan.</p>
<p>"I’ll lead this company. But for our first work, I propose hunting pirates."</p>
<p>"Pirates?" Felicity asked her father.</p>
<p>"I have the perfect excuse. As far as the people of Sarthal know, I’m a foreigner whose daughter was just kidnapped by them. Nobody knows the extent of my means or connections. If I gather a force to hunt the bastards down, who would find that strange?</p>
<p>"Furthermore, should I be funded by Adventurer Black, then you’ll also pave the way to one day commanding your armies on Avaranth soil without anyone batting an eye."</p>
<p>Quinlan’s smile grew as he listened. "Finally, you remind me why I haven’t killed you yet."</p>
<p>"Because you could not. Not in battle," Alexios smirked, then continued. "We can choose which bands to pursue. Those battles will let our people become accustomed to facing higher-level enemies and build the company before hiring ourselves out for anyone else’s war."</p>
<p>"That could work," Quinlan smirked back. "But those Fomorian pirates were dangerous even for me. A company of men who’ve never fought at sea would be straight-up massacred, old friend."</p>
<p>He remembered the water lance punching through his ice barrier and hurling him onto the frozen sea. Even with Vex and Lucille beside him, the captain had been a dangerous opponent.</p>
<p>"Now that I propose something you like, I’m once again your old friend, huh?"</p>
<p>"You never ceased being one. Maybe I just forgot to call you that."</p>
<p>Serika and the girls giggled at that, always happy to hear their man and his best friend bicker with each other.</p>
<p>"Mhm," Alexios nodded, not buying his bullshit for a single second. "As for your concern... Who said I intend to sail? I’m too old for that.</p>
<p>"Even pirates go ashore to rest. Beastkin trackers can follow their scent from the landing sites. Dwarven engineers can help besiege their camps. And where beastkin noses fail, elven scouts can follow tracks and find routes through the terrain.</p>
<p>"And if we ever do have to fight on the water..."</p>
<p>His gaze moved to the corner beside the windows, where Morgana stood with her hands folded in front of her lap.</p>
<p>"I’ll have my own water mage."</p>
<p>Her crimson eyes widened at him.</p>
<p>The old swordsman smiled at Quinlan.</p>
<p>"She might never have loved me, but I still prefer looking at her face to your hideous mug."</p>
<p>"You wound me, old friend."</p>
<p>Quinlan turned toward the woman in question. Morgana was still looking at her husband, caught off guard by the invitation.</p>
<p>"What do you think?"</p>
<p>"I’m your slave. I do as you order me to."</p>
<p>"Then I order you to voice your mind."</p>
<p>Her attention returned to Alexios. He waited for her answer, and after a while, Morgana gave Quinlan a nod.</p>
<p>"Then you and Alexios can discuss-"</p>
<p>"I was ordered to voice my mind."</p>
<p>Quinlan paused.</p>
<p>"I did not mean that command literally. Your nod is answer enough."</p>
<p>Morgana shook her head at him.</p>
<p>"It isn’t."</p>
<p>Her gaze passed over Quinlan’s women one by one, then moved to her husband and the daughter sitting beside him.</p>
<p>"I am a great mage of the elements. A peerless one, even. At least among mortals..."</p>
<p>Her fingers caught in a fold of her robes as she faced the people waiting for her to continue.</p>
<p>"And in the pursuit of magic, I’ve failed at everything else in life."</p>
<p>It was humiliating to stand before them and say it. Even now, she wanted to snap at someone for staring, though she was the one who’d insisted on having their attention.</p>
<p>"I have a terrible personality. I’ve never had a friend in my life. I make it difficult for anyone to bear my company."</p>
<p>She made herself meet Kitsara’s eyes.</p>
<p>"And I nearly destroyed your family because I wanted to understand his magic. I treated your lives as though they were worth less than another discovery."</p>
<p>The foxkin’s lips parted, but Morgana had already turned toward Alexios.</p>
<p>"You honored our marriage vows. You even supported my research more than any court mage should’ve received. I repaid you with contempt and mockery."</p>
<p>Her husband watched her from his chair, the dry amusement gone from his face.</p>
<p>"I’ve been a worse wife than you deserve."</p>
<p>Looking at Felicity was harder. The girl who’d been laughing with her friends and teasing her father sat motionless beside him, waiting to hear what her mother would say.</p>
<p>"I brought five children into this world, but I failed to be a mother to any of them."</p>
<p>Morgana had blamed Felicity for leaving. Called it abandoning her family, as though the girl had been wrong to seek affection elsewhere.</p>
<p>"I failed you, Felicity."</p>
<p>Her voice had grown quiet. For all her pride in what she’d mastered, she could offer this child no comforting account of the years she’d spent choosing her research instead.</p>
<p>Felicity’s lips moved, but her mother continued before she could speak.</p>
<p>"I still want to see the depths of magic. That hasn’t changed. I won’t pretend I’ve suddenly become easy to get along with, or that I can give you the wife you wanted, Alexios Valorian."</p>
<p>She swallowed, struggling to finish with her daughter looking at her like that.</p>
<p>"And as for being a mother... I’m afraid that might be too late as well."</p>
<p>She wanted to turn away from Felicity’s face, to sit down and let someone else speak about the company, the pirates, anything she could discuss without feeling so... exposed.</p>
<p>"All that is to say... I am sorry."</p>
<p>Morgana bowed to Quinlan, his women, Alexios, and Felicity.</p>
<p>’She actually did it.’</p>
<p>Quinlan was surprised. Saying those words had made her plainly discomfortable, yet she’d kept going and did it much more convincingly than he’d ever thought possible for the Mad Dog of Vraven.</p>
<p>A smile reached his lips as he looked toward his girls, only to find them staring in open disbelief. Sera had lifted her head from his neck, and even Kitsara seemed unable to find anything to say.</p>
<p>Alexios had gone utterly still.</p>
<p>"Mother..."</p>
<p>Felicity pushed her chair back, tears spilling down her cheeks as she ran around the table.</p>
<p>"It’s not too late!"</p>
<p>Before Morgana could straighten from her bow, her daughter threw both arms around her waist and buried her face against her.</p>
<p>"I’m still here! We all are! No longer little babies, yes, but all five of your children live!"</p>
<p>Morgana’s fingers loosened their grip on her robes. For a moment, her hands hovered beside the girl clinging to her, uncertain where to go.</p>
<p>"..."</p>
<p>"Don’t say it’s too late. Please."</p>
<p>The young queen looked up at her mother through her tears, holding on so tightly that the fabric bunched in her fists.</p>
<p>"You can still be our mother."</p>
<p>She was afraid Morgana would withdraw again, take refuge in that cold voice and shut her out before she could reach her. Felicity held on harder, too overwhelmed to care who saw her cry.</p>
<p>Morgana looked at her daughter’s wet face. Unable to form words, she set a hand between Felicity’s shoulders. The touch was tentative, and when the girl pressed closer, Morgana brought her other arm around her too.</p>
<p>"Mother..."</p>
<p>Felicity’s voice broke against her robes. Morgana held her awkwardly, feeling each shudder of the child in her arms while her own eyes began to sting.</p>
<p>Alexios rose from his chair, then stopped beside the table to watch them. His daughter’s sobs filled the room, and he let her have her mother.</p>
<p>Morgana had apologized. Now Quinlan would keep his promise to vouch for her. He drew Sera closer as he said,</p>
<p>"&gt; &lt;I propose we accept the apology.&gt;</p>
<p>Kitsara looked from Morgana to Quinlan with growing suspicion.</p>
<p>"Are you saying all this so we’ll stop bullying you? No more gobbo escapades?"</p>
<p>"The what escapade?" Felicity gasped.</p>
<p>Still holding her daughter, Morgana looked toward the foxkin.</p>
<p>"...Partly, yes."</p>
<p>Kitsara stared at her, then broke into a giant grin.</p>
<p>"You shameless bitch. I can’t believe I’m saying this, but you’re kinda growing on me."</p>
<p>"The sentiment is not mutual," Morgana replied with perfect flatness, which only made the foxkin grin even wider.</p>
