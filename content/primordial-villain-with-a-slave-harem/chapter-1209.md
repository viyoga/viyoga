<p>Felicity reached for the pendant, pressing it between her fingers. "Hello, Father."</p>
<p>A pause followed. The sound of his breath came through first. "Is everything okay?"</p>
<p>She smiled, eyes half-lidded. "Okay? Goddess, no!"</p>
<p>Quinlan’s brow lifted.</p>
<p>Alexios’ tone hardened. "What happened?!"</p>
<p>Felicity giggled, light and easy. "I’m much better than alright! I’m feeling fantastic!"</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy enough to fill the room.</p>
<p>Both men had the same flat stare.</p>
<p>She was enjoying this.</p>
<p>But soon, as he listened to the grumbling of the king, Quinlan caught himself smirking.</p>
<p>There was something different about her.</p>
<p>The way she held herself was ever so slightly calmer, steadier. Her posture had lost the soft uncertainty she used to carry. She looked a bit older, though nothing physical had changed beyond her eyes.</p>
<p>Quinlan would later learn that for Felicity, years had passed in the trial. However, the girl did not suddenly undergo a drastic shift in attitude; she was still the same girl he knew.</p>
<p>One might assume she should be a mature woman right now, but Felicity spent those years doing nothing but studying magic with Morgana.</p>
<p>Morgana, who, even 400 years later, was not the most responsible or ’grown-up’ of adults.</p>
<p>Felicity did not go through enough stimuli to drastically alter her persona, and her brain was still the same as it had been before leaving for the trial.</p>
<p>To become an adult, she would have to grow physically.</p>
<p>Quinlan shifted his attention to Feng.</p>
<p>She was perched on the edge of the bed, legs swinging lazily. Usually, she’d be throwing a smug look his way, proud to have succeeded.</p>
<p>This time, she didn’t bother.</p>
<p>Her gaze tracked him quietly instead, unhurried and unreadable. The air around her had changed as well, becoming more controlled and less abrupt.</p>
<p>It was the first time Quinlan saw both girls like this. Whatever they went through in those trials had done more than just upgrade their classes.</p>
<p>It had rewritten how they saw themselves.</p>
<p>But it was not the time to dwell.</p>
<p>"What about our deal, My Great King? Do you accept?"</p>
<p>"From old bastard to Great King, huh?" Alexios grumbled.</p>
<p>"Did you call my father an old bastard?" Felicity quipped.</p>
<p>"I would never," Quinlan denied seamlessly, making the purple-haired princess giggle as she saw through him easily.</p>
<p>Alexios let out a long, tired sigh, though his tone carried a thread of amusement. "You want Gideon and Naomi Lorien in exchange for information, is that correct?"</p>
<p>"Indeed." Quinlan’s reply was calm and deliberate.</p>
<p>The king leaned back in his grand throne and began stroking his beard thoughtfully. "Gideon and Naomi Lorien..." he muttered, drawing the words out like a stage actor savoring his lines. "Those names do ring a bell... where have I heard them before?"</p>
<p>The chamber fell quiet for a moment before a soft voice spoke from the side.</p>
<p>"My lord, if I may," said a woman in Aegis Vanguard armor, bowing deeply.</p>
<p>Alexios gestured with a flick of his hand. "Go on."</p>
<p>"They were alchemists who financed a group of bandits to attack ’the outpost.’"</p>
<p>"Ah, yes... I remember now," Alexios nodded slowly. The corners of his mouth moved upward. "That’s right. Gideon and Naomi Lorien. The outpost attack."</p>
<p>His gaze shifted. "But I also heard something else."</p>
<p>The king’s tone sharpened. "Future Mommy Iris, was it, little one?"</p>
<p>Rosie froze.</p>
<p>Her hands shot to her mouth. "Ah!"</p>
<p>Hearing her panicked yelp, Alexios’ grin widened. "Iris... yes, that name rings a bell as well. If memory serves, I placed a bounty not long ago. A thousand gold, on a woman named Iris Ravenclaw."</p>
<p>He paused, savoring the tense silence coming from the other end of the line.</p>
<p>"And, coincidentally, she was stationed at the very same outpost those two alchemists hired their brigands for before she mysteriously vanished, abandoning her post. Just when something strange happened..."</p>
<p>He let the implication hang.</p>
<p>Quinlan knew exactly what he meant. Iris abandoned her post after realizing that he and the others had stolen the legendary artifact from right under her nose.</p>
<p>Not wanting to face punishment for failing to guard the kingdom’s property, she deserted and became a criminal after joining the Vesper Consortium.</p>
<p>Quinlan spoke quickly, so much so that his words were overlapping. "That’s pure coincidence, Your Majesty. Iris is a wonderful name that many mothers bestow on their daughters. The Iris I know has nothing to do with this Ravenclaw woman."</p>
<p>The king’s chuckle came low and pleased. "Oh, is that so?"</p>
<p>Alexios’s eyes glinted with satisfaction as he enjoyed finally having the upper hand since the conversation began. "I must say, it’s rare to see you sound so... concerned, Quinlan. I was starting to think you didn’t have that tone in your repertoire."</p>
<p>Quinlan’s jaw shifted ever so slightly, his smile tightening.</p>
<p>Alexios smiled wider. He liked this version of the boy, one that wasn’t perfectly composed, not entirely untouchable.</p>
<p>Quinlan let out a quiet sigh, the kind that sounded exactly like surrender.</p>
<p>"Alright. I’ll fess up."</p>
<p>Alexios raised a brow, clearly intrigued.</p>
<p>"It was me who invaded the outpost," Quinlan continued. "I heard of its existence whispered about in the underworld and... curiosity got the better of me."</p>
<p>The king’s expression darkened slightly, though his tone remained measured. "The Geim... did you kill it?"</p>
<p>Quinlan shook his head. "No. It spoke to me."</p>
<p>That made the entire chamber still.</p>
<p>Alexios leaned forward. "It spoke?"</p>
<p>"Yes," Quinlan said calmly. "Not in words you’d understand. It couldn’t communicate with humans. But I’m not human, Your Majesty, thus I understood its words."</p>
<p>His gaze hardened. "It told me it was dying. That as a primordial being, its [Geim Roots] should belong to me, not to the filthy humans who chained it up in that little cave."</p>
<p>Alexios stayed silent for a few seconds, studying the man’s voice. Then, with a quiet hum, he nodded. "I see. So it gave you the last of its roots... and then died."</p>
<p>"That is the case."</p>
<p>The king leaned back in his throne, and his fingers began drumming against the armrest. "Now I understand. You want those two alchemists because you believe they might be able to do something with the Geim roots."</p>
<p>Quinlan clicked his tongue sharply, looking away. "I suppose you could say that."</p>
