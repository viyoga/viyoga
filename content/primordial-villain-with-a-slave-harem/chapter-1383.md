<p>Her glare sharpened. Her shoulders drew up as she hugged her sore hand closer to her chest. "I hate you so damn much…"</p>
<p>The words came out small, tight, and sincere.</p>
<p>Quinlan laughed. It was a lot of fun to mess with this expressive nun.</p>
<p>But then, his laugh faded as abruptly as it began. "Alright. I think I've bullied you enough for now."</p>
<p>"…"</p>
<p>Mira only pouted harder, cheeks rounding even more as she turned her face away from him.</p>
<p>He shifted his grip.</p>
<p>Then he tossed her.</p>
<p>"Wha-!"</p>
<p>Her yelp cut off as Quinlan kicked off the air and caught her on his back.</p>
<p>"Hold on," he said nonchalantly.</p>
<p>She scrambled, arms locking around his neck, legs hooking at his sides. "You're the worst! I bet that when you greet the Goddess, she'll slap you, too! And that time, you'll feel it!"</p>
<p>"My mommies will protect me."</p>
<p>"Huh?"</p>
<p>They took off.</p>
<p>Orphanages were cleared one after another.</p>
<p>Every time, it was the same. Fear behind the door. Whispers. Prayers cut short when his presence was felt.</p>
<p>Then Mira stepped forward and quickly, briefly, explained what they had to know.</p>
<p>Wide eyes stared up at Quinlan's armor, then flicked back to her face. The tension eased just enough.</p>
<p>Children were gathered and passed into the waiting arms on the other side of the portal. Some were nuns, others were women and mothers.</p>
<p>Many of the original citizens of Miri Town were peasants who were driven to such poverty that they had to go rogue and join the Wraithclaws, which was a criminal organization that terrorized a few villages.</p>
<p>Quinlan conquered it and relocated the willing people to Miri Town.</p>
<p>As a result of their poverty and risky lives, many women had no children. Either due to sickness, malnutrition, stress, or a myriad of other effects. Now, those same women were treating these rapidly arriving orphans as pure blessings.</p>
<p>Just like that, one by one, the city emptied of them.</p>
<p>…</p>
<p>Mira stood alone at the edge of the portal.</p>
<p>Light spilled around her feet. The sounds of the city felt distant now, dulled and pushed aside. She did not turn to leave right away. Her hands came together at her waist. Her shoulders settled.</p>
<p>Then she bowed.</p>
<p>It was deep. Proper. Not rushed.</p>
<p>"Thank you for letting me do this, Quinlan Elysiar."</p>
<p>Quinlan watched her for a moment. "You thanked me last time, too."</p>
<p>She straightened and looked at him. There was no flare, no puffed cheeks, no sharp retort waiting behind her teeth.</p>
<p>"And I will thank you each and every time. Even if you do it a hundred times."</p>
<p>That gave him pause.</p>
<p>Quinlan's gaze lingered on her longer than before. Then he spoke quietly. "So you like it there."</p>
<p>Mira nodded. "Yes. It's only been a few minutes, but…" She glanced back through the portal. Although the nun couldn't see through with her eyes, her mind told her all she needed to know. Shapes moved on the other side. Women guiding children, who were already starting to regain their laughter despite being besieged by unholy monstrosities mere moments ago. "I can feel it just from the air. These people are kind beings. And Jasmine is a woman who takes her role seriously… I can tell that corrupting a governor like her will be near impossible. I feel hopeful about the future of Miri Town and its residents."</p>
<p>"Good," Quinlan said.</p>
<p>She smiled at him then. Small. Careful. No teeth. No hiss waiting behind it.</p>
<p>"G-good luck," Mira said, prompting Quinlan to raise an eyebrow. She knew he was a villain, here to kill human soldiers. Yet she, a nun, a devout follower of the Goddess, wished him good luck. Granted, there was nothing in the scriptures about human supremacy per se, for the Goddess loved all equally. But over time, most such motions were thrown aside. All races believed themselves to be the chosen ones, the favorites.</p>
<p>That's why her words carried immense weight.</p>
<p>Then she turned and stepped through the portal.</p>
<p>It closed behind her, leaving Quinlan alone in the city once more.</p>
<p>Next came the slave quarters.</p>
<p>His girls were already there.</p>
<p>The fighting had passed through the district hard and fast. Doors hung open. Chains lay coiled on floors where keys had been forced into hands. A few overseers knelt in lines with their wrists bound behind them, guarded by silent figures that did not breathe.</p>
<p>The contracts were stacked on a broken table. All accounted for.</p>
<p>They had done exactly what he expected.</p>
<p>Quinlan moved.</p>
<p>House by house. Yard by yard.</p>
<p>Locks gave way. Sigils burned out. The pressure that had sat on chests for years lifted in uneven waves. Some slaves fell to their knees. Some backed away from him in silence. Some stared at their hands as if waiting for the pain to return.</p>
<p>It did not.</p>
<p>Humans. Elves. Dwarves. Beastkin with collars worn thin from years of rubbing against skin.</p>
<p>Quinlan did not address them alone.</p>
<p>Seraphiel stepped forward for the elves. She spoke of the Lumi Forest that wrapped around Miri Town. Untainted nature. Clean water. Open paths beneath wide canopies. At times, Sylvaris stood beside her. Her presence drew murmurs. Some captives recognized her by name or by face, and the weight of her standing carried the words even further.</p>
<p>Felicity, sometimes accompanied by Ayame or Lucille, two dukes' daughters, addressed the humans. But it was the young princess who carried the show. She bowed first. She said her family had failed them. She did not soften it. She offered no orders, only a path forward. A city is being built far from her family's reach.</p>
<p>Kitsara took the beastkin who lingered at the edges. She spoke to the sharper-eyed ones first, the quick thinkers, the ones who watched from behind others. Terms. Opportunity. Space to breathe and live without chains. When that left gaps, Blossom filled them. The dogkin stood close to Quinlan, back straight, tail energetic. She spoke plainly. She spoke of him. What he did. How he treated those who stood with him. Her steady presence said the rest. For those who valued simple truths, it was enough.</p>
<p>Only the dwarves remained.</p>
<p>Quinlan stepped in himself.</p>
<p>He talked numbers. Metal quality. Booze access. Trade routes. He talked about the smithies already planned, about ore coming in steadily. About a drink that did not need to be watered down to stretch the supply. About a place where their work would not be claimed by another name.</p>
<p>He negotiated.</p>
<p>Some hesitated. Many did not.</p>
<p>Quinlan did not rush them.</p>
<p>Then the horns sounded again.</p>
<p>Another city followed. Another siege. Another massacre.</p>
<p>He rose back into the air.</p>
<p>More orphanages. More slave districts. The same pattern, repeated until the next cities felt hollowed out as well. Each siege fed the next. Each assault brought him XP and a growing soul army.</p>
<p>Elvardia and the Covenant bled.</p>
<p>Quinlan and his girls harvested.</p>
<p>Again.</p>
<p>And again.</p>
<p>And again.</p>
<p>Until the sound returned.</p>
<p>[Ding!]</p>
<p>[XP threshold reached. Pass the Primordial Rank-Up Mission to proceed into the level 50s.]</p>
<p>The long-awaited mission was here, ready to begin immediately. However, this time, it would be nothing like Quinlan could've predicted. No Zhenwu-like lengthy escapades into other worlds, nor Iris-like simulations lasting years. It would be something new. Something shocking.</p>
