<p>"She didn’t even get to finish her tea that time..." Seraphiel continued with a sigh.</p>
<p>The silence from the little dryad was damning. But it wasn’t shameful silence... It was purely strategic. Rosie was doing her utmost to perfect the art of dodging consequences.</p>
<p>"Mommy Sera is crueeelll!" Rosie wailed, lifting her head in over-the-top heartbreak, her eyes wide and glistening.</p>
<p>Seraphiel’s smile widened victoriously, looking at her daughter with her trademark smug expression. "Mm. I thought so. You’re still far too wet behind the ears, young lady."</p>
<p>Rosie huffed. Her cheeks puffed up like dumplings, and she crossed her arms with enough drama to rival any noblewoman at court.</p>
<p>But then... From one moment to the next, Rosie stilled.</p>
<p>Not a shift of wind. Not a flick of motion.</p>
<p>Her laughter and exaggeration stopped all at once as if someone had hit pause on her entire being.</p>
<p>The little dryad’s ears twitched. Slowly. Sharply. Once. Twice.</p>
<p>Her pupils dilated as though her entire body had become a sensory antenna.</p>
<p>"...Rosie?" Seraphiel asked, tone turning serious.</p>
<p>The other women halted too. Blossom, who’d been sniffing to check if anyone managed to somehow get into the stronghold in their absence, lifted her nose higher, sensing something was... off.</p>
<p>The silence was thick. Unnatural. Tense.</p>
<p>Rosie floated upward, her back to the others, staring at nothing.</p>
<p>The forest felt still. Birds didn’t chirp. Leaves barely rustled.</p>
<p>As a dryad bound to her tree, her sacred sapling rooted at the heart of their stronghold, Rosie wasn’t merely a resident of the land.</p>
<p>She was part of it.</p>
<p>Through that ancient, living connection, she could feel vibrations others could not. The subtle shiver of energy rippling through the roots. The echo of footsteps not yet heard. The pressure of a soul returning... no, ascending.</p>
<p>Something massive—someone beloved—had just crossed an invisible threshold.</p>
<p>Rosie gasped. A real one this time. There was not a shred of pretense present in her entire being.</p>
<p>Her hands rose to her mouth, trembling.</p>
<p>Tears welled in her bright green eyes—not from fear, not from sadness—but pure, unfiltered joy.</p>
<p>She didn’t even realize she was whispering until the words passed her lips:</p>
<p>"... He is coming home."</p>

<p>A pulse ripped through the forest clearing, akin to a silent thunderclap slamming into the bones of the earth.</p>
<p>It wasn’t hostile.</p>
<p>It didn’t need to be.</p>

<p>Quinlan.</p>
