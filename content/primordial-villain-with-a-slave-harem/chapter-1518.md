<p>Maren broke.</p>
<p>She collapsed against her mother's chest, sobbing so hard her whole body shook, and the healer held her the way she always had, one hand on her hair, the other rubbing slow circles on her back with the practiced motions of someone who had comforted this particular child through a lifetime of scraped knees and broken hearts.</p>
<p>"I'm still me, you ridiculous girl," she said softly. "Stop getting snot on my robes."</p>
<p>The old officer on the platform had found his son in the crowd, a boy barely old enough to hold the short sword strapped to his hip. The boy was frozen, his cheeks wet, his mouth working without sound.</p>
<p>The officer's stern face softened. "I'm here, boy. Different, yes, but here. Stop crying. You'll rust your scabbard."</p>
<p>A sob broke through the crowd. Then another. Then a wave of them, as the people of Whisperfield looked up at the platform and recognized the dead they'd been mourning.</p>
<p>The expressions. The voices. The mannerisms. The way Dorian folded his arms when he was angry. The way the healer held her daughter like nothing changed. The way the old officer's voice went quiet when speaking to his boy.</p>
<p>No Corpse Animator could replicate these things. Corpse Animators created hollow puppets, rotting shells that shambled and groaned and obeyed. They didn't fold their arms. They didn't scold. They didn't remember how their son liked to be comforted.</p>
<p>Every person in the square knew what necromancy looked like. This wasn't it.</p>
<p>The soldier who had been trying to kill Quinlan moments ago dropped his sword. It clattered against the cobblestones and he didn't hear it. He was staring at his brother on the platform with an expression that no amount of training had prepared him to wear.</p>
<p>"You… you're really him," Havel whispered.</p>
<p>"Of course I'm really me, you idiot!" Dorian jumped down from the platform and gripped his brother by the collar. "Stop gawking and say something!"</p>
<p>"You're… you're blue…"</p>
<p>"I'm aware of my new complexion, thank you!"</p>
<p>The old officer's son had finally unfrozen and charged the platform, slamming into his father's legs with enough force to stagger the man backward. The officer put a hand on the boy's head and looked out over the crowd with the quiet dignity of a man who had served, died, and somehow still wasn't done.</p>
<p>The soldiers who had been gripping their weapons moments ago watched with faces that cycled through horror, confusion, wonder, and grief in rapid succession. Some wept openly. Some looked at the sky as if checking whether reality was still functioning. Some looked at Quinlan with an intensity that hovered between reverence and hatred and hadn't decided which side to land on.</p>
<p>The soul soldiers looked at Quinlan. Several pressed fists to their chests.</p>
<p>"Master."</p>
<p>"Go," he said.</p>
<p>They leapt from the platform and walked into the crowd, finding the people they'd known, the families they'd left behind, and the reunions that followed were louder, messier, and more human than anything a Corpse Animator's hollow puppets could have produced.</p>
<p>Velara watched the scene unfolding before her. Her staff had lowered to her side. Her face was caught between theological fury and awe.</p>
<p>"What in the name of the Goddess…"</p>
<p>Quinlan turned to her and grinned.</p>
<p>"See? Told you I'm no Corpse Animator."</p>
<p>Velara's mouth moved. No words came out. She looked at the soul soldiers embracing their families, at the healer patting her sobbing daughter's back, at Dorian shaking his brother by the collar, and the Arch Priestess of the Goddess of Purity could not reconcile a single piece of what she was seeing with anything she had ever been taught about necromancy.</p>
<p>"This is…" She trailed off.</p>
<p>"Heretical? Blasphemous? An affront to the natural order?" Quinlan offered helpfully.</p>
<p>"…unprecedented."</p>
<p>Quinlan's grin widened. "I'll take it. Coming from you, that might be the kindest thing anyone's said about my magic."</p>
<p>"It was not a compliment."</p>
<p>"And yet you're not hitting me with the staff. Progress."</p>
<p>Velara's grip tightened on the staff as if reminding herself that the option still existed.</p>
<p>Count Aldren stood a few paces behind them on the platform, his torn cloak hanging from one shoulder and his sword long since lowered. He watched the reunions happening across the square with the expression of a man whose understanding of the world had just been disassembled and rebuilt in the wrong order.</p>
<p>"Son of a bitch," the Count muttered.</p>
<p>No one heard him over the crowd. He said it again, louder.</p>
<p>"Son of a bitch."</p>
<p>Velara shot him a scandalized look. Aldren didn't notice. His eyes were locked on the old officer holding his boy, on the healer smoothing her daughter's hair, on a dead city that was somehow, impossibly, less dead than it had been five minutes ago.</p>
<p>Quinlan glanced back at him. "All three of my mothers are loving and kind women, so…"</p>
<p>"Mothers?"</p>
<p>"Yeah. I got a couple."</p>
<p>"… I apologize, I didn't mean insult to your mothers. And please give me a moment. I'm processing."</p>
<p>Quinlan let him have it.</p>
<p>His gaze drifted to the edge of the square.</p>
<p>His girls had stayed back. They'd known this wasn't their moment, that the crowd needed to see him and only him at the center of what had just happened, and they'd kept to the periphery without being asked.</p>
<p>But they were watching.</p>
<p>Vex was grinning, wide and shameless, her arms crossed and her weight cocked to one hip like a woman who had expected exactly this and was pleased to be proven right.</p>
<p>Ayame shook her head with a quiet smile, the kind that said 'this man is at it again' without needing to say it out loud.</p>
<p>Serika gave him a thumbs up.</p>
<p>Blossom's tail was wagging so fast it was a blur.</p>
<p>The rest of his girls stood with them, and the pride on their faces was unanimous. They'd watched their man stand before a conquered city and turn grief into something no one had a word for yet.</p>
<p>Quinlan smiled back. A real one, brief and warm, before it disappeared behind the helmet's shadow.</p>
<p>Then he turned to the crowd.</p>
<p>The reunions were still happening across the square.</p>
<p>Quinlan's voice carried across the plaza.</p>
<p>"You call me a man worse than the undead lords."</p>
<p>The crowd stilled.</p>
<p>"You claim I desecrate life itself. You claim I am the antithesis of everything the Goddess represents. You claim what I do to the dead is a crime beyond forgiveness."</p>
<p>He let the words sit.</p>
<p>"So I'll give you a choice."</p>
<p>His aura swelled.</p>
<p>Slower this time, heavier, a pressure that rose from beneath the platform like the tide coming in, carrying with it every soul bound to his saber. The air thickened. The people closest to the platform stepped back without meaning to, and even the soul soldiers paused in their reunions, turning toward the source.</p>
<p>Quinlan stood on the platform, and he seemed larger than he had a moment ago. Taller. The dark armor drank the light around him, and the pale blue glow of the saber at his hip cast his shadow long across the stone.</p>
<p>Every eye in the square was on him, living and dead alike, as he announced,</p>
<p>"If you ask me to, I will release my claim on your loved ones. Every one of the dead I have claimed, I will release. They will pass on. They will reach the Goddess. They will greet her, as all dead are meant to."</p>
<p>The square stopped breathing.</p>
