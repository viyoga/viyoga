<p>The gate opened at the forest’s edge, and Luminareth was already there.</p>
<p>The city had no walls and no skyline.</p>
<p>It had a forest so vast it filled the horizon, and every tree in it was alive with civilization.</p>
<p>Walkways of living wood connected trunk to trunk in a web that stretched for miles, buildings growing from the bark at every level, their walls half-timber and half-tree, windows catching morning light.</p>
<p>Bridges arced between canopy heights in curves no architect had drawn, because the wood had been asked, and the wood had agreed. At ground level, elves tended crops between the roots and berry-gatherers worked the understory with baskets on their backs. Above, spires of shaped heartwood rose through the canopy where the sunlight hit first.</p>
<p>The whole of it breathed with the patience of millennia.</p>
<p>Rosie leaned forward over Myrasyn’s head, fingers tightening in blonde hair, and stared.</p>
<p>"Wow..." she breathed.</p>
<p>Seraphiel stepped through the gate behind him and stopped in her tracks, blue eyes wide and shining.</p>
<p>"Quin..." She seized his arm. "It’s beautiful."</p>
<p>"...Yeah."</p>
<p>It truly was a breathtaking sight, the gem of elven civilization.</p>
<p>Seraphiel was tracking every bridge, every spire, every building growing from the bark, and her usual cheeky composure had been replaced by undisguised wonder. "I heard many tales, but they don’t do it justice..."</p>
<p>Kaelira came through last, and went quiet.</p>
<p>Her violet eyes traced the grown architecture from root to crown with the stillness she brought to masterwork inspection, the kind of silence that meant she was learning faster than words could keep up.</p>
<p>"What marvelous architecture..."</p>
<p>But before the newcomers could keep gawking, two elven guards stepped from behind the nearest trunk in pale armor, planting themselves in the road.</p>
<p>Their eyes went to Quinlan first. A human male, two meters tall, radiating a signature that made trained soldiers reconsider career choices. Then to Seraphiel and Kaelira, elves traveling in his company, which raised different questions. Then to the two disheveled women leading the group, one carrying a small green child on her shoulders, and the questions stopped fitting into categories entirely.</p>
<p>"State your business," the taller guard said, hand settling near her blade. Her eyes kept drifting to Rosie.</p>
<p>"My business," Myrasyn said, through twenty hours of visible exhaustion, "is to enter my own capital."</p>
<p>The guard blinked. Her partner leaned in. They studied the woman in the wrinkled dress, the ruined braids, the general air of having recently lost a fight with a hedge, and searched for the Queen of the Elves in there somewhere.</p>
<p>"...Your Majesty?"</p>
<p>"Present and accounted for," Isveth confirmed beside her, in a shrine maiden dress that had creases in places creases should not exist.</p>
<p>"Head Maiden?!"</p>
<p>Both guards snapped to attention, hands to their chests in salute to Myrasyn, then to Isveth. The taller one was already turning to call for an escort.</p>
<p>"That won’t be necessary." Myrasyn adjusted Rosie on her shoulders. "We’ll walk."</p>
<p>"Your Majesty-"</p>
<p>"I’m sure of it, yes," the queen repeated, and started forward. "Thank you for your hard work."</p>
<p>The guards parted, ears perking as the praise reached them.</p>
<p>One glanced at Rosie, who offered a small wave. "Hello, pretty guard lady!"</p>
<p>"G-greetings, child..." The guard waved back before catching herself and snapping to attention, and her partner’s mouth twitched.</p>
<p>The city received them with more than staring.</p>
<p>Elves filled the walkways at every level, and the first reactions were predictable enough: the queen’s ruined braids drew double-takes, Rosie’s green skin drew pointing, and the two-meter human drew the kind of cautious attention that large predators earned in public spaces.</p>
<p>But as the group moved deeper along the main thoroughfare, something else began.</p>
<p>A merchant sorting berries on the nearest walkway turned toward Quinlan with quiet reverence, one hand drifting to her chest, eyes going glassy.</p>
<p>Behind her, an older elf tending a window garden froze mid-planting, eyes gone distant, and forgot the trowel in her hand.</p>
<p>A pair of young elves on a bridge overhead leaned over the railing, and one pressed both hands to her mouth. The other grabbed her friend’s arm and whispered something that made both of them bow their heads without deciding to.</p>
<p>But these actions weren’t aimed at Myrasyn or Isveth. Not even Rosie, Quinlan realized.</p>
<p>An old elf on a root-bench dipped her head as he passed. A shopkeeper stepped from her doorway, froze, and slowly sank to one knee with her fist pressed over her heart.</p>
<p>’...Hmm.’</p>
<p>He hadn’t taken a single drop from the Requiem before walking through the gate.</p>
<p>He should appear as a large and overly handsome human, yes, he reasoned, but...</p>
<p>Why do they react like that to a human in casual clothes strolling through an elven capital?</p>
<p>They should be standing at attention, preparing for conflict. Yet the elves were responding to him like he’d carried the spring in with him.</p>
<p>"What’s going on?"</p>
<p>Seraphiel noticed because Seraphiel always noticed when women reacted to her man. She hooked her arm through his, blue eyes bright with interest. "Quin~ We’re on a business trip... You know that, right?"</p>
<p>"How can you blame me? I have done nothing."</p>
<p>"Their ears go low when you pass," Kaelira said from his other side, quiet and focused. "They’re being pulled toward you."</p>
<p>"They all feel the same thing," Rosie observed from atop Myrasyn’s shoulders, chin propped on the queen’s crown. "Warm."</p>
<p>Quinlan turned toward Isveth and Myrasyn, who walked half a step ahead. Both had been watching the citizens’ reactions, and when they glanced back, neither looked confused.</p>
<p>They looked proud.</p>
<p>"You’ve consumed several drops from the Requiem over recent days, Holy Son," Isveth said, and her voice dropped into the low, reverent register she kept for matters of the faith. "Their effects seem to linger..."</p>
<p>Myrasyn shifted Rosie’s weight on her shoulders, and her smile went soft.</p>
<p>"When you take a drop, we feel the First Elf’s screaming declaration of her love for you." The queen’s gaze moved across the walkways, across the citizens who kept turning toward the human in their midst like flowers following a light they could not name. "Now it has become her soft, constant embrace. Quieter, but no less real."</p>
<p>Quinlan watched the merchant still pressing her hand to her chest, and the old elf who had gone back to her planting with wet cheeks, and let the information settle.</p>
<p>His mother. Still reaching for him through whatever she’d left in his bones.</p>
<p>’...Damn. To think she really cares this deep about me... I mean, I always knew, but...’</p>
<p>Seeing the extent of her motherly affection for her adoptive son was overbearing sometimes, but far be it from Quinlan to reject her love.</p>
<p>He said nothing, and they kept walking.</p>
<p>As they did, Myrasyn’s exhaustion began losing ground to something else entirely. She pointed toward a cluster of spires rising through the canopy on the group’s left.</p>
<p>"The High Arbors." Pride crept into her voice like sunlight through leaves. "Every trunk in that grove is said to have been planted before the first human kingdom drew its borders."</p>
<p>"Every one?" Seraphiel craned her neck.</p>
<p>"Every one." The queen pointed again, toward a bridge that arced between two enormous trunks in a curve that seemed to ignore weight entirely. "That bridge was grown in a single season, forty thousand years ago. It has never been repaired. The wood remembers the shape it was asked to hold."</p>
<p>"Forty thousand..." Kaelira whispered, and her hand twitched toward the nearest bark.</p>
<p>"The roots go deeper than any hole dwarves dig to hide," Isveth added, and the shrine maiden’s pride was quieter than the queen’s but no less present. "Every structure you see is still part of a living tree. The trees give form, and we give purpose. Neither one owns the other."</p>
<p>"So the whole city is alive?" Rosie bounced once on Myrasyn’s shoulders. "Like Rosie’s tree?"</p>
<p>"Your tree is far too unique for me to say yes, little one." Myrasyn smiled up at her passenger. "But these trees are the pride of my people."</p>
<p>Rosie considered this with appropriate gravity, then nodded. "Rosie approves. The trees are gorgeous and Rosie senses how happy they are!"</p>
<p>Myrasyn held her chin up through it all, composure stitched together by will alone, though the effect was slightly undercut by the dryad princess using her head as a viewing platform and the human beside her making elves weep by doing nothing more strenuous than existing.</p>
<p>A polished shopfront ended the performance.</p>
