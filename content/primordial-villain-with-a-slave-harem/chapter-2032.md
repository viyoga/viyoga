<p>Outside the Guild, Veshara stretched both arms above her head, enjoying the pull through her shoulders after sitting in that ungrateful old man’s office.</p>
<p>A pair of dragonkin guards were coming down the street. They spotted her beside the entrance and straightened before saluting Veshara.</p>
<p>She smiled at them.</p>
<p>"Working hard?"</p>
<p>"Yes, Captain."</p>
<p>"Wonderful. Keep it up"</p>
<p>Their boots continued down the paving while she lowered her arms, watching them go with a thoughtful hum.</p>
<p>"Hmm..."</p>
<p>She ought to be doing that too. Working. There were ships at the harbor, men awaiting their assignments, and probably someone somewhere doing something they shouldn’t.</p>
<p>She missed her proper command. Leading one of Sarthal’s elite combat companies had suited her far better than listening to merchants argue about cargo.</p>
<p>That posting had ended after she’d suggested that her superior retire.</p>
<p>She’d called him a cowardly old man while doing so, yes, but someone willing to fight ought to have his position. What was wrong with saying it?</p>
<p>Her superior had answered by sentencing her to a year of harbor duty.</p>
<p>Veshara still held her captain’s rank. Her elite company, however, had been handed to someone else while she supervised arriving ships and guards who spent their days checking crates.</p>
<p>’Hmph. He should’ve thanked me.’</p>
<p>Black had finally given her something interesting to do. Unfortunately, he’d made his feelings about her company rather clear.</p>
<p>"I really should go back... and work... ugh..."</p>
<p>Veshara looked toward the harbor, then along the much more inviting street leading away from it.</p>
<p>"Hmm..."</p>
<p>Perhaps there was an excellent reason for a captain to take the longer route. Something requiring her personal attention. She searched the passing crowd with renewed diligence, hoping somebody would provide one.</p>
<p>A merchant was arguing over a delivery. Children chased each other around a fountain. The patrol passed a woman carrying a basket, who thanked the guards when they moved aside for her.</p>
<p>Everyone was behaving themselves.</p>
<p>How inconsiderate.</p>
<p>Her hand found the purse at her belt, and the cheerful street began to look considerably less cheerful.</p>
<p>Black had taken her money. His dogkin had taken her money. If that wasn’t enough... Her Majesty had decided that a fine would help her reflect upon her foul personality.</p>
<p>"Muu..."</p>
<p>Her golden eyes grew wet.</p>
<p>’I reflected when I paid the first time...’</p>
<p>Apparently that hadn’t been enough reflection.</p>
<p>Now she was poor, scolded, and expected to return to work with a pleasant attitude. The Guildmaster had even thrown her out while she was confiding in him about her maiden heart.</p>
<p>"Cruel people..."</p>
<p>"Did you hear? Adventurer Black’s looking for a house."</p>
<p>Veshara’s hand left her purse.</p>
<p>Two women had stopped beside the Guild steps, one carrying a wrapped loaf while the other peered through the open doors.</p>
<p>"Here in Sarthal?"</p>
<p>"Where else? My sister saw him at a property agency. Brought all those pretty women with him."</p>
<p>"Oh, that poor agent. Imagine finding somewhere that pleases the whole lot."</p>
<p>"I’d start with a very big bed."</p>
<p>They laughed together. Veshara was already turning toward them, her financial troubles forgotten.</p>
<p>A house?</p>
<p>He’d barely arrived in Sarthal! How had he earned enough to start shopping for a home already?</p>
<p>Then she remembered his account of taking his wives abroad, with a company of female bodyguards accompanying them. Some foreign lord enjoying his honeymoon, supposedly. Perhaps a prince, considering how thoroughly accustomed he was to getting his own way.</p>
<p>Her mouth fell open.</p>
<p>’And he still made me pay him!’</p>
<p>She’d emptied her purse into the hands of a rich man who was now taking his beautiful wives out to buy a vacation mansion.</p>
<p>"How could you..."</p>
<p>The woman holding the bread glanced toward her. Veshara gave her a lovely smile before looking up the street again.</p>
<p>What was wrong with the inn, anyway? It was a perfectly respectable establishment. Spacious rooms, good service, a private dining room where a man could sit with his family...</p>
<p>Through whose floor she’d driven him.</p>
<p>"Ah. Right."</p>
<p>There had also been the roof.</p>
<p>"...Oops."</p>
<p>The bread-carrying woman had begun edging closer to her friend when Veshara turned back toward them.</p>
<p>"Ladies, could you tell me which agency your sister meant?"</p>
<p>"The one up that way, Captain. With the green sign. They’d already left to look at a place along the next street."</p>
<p>"How helpful of you. Thank you"</p>
<p>Veshara set off in the direction she’d been shown, her copper tail swinging happily behind her. After a few steps, a pleased little laugh escaped her, and she skipped over a gap in the paving with the carefree delight of a maiden on her way to meet someone special.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Quinlan waited beside the front door of the first house while their property agent tried another key.</p>
<p>His women were discussing where they’d sit on the balcony above the entrance. Kitsara had already made her choice.</p>
<p>"On Hubby’s lap, presumably," Vex muttered.</p>
<p>"You know me so well"</p>
<p>"It must have been very difficult to guess," Ayame sighed.</p>
<p>The agent glanced back at them, caught the red glow of his helmet, and promptly returned to the lock.</p>
<p>"My apologies. The owner keeps all the keys together."</p>
<p>"Take your time."</p>
<p>Every time the agent answered a question, another gorgeous face appeared beside Quinlan to ask something else.</p>
<p>His girls were enjoying themselves enormously. They were finally looking for a home here, somewhere they could arrange to their liking and have room for everyone.</p>
<p>"Miss Agent?"</p>
<p>The dogkin beside her spoke so sweetly that the woman stopped struggling with the keys.</p>
<p>"Yes, my lady?"</p>
<p>"Blossom would really like somewhere the smells aren’t so overwhelming!"</p>
<p>She pointed at her own nose, wrinkling it for the woman’s benefit.</p>
<p>"This one is very sensitive!"</p>
<p>"Oh. Of course. We can look farther from the market."</p>
<p>"So, away from the poor people?" Kitsara asked, her voice full of interest. "Since when have you become so discriminating, Blossom?"</p>
<p>"Wha-?!"</p>
<p>The dogkin spun toward her.</p>
<p>"Blossom didn’t say that!"</p>
<p>"You want a lovely house where the common folk’s nasty stench can’t reach your nostrils."</p>
<p>"Blossom just can’t rest with so many smells around her! There’s rotting fish over there, somebody’s burning their lunch, and that cart is leaking beer!"</p>
<p>"Eh?!" The driver jumped off the cart. After a quick inspection, he saluted Blossom. "Thank you, miss! You saved me!"</p>
<p>"Welcome..." she replied, nose scrunched, then turned back to Kitsara with her ears flattened.</p>
<p>"Blossom likes sleeping!"</p>
<p>"A very good excuse. I, too, prefer to live away from the stinking homeless. Nothing to be ashamed of."</p>
<p>"It’s the truth!!"</p>
<p>The agent laughed before catching herself. She pressed her lips together, but Blossom’s wounded look toward Quinlan made the effort hopeless.</p>
<p>"I’m sorry, my lady. I understand. I wouldn’t want to sleep beside the fish market either, and I don’t have a dogkin’s powerful nose."</p>
<p>"!!" Blossom beamed at her, then turned that newly vindicated smile upon Kitsara. "Miss Agent understands Blossom!"</p>
<p>"Your husband’s money will buy you a wonderful nose-friendly mansion."</p>
<p>"It will," Quinlan agreed, rubbing the dogkin’s head as she came to stand beside him. "Could we favor the quieter streets?"</p>
<p>"Certainly, sir. There’s another with private grounds, well away from the market."</p>
<p>She put the keys away and led them back down the steps.</p>
