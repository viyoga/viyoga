<p>The Warrior King stood on a hill with his hands clasped behind his back and watched his armies march.</p>
<p>Columns of infantry stretched from horizon to horizon, their boots grinding the earth into a single continuous drumroll that shook the hilltop beneath him.</p>
<p>Alexios had been standing here for the better part of an hour. His golden armor had been cleaned and repaired, the dents hammered out, the blood scrubbed from the joints. The cut across his face had closed hours ago, his Vitality itself knitting the wound shut before the blood had time to dry.</p>
<p>The grin was gone. The wild energy that had carried him through the battle, the joy that had made him laugh while trading blows with Ragnar, the reckless freedom he'd felt when he'd finally stopped thinking and started swinging, all of it had drained out of him somewhere between the battlefield and this hill. What remained was the king.</p>
<p>Hands behind his back, spine straight, face composed. Nine centuries of rule settling back into the lines of his jaw and the set of his shoulders.</p>
<p>The sound of boots on grass came from behind him.</p>
<p>"Your Majesty."</p>
<p>Tharion Ravenshade climbed the last of the hill and stopped three paces back. The duke was armored in the dark steel of his house, his helmet tucked under one arm, his black hair matted with sweat.</p>
<p>He bowed.</p>
<p>"I will not waste your time with flattery. But the Ravenshade family owes the crown a debt for your timely arrival."</p>
<p>"You owe the crown nothing new," Alexios said without turning. "The contract is the contract. Your ancestors bent the knee and the Valorian family pledged its protection. That was the agreement then. It is the agreement now."</p>
<p>Tharion straightened. "And our arrangement?"</p>
<p>"Is unchanged."</p>
<p>"Even after I had to pull out of Greenveil?"</p>
<p>Alexios's gaze didn't leave the columns. "We've already discussed this. I am not a man who goes back on his word."</p>
<p>The duke studied the king's profile for a moment, then nodded.</p>
<p>He stepped forward to stand beside Alexios and turned his attention to the march below.</p>
<p>The two watched in silence.</p>
<p>The battle had been devastating. Hundreds of thousands of soldiers lay dead on the fields behind them, the losses split roughly evenly in the early hours when the forces had clashed in open formation. But the scales had tipped when Ragnar and Aelindra ordered the retreat. Retreating armies bled, and Alexios's charging warriors had bled them dry along the withdrawal corridors.</p>
<p>Now the survivors had burrowed into the cities they'd seized during their invasion, fortifying behind walls that had belonged to Vraven a few days ago.</p>
<p>Retaking them would be ugly. Dwarves dug into stone were a different breed from dwarves caught in open formation. Given even a few hours, they reinforced walls, embedded runic defenses into the foundations, and turned civilian architecture into something that required siege engines and blood to crack.</p>
<p>The elves complicated it further. They didn't love stony settlements, and, if given the choice, they'd steer clear of one, living in their tree houses. But it wasn't like they were incapable of living in stone.</p>
<p>And during these times, personal comfort was likely not the main concern of their leaders.</p>
<p>An elf with a clear sightline, standing on a wall reinforced by dwarven technologies was worth ten soldiers on the ground.</p>
<p>"Lilith is furious," Tharion said.</p>
<p>"Her sister has been captured," Alexios replied. "It's understandable."</p>
<p>A pause sat between them.</p>
<p>"What we witnessed on that battlefield..." Tharion chose his words carefully. "The Primordial Villain's slave-like ability is beyond anything we'd accounted for. Her Majesty was unconscious for the entirety of the engagement, and yet she fought. She cast. She obeyed his will without being aware she was doing so."</p>
<p>Alexios said nothing.</p>
<p>"A Slave Master style class that functions on unconscious subjects is unprecedented. The Slave Master class itself is widely under utilized and thought to be one of the weakest classes. As such, not many have studied its effects. Nothing in our records indicates that the subject can be unaware of the commands being given to them. His version bypasses that limitation entirely."</p>
<p>Alexios said nothing. His hands were still clasped behind his back, but the knuckles had gone white.</p>
<p>He remembered.</p>
<p>Morgana walking across the battlefield with caved ribs and empty hands, her body moving with purpose that wasn't hers. His wife. The mother of his children. Turned into a weapon against her own kingdom by someone who didn't even need her awake to use her.</p>
<p>And Felicity.</p>
<p>The little girl with the purple hair who'd jumped through a portal because her mother had never once made her feel like staying was worth it. He'd fought his way to the front lines, committed the full might of the Vraven Kingdom, and she was still gone.</p>
<p>Quinlan was gone as well.</p>
<p>Again.</p>
<p>After lying in wait for the perfect ambush, after cutting off both his arms, Quinlan still managed to slip away from Alexios.</p>
<p>The afternoon light moved across the valley below them and the columns kept marching. Alexios watched them and felt the burden settle.</p>
<p>'They will not sleep easier.'</p>
<p>The words surfaced the way they always did now. Uninvited, patient, heavier each time.</p>
<p>He'd asked the question through the Primordial Villain, of all people. A moment of weakness, or honesty, or both. 'Will my people sleep easier when I am gone?'</p>
<p>The Goddess of Thalorind had answered.</p>
<p>'They will not sleep easier.'</p>
<p>He'd turned those words over a hundred times since. Was he not good enough, or was what came after him going to be worse?</p>
<p>The more time passed, the more the words sounded like a warning he'd already failed to heed.</p>
<p>"Your Majesty."</p>
<p>Tharion's voice pulled him back. The duke's tone had shifted, careful now.</p>
<p>"The princess has spent months in the Primordial Villain's company. At an impressionable age, under the influence of someone whose abilities include bending the will of others." Tharion paused. "Our arrangement did not account for this. If she returns to us with her loyalties reshaped, the complications would be significant."</p>
<p>Alexios's hands unclasped.</p>
<p>"You're speaking about my daughter."</p>
<p>The duke went quiet.</p>
<p>The columns kept grinding forward below them. A kingdom in motion, on their way to reclaim what had been taken.</p>
<p>At the same time, far away from battlefields, a man was about to wake up.</p>
