<p>What remained was a pillar of pale ice with a shadow at its core, and a quiet so complete they could hear the lanterns burn.</p>
<p>Quinlan turned back to Iris as if nothing had happened at all.</p>
<p>She was looking up at him. The fury had spent itself and left her hollowed out, and for a long, long moment she just looked, blood dripping off her chain onto the stone.</p>
<p>Then she let him see it.</p>
<p>The rage was armor, and everyone in this mansion had seen the armor. What she let surface now, only for Quinlan, was the real thing underneath: fear. Plain, bald, childlike fear, sitting naked on a face that never showed it.</p>
<p>"He doesn’t know." Her voice came out small and stripped. "Every rung said Covenant. I tried so hard, and there’s..." The words wobbled, and she forced them level. They wobbled anyway. "There’s no fucking name at the top. It’s as if it never existed."</p>
<p>Her fist finally came down. The chain sagged against her thigh.</p>
<p>"So what do I kill?" she whispered. "How can I avenge Damian?"</p>
<p>A whole life aimed like a blade at a single point, and the point had just been subtracted from the world.</p>
<p>Quinlan felt the size of the hole through the mark on her spine, a cold, weightless lurch across the bond. She was despairing.</p>
<p>He drew her in against his side, and the untouchable Iris, the feral one, the one who bared her teeth at kindness on principle, pressed her face into his shoulder and let him.</p>
<p>Across the cellar, Alexios finally left the corridor’s mouth.</p>
<p>The king moved slowly, boots quiet on the stone, and stopped a respectful distance away.</p>
<p>He looked at the sealed pillar, then at the ruined frost where her fists had landed. His gaze ended on the girl folded into the side of the - now clearly - most dangerous being on the continent. His face went grim, years of certainty rearranging themselves in silence.</p>
<p>"...Will you tell me the real story?" Alexios asked quietly. "Please."</p>
<p>Quinlan told him.</p>
<p>He kept it level and spare. "Ravenclaw. A baron family on the Elvardian border, poor as dirt and proud anyway. Cadet cousins to their duke, for whatever that was ever worth."</p>
<p>"Her father already owed one lender through proper channels, and repayment was looking grim. So he took a second loan, quietly, from the kind of men who exist for exactly that moment, and sank it into rebuilding the roads that linked his land to the nearest city. He gambled on trade coming and lost."</p>
<p>Alexios’s brow lowered by a degree. He said nothing yet.</p>
<p>"Then one day new collectors came to the door, but they were not the lender’s men. They said the debt had been bought, and they named the Covenant of Eternity, and they took surety for it."</p>
<p>"Surety," Alexios repeated. The word already sat wrong in his mouth.</p>
<p>"Her big brother." Quinlan’s tone didn’t move. "He was a toddler, yet he stepped in front of his little sister so they’d take him instead, and they did."</p>
<p>Quinlan didn’t need to add extra details, such as how what came back to the family afterward was buried in a box the size of a small dog.</p>
<p>Iris’s hand closed slowly around a fistful of his shirt. She didn’t lift her face. She let every word pass without correction, and Alexios noticed that too, and did not ask the primordial how he came to know any of it. The answer was standing inside the man’s arm.</p>
<p>"The baron petitioned his duke for justice. Ravenshade told him they would look into it."</p>
<p>A pause, thin as a knife.</p>
<p>"Nobody looked into anything. After that the family came apart in ways I won’t list."</p>
<p>Alexios stood very still, extremely grim, before he nodded, accepting the brief explanation.</p>
<p>The lantern light moved on his face while he paged backward through his own reign, and Quinlan watched the exact second the record surfaced, because the king’s eyes went dark.</p>
<p>"Ravenclaw." The word came out toneless. "The barony reverted to Ravenshade due to the dissolution of the line with owed debts cited as cause. This one I didn’t even need to sign as my dukes do not require my permission to administer their vassals." The dejection in his eyes settled all the way down to the bone. "But I can tell you that nowhere in that paragraph was there a child."</p>
<p>"With this foul thing’s testimony, the only question left is what role Ravenshade played. Lucky outsiders who received a barony with freshly built roads, or..." Quinlan muttered, and his voice wasn’t doubtful.</p>
<p>The king took it standing, spine straight, one more potentially filthy deed in his kingdom settling onto the pile beside the rest of them.</p>
<p>"Iris Ravenclaw." He addressed her directly, iron and ash in equal measure. "If the trail truly ends at Ravenshade, if that house bought your family’s debt and sent those men and buried it, then the crown will give back every single thing it can reach. Pardon. The bounty burned. Your line restored, your barony returned, your brother’s name entered in the record the kingdom keeps. Everything."</p>
<p>Iris lifted her face from Quinlan’s shoulder.</p>
<p>She looked at the King of Vraven with wet, red-rimmed eyes and an expression that didn’t look relieved of even an iota of pain.</p>
<p>It was Quinlan who answered, quietly, over the top of her head.</p>
<p>"Keep your investigations and pardons, old friend."</p>
<p>The temperature fell.</p>
<p>The warmth left the cellar all at once, confiscated, and frost tore outward across the floor from where the primordial stood, white crystals unrolling over the stone faster than a man could sprint.</p>
<p>The wine inside the nearest casks froze hard enough to split their oak with a crack like snapping bone, and Alexios’s next breath crystallized before it cleared his lips, coming down as a small, private snowfall.</p>
<p>"I’ll do the research myself," the Primordial Villain announced.</p>
<p>Both lantern flames shrank to drowning blue points. The dark that flooded in behind them had mass, a pressure that leaned on the eardrums and sat against the backs of the eyes, and every ounce of it was aimed at the king.</p>
<p>The sealed pillar moaned as ice packed itself over the dead lord in layer after uninvited layer, compressing over and over again.</p>
<p>Every battlefield instinct Alexios owned came back from the room with the same report: a terrible monster stood before him.</p>
<p>But the old king did not buckle. A slow breath hissed out through his teeth as he held his ground.</p>
<p>"And if it turns out Ravenshade was involved..."</p>
<p>Quinlan watched the king over Iris’s head, and the theater was gone, the grin, the jokes, the lazy shoulder propped against old stone, all of it folded away like a stage set between acts.</p>
<p>What regarded Alexios out of that familiar face was the winter itself, and it was calm. Fury could have been waited out, fury burns through its fuel and gutters, but this calm had nothing to exhaust.</p>
<p>And through the whole of it, the arm around Iris stayed warm. The frost had claimed every surface in the basement by now yet it stopped dead in a clean curved line a hand’s width from her body.</p>
<p>The rest arrived at a murmur, the buried shadow in the ice all straining toward the same few words.</p>
<p>"I’ll butcher them all."</p>
<p>He was telling the King of Vraven, to his face, inside his own borders, that he would slaughter one of the crown’s ducal houses, and it had come out as a forecast, winter naming its arrival date, with nothing required of anyone present but acceptance.</p>
<p>What Alexios did not know was that Iris’s tragedy was deeply, deeply personal to Quinlan. He had lived through it himself, seen what she saw, felt what she felt, and never forgotten the terror forced on such a young and innocent soul.</p>
<p>Through it all, Alexios Valorian, to the eternal credit of whatever made him, did not step back even if the muscles of his neck stood like cables.</p>
<p>The wry old man was gone. What stood in his place was the thing his soldiers followed into dead ends and darkness, the strongest living human on the continent, planted on his own two feet in a grave that hadn’t closed, meeting the menace eye to eye because kings did not get to look away.</p>
<p>"I won’t allow you to massacre my dukes, even if they’re the worst criminals in the world. That is my duty as king."</p>
