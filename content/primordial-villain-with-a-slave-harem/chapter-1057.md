<p>Calienne’s voice had a slight edge to it now.</p>
<p>"If that were the case, the woman still had plenty of chances to go to the authorities."</p>
<p>Quinlan’s gaze didn’t waver as he responded.</p>
<p>"Does it look like this woman was ever allowed to exercise such freedom? Husbands can be controlling. Criminal husbands, even more so. It is entirely plausible she was chained to a basement for decades, long before the duke ever imprisoned her."</p>
<p>The words made the air in the banquet hall feel heavier. Not only was this man questioning the royal family’s and the whole nation’s justice, but he seemed to be more than happy to start an argument with the princess.</p>
<p>To her credit, Calienne didn’t seem to take offense. She didn’t live for multiple centuries to act like a young brat. Instead, the woman’s eyes lingered on the alleged criminal’s ruined form. Her thin frame was wrapped in a dull prison gown, wrists raw from shackles, skin pale from years without sunlight. Her once-dark hair was streaked with white, tangled beyond care. There was no dignity left in her posture, only a hollow shell.</p>
<p>But the woman herself didn’t look toward the princess.</p>
<p>Her attention was elsewhere, fixed not on Quinlan, but on his table.</p>
<p>Specifically, on a brown-haired woman seated a few chairs away, a half-mask concealing the curve of her cheekbones and nose.</p>
<p>Jasmine.</p>
<p>Even with the mask, even if she was only permitted to see her daughter once a year, the mother recognized her daughter.</p>
<p>No twitch of her lips, no overt sign gave her away. She was too cautious for that. But in her eyes, dull and lifeless only moments before, there was a little spark. Just enough to betray that she had seen her.</p>
<p>When she finally spoke, her voice was as Quinlan had painted it in the heads of the nobility. Thin and ragged, the voice of someone who had been tormented for as long as she could remember.</p>
<p>"My ’husband’..." She spat the word with such incredible hatred that even Calienne’s heart skipped a beat. "Had ensured I was never without at least one guard who would stop me from speaking to the authorities."</p>
<p>A murmur rippled across the hall.</p>
<p>"When I became pregnant with our daughter... I knew this was not the life I wanted for her. I tried to run."</p>
<p>She sighed deeply, letting a long exhale leave her lungs. No one doubted the amount of grief she held as she said, "I didn’t make it far. He caught me and... handed me over to the duke himself."</p>
<p>The audience gasped.</p>
<p>A criminal, giving his own wife to a duke? It defied reason. Such a move risked his secrets, his leverage.</p>
<p>That was when she added the final blow.</p>
<p>"There was a deal struck. Even in the duke’s prison, my husband was allowed to visit me any time he wished. I was tortured there by him... over and over again... as the guards watched."</p>
<p>The reaction was immediate. Dozens of nobles jolted in their seats, whispers swelling to near chaos.</p>
<p>And then...</p>
<p>The king’s aura flared like a sudden storm, a tangible wave of pressure rolling over the hall. Conversations died in an instant.</p>
<p>"Alastair," the king’s voice thundered, "explain yourself."</p>
<p>The Duke of Greenvale visibly trembled, his complexion draining. But before he could stammer a word, his wife, Ophira, spoke up quickly.</p>
<p>Perhaps too quickly.</p>

<p>Quinlan ignored them. "The slavery spell itself has many advantages."</p>

<p>Even before Quinlan could respond, King Alexios had already flicked his fingers.</p>
