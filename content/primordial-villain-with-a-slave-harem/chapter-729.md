<p>729: War Council</p>
<p>729: War Council</p>
<p>"You’re here, Grim Reaper.</p>
<p>Red-Eye,” Gorruk rumbled, voice deep as ever.</p>
<p>The bearkin chieftain was an imposing sight, even more so now with his hulking form drenched in lionkin gore from head to toe.</p>
<p>Viscera clung to his skin like some grotesque war paint, his claws still dripping with blood.</p>
<p>He didn’t seem to notice, let alone care.</p>
<p>The smell of carnage clung to him like a second skin.</p>
<p>He probably took it as a prestige.</p>
<p>Beside him, I stood with Vex and my girls, our eyes turning to the ruined city of Sunscar.</p>
<p>The settlement had been utterly crushed beneath the joint onslaught of the dogkin and bearkin armies.</p>
<p>Crumbling towers, shattered gates, and blood-slicked streets lay beneath plumes of smoke.</p>
<p>Fires still licked at the sky in the distance, and corpses littered the ruins in every direction: lionkin warriors, their beasts, and the few necromantic remnants from the Covenant who’d been unlucky enough to remain behind.</p>
<p>And yet, this was only the appetizer.</p>
<p>The main meal awaited us ahead.</p>
<p>After an hour’s march, we saw it: Lionheart.</p>
<p>It loomed on the horizon like a sleeping colossus, far too large and too proud for the scorched plains that surrounded it.</p>
<p>Towering white walls glinted under the sun, clean and unbroken, flanked by defensive ballistae, mana turrets, and watchtowers filled with lionkin soldiers.</p>
<p>But it wasn’t just a city.</p>
<p>It was a fortress.</p>
<p>A bastion.</p>
<p>A sanctuary.</p>
<p>And a prison.</p>
<p>Lionheart held over a million people before the war, a number that was surely swollen by retreating civilians and armed forces.</p>
<p>I wouldn’t be shocked to learn that there were now over two million people inside, with hundreds of thousands of combatants among their midst.</p>
<p>We made camp in the fields beyond, joining the other factions who had arrived before us.</p>
<p>The tigerkin had set up in rows of tents to the west, their formations neat, their warriors efficient.</p>
<p>The foxkin camp, by contrast, was more chaotic and colorful, trickster banners flapping with whimsical, mocking designs.</p>
<p>The wolfkin stood to the north in rigid silence, their banners fewer, but their numbers no less impressive.</p>
<p>They were stoic, fierce, and unshaken.</p>
<p>And then they came.</p>
<p>Thunder rolled across the plains, not from the sky, but from the arrival of the Consortium.</p>
<p>Their black banners stretched as far as the eye could see, adorned with the golden eye insignia of the Vesper Consortium.</p>
<p>At the forefront rode Maelstrom, a towering figure cloaked in winds, whose armor was marked by dried blood and fractured runes.</p>
<p>His gaze was grim, surveying the field ahead.</p>
<p>The army behind him, once more than 30,000 strong, had visibly shrunk: roughly 25,000 now.</p>
<p>Many bore visible wounds.</p>
<p>Their armor was scorched and dented, their eyes ringed with fatigue from countless ambushes and skirmishes with the Covenant’s dark forces.</p>
<p>But their steps didn’t falter.</p>
<p>The Consortium had bled, but they had not broken.</p>
<p>As night began to fall, the fires of war camps lit the horizon in every direction.</p>
<p>It was time for the final war council.</p>
<p>A massive pavilion was erected at the center of the encampments, serving as a neutral ground for all sides.</p>
<p>There, the leaders gathered.</p>
<p>Gorruk, wreathed in dried blood and silent menace.</p>
<p>Vex, wrapped in her now cleaned kendo attire, was seated beside me with her usual smug grace.</p>
<p>Maelstrom, standing with subdued wind elemental energies shimmering around his form.</p>
<p>Rajah, the tigerkin leader, eyed me with a clear lack of trust in his gaze.</p>
<p>Silver, the leader of the foxkin people, who, unlike Rajah, glared at me with clear hostility.</p>
<p>Skarn, the wolf boss, was silent, observing all leaders in equal measure.</p>
<p>Lastly, Vargis, who was returning Silver’s glare in my stead.</p>
<p>I would’ve liked to deal with Silver before today, ideally right after I humiliated him by beating his two sons, but I wasn’t presented with such an opportunity.</p>
<p>It wasn’t like I could demand his death, and the other beastkin lords would jump to complete my order.</p>
<p>I had to let him go home from the summit, for he had done no wrong as far as beastkin laws were concerned.</p>
<p>I didn’t know if we would clash in the future, but one thing was for certain: I would have to watch my back so long as I was in Beastman Confederation lands.</p>
<p>Anyhow.</p>
<p>We stood around a wide table strewn with maps, siege plans, and diagrams of Lionheart’s known defenses.</p>
<p>Everyone was watching.</p>
<p>Everyone was waiting.</p>
<p>"So, why exactly are these two present?” the nasty fox asked, dismissively gesturing at me and Vex.</p>
<p>"Maelstrom is the leader of the Vesper Consortium’s forces, Vargis is the leader of the dogkin people, so what right do they have to be here?”</p>
<p>"I don’t need your permission to invite these two people here,” Vargis barked.</p>
<p>"You have your sons present as well—I mean the sons that aren’t with the Goddess or suckling on their thumbs in the recovery room back home after my son-in-law put them in their place.”</p>
<p>"Grr!” A hostile growl sounded from the foxkin leader, eyes narrowing into fiery slits.</p>
<p>He elected to ignore Vargis’s latter statement, addressing the one about his children being present.</p>
<p>"Yes, they’re here, standing loyally behind me just like your two sons standing behind you, not around the table as if they were equal to us!”</p>
<p>Both Vex and I were doing our utmost to hold our snickers back because he was perfectly right.</p>
<p>We had no right to be here, perhaps not even present at the meeting at all.</p>
<p>But…</p>
<p>"I’m not in the mood for this petty back-and-forth game of yours,” Gorruk grumbled in a low tone.</p>
<p>"The Grim Reaper and Red-Eye made all of this possible, without them, we would never have gotten the fuel needed to declare open war against that Leohtar scum.</p>
<p>I don’t care if they stay.”</p>
<p>"Fine by me as long as they don’t interrupt too much,” Skarn said, shrugging his shoulders.</p>
<p>Rajah nodded.</p>
<p>"With this irrelevant matter settled,” Vargis said, giving Silver a side-eyed, mocking glance, “I say we should hear what sort of opposition Maelstrom experienced from the nasty allies of the lionkin.”</p>
