<p>The elves stationed along the hill gasped as one as they witnessed the sight of every woman rising from her seat at the same instant. As soon as the barrier protecting the city came off, laughter cut off mid-breath. Smirks vanished. Postures straightened, spines aligning as if drawn by a single thread.</p>
<p>Then space folded.</p>
<p>A dark seam opened in the air a dozen steps ahead of them, edges curling inward. The man stepped through first. The blonde dogkin followed, pressed close at his side. The elves recognized the pair at once.</p>
<p>Then came the third.</p>
<p>A figure clad head to toe in heavy armor stepped through the gate. Broad pauldrons. Reinforced greaves. A warhammer held at her side. The metal was practical, built to endure rather than impress.</p>
<p>The elves' ears twitched.</p>
<p>Something tugged at their senses. A trace of familiarity that refused to settle. The presence carried a faint resonance they had felt since childhood.</p>
<p>Elf.</p>
<p>But no elf they knew wore armor like that.</p>
<p>No elven warrior would wear such an ugly, masculine thing. It was too cumbersome. Too enclosed. Too far removed from the living world. Heavy armor severed one's connection to Mother Nature, and elves did not tolerate that kind of separation. From the moment they could walk, they were taught to feel the ground beneath their feet. Bare soles against soil, stone, grass. That contact was not traditional for tradition's sake. It was communication. The land whispered warnings, lent balance, guided movement. To encase one's body in layers of dead metal was to willingly go deaf.</p>
<p>Elves were never clumsy creatures. Not even the scholars.</p>
<p>While not every elf was a ranger, every elf was a lithe fighter. Mage, assassin, archer, duelist, even those who claimed to be pure fighters still moved with a grace born from centuries of instinct. Their garbs were light, flexible, cut to flow rather than resist. Fabric and leather that bent with motion. Armor that protected without obstructing. Speed and precision were their creed. Agility was survival.</p>
<p>That woman violated all of it.</p>
<p>Her armor was thick. Reinforced. Built to absorb impact rather than avoid it. Every plate locked into place with brutal purpose. Nothing about it yielded. Nothing invited motion. It was the kind of equipment meant for the clumsy denizens of Iksaris, also known as humans and dwarves, who relied on endurance and brute strength rather than finesse.</p>
<p>So the elves dismissed the thought.</p>
<p>She had to be human.</p>
<p>Why not a halfling? 'Halfling,' the child born from the union of two races, was not exactly a thing on the continent of Iskaris. Tries have been made, of course, but they have not led anywhere. Some scholars argued that it was because these trials took place between a master and a slave, who tended to be unwilling and stressed.</p>
<p>It was observed that animals didn't breed well while in a stressful environment, so they reasoned, why would it be different for humanoids?</p>
<p>But that logic was brought into question by saying that not only should it happen at least rarely, even if the female was under duress, but also, throughout history, there were loving relationships between members of different races.</p>
<p>The Alliance of Elvardia came to be because the elf queen of old fell in love with the dwarf king, and the two decided to join their people in creating the empire they have now. Both of them being on the brink of destruction due to the Vraven Kingdom and the Beastman Confederation certainly helped make the unlikely alliance happen.</p>
<p>However, despite their loving relationship, the elf queen never conceived a child from her husband, and similar outcomes were recorded the few times romance occurred between members of the two races.</p>
<p>This was the main reason why elven women, cursed with having elven men who can't level up and thus die much earlier than their female counterparts, tended to either live life without a partner or find solace in the company of other elven women.</p>
<p>Since they couldn't conceive children from unions with men of other races, who would die sooner than they anyhow, they decided it was for the best to treat their elven men as studs when they felt ready to have kids, but only have romantic relationships with other elven women.</p>
<p>This all explained why the armored woman could not be an elf. No one would ever choose to limit themselves like this.</p>
<p>So the elves turned their full attention back to Quinlan, unaware of how wrong they were. Being a tanker didn't limit Kaelira. In fact, thanks to her new class, which evolved after inhaling Rosie's flower, Kaelira's dream of being the best smith while also protecting her loved ones could be achieved all at once.</p>
<p>After all, being the Runeweaver Titan, she could do both at once.</p>
<p>They shook the thought off and refocused on the tall and ominous man. His mere presence made the elves feel uneasy, especially after learning of who he was and how he thought Elvardia had to prove itself worthy of his alliance, not the other way around.</p>
<p>They found him greatly arrogant, but just as unnerving as well, making them act reserved.</p>
<p>Quinlan stood at the edge of the hill, armor sealed and silent. The wind tugged at his cloak as he looked over his allies. He did not address the women, nor did he give orders. Instead, he merely looked back once, with the red orbs that were his eyes passing over them with ominous intent.</p>
<p>Then he raised one hand.</p>
<p>No chant followed.</p>
<p>No flourish.</p>
<p>The air changed.</p>
<p>A pressure settled over the hill, subtle at first, then unmistakable. Grass bent outward in a slow ripple. Dust lifted from the soil, circling his boots in widening arcs. The elves stiffened as their instincts screamed at them that whatever this being truly was, his relationship with Mother Nature and the four elements was vastly different than what their kind knew.</p>
<p>The breeze deepened. Thickened. It wrapped around the women like unseen arms.</p>
<p>Together, their feet left the ground. Bodies rose in perfect sync, and then the wind surged.</p>
<p>They shot forward as one, silently shooting above the battlefield and streaked toward the city where the barrier had fallen. Smoke rolled up to meet them. Firelight reflected off steel and skin alike. Then they were gone, swallowed by distance and haze.</p>
<p>Only then did the hill breathe again.</p>
<p>Several elves sagged where they stood. One braced both hands against her bow, exhaling slowly. Another rubbed at their chest as if easing a tight knot.</p>
<p>"… I couldn't move," one muttered.</p>
<p>"I've stood before ancient treants," another said quietly. "This was worse."</p>
<p>A third swallowed. "It felt like the sky was watching us."</p>
<p>Their ears flicked, greatly unsettled. "Lady Luminara protect us…" a fourth whispered.</p>
<p>Then one of them frowned. "Wait. That woman. The one with the purple sheen in her eyes."</p>
<p>A pause came as she nearly cried, "Wasn't that the notorious Black Fang?!"</p>
<p>Murmurs spread at once.</p>
<p>"The criminal?"</p>
<p>"One of the three strongest human women alive?"</p>
<p>"Can that be?"</p>
<p>"The pink-haired woman that was next to her! I heard her second-in-command is a short, curvaceous, floral-tattooed, pink-haired human woman!"</p>
<p>"Orianna is the name," one nodded grimly. "I also saw two who fit the two other members of her lieutenant squad, Raika and Vex."</p>
<p>"Why are they following him… Why is that young upstart with her terrifying class not calling the shots?" someone whispered.</p>
<p>To these elves, Black Fang was but a new kid on the block, showcasing the difference between the two races and their perception of time. Black Fang was worried about growing wrinkles, while to the elves, she was a newcomer. Barely an adult.</p>
<p>Another shook their head slowly. "Not just following. She moved when he moved. I'm not sure what's going on between them, but I'm confident she trusts him greatly, just like the rest of his companions do."</p>
<p>Silence fell again.</p>
<p>"That man…" an elf said at last, voice low. "He's terrifying."</p>
<p>No one argued.</p>
<p>"D-do you think he's doing… all of them?"</p>
<p>The question slipped out before the young elf could stop it.</p>
<p>She stood at the back of the sentry line, hands clasped together before her chest, ears tipped forward despite herself. Compared to the others, she was still new to the watch. Her armor fit a little too cleanly. Her bowstring had not yet frayed from years of use.</p>
<p>The older elves turned toward her in unison.</p>
<p>Dry looks met her wide eyes.</p>
<p>"Eilwen…" one of them said flatly, "do not get ideas."</p>
<p>Another snorted. "That path leads to disappointment and a lot of unnecessary drama."</p>
<p>Eilwen's face lit up in a furious shade of red. "I am not getting ideas!" she snapped, stamping a foot. "I'm just asking. So answer me, you old hags!"</p>
