<p>…</p>
<p>Quinlan and his group hovered high in the skies, watching.</p>
<p>Below, the battlefield was in motion.</p>
<p>Undead surged through the lower districts in uneven waves, pushing human defenders out of streets and courtyards that had turned slick with churned dirt and shattered stone. Their movements were ugly but relentless. They climbed over fallen bodies without breaking pace, drawing fire that would have pinned living troops in place.</p>
<p>Outside the walls, the Elvardian army advanced. Dwarven tankers marched at the front, drawing in devastating ranged attacks that would've pulverized those that stood behind them.</p>
<p>On the walls, human defenders scrambled.</p>
<p>Quinlan could see it clearly from above. Crossbow teams rushed into position, hands shaking as they cranked mechanisms that had already overheated once today. Mages leaned over parapets, trying to angle spells downward now that the barrier was gone. Arrows and bolts rained toward the approaching ranks.</p>
<p>Most fell short.</p>
<p>Then the elves answered.</p>
<p>From behind the dwarven lines, volleys rose in brutal arcs. There were no chaotic storms, but clean lines of fire released in staggered timing, each shot placed delicately rather than being thrown and hoping for the best.</p>
<p>Human defenders vanished from the walls in clusters. A figure would rise to aim, then drop out of sight before the string finished vibrating. Spellcasters slumped backward, hands still raised, light fading from unfinished constructs.</p>
<p>Quinlan watched it unfold and let out a slow breath.</p>
<p>"I always knew elves were devastating archers, but seeing this… It puts everything in a new line for me." He'd already seen elven archery, but never a thousand of them standing shoulder to shoulder and releasing pure death in the form of a relentless downpour of devastation.</p>
<p>Sylvaris, the moon mage elf, spoke up suddenly. Her long hair was bound tight for war, and her robes drawn in close so they would not interfere. Her eyes followed the volleys as she explained,</p>
<p>"Elves live long lives. Long enough to repeat the same motion millions of times. Drawing. Anchoring. Release. Correction. Again. Furthermore, we elves are serene, stationary people by nature. This allows us to practice our craft with extraordinary patience. Where members of other races would've grown bored and burned out of repeating the same motions, we feel at peace."</p>
<p>Another volley fell. Another stretch of wall went quiet.</p>
<p>"Most of us have a natural inclination for ranged combat," she continued. "Balance, perception, spatial awareness. When you give people like that thousands of years to train, combined with extreme amounts of patience, you don't get mere archers. You get instruments of death."</p>
<p>Quinlan glanced at her.</p>
<p>She did not look away from the battlefield.</p>
<p>"Faster release cycles," Sylvaris went on. "Tighter groupings. Better judgment of wind, distance, and movement. Other races compensate with numbers or power. We refine."</p>
<p>A ladder reached the wall. Dwarven shock troops rushed up behind their shields. Above them, an elven volley cut down the defenders trying to push the ladder back.</p>
<p>Sylvaris finally allowed herself a small lift of her chin.</p>
<p>"I may be a mage who can't even handle a bow without embarrassing myself, but I am proud of my people."</p>
<p>"And for a good reason," Quinlan nodded with a fond smile. He was amazed by this display as well. Based on the sight, so were most of his allies.</p>
<p>"Now then, ladies. Let's get to work, shall we?"</p>
<p>They answered without words. A shift in stance. A tightening of grips. One by one, they nodded.</p>
<p>Sylvaris spoke again.</p>
<p>"Prodigious Son. I have a suggestion. If we repeat the same strategies too often, our enemies will adapt."</p>
<p>Quinlan turned his head toward her, and he smiled, already aware of what she had in mind.</p>
<p>"I know," he replied. "Go ahead, mother-in-law."</p>
<p>For the briefest moment, she blinked. But no protest followed. Seraphiel might not carry his mark in any formal sense yet, but the bond was already settled in quieter ways. In their hearts, those two have been engaged for months now.</p>
<p>Sylvaris raised both hands.</p>
<p>Silver light gathered around her fingers, dense and controlled, flowing outward in clean lines rather than bursts. The air bent as shapes took form. One after another, constructs assembled themselves. They sported smooth frames of pale magic and wings unfolded from their backs. Winged mounts, long-bodied and stable, built to carry riders rather than for speed alone.</p>
<p>The girls moved without needing instruction.</p>
<p>Each took a seat, balance natural, knees locking in as if they had done this countless times before. They had. This was not a new tactic. It was a practiced one.</p>
<p>Aurora cast next.</p>
<p>She lifted her staff and drew a wide arc through the air. Light spread outward and settled over the group, forming a layered barrier that moved with them.</p>
<p>Flying shock troops.</p>
<p>Fast. Protected. Able to strike, reposition, and strike again before ground forces could respond.</p>
<p>They were incredibly deadly.</p>
<p>Sylvaris lowered her hands as the last construct stabilized.</p>
<p>"See you later, Quin!"</p>
<p>"Have fun becoming the person you want to be!"</p>
<p>"Have no regrets!"</p>
<p>"I love you!"</p>
<p>The girls cheerily said their goodbyes, many blowing him kisses or just offering loving smiles.</p>
<p>Then they leaned forward, mounts angling down, wings biting into the air. In seconds, they peeled away from Quinlan, forming silver shapes that were streaking toward the walls.</p>
<p>Quinlan watched them go until distance swallowed their forms.</p>
<p>Only he remained, hovering with the Black Fang gang at his side.</p>
<p>"Why did she not summon horsies for us?" Vex whimpered. "Such a cruel auntie!"</p>
<p>"Because I told her not to," Quinlan replied with a nonchalant voice.</p>
<p>"Huh? Why did you do that, Hubby?" Vex gasped. "She can create four more horsies easily! It wouldn't tax her too much."</p>
<p>"Because horsies reduce the effectiveness of you four ancient ladies. My girls have been training for combat using Sylvaris's moonlit constructs, but you four have been fighting together for over a hundred years, while Black Fang, Orianna, and Raika have never ridden a horsie like that before."</p>
<p>"You ancient ladies."</p>
<p>That was as far as the sentence got.</p>
<p>As far as the women were concerned, the rest of Quinlan's explanation vanished into the void. Or, rather, it never even existed.</p>
<p>Vex's ears twitched once. Orianna's expression went very still. And Black Fang… The Venomborne Terror in her frame was seeping out.</p>
<p>The women slowly turned their heads toward him.</p>
<p>Their eyes burned with the unified, concentrated fury of offended femininity.</p>
<p>"Ancient?" Vex repeated softly.</p>
<p>Quinlan felt it immediately. The pressure. The silent, suffocating judgment. He cleared his throat.</p>
<p>"What I meant was-"</p>
<p>Too late.</p>
<p>The glares sharpened. Three of them weren't even blinking anymore.</p>
<p>Raika, meanwhile, looked between them, then up at Quinlan, completely unbothered.</p>
<p>"Then what? I hope you don't expect me to sit this one out just because I can't ride horses. If that's the case, you'd better let me down before I begin swinging."</p>
<p>Her raven hair whipped around her face as she leaned forward, eyes bright, fists already clenching. "I hate watching without partaking."</p>
<p>Quinlan's lips curved.</p>
<p>"You, dearest Raika," he said, tone suddenly pleased, "have hit the nail right on the head."</p>
<p>She blinked. "What do you-"</p>
