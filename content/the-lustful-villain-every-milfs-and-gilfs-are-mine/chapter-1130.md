<p>Rex stood frozen between them, the silence of the training yard amplifying the distant sounds of other soldiers preparing for war, the metallic clang of weapons being sharpened, and the rhythmic shouts of drills. His mind was a battlefield of its own.</p>
<p>On one side lay the cold, tactical calculus: two pregnant women in a high-intensity magical conflict were liabilities, potential casualties that could shatter his entire operational strategy.</p>
<p>On the other side lay the brutal reality of leadership: if he stripped these two of their agency, he would break the very spirit that made them the elite forces they were.</p>
<p>Their stubbornness wasn’t just a flaw; it was their greatest asset, the same unyielding steel that had seen them through countless horrors.</p>
<p>He studied them, his gaze hard enough to cut through their defiance. He saw the fire in Iris’s eyes—the same fire that had survived Veylor’s death and the unshakable duty in Aurelia’s stance.</p>
<p>"Reserve positions only," Rex said finally.</p>
<p>The words were clipped, leaving no room for the emotional appeal they were clearly hoping for. "That is the only term under which you participate."</p>
<p>"Both of you will remain with the secondary formation, positioned well behind the primary line."</p>
<p>"You will not be engaging in direct melee unless the formation collapses... No closer than absolutely necessary."</p>
<p>He stepped closer, his shadow falling over them both, his voice dropping into a dangerous register. "If the situation shifts, if the magical feedback becomes too volatile, if the Underlayer forces breach the perimeter, or if anything puts either of you at genuine risk, you retreat immediately."</p>
<p>"No arguments... No heroics... No ’one last strike’ to prove your worth."</p>
<p>"If you disobey that order, I will personally drag you from the field... I am not negotiating that condition."</p>
<p>Iris’s jaw tightened so hard the tendons in her neck stood out like cords. Her hands curled into fists at her sides, the knuckles whitening against her leather gloves.</p>
<p>She looked like she wanted to spit in his face, to prove that she was more dangerous than any risk he imagined. But the assassin in her was the part of her that understood survival better than anyone knew the tactical truth.</p>
<p>She nodded once, a sharp, jerky motion that betrayed her simmering frustration.</p>
<p>Aurelia, however, let out a breath she seemed to have been holding for an eternity. Her shoulders dropped fractionally, the rigid commander giving way to the relieved woman beneath.</p>
<p>"Understood, Rex," she said softly. "We will hold the rear..."</p>
<p>"We will be the anvil when the hammer fails."</p>
<p>The tension in the yard didn’t vanish; it merely changed shape, shifting from confrontation to a grim, shared understanding. Rex closed the remaining distance between them.</p>
<p>The harshness in his expression didn’t disappear, but it softened around the edges as he reached out.</p>
<p>He rested one hand gently against Iris’s belly, feeling the faint, almost imperceptible flutter of life beneath the leather and steel. Then, he moved his hand to Aurelia’s, the gesture slow and deliberate.</p>
<p>For a moment, the training yard, the looming war, and the political machinations of the Academy ceased to exist. There was only the heat of their skin and the terrifying weight of what they represented.</p>
<p>"You have no idea," he whispered, his voice so low it was almost lost to the wind, carrying a raw vulnerability he allowed no one else to see. "How much both of you and what you’re carrying matter to everything I’m building."</p>
<p>"You aren’t pieces on a board to be sacrificed for a better position..."</p>
<p>"You’re people... You’re MY people."</p>
<p>"And I will burn this entire world to ash before I let anything happen to you."</p>
<p>The confession landed between them like a physical blow. Iris’s carefully constructed mask of fury cracked; her lips trembled for a heartbeat before she pressed them together to maintain her composure, though her eyes betrayed a sudden, wet brightness.</p>
<p>Aurelia’s breathing faltered, her eyes glistening with an emotion she fought to blink away, refusing to let a single tear fall in the face of the coming slaughter.</p>
<p>Neither spoke. Words would have cheapened the moment.</p>
<p>Instead, the shift in their posture spoke volumes of the defiance that had drained away, replaced by a profound, terrifyingly loyal trust. They weren’t just following an order anymore; they were bound to him by something far more dangerous than any military contract.</p>
<p>The remainder of the afternoon dissolved into a blur of calculated intimacy and strategic farewells, scattered like blood droplets across the marble corridors of Aethelgard.</p>
<p>Each encounter was brief, each one a deliberate stitch in the web Rex was weaving, ensuring that every woman who held power or potential power felt the weight of his presence before the chaos began.</p>
<p>He found Elaris near the household gates, the late afternoon sun casting long, skeletal shadows across the courtyard.</p>
<p>She approached him with that familiar, playful smirk, her usual teasing warmth dancing across her features, but Rex could see the tightness around her eyes. The performance was flawless, but beneath the surface, her voice held a serrated edge of genuine concern.</p>
<p>"Try not to get yourself killed, Rex," she murmured, leaning in close enough that her breath ghosted against his ear, making the warning feel like a caress. "Elizabeth and Diana are already nervous enough."</p>
<p>"If you don’t come back in one piece, I’ll never forgive you for making me babysit." Her tone was light, but the unspoken plea to stay alive for us hung between them like a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>Later, as he navigated a crowded hallway lined with nobles and sycophants, his gaze collided with Elizabeth’s. She was surrounded by admirers, a vision of composed elegance, but when her eyes locked onto his, the noise of the crowd seemed to fall away into a vacuum.</p>
<p>No words were exchanged; they couldn’t afford to, but the look she gave him was loaded with a devastating private understanding.</p>
<p>She knew the stakes; she knew that beneath the veneer of duty and political alliance, they were all walking into a crucible that would melt away everything they believed they knew about themselves. Her silence was a confession of shared fate.</p>
<p>By the time evening bled into a bruised purple twilight, the gathering point for departure had transformed into a hive of controlled violence.</p>
<p>The air was thick with the metallic tang of oiled mail, the sulfurous bite of alchemical reagents, and the low, ominous murmur of thousands of soldiers preparing for slaughter.</p>
<p>Boots crunched on gravel; weapons clattered against shields with bone-deep echoes.</p>
<p>Rex stood alone at the periphery of the assembling mass, a predator observing his pack. He watched the sheer, terrifying scale of what he had engineered.</p>
<p>His eyes scanned the formations, cataloging faces, noting positions, and measuring heartbeats. Almost every woman who mattered to his grand design, whether bound to him by genuine, raw affection or by the cold, calculated threads of strategic cultivation, was present.</p>
<p>They were all there, each one a vital component in a machine he had spent months constructing with surgical precision.</p>
<p>’They think they know what this is,’ Rex thought, his inner voice as cold and detached as a blade. ’They think this is a war of conquest, a defense of the city, a fight for honor.’</p>
<p>A dark, almost imperceptible smile touched his lips. ’They have no idea they are merely players in a masterpiece of deception.’</p>
<p>’They think they are fighting for their lives, but they are fighting for my vision.’</p>
<p>None of them, not even Elizabeth, grasped the true depth of the performance they were about to execute.</p>
<p>They didn’t realize that every move they made, every drop of blood they spilled, was a rehearsed note in a symphony he had written alone in the dark. They were the instruments; he was the conductor.</p>
<p>There was a grim, quiet satisfaction that settled in his chest as he watched the flickering torchlight play across the steel of their armor. It was the satisfaction of a craftsman who sees his work nearly complete, a predator who has successfully baited the trap.</p>
<p>He allowed himself to savor that feeling—the intoxicating power of absolute control—just before the cacophony of logistics and the approaching dawn pulled him back from his private triumph and forced him toward the final preparations for the slaughter to come.</p>
