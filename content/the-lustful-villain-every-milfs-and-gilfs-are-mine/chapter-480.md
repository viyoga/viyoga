<p>Rex understood Valentina’s movement toward the door as a signal that the meeting had concluded, prompting him to stand and follow her. She opened it herself and walked out into the corridor, which was not the usual procedure, and Rex understood that she was going to continue something rather than end it.</p>
<p>She stopped when she saw Diana and Lily waiting near the corridor junction.</p>
<p>The reunion was brief and had the specific quality of something that didn’t need to be long to be complete. Valentina held Lily’s face in her hands for a moment and said something low enough that Rex couldn’t hear it.</p>
<p>Diana was looked at with the evaluating pride of someone who had observed a person develop into their capabilities over many years.</p>
<p>"I need ten minutes with Elizabeth," Valentina said and looked at Rex. "Would you mind?"</p>
<p>"Of course not," Rex said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was found at the far end of the corridor, where she had positioned herself close enough to be available and far enough to give Rex the space he’d asked for. She looked at Rex as he approached, and her expression conveyed the question she was not asking aloud.</p>
<p>"She wants ten minutes," Rex said. "It’s fine."</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at him for a moment longer, reading whatever she read there. Then she followed Valentina back into the office, and the door closed.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the corridor with Diana and Lily and thought about the name Celestina Von Starlight sitting in Valentina’s processing infrastructure right now and how the specific shock he’d seen cross her expression before she controlled it told him more about what he needed to know than anything Kregg had said in the canyon.</p>
<p>There was history there—complicated, personal, and long-standing. It was the kind of history that people carry quietly for decades.</p>
<p>"What happened in there?" Lily said.</p>
<p>"Expedition debrief," Rex said. "Standard."</p>
<p>Lily looked at him with the expression she always used when he was giving her the accurate-but-not-complete version of something. She filed it.</p>
<p>She would ask later, at a moment she considered appropriate, and he would give her more of it than he was giving her now, though not all of it.</p>
<p>Diana said nothing, which was its own version of the same position.</p>
<p>They waited.</p>
<p>It was nearly thirty minutes before the door opened again, and Elizabeth emerged with a familiar expression. Rex had anticipated the composed, post-processed demeanor of someone who had navigated a challenging conversation and emerged with the resolution they required.</p>
<p>It wasn’t comfortable or easy, but it was resolved.</p>
<p>She stopped in front of Rex.</p>
<p>"She forgave the Key situation," Elizabeth said. "Officially, the field conditions made the risk assessment reasonable."</p>
<p>She paused. "Unofficially, she was angrier than she appeared, and she expressed it in the room, and it’s done."</p>
<p>"Okay," Rex said. "At least that’s good enough."</p>
<p>"She authorized the recovery analysis," Elizabeth said. "Full resource access."</p>
<p>"She wants results within six months."</p>
<p>"We’ll have them," Rex said. ’It seems like Valentina really did keep her word.’</p>
<p>’Good... not like some bitch I know.’</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at him for a moment, her expression resembling that of someone making a decision, and then she did something unexpected that Rex had not predicted with his usual precision, which was informative in itself. She put her arms around him briefly, the quick, sincere embrace of someone who is genuinely grateful and is expressing it through the most direct available channel.</p>
<p>Diana and Lily saw that they both jumped together full of excitement without having Elizabeth notice what they were doing. And Rex himself felt confused that she just did something that even his foresight couldn’t predict.</p>
<p>’This is... surprising.’</p>
<p>"Thank you," she said, close enough that it was for him and not for the corridor.</p>
<p>Rex accepted this without making it into more than it was.</p>
<p>"You’re welcome," he said, which was honest.</p>
<p>She stepped back, and Diana and Lily were both still watching with the particular expressions of people who have observed something they will discuss at a later time.</p>
<p>"Rex is willing to continue the analysis work," Elizabeth said to Diana and Lily, with the professional framing that was her natural register for this kind of thing. "Which means he’ll be around in the household longer."</p>
<p>"This is convenient because the household is near the Academy’s resources."</p>
<p>"Hah, yeah, very convenient," Diana said.</p>
<p>"The timing works well," Lily said, with the specific warmth of someone whose face was not quite as composed as she thought it was. "And I love it~!"</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at Rex. "Will you walk me partway?"</p>
<p>"I need to stop by the review office before I go to the household."</p>
<p>"Sure," Rex said.</p>
<p>Diana and Lily said their goodbyes with the easy warmth of people who knew they would see everyone soon, and Rex walked beside Elizabeth as she moved through the academy’s ground floor toward the review wing.</p>
<p>She was quiet until they had cleared the main staircase and turned into the side corridor that led toward the administrative wing, where foot traffic thinned out enough that a conversation in a low voice would not carry.</p>
<p>Then she said, still looking ahead, "She told me."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her.</p>
<p>"Not the name," Elizabeth said, carefully. "She didn’t say it aloud."</p>
<p>"She asked me how much you had told me about the intelligence piece you brought to her directly, and I told her the review had been thorough, and she looked at me for a long moment, and then she said—" Elizabeth paused, choosing the words exactly. "She said that there is a piece of what you gave her that does not leave this room."</p>
<p>"Meaning her office. Meaning not me, not the institutional review, and not anyone else until she decides otherwise."</p>
<p>Her voice stayed level, but the effort it took to keep it that way was audible to Rex in the way he read people.</p>
<p>"She told you that directly," Rex said.</p>
<p>"She wanted me to understand the scope," Elizabeth said. "She wanted me to know that the intelligence review I’m authorized to run has a ceiling and that it was set in that meeting before I walked in."</p>
<p>She glanced at him once, briefly, still walking. "Whatever you gave her, Rex, she received it the way people receive things that change the shape of everything they thought they understood."</p>
<p>"I’ve known my mother for twenty-three years, and I have seen her surprised exactly twice. Today was the third time."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>"I’m not asking you to tell me what it was," Elizabeth said. "I understood this morning when you went in alone that there was a reason for that, and I’ve accepted the reason even without knowing its content."</p>
<p>She paused. "I’m telling you what she said because I think you should know that she has now communicated to me, explicitly, that the boundary exists."</p>
<p>"Which means she considers me informed about the boundary."</p>
<p>"Which indicates she trusts you to uphold it," Rex replied.</p>
<p>"Yes," Elizabeth said. "And so do you, I assume."</p>
<p>"I do," Rex said.</p>
<p>She nodded once, in the way she nodded when she had confirmed something she already believed but needed stated plainly.</p>
<p>"Then we understand each other," she said. "The recovery analysis proceeds."</p>
<p>"Everything that comes through it goes through standard channels except what Valentina has designated as separate, and we don’t discuss the separate piece in any context that isn’t this one."</p>
<p>"Agreed," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Good," she said, and the word carried the weight of a door closing—not against something, but around something. It was the distinct closure of someone who has taken a challenging situation and organized it into a manageable form, now prepared to work within those confines.</p>
