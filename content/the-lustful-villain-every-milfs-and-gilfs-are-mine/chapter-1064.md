<p>Rex met Kaeleth’s perceptual equivalent of eyes, two blazing vortices of chronological data that burned with the fury of a collapsing star. For a moment, the two stood locked in a silent, terrifying standoff: the ancient architect of eternity versus the anomaly that had learned to speak his language.</p>
<p>"The contract is over," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice didn’t carry emotion; it carried finality. "The leverage you thought you held has evaporated."</p>
<p>"Celestina’s operational network in the Underlayer has been systematically erased."</p>
<p>"Her surface network is being dismantled as we speak... The time loop architecture you relied upon to cage this reality has collapsed into static."</p>
<p>Rex took another step, the golden floor cracking beneath his weight.</p>
<p>"And you?" Rex’s voice dropped to a predator’s purr. "You are standing in your own sanctum, naked before a mortal who has not only survived your wrath but has learned to breathe your air."</p>
<p>"I have adapted to everything your domain produces."</p>
<p>"Your power is no longer your weapon... it is my fuel."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t raise his hands. He didn’t need to.</p>
<p>He simply reached out with his mind and seized the fundamental concept of weight.</p>
<p>[Gravity Manipulation: Active.]</p>
<p>This was not the blunt force trauma of a meteor strike. This was something far more intimate and excruciating.</p>
<p>Rex applied a targeted force differential, a precision pressure designed not to shatter the surface but to find the exact frequency of the soul register anchor.</p>
<p>Every divine entity requires a metaphysical "hook" to maintain its coherence and authority within its own domain; it is the nail that holds its divinity to its throne.</p>
<p>Rex found that nail. And he began to press down.</p>
<p>"I am not here to play judge, Kaeleth," Rex said as the first sounds of divine distress began to echo through the domain. "The divine framework’s political consequences for your code violation of the tribunal, the sanctions, and the stripping of your authority will be handled by the framework’s governance structure."</p>
<p>"That is bureaucracy. That is not my domain."</p>
<p>A wet, grinding sound reverberated through the amber void.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s form began to buckle. The golden symbols that composed the God’s body began to warp and bend unnaturally, folding inward like metal under a hydraulic press.</p>
<p>The divine light flickered violently, casting jagged, strobing shadows across Rex’s impassive face.</p>
<p>"What is my domain," Rex continued, his voice cutting through the shriek of protesting reality, "is the specific operational relationship between your agenda and Aethelgard."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes flared with a cold, murderous light. He didn’t just increase the power; he tightened the grip.</p>
<p>The pressure intensified exponentially.</p>
<p>The sound changed from grinding to something more visceral—the sound of something fundamental breaking. It was the sound of divine bones snapping, not made of calcium, but of crystallized time and solidified will.</p>
<p>Cracks appeared across Kaeleth’s manifestation, bleeding not red blood, but raw, uncontained temporal energy that sizzled like acid where it touched the floor.</p>
<p>The God’s form began to compress, his massive presence forced into a smaller and smaller space, the divine essence screaming as it was crushed against itself.</p>
<p>Rex watched the god’s agony with the detached interest of a scientist observing a reaction. He felt the feedback through his own senses—the way the gravity warred with the temporal substrate—but he remained unmoved.</p>
<p>"That relationship," Rex said, his words punctuated by the wet crunch of Kaeleth’s collapsing divinity, "is going to end."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t just press the pressure; he drove it home. He channeled his full telekinetic force directly into the soul register’s anchor point, the metaphysical carotid artery of a god.</p>
<p>The reaction from the domain was cataclysmic. Because Rex was addressing the structural foundation rather than the operational surface, the domain didn’t just react; it convulsed.</p>
<p>The amber gold reality began to fracture, not like breaking glass, but like tectonic plates grinding together. The divine architecture was being compressed beyond its design parameters, forced into a density that reality itself refused to permit.</p>
<p>The sound was a continuous, low-frequency shriek of reality being crushed.</p>
<p>Kaeleth, sensing his very existence slipping into the void of compression, unleashed a working. A torrent of temporal distortions accelerated entropy and reversed causality, slamming into Rex, attempting to age him into dust and unmake him into nothingness simultaneously.</p>
<p>[Adaptation engaged.]</p>
<p>Rex’s body twisted, his muscles corded like steel cables as his cellular structure reorganized mid-assault. He absorbed the entropy, neutralized the reversal, and stood unmoved.</p>
<p>Kaeleth roared a sound that was less a voice and more the grinding of centuries and produced a second working.</p>
<p>This one was a spatial severance, a series of temporal blades designed to cut through the concept of ’distance’ to slice Rex apart from within.</p>
<p>[Adaptation engaged.]</p>
<p>Rex’s nervous system flickered as he remapped his own spatial coordinates, dancing through the blades of time as if they were mere shadows.</p>
<p>Desperate, Kaeleth reached deeper than he ever had before. He summoned a third working, the domain’s native expression, which was the most sophisticated and devastating mechanism available to a God of Time.</p>
<p>It was a complete collapse of the local timeline, a singularity of ’Now’ that intended to freeze Rex in a moment of eternal, excruciating stasis while his body was torn apart by the sheer force of time trying to exist in two places at once.</p>
<p>Rex met the singularity head-on. His vision blurred as the first contact effect tore through his consciousness.</p>
<p>For four point two seconds, Rex existed in a state of pure agony as his organs felt themselves being pulled across millennia.</p>
<p>His bones creaked and splintered under the temporal shear, blood spraying from his nose and mouth as his capillaries burst under the strain of existing outside of linear time.</p>
<p>’Faster,’ Rex thought through the white noise of pain. ’I need to be faster than time itself.’</p>
<p>[First Contact Effect: Absorbed.]</p>
<p>[Temporal Singularity: Analyzed.]</p>
<p>[Adaptation: Complete.]</p>

<p>"Last category," Rex said, his voice dripping with the satisfaction of a predator who has cornered a wounded animal. "Same as Theo."</p>
<p>He began to close the remaining distance. Each step was a death knell.</p>

<p>Every temporal blade, every entropic wave, and every spatial distortion—Rex had cataloged them all. His coverage list was no longer a collection of defenses; it was a complete map of Kaeleth’s entire arsenal.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s divine eyes widened. For the first time in two thousand years, the God felt something he had forgotten existed: helplessness.</p>
<p>He threw everything he had left, every last shred of his authority, and every hidden mechanism of his domain at Rex.</p>
<p>He unleashed chronal storms, causality loops, and reality-warping distortions that would have obliterated entire civilizations.</p>
<p>But Rex walked through them as if walking through a light rain.</p>
<p>Every attack hit Rex’s adaptive field, and he died. Every divine mechanism was met with the perfect counter frequency. The God of Time was firing bullets at a man who had already learned how to become a bullet.</p>
<p>Kaeleth had run out of new mechanisms. He was standing naked before a mortal who held the blueprint to his entire existence.</p>
