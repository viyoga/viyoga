<p>"I am aware of the infection," Kaeleth said, the vibration of his voice carrying a note of weary disgust.</p>
<p>"My organization’s current methodology is failing against that density," Celestina said. "We operate on surgical precision: identify, profile, and eliminate."</p>
<p>"We kill them in the shadows, ensuring our existence remains a myth. But surgery fails when you are dealing with a tumor that has already claimed the entire organ."</p>
<p>"If we continue to pick off targets one by one in Aethelgard, we will inevitably trigger a coordinated response."</p>
<p>"The reincarnators will realize they are being hunted, they will unite, and they will burn my organization to ash."</p>
<p>"And the destruction of your organization would create a cascade of unmanaged anomalies," Kaeleth observed. "The wound would fester beyond repair."</p>
<p>"Exactly," Celestina said. "Which is why I need to stop treating them as individual targets."</p>
<p>"I need to treat them as a contained system."</p>
<p>"Speak," Kaeleth commanded. "Show me the shape of your madness."</p>
<p>Celestina began to speak, and as she did, her mind raced through the intricate, terrifying blueprints she had constructed over years of sleepless nights. She described the time loop architecture not as a simple circle but as a complex, multi-layered temporal cage.</p>
<p>She explained how she would lock Aethelgard’s entire population into a defined period, a controlled segment of time that could be managed, monitored, and, most importantly, reset at the whim of the operator.</p>
<p>She detailed the reset condition: a trigger tied to the Apostle network’s psychological state, a divine tripwire that would snap the loop shut the moment the variables deviated from her plan.</p>
<p>She described how the working would be seamless, invisible even to those trapped within it, because temporal manipulation at the foundational level does not feel like an external force; it feels like destiny.</p>
<p>"There is a fatal flaw in your calculus," Kaeleth interrupted, the divine weight suddenly intensifying until Celestina’s teeth began to ache. "A working of this magnitude cannot simply exist in a vacuum... It must displace reality."</p>
<p>"If a population’s sense of elapsed time is severed from the natural passage of days, they will notice."</p>
<p>"They will feel the hollow ache where their history should be."</p>
<p>"They will begin to ask why the sun rises but the years do not turn, and they will notice the absence."</p>
<p>Celestina felt a bead of sweat roll down her temple. ’Don’t break now... Not when you can taste the victory.’</p>
<p>"I anticipated that concern," she said, her voice steady despite the hammering in her chest. "I spent three years accounting for the perception of absence."</p>
<p>"Then give me your answer, mortal..."</p>
<p>"How do you prevent them from realizing they are living in a lie?"</p>
<p>"The working does not manufacture a false sense of time passing," Celestina said, her eyes narrowing as she delivered the killing blow to his doubt. "It simply removes their ability to measure it with certainty."</p>
<p>"There is a profound psychological difference. A man who believes six months have passed when only six weeks have passed is a man who will notice the discrepancy the moment he speaks to an outsider."</p>
<p>"He will see the contradiction and panic. But a man who simply cannot say how long it has been?"</p>
<p>"A man who reaches for a date and finds the number soft, blurred at the edges?"</p>
<p>She watched the divine presence hesitate.</p>
<p>"Uncertainty about duration is not a foreign concept to mortals," she pressed on. "We lose ourselves in time constantly."</p>
<p>"Grief blurs the weeks... War erases the months... A prolonged crisis makes days bleed into one another without any divine hand involved..."</p>
<p>"I am not giving them a lie; I am giving them a fog."</p>
<p>"You propose to weaponize ordinary human confusion," Kaeleth murmured, the vibration shifting to something closer to respect. "You wish to make the supernatural feel mundane."</p>
<p>"Precisely," Celestina said. "No one in Aethelgard will stop mid-stride to demand the exact date from the sky."</p>
<p>"They will reach for an approximation, ’six months’ or ’nearly a year,’ and that approximation will hold because nothing in their immediate sensory experience will contradict it."</p>
<p>"The loop doesn’t need to convince them that time has passed; it only needs to ensure they never feel desperate enough to seek an exact answer."</p>
<p>"And when the desperate inevitably come looking?" Kaeleth asked, his voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "When the scholars and the paranoid demand proof?"</p>
<p>"Then they will find the proof they seek," Celestina said with a cold smile. "They will find records... even journals like the academy archives with dated entries."</p>
<p>"The loop’s architecture will produce internally consistent documentation as a fundamental side effect."</p>
<p>"The calendars will align, the ledgers will balance, and the correspondence will flow in perfect chronological order."</p>
<p>"The paper will agree with itself, even if the paper contradicts the true number of days passing outside the radius."</p>
<p>"Mortals have always been more willing to trust a dated document than their own faltering memories. I am counting on that fundamental weakness."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was not empty; it was a pressurized chamber. Kaeleth did not respond immediately.</p>
<p>Instead, the divine presence seemed to withdraw slightly, not in retreat, but in calculation, the way a predator might pause to weigh the lethality of a new prey. The temporal vibrations ceased their aggressive assault, leaving Celestina standing in a ringing quiet that felt more dangerous than the storm.</p>
<p>"That is more sophisticated than any deception I expected from a mortal," Kaeleth finally spoke.</p>
<p>His voice was no longer a crushing weight but a cold, razor-sharp edge that cut through the air. "When you first approached me, you asked for a work that would go unnoticed."</p>
<p>"I expected a veil, a simple shroud of illusion. But you are not asking me to lie to them."</p>
<p>The pressure around Celestina’s temples eased just enough for her to draw a steadying breath.</p>
<p>"You are asking me to amputate their ability to perceive reality," he continued. "And you are asking me to remove the one tool they would need to expose the lie themselves."</p>
<p>"You aren’t hiding the truth; you are dismantling the compass they would use to find it."</p>
<p>"Yes," Celestina said, her voice gaining a harder, more clinical edge. "I have no interest in the crude mechanics of deception."</p>
<p>"Lying creates vulnerabilities and contradictions that must be maintained, patterns that can be spotted, and threads that can be pulled."</p>
<p>"A lie is a wound that requires constant tending. But removing their capacity for precise measurement? That is clean and that is... absolute."</p>
<p>"Nobody has to be told something false; they simply cease to be equipped to verify what is true."</p>
<p>"Even the reincarnators," Kaeleth said, the vibration of his voice carrying a sudden, ominous warning. "You speak as if this is a net that catches everything, but you forget their nature."</p>
<p>"A reincarnator’s entire existence in this world is built upon the fact that they arrived from somewhere else."</p>
<p>"They carry a different clock in their minds, a different rhythm in their souls."</p>
<p>"Their relationship to time is already fractured, already foreign... How do you plan to cage something that already exists outside of linear causality?"</p>
<p>Celestina felt a flicker of something not fear but the adrenaline of a gambler seeing the opponent reveal their hand. She had anticipated this.</p>
<p>"I am counting on that fracture, Kaeleth," she said, stepping forward until she was nearly touching the epicenter of his divine aura. "That is exactly why it will work."</p>
<p>"A native of this world who feels their sense of time slipping might panic and investigate. But a reincarnator...?"</p>
<p>"A reincarnator who feels their internal sense of duration drifting out of alignment with the world around them will not suspect a divine working."</p>
<p>"They will suspect themselves."</p>
<p>Her mind flashed to the faces of the forty-three; she had already eliminated the confusion in their eyes when they realized their powers couldn’t save them from a world that no longer made sense.</p>
<p>"They already live with the expectation that this world feels wrong at the edges," Celestina pressed, her words coming faster now, fueled by the conviction of three years of obsession. "They expect dissonance..."</p>
<p>"They expect to feel like foreign bodies in a strange anatomy."</p>
<p>"When the temporal fog rolls in, they won’t look to the sky for answers; they will look inward, blaming their own disorientation on the strangeness of their own nature."</p>
<p>"I am not inventing a new fear; I am simply cloaking my working in an expectation they already possess."</p>
<p>"That is cruelty dressed as efficiency," Kaeleth observed.</p>
<p>There was no moral outrage in his tone, no divine condemnation. It was merely the flat, terrifyingly objective observation of a scientist naming a new phenomenon.</p>
<p>Celestina didn’t flinch. She didn’t apologize.</p>
<p>"It is an efficiency that happens to be cruel."</p>
<p>Her inner voice screamed at the coldness of her own words, but she suppressed it with iron discipline. ’Emotions are variables, and variables create errors... Errors kill.’</p>
<p>"I am not going to pretend the distinction matters to the people trapped within the loop," she said aloud, meeting the void where his eyes would be. "Their suffering and their confusion will be real regardless of how I label it. But it matters to me."</p>
<p>"Because I would rather build something precise than something merely vicious, and precision is what I am offering you."</p>
<p>"I am offering you a way to fix the wound without ever having to touch the flesh."</p>
