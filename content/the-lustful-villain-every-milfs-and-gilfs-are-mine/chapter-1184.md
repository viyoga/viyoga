<p>Rex let the silence stretch, thick and poisonous, watching with predator-like focus as each name landed like a fresh blade twisting in Apollo’s chest.</p>
<p>He could see the gears turning behind Apollo’s bloodshot eyes, the horrifying realization dawning as the list moved from strangers to allies to something far more intimate.</p>
<p>"And your mother," Rex finished, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial murmur that somehow carried further than his earlier screams. "Amelia."</p>
<p>The name hung between them like a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>The color drained from Apollo’s face in slow, nauseating degrees, leaving him a ghastly, waxen pale beneath the dried blood spatter. His mouth opened, his throat working convulsively, but no sound came out, only a pathetic, wet wheeze.</p>
<p>The divine energy still clinging to his skin didn’t flare with power; instead, it shuddered and flickered like a dying lamp, responding to the full body tremor that now wracked his frame.</p>
<p>Every ounce of certainty that had carried Apollo through the slaughter, every conviction that had fueled his righteous fury, collapsed instantly into a hollow, directionless despair. The path to the vengeance he craved—the singular purpose that had kept him upright while his world burned—had just been revealed as a trap.</p>
<p>To kill Rex, he would not simply be a liberator or a judge; he would be a butcher. He would have to become the very monster he sought to destroy, systematically executing every woman he had ever protected, every friend he had ever held dear, ending with the mother who had taught him what kindness was.</p>
<p>’No... no, that’s impossible... he’s lying... he has to be lying...’ Apollo’s mind shrieked, but there was no comfort to be found in his own thoughts.</p>
<p>His intellect, sharp and divine, betrayed him by confirming the truth. Rex wasn’t bluffing.</p>
<p>The metaphysical tether was real. The cost was absolute.</p>
<p>Apollo tried, once, to find an argument against it. His lips moved silently for several seconds, his brain frantically scanning for a flaw in the logic, a loophole, a hidden weakness in Rex’s claim that would allow him to keep the version of vengeance he had been building since the day his world shattered.</p>
<p>He searched for a way to kill Rex without becoming a demon.</p>
<p>But there was nothing. The silence of the domain seemed to mock him, echoing the emptiness in his own soul.</p>
<p>He understood then, with the devastating clarity that comes only at the absolute bottom of a person’s capacity to endure, that Rex had not offered this information out of some twisted sense of honesty.</p>
<p>Rex hadn’t been sharing a secret; he had been deploying a weapon. Every name had been chosen with surgical precision; every pause between them had been measured to maximize the psychological trauma.</p>
<p>Rex wasn’t trying to win a fight anymore; he was winning a war of attrition against Apollo’s very humanity. He wanted Apollo to stand there, broken and bleeding, knowing that his only path to justice was a corridor lined with the corpses of everyone he loved.</p>
<p>Rex began to rise.</p>
<p>It started as a grotesque display of biological reconstruction.</p>
<p>CRACK POP. SHHHH TICK.</p>
<p>One broken joint reset itself with a wet snap, followed by the grinding and sliding sounds of vertebrae reassembling.</p>
<p>At first, it was agonizingly slow, but then something shifted. The healing accelerated with terrifying purpose, the pace of his regeneration surging past its earlier limits as though the very effort required to maintain the illusion of being wounded had become beneath him.</p>
<p>Whatever restraint Rex had been exercising throughout this entire butchery, the deliberate, calculated choice to absorb bone-breaking impacts and organ-crushing blows just to harvest data on Apollo’s divine mechanism ended in that instant.</p>
<p>He had learned what he needed to know about the architecture of the open channel, and continuing to play the victim was no longer a strategy; it was an indulgence he could no longer afford.</p>
<p>’Data acquisition complete,’ Rex’s mind hummed, cold and detached behind his eyes. ’The divine conduit is a finite resource.’</p>
<p>’A massive one, yes, but finite nonetheless.’</p>
<p>He could see the truth written in Apollo’s failing body. The divine channel Apollo had tapped into was not an infinite well, despite its terrifying display.</p>
<p>That final, apocalyptic strike that had stopped Rex’s own heart had been a catastrophic withdrawal from a cosmic bank account, a debt Apollo’s own flesh was now paying in real time. The pallor spreading across Apollo’s face wasn’t just shock; it was exhaustion.</p>
<p>The visible tremor in his stance wasn’t fear; it was the collapse of his spiritual stamina. Whatever goddess had opened that door had not done so without a price, and she had deliberately withheld the location of the limit from her vessel.</p>
<p>’Elegant cruelty,’ Rex thought with a flicker of genuine appreciation. ’Very well played, Goddess.’</p>
<p>Then came the flood.</p>
<p>VROOOOOOM.</p>
<p>Peak Superhuman Physique flooded back through muscle fibers that had been shredded into ribbons minutes earlier. The regeneration didn’t just restore him to function; it drove him to full, lethal output.</p>
<p>His muscles coiled with new, dense power, his tendons tightening like steel cables. Simultaneously, the violet-black aura of his Lustful Villain identity erupted from his pores.</p>
<p>It didn’t just flicker; it climbed around him in a steadily building corona of shadow and malice, burning brighter and more oppressive than at any other point in the engagement. The darkness seemed to drink the little light that remained in the domain.</p>
<p>When Rex finally stood fully upright, there was no trace of the wreckage Apollo had spent the last hour producing. There was no blood dripping from his chin, no labored breathing, and no twisted ribcage.</p>
<p>He stood as a monument of perfect, predatory vitality.</p>
<p>The shift in pressure was immediate. The air around them grew heavy, charged with a static that made the hair on Apollo’s arms stand up.</p>
<p>"You know, Apollo," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice was no longer a gravelly rasp or a death rattle. It had returned entirely to its usual level of calm, a smooth, terrifyingly composed baritone that cut through the silence of the ruined domain like a scalpel. "I have let you feel like you had a chance for long enough now."</p>
<p>Rex took a single step forward, the violet-black aura pulsing with every heartbeat.</p>
<p>"I think you’ve suffered enough to earn your final lesson."</p>
<p>Apollo did not answer. He could not.</p>
<p>The words Rex had spoken—the names, the implications, the horrific price of victory—were lodged in his throat like shards of broken glass. He stood frozen in the center of the shattered domain, a fallen monument to a divine purpose that had been systematically dismantled.</p>
<p>The last of the divine channel had not merely drained; it had been violently evacuated from his system, leaving his veins feeling like hollow, frozen conduits. There was no reserve left anywhere in his body.</p>
<p>No spark of holy fire, no residual warmth, no hidden wellspring of courage. He was a husk, a spent shell of a man, his face still drained of color from the psychic mutilation. Rex had been inflicted with nothing more than a list of names.</p>
<p>For one heartbeat, Apollo believed he might still be able to summon something or anything to deflect what was coming.</p>
<p>He tried to call upon the light, to scrape the bottom of his soul for one last defensive barrier, but there was only a cold, yawning emptiness where his power used to live.</p>
<p>The fight that had briefly, impossibly, turned in his favor had not just shifted; it had collapsed, imploded, the instant Rex decided that the game was over.</p>
<p>Rex moved.</p>
<p>There was no wind-up.</p>
<p>No warning shout.</p>
<p>No dramatic gathering of energy.</p>
<p>There was only the sudden, terrifying displacement of air as Rex launched himself forward.</p>
<p>WHOOSH!!</p>
<p>He came forward at a speed that Apollo’s exhausted, traumatized senses could barely track. To Apollo’s failing vision, Rex didn’t seem to run; he seemed to simply exist in one space and then suddenly exist in another, closer to him.</p>
<p>The violet black corona of Rex’s aura tore through the air like a physical blade, leaving behind a trail of distorted reality that screamed with malevolent energy.</p>
<p>Apollo’s pupils dilated, his heart hammering a weak, panicked rhythm against his ribs. Time seemed to dilate, stretching the seconds into agonizing minutes as he watched the predator close the distance.</p>
<p>He could see the minute details now: the terrifyingly calm expression on Rex’s face, the way the shadows coiled around his fist like living serpents, and the absolute certainty in every muscle twitch.</p>
<p>’Move,’ Apollo’s mind screamed. ’Gods, please, just let me move...’</p>
<p>But his limbs were leaden, and his reflexes shattered. The last thing he saw before the world exploded into violence was not a god, but the inevitable conclusion of his own failure.</p>
