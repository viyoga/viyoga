<p>The silence that followed Theo’s outburst wasn’t empty; it was heavy, pressurized, like the air before a cataclysmic storm. Every eye in the hall swiveled toward the shadows where the young noble stood.</p>
<p>The celebratory hum died instantly, replaced by a tension so sharp it felt as though a single wrong movement would draw blood.</p>
<p>Theo stepped into the flickering torchlight. His face was pale, but his eyes burned with a desperate, poisonous conviction.</p>
<p>He didn’t look like a grieving prince; he looked like a man standing on the edge of an abyss, determined to push himself in rather than watch someone else cross it.</p>
<p>"Mother," Theo began.</p>
<p>His voice lacked its usual aristocratic cadence; instead, it possessed that brittle, strained quality of someone who has rehearsed a martyrdom in his mind a thousand times. "The household’s acting positions require the household’s endorsement."</p>
<p>"The unit’s confidence in the director."</p>
<p>"Our traditions exist to prevent us from becoming whatever... this is." He gestured vaguely toward Rex with a disgust he couldn’t quite mask. "An external candidate for a position of such sacred authority regardless of any display of raw violence should require a formal challenge."</p>
<p>A low murmur of unease rippled through the assembled knights. They looked from the blood-soaked Rex to the pristine, trembling Theo.</p>
<p>Theo turned his gaze toward Rex, his nostrils flaring.</p>
<p>"Me," he said, the word cutting through the hall like a blade. "If he truly believes he has the right to reshape our lineage, if he believes he has earned the right to command our blades, then he should hold that position against the Nightwing heir."</p>
<p>Aurelia’s expression was unreadable. She stood tall despite the agony screaming from her broken ribs, her bloodied face a mask of ancient stone.</p>
<p>She looked at her son with the devastating patience of a mother who is weighing whether to comfort a child or punish a traitor.</p>
<p>"Theo," she said, the single word carrying the weight of a warning.</p>
<p>"It is the legitimate process, Mother!" Theo snapped, his composure fracturing.</p>
<p>The desperation in his voice betrayed the aristocratic veneer. "I am not disputing what he demonstrated in that pit."</p>
<p>"I am not denying his strength. But I am stating that the process is the process!"</p>
<p>"We cannot simply surrender our sovereignty to any mercenary who can bleed us properly!"</p>
<p>’He’s fighting for his pride, not the House,’ Diana thought bitterly, her hand tightening unconsciously on Rex’s arm.</p>
<p>She could see the gears turning in Theo’s mind; he wasn’t trying to protect their traditions; he was trying to reclaim the dignity he felt Rex had stolen from him. He was trying to prove he existed.</p>
<p>Aurelia took a step toward him, each movement a visible struggle against her injuries.</p>
<p>"You do not need to prove anything to me, Theo," she said, her voice dropping to a dangerous, intimate register that made the nearby guards stiffen. "I already know what you are capable of."</p>
<p>"I know your skill, I know your mind, and I know your heart."</p>
<p>"This isn’t about the House. This is not going to give you what you are actually looking for."</p>
<p>"I am not asking for your permission, Mother," Theo hissed, his voice trembling with the effort to remain upright under her scrutiny. "I am asking for the law of this House to be honored."</p>
<p>"If the heir is denied the right to challenge a usurper of authority, then tell me directly."</p>
<p>"Tell me that the Nightwing blood means nothing now."</p>
<p>"Tell me I am owed nothing, and I will stand down and vanish."</p>
<p>The challenge hung in the air like a guillotine blade. The tension was unbearable; one wrong word would shatter the family forever.</p>
<p>Aurelia stared at him, her eyes searching his face for any trace of the boy she had raised, finding only the hollow shell of a man consumed by resentment.</p>
<p>Finally, she exhaled a wet, painful sound.</p>
<p>"The formal challenge," she said, her voice echoing off the bloodstained stones with finality, "is accepted on his behalf... if he accepts it."</p>
<p>The room held its breath. All eyes shifted to Rex.</p>
<p>Rex stood motionless, a titan of scarred flesh and dried crimson. He looked at Theo not with anger, not with mockery, but with the cold, analytical gaze of a predator assessing a new kind of prey.</p>
<p>He saw the boy’s desperation, the way his hands shook despite his rigid posture, the way his pride was a fragile thing held together by nothing but spite. Rex knew that this wouldn’t be a spar.</p>
<p>This would be a collision of two different worlds: the inherited privilege of a prince versus the earned brutality of a survivor.</p>
<p>’This fucking bum really loves to mess things up, huh? ’ Rex thought. ’I’ll show him real good this time...’</p>
<p>Theo met Rex’s stare. He didn’t flinch.</p>
<p>He stood with the grim, suicidal pride of someone who had already decided that even if he died, he would die on his own terms. He was committing to the moment, offering up his body to the furnace to prove he still had fire.</p>
<p>Rex’s lips didn’t curve into a smile this time. His expression remained as hard as the steel he wielded.</p>
<p>"Accepted."</p>
<p>The training hall was stripped bare. The assembled nobles and warriors retreated to the edges of the chamber, forming a living ring of witnesses.</p>
<p>The air felt thinner now, charged with an electrical current of dread and anticipation. This was no longer a demonstration; this was a trial by fire, a collision between institutional tradition and primal dominance.</p>
<p>Theo stepped into the center of the ring. His movements were precise, almost ceremonial.</p>
<p>He moved with the rigid, flawless posture of someone who had been raised to believe that form was everything, that if one followed the correct steps, the universe would inevitably bend to one’s will. His breathing was controlled and his eyes sharp, but Rex could see the microtremors in his fingers.</p>
<p>’He’s terrified, Rex thought,’ his own pulse steady and slow, like the rhythmic beating of a war drum. ’But he’s not running... That’s the only thing saving him right now.’</p>
<p>Rex had analyzed Theo months ago through the subtle shifts in his energy signature. Theo was not a combatant; he lacked the raw, explosive ’kill drive’ that defined Aurelia or the vicious desperation that defined Rex himself.</p>
<p>His designation was that of a scholar prince, a strategist. Yet, his training under Aurelia had been relentless.</p>
<p>He had been forged in the fires of Nightwing discipline, and that institutional pride had produced something genuine: a dangerous capability, even if it wasn’t lethal.</p>
<p>But there was a gulf between being a trained practitioner and being a predator. That gulf was about to become a canyon.</p>
<p>Theo initiated. As the challenger, he claimed the first strike.</p>
<p>He launched into the Nightwing House’s formal opening gambit, a sequence designed to establish dominance through flawless geometry and controlled pressure. His blade, a ceremonial rapier of exquisite craftsmanship, moved in a silver arc that seemed to slice through the very air.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even blink. His mind worked with the cold efficiency of a machine.</p>
<p>’Nine seconds to read the pattern.’</p>
<p>’Four seconds to dismantle it.’</p>
<p>Rex stepped inside the arc, the movement so economical it was almost invisible. He didn’t use a weapon; he used his body as a blunt instrument.</p>
<p>His palm struck Theo’s wrist just as the blade reached its apex.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of cartilage grinding against bone echoed off the walls as Rex’s strike redirected the force of the thrust harmlessly into the stone floor.</p>
<p>Theo stumbled back, his face flushing crimson with humiliation. But he didn’t break.</p>
<p>He reset, his jaw clenched so tightly that Rex could see the muscle jumping in his cheek. Rex grudgingly respected his desperate, genuine commitment and refusal to be cowed.</p>
<p>’There it is,’ Rex mused. ’The spark... He’s not just a puppet of his title; he has enough spine to hate me properly.’</p>
<p>But respect would not save his bones.</p>
<p>Theo came again, his movements faster this time, more aggressive. He attempted a mid-level feint followed by a thrust aimed at Rex’s solar plexus.</p>
<p>It was a textbook execution, flawless in its adherence to the curriculum.</p>
<p>’Three seconds.’</p>
<p>Rex pivoted on his heel, the motion fluid as water. He caught Theo’s blade between his forearms and delivered a short, brutal elbow to the young noble’s ribs.</p>
<p>THUD.</p>
<p>The sound was wet and sickening. Theo gasped, the air leaving his lungs in a pained wheeze as he spun away from the impact, clutching his side.</p>
<p>The crowd gasped. Even Aurelia flinched.</p>
<p>Theo’s eyes were watering, his breathing becoming ragged. He knew he was losing.</p>
<p>He could feel the cold reality of Rex’s power pressing down on him like a physical weight. His mind raced through the archives of his training, searching for an answer, a crack in the mountain.</p>
<p>In a moment of raw improvisation, Theo abandoned the textbook. He remembered something he had seen Aurelia do earlier that morning: a subtle shift in weight, a deceptive angle that broke the symmetry of a standard engagement.</p>
<p>It was a move born of instinct rather than rote memorization, a reach for something less rehearsed and more dangerous.</p>
<p>He lunged, twisting his body mid-motion to create an unpredictable angle of attack, aiming for the gap Rex had left exposed during his last pivot. It was a brilliant move, and it was a move that could have killed a lesser man.</p>
<p>Rex watched the improvisation unfold. For a fraction of a second, something akin to approval flickered in his dark eyes.</p>
<p>’Better,’ Rex thought. ’Much better.’</p>
<p>But the gap remained. The gulf between training and slaughter was still too wide.</p>
<p>It was still not fast enough.</p>
