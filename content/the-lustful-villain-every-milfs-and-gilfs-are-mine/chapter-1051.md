<p>She emerged from the twisted skeleton of the Nightwing household just as Theo’s attention was fractured by Apollo’s work below.</p>
<p>She didn’t stumble out from under debris or rush out in a panic; she moved with the measured, relentless momentum of someone who had been holding back a flood within the walls while the world collapsed outside.</p>
<p>She had spent the last several minutes managing the internal chaos, triage, and containment, the brutal reality of holding people together while their bones snapped under shifting foundations, and now she was emerging to assess the external catastrophe.</p>
<p>Her eyes, sharp and unblinking, performed a rapid tactical sweep.</p>
<p>She cataloged the devastation: the cratered street where civilians lay convulsing; the jagged breach in the Academy’s outer wall that looked like a wound torn in flesh; the hairline fractures spiderwebbing across the Nightwing estate’s foundation; and finally, the residual shiver of the temporal distortion field still clinging to the air like radioactive dust.</p>
<p>Then, her gaze climbed upward. She found him.</p>
<p>Theo hung suspended in the air, a broken monument to power without control. When his eyes met hers, the reaction was instantaneous and visceral.</p>
<p>The amber aura surrounding him didn’t brighten with hope or dim with shame; it underwent a violent, turbulent shift.</p>
<p>It became unstable, the energy flickering with the erratic frequency of a heart arrhythmia, the unmistakable signature of an emotional state that had been violently overridden by an unexpected input.</p>
<p>"Theo," Aurelia said.</p>
<p>The name didn’t carry the authority of a matriarch, the discipline of a combat instructor, or the practiced neutrality of a political figure. It was something far more dangerous.</p>
<p>It was the voice she had used in those quiet, fragile hours of his childhood, the voice of a mother who had spent years trying to navigate the impossible terrain of raising a child who was born to be a storm.</p>
<p>Rex watched, his mind racing, analyzing the shift. His emotional insight and soul manipulation systems tools, designed to read the deepest currents of human intent, suddenly spiked with error messages.</p>
<p>They had predicted resistance, anger, or guilt. They had not predicted this.</p>
<p>Theo’s expression had been a fortress. For years, he had been building it, layer by layer, until he was nothing but a closed system of controlled trauma and divine detachment.</p>
<p>But as Aurelia spoke, something unprecedented happened. The fortress didn’t crumble; it simply opened.</p>
<p>It was a microscopic breach at the one point Rex’s calculations couldn’t fully account for: the raw, primal hinge of the mother-son bond.</p>
<p>It was that involuntary crack in the armor that occurs in the split second before a man decides whether to break down or stand tall. For one heartbeat, the god disappeared, and only the exhausted boy remained.</p>
<p>"You look terrible," Aurelia said.</p>
<p>Her tone was almost conversational, cutting through the apocalyptic tension of the morning with a devastating normalcy.</p>
<p>Theo’s throat worked visibly.</p>
<p>"I know," he rasped, his voice cracking like dry earth.</p>
<p>"You are bleeding from your eye," she said.</p>
<p>Her eyes traced the crimson trail that had dried across his cheekbone, the blood seeping from a tear in his socket where the pressure of his own power had literally ruptured the capillaries.</p>
<p>"I know," he said again.</p>
<p>The admission was hollow, stripped of all pretense.</p>
<p>"Come down," she commanded, though it sounded less like an order and more like an invitation to stop pretending. "Not because I am telling you to."</p>
<p>"Not because I am your superior. But because standing up there bleeding and staring at nothing is not doing anything productive."</p>
<p>Theo didn’t look at Aurelia first. His gaze snapped sideways, sharp as a blade, landing on Rex. His eyes were bloodshot, pupils blown wide with adrenaline and something far more corrosive.</p>
<p>He was searching Rex’s face for something—a confession, a smirk, a sign of triumph—but Rex offered nothing but a terrifying, analytical stillness.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. He didn’t acknowledge the accusation in Theo’s eyes.</p>
<p>He simply calculated the distance between Theo’s suspended form and the cracked asphalt below, measuring the potential energy of a fall that would be catastrophic if the temporal enhancement failed mid-descent.</p>
<p>He said nothing, his silence a wall of cold marble.</p>
<p>"Theo," Aurelia said, her voice cutting through the static. "Whatever you believe Rex is or has done, whatever the god that gave that power told you, and whatever you felt at that cliff, you are still my child."</p>
<p>"And my child is standing forty meters above a street bleeding from his eye, and I am asking him to come down."</p>
<p>The wind howled between them, carrying the metallic tang of blood and the burnt ozone smell of spent energy. Theo’s jaw clenched so hard the muscle spasmed visibly.</p>
<p>His voice came out in a low murmur, a sound that shouldn’t have carried thirty meters, yet Rex caught it with perfect clarity:</p>
<p>"He took everything."</p>
<p>"I know," Aurelia replied.</p>
<p>There was no denial in her voice. No defensive lie could soften the blow.</p>
<p>She met his accusation with the brutal honesty of someone who had already mourned what was lost. "Come down."</p>
<p>"He took you," Theo spat, the words dripping with a raw, childlike betrayal that made the godlike power surrounding him seem like a grotesque costume.</p>
<p>Aurelia went silent. For several seconds, the only sound was the distant scream of a siren and the wet coughing of civilians trying to remember how to breathe.</p>
<p>The air between them felt pressurized, as if the universe itself were holding its breath, waiting to see if the boy would break or the mother would bend.</p>
<p>"Theo," she said finally, her voice steadier than steel. "Come down."</p>
<p>The amber-gold corona around him shuddered. It wasn’t a sudden collapse or a dramatic surrender.</p>
<p>It was something more profound: the soundless surrender of a dam that had finally stopped fighting the water. The effort required to maintain the temporal field, the sheer cognitive and biological tax of holding reality apart, was suddenly withdrawn.</p>
<p>The aura didn’t explode; it simply bled away, dissipating from the radius in a slow, exhausted exhale. The field wasn’t defeated; it was simply abandoned.</p>
<p>As the energy faded, the world snapped back into focus.</p>
<p>The buildings’ stone walls, which had been flickering and phasing between temporal states like bad film stock, solidified with an audible groan of settling masonry. The air lost its nauseating shiver, returning to standard physical parameters.</p>
<p>At the field’s edge, the people who had been convulsing in temporal loops began the painful, graceless process of biological recovery, gasping for air and vomiting from disorientation, their bodies slowly reacquainting themselves with the linear flow of time.</p>
<p>Then came the descent.</p>
<p>Theo didn’t float down like a god; he fell like a man. His temporal enhancements were still functioning at a residual level, preventing a terminal impact, but his body was spent.</p>
<p>The muscles in his legs were twitching from overexertion, and his breathing was ragged. He hit the pavement with a bone-jarring thud, the impact sending a spiderweb of cracks through the street.</p>
<p>He stayed down for a heartbeat, hands pressed against the asphalt, head hanging low. Blood dripped from his eye socket, pattering onto the concrete with rhythmic, wet sounds.</p>
<p>Then, he forced himself upright. His legs shook, but he stood. He looked at Aurelia with the hollow stare of someone who had seen the end of the world and found it wanting.</p>
<p>Aurelia didn’t hesitate. She crossed the distance between them, stepping over debris and bloodstained rubble without breaking stride.</p>
<p>When she reached him, she performed the singular, instinctive ritual of a mother who has seen her child reduced to broken fragments: she reached up and cupped his face. Her palm pressed against the uninjured side of his cheek, her thumb brushing just below his temple.</p>
<p>She didn’t coddle him; she searched him. Her eyes moved over his features with a flat, clinical assessment, checking the bone structure, the pupil response, and the reality of his presence, ensuring that the person standing before her was still the son she knew, despite the carnage.</p>
<p>"I am still angry with you," Theo said. His voice was hoarse, stripped of its divine timbre.</p>
<p>"I know," she said.</p>
<p>"That does not go away because you came out," he challenged, his voice cracking with the remnants of his fury.</p>
<p>"I know," Aurelia said, her grip on his face tightening just enough to ground him. "Come inside..."</p>
<p>"Let someone look at your eye."</p>
