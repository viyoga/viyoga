<p>The morning light crawled across Aethelgard like a pale, sickly wound, bleeding through the tall windows of the Nightwing estate. The city outside was unnaturally quiet, the kind of silence that follows a massacre a forced calm, as if the cobblestone streets and stone spires were holding their breath, terrified to acknowledge the carnage of the previous night.</p>
<p>Rex opened his eyes slowly, but there was no comfort in waking. Sleep had become a transactional resource, something he rationed out like ammunition between campaigns.</p>
<p>His muscles screamed with the phantom memory of violence the wet crunch of cartilage, the resistance of sinew snapping under his grip, the way blood had felt hot and slick against his knuckles when he finally broke Theo.</p>
<p>He could still see Theo’s jaw hanging at an impossible angle, the man’s pleas dissolving into wet, gurgling sounds as Rex systematically dismantled him.</p>
<p>’One more monster silenced,’ Rex thought, his inner voice cold and detached, ’one more obstacle ground into the dirt.’</p>
<p>But the room around him wasn’t filled with the stench of iron and death. Instead, there was the faint, intoxicating aroma of cedar oil the signature scent of Morwenna Nightwing.</p>
<p>He didn’t move. He remained motionless, staring at the ceiling while the weight of the woman beside him anchored him to the bed.</p>
<p>Morwenna, the matriarch who had commanded legions, the woman who had looked at him across war tables with eyes full of venomous hatred, calling him a two faced demon, a plague upon the Apostles she was here.</p>
<p>Her breathing was shallow, rhythmic, the only sound in a chamber that felt like the sanctum of a fallen god. The irony tasted like copper in his mouth; he had conquered the city not with siege engines, but by dismantling the very foundations of her will, piece by piece, until she had no choice but to surrender everything.</p>
<p>Then, the air shivered. The interface flickered into existence, a cold, translucent rectangle of light that burned against his retinas.</p>
<p>[Desire Level: Morwenna Nightwing--MAXIMUM (100/100)]</p>
<p>[First Maximum Bond Bonus: +1,000 Energy]</p>
<p>A muscle twitched in Rex’s jaw. A laugh threatened to bubble up something dark and humorless but it died in his throat.</p>
<p>The sheer brutality of the victory settled in his gut. He hadn’t just beaten her; he had rewritten her entire existence. Her devotion wasn’t earned through chivalry; it had been forged in the fires of absolute subjugation.</p>
<p>He had taken her authority, her pride, and her hatred, and twisted them into this this terrifying, absolute loyalty.</p>
<p>’You called me a monster, Morwenna,’ he thought, glancing sideways at the pale curve of her shoulder. ’Now you belong to the monster.’</p>
<p>Before he could process the surge of power flooding his veins from the energy bonus, the system chimed again. This time, the text didn’t just hover it pulsed with a crimson urgency that made his heart hammer against his ribs.</p>
<p>[Pregnancy Confirmed: Morwenna Nightwing]</p>
<p>[Bonus: +1,000 Energy]</p>
<p>[Free Skill Selection Unlocked may be claimed at any time]</p>
<p>Rex stared at the pulsating crimson text, his pupils contracting as he processed the implications.</p>
<p>He had grown almost numb to the pregnancy notifications by now, a grim routine of conquest that had become as predictable as the sunrise, but seeing Morwenna’s name attached to that biological fact struck something different.</p>
<p>This wasn’t just another claim; it was a seismic shift in the power structure of Aethelgard. She was the matriarch, the iron spine of one of the city’s oldest bloodlines.</p>
<p>Now, her womb was a vessel for his legacy, tying her ancient lineage to his chaotic, violent ascension in a way that could never be undone. Whether she had surrendered to this moment out of desire or out of a desperate need for survival, the result was the same: she was irrevocably his.</p>
<p>A sidelong glance revealed she was already awake. She hadn’t been sleeping; she had been waiting.</p>
<p>She lay perfectly still, eyes fixed on the ornate ceiling as if searching for answers written in the plaster. The expression on her face was foreign to him; the sharp, venomous calculation she wore in council meetings was gone.</p>
<p>The barely restrained fury that had defined their months of confrontation had evaporated. In its place was something terrifyingly quiet.</p>
<p>It looked like peace, but Rex knew better; it was the hollow, echoing stillness of absolute surrender.</p>
<p>"You’re not going to ask what I’m thinking," she said.</p>
<p>Her voice was low, steady as a blade, carrying none of the tremor of a broken woman, only the flat tone of someone who had already accepted their fate.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He leaned back slightly, his gaze unreadable.</p>
<p>"I already know what you’re thinking."</p>
<p>"You’re calculating the cost... You’re wondering how much your family’s name is worth now and whether you just paid too much for it or if this was exactly the price required to keep it alive."</p>
<p>A ghost of something passed over her features, not quite a smile, but something sharper, more dangerous. "You always did have a talent for saying the ugly version of the truth out loud, Rex."</p>
<p>"The truth doesn’t need to be pretty to be useful," he countered coldly. "And it saves time."</p>
<p>Morwenna moved then, sitting up slowly. The silk sheets slid down her pale skin like water.</p>
<p>She didn’t reach for a robe; she simply sat there, staring at her own hands resting in her lap as if they belonged to someone else. When she spoke again, the iron in her voice had been replaced by something raw, something exposed.</p>
<p>"I spent thirty years building a reputation for this household," she began, her words each one a deliberate strike. "I buried a husband I loved more than I ever dared to admit."</p>
<p>"I raised three sons who treat each other like strangers across a battlefield."</p>
<p>"I watched Theo’s own blood rot from the inside out until he became something unrecognizable, and then I watched him die like a common criminal, his corpse discarded without so much as a prayer." Her fingers curled into fists, tendons standing out stark against her skin. "And last night, I realized that everything I built, every brick of this legacy, is safer in your hands than it could ever be in mine."</p>
<p>Rex remained silent. He knew the weight of confession.</p>
<p>He had seen men beg for death and women beg for mercy, but this was different. This was a confession of strategy wrapped in grief.</p>
<p>He watched her, letting the silence stretch between them like a taut wire, waiting to see if she would break.</p>
<p>"I know what you are," Morwenna continued, her gaze snapping to his. "I’ve known longer than you think."</p>
<p>"When I accused you in that meeting when I called you a monster before the entire Apostle network, it wasn’t because I believed you were Tremor."</p>
<p>"I suspected it, yes. But I needed to see how you would react when the world turned its teeth toward you."</p>
<p>"I wanted to see if you would flinch, if you would beg, if you would crumble under the weight of their judgment."</p>
<p>She leaned forward slightly, the intensity in her eyes enough to burn. "But you didn’t."</p>
<p>"You stood there like a god among insects."</p>
<p>"You let Elizabeth, Apollo, and Aurelia throw themselves into the fire for you, and you didn’t even bother to defend yourself."</p>
<p>"Because you already knew you didn’t need to... You already knew you had already won." Her voice dropped to a near whisper, dark and intimate. "That was the moment I understood that fighting you was suicide."</p>
<p>"That attempting to oppose you would only result in more Nightwing blood staining the streets."</p>
<p>"So, I chose the smarter path. I chose to belong to you instead."</p>
<p>Rex’s inner mind hummed with a cold satisfaction. ’She didn’t break; she pivoted. ’</p>
<p>"And now?" he asked, his voice a gravelly challenge.</p>
<p>"Now I am carrying your child, and my family’s legacy hangs on whatever whims you decide to indulge," she said, letting out a breath that was almost a laugh, bitter, hollow, and terrifying. "I should be terrified."</p>
<p>"I should be screaming at the heavens."</p>
<p>"But I find that I’m not." Her eyes locked onto his with a frightening clarity. "And that terrifies me more than anything else could."</p>
