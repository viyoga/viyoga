<p>Elizabeth sat down beside him on the step, an action she had not intended but felt was necessary in the moment. The stone beneath them was cold, and the night air held the distinct quality of early autumn evenings in Aethelgard—just cool enough to be refreshing but still a reminder of the chill that was to come.</p>
<p>"You said it properly," she said. "I heard it."</p>
<p>"You were very professional about it," he said.</p>
<p>"I’m always professional," she said.</p>
<p>"You do the thing with your voice," he said. "When something is hard and you’re managing it. Your voice gets very even."</p>
<p>"I know," she said.</p>
<p>He paused to study her for a moment. Then, in a soft voice, he asked, "Where were you last night?"</p>
<p>"Why is it... taking so long?"</p>
<p>Elizabeth had anticipated the question and knew it would eventually arise. However, knowing it was coming did not fully prepare her for the unique quality of his voice when he finally asked it.</p>
<p>He was neither accusatory nor theatrical; his tone reflected someone who had been grappling with the issue all day and had concluded that posing the question directly was preferable to harboring it in silence.</p>
<p>"At the inn," she said. "I was working late, and I left you a message on the table, right?"</p>
<p>"You did," he said. "I got the message."</p>
<p>He was quiet for a moment. "I just—when you say ’working late,’ I used to know exactly what that meant."</p>
<p>"I used to know which work you were doing, which hour you would be working, and what you would look like when you got home." He looked at the street. "I feel like I’ve lost the map to something."</p>
<p>"And I know that sounds—"</p>
<p>"It doesn’t sound unreasonable," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>"We’ve both been on expeditions before," he said. "We’ve both come back changed by them."</p>
<p>"It’s part of what we do." He turned to look at her. "But this one was different..."</p>
<p>"I can’t explain exactly how."</p>
<p>"You came back, and you’re here, and everything is correct, and I keep feeling like I’m missing something."</p>
<p>Elizabeth held his gaze in silence, a quiet moment that he met with the patience of someone accustomed to her processing things at her own pace. He understood that the worst thing one could do was interrupt her thought process.</p>
<p>"I’m going to be working closely with Rex on the analysis," she said. "For the next several months."</p>
<p>"Valentina’s authorization includes collaborative access, and he was inside the operational situation in a way that makes his direct input more useful than working from external documentation alone."</p>
<p>Something flickered in Alexander’s expression. He quickly masked it, demonstrating the skill of someone adept at controlling their reactions in professional settings. However, Elizabeth had observed his face for two years, and she noticed it.</p>
<p>"With Rex, huh?" he said.</p>
<p>"Yes," she said. "He’s the one that made Lady Valentina forgive us..."</p>
<p>He was quiet for a moment.</p>
<p>"He’s good," Alexander said. "I don’t like saying it, but he is."</p>
<p>"The canyon—what he did in the canyon was impressive."</p>
<p>"The way he handled the situation with the Legion, the way he managed the group afterward."</p>
<p>"I’ve worked with a lot of reincarnators, and most of them have one or two things they’re genuinely good at, but for me, honestly... He doesn’t seem to have a ceiling."</p>
<p>"I agree too," Elizabeth said. "He doesn’t reveal everything, possibly because he is a former reincarnator from this world rather than from any other worlds."</p>
<p>"Which is useful," Alexander said. "For the analysis."</p>
<p>"Yeah," she said. "It is."</p>
<p>He looked at her with the careful attention of someone who is holding a thought and deciding whether the moment is right to say it. "Say..."</p>
<p>"Elizabeth," he said, "if I ask you something, will you answer me honestly?"</p>
<p>She looked at him. "You can ask."</p>
<p>"Is there something going on," he said, "that I should know about?"</p>
<p>The night air moved between them. Somewhere down the street a door opened and closed, and the sound carried and faded.</p>
<p>"I’m managing a complex intelligence situation," Elizabeth said. "There are pieces of it that I can’t discuss outside the authorized channels, which now include Lady Valentina’s direct oversight."</p>
<p>"It’s not that I don’t trust you. It’s that the access structure requires it."</p>
<p>He held her gaze for a long moment.</p>
<p>"That’s not what I asked," he said, quietly.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was very still.</p>
<p>"I’m not going to push," he said. "I know better than to push, and I know that even if I pushed it wouldn’t change anything."</p>
<p>"I just—" He stopped. "I need you to know that I’m paying attention."</p>
<p>"Not in a way that’s about distrust."</p>
<p>"Just in the way that happens when you love someone and something is shifting and you can’t locate it exactly."</p>
<p>"I know," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>"And you’re going to tell me it’s fine," he said.</p>
<p>"I’m going to tell you to give me time," she said. "Which is different."</p>
<p>He looked at her for a long moment, displaying the expression of someone who loves another person enough to understand that acceptance, in this moment, is the only option available to him.</p>
<p>"I’m going to stay at the Starlight household while the analysis work is running," she said. "A week, maybe more."</p>
<p>"The proximity to the Academy resources is practical, and my mother’s authorization makes it appropriate."</p>
<p>"For how long?" he said. "I need a clear answer."</p>
<p>"As long as the work requires it," she said.</p>
<p>He said nothing. He looked at the street again, and Elizabeth watched his profile in the dark and thought about how well she knew it and how much of the past two years had been embedded in the ordinary geography of his face.</p>
<p>"All right," he said. "I understand."</p>
<p>"I’ll be back when I can," she said. "This isn’t—"</p>
<p>She stopped herself before she finished the sentence, because the sentence she had been about to say was a reassurance she could not honestly give, and she had decided this morning that she was not going to make promises she did not intend to keep.</p>
<p>"I’ll see you soon," she said instead.</p>
<p>He nodded, and she stood, and he stayed sitting on the step in the way of someone who had decided the outside was still better than the inside.</p>
<p>"Sugarplum," he said when she was two steps away.</p>
<p>She stopped.</p>
<p>"Whatever it is," he said, "I’m still here."</p>
<p>She turned and looked at him sitting on the step in the cold, and something in her chest did what it did when she encountered sincerity she had not prepared for. She held her emotions in the same way she managed difficult situations: quietly and without allowing them to show on her face.</p>
<p>"I know," she said. "Thank you."</p>
<p>She turned toward the Starlight household and began walking.</p>
<p>He raised his hand as she turned the corner.</p>
<p>She didn’t look back.</p>
<p>But in the space behind her sternum, in the part of herself she carefully managed and revealed to no one, she whispered, ’I’m sorry.’</p>
<p>’I understand what I’m doing and the price it entails, yet I am proceeding regardless, and I regret that the burden partially falls on you.’</p>
<p>The household was lit in the upper windows. She could see it from the end of the street, warm against the dark.</p>
<p>’Whatever Rex intends for tonight,’ she thought, ’I already know how this goes.’</p>
<p>’I told him it was a transaction. I meant it. I still mean it.’</p>
<p>She repeated this to herself twice, as people often do with thoughts they need to reinforce, and she walked through the night toward the lit windows, the work that awaited her, and the commitments she had made, not stopping until she reached the door.</p>
<p>’If I came back different... just so you know, Alexander, I loved you once.’</p>
