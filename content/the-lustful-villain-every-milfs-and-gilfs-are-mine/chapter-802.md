<p>Aisella’s gaze sharpened, her eyes cutting through the Avatar’s composed exterior like a surgical scalpel. The professional calm she maintained was a thin veil over a growing, clinical frustration.</p>
<p>"You could have told me that at the start of the expedition," she said, her voice tight with the weight of wasted effort. "I have spent weeks calibrating restoration workings for you based on standard reserve models."</p>
<p>"I have been treating you like a man who depletes a finite well, not a man who is part of the well itself."</p>
<p>"The distinction was not operationally relevant until this morning," the Avatar replied.</p>
<p>His voice was a masterpiece of logical detachment, providing an answer that was technically correct but emotionally hollow.</p>
<p>"It is operationally relevant every time I am trying to assess whether you are about to collapse into a heap of broken bone and exhausted spirit!" Aisella snapped.</p>
<p>The flat, dangerous quality of her voice signaled that she wasn’t merely annoyed; she was stating a fundamental truth of their survival. "It is relevant every time you push your limits and leave me to guess how much of your life force is left to salvage."</p>
<p>"That is every engagement you have ever participated in, Rex or whatever you are pretending to be right now."</p>
<p>The Avatar didn’t flinch at the slip of the name, nor at the bite in her tone.</p>
<p>"I will provide you with the full architecture breakdown tonight," he promised, his tone shifting to the soothing, authoritative register Rex used to settle her when she was spiraling. "The energy system integration of the Earthen Authority’s geological function with the standard designation reserve is documented in the restricted archives."</p>
<p>"I will walk you through the mathematics of it... Every layer... Every variable..."</p>
<p>Aisella stared at him, her eyes performing a silent, high level assessment. She was weighing the validity of his promise against the exhaustion in her own hands, deciding whether to demand answers now or to accept the reprieve.</p>
<p>"Tonight," she finally said, the word a command rather than a request.</p>
<p>"Tonight," the Avatar confirmed. "Maybe..."</p>
<p>As she stepped back, the air around them seemed to drop several degrees. Mireya moved in from Aisella’s shoulder, her presence accompanied by a lingering, crystalline chill.</p>
<p>Her ice workings were still partially active, the frost clinging to her skin and the air shimmering with the residual energy of a mage who had just emptied her soul into a battlefield. Her eyes were wide, bright with a frantic, unresolved energy the look of someone whose adrenaline was still screaming, even as her body tried to settle.</p>
<p>She looked at the Avatar, her breath hitching in a way that suggested she had been holding a question in her throat since the first dragon’s roar.</p>
<p>"The thermal working channels," Mireya began, her voice trembling slightly from the aftershocks of the fight. "I need to ask you something about them... Something... unsettling."</p>
<p>The Avatar turned his full attention to her, the simulation of undivided interest perfectly tuned.</p>
<p>"When I extended the ice base into the deep stone to anchor the containment structure," Mireya said, her hands gesturing toward the fractured earth beneath them, "the temperature differential between my working and the geological substrate was... wrong."</p>
<p>"It was significantly lower than the physics should allow..."</p>
<p>"The stone wasn’t just cold. It wasn’t surface cold, or even the chill of a passing storm. It was a deep, ancient cold."</p>
<p>She stepped closer, her eyes searching his for a sign of the truth she was uncovering.</p>
<p>"It was the kind of cold that only a geological working produces when it has been running at a sustained, massive output for an extended period," she whispered, the implication hanging heavy in the air.</p>
<p>"The earthquake protocol runs through the geological substrate at a resonant frequency," the Avatar explained, his voice steady, attempting to steer her back toward the safety of known science. "Resonant frequency propagation at that scale produces a temperature differential in the deep stone as a secondary, thermal byproduct."</p>
<p>Mireya shook her head, her expression hardening into one of dawning realization.</p>
<p>"No," she countered, her voice dropping to a chilling, intense whisper. "The temperature of the deep stone where I was anchoring... it matched the specific, unique temperature profile of a geological working that had been actively maintaining the island’s substrate for an extended period."</p>
<p>"And it wasn’t just during the earthquake protocol, Rex... The data doesn’t lie. It has been running like that for much, much longer."</p>
<p>The Avatar met Mireya’s gaze, his eyes as unyielding as the stone she had just interrogated. He could feel the tension radiating off her, the cold, sharp suspicion of a woman who had felt the very bones of the world shifting beneath her feet.</p>
<p>"The Earthen Authority’s passive geological awareness requires a baseline maintenance contact with the island’s substrate," the Avatar said, his voice a calm anchor in the wake of her revelation. "To maintain the integrity of the tectonic plates, a constant, low-level connection is necessary."</p>
<p>"This maintenance contact produces a minor, consistent temperature effect in the deep stone over extended periods..."</p>
<p>"The duration of that effect is directly proportional to the duration of the passive contact."</p>
<p>Mireya didn’t blink. She leaned in, her voice dropping to a dangerous, searching whisper.</p>
<p>"How extended a period, exactly?"</p>
<p>"Approximately fourteen months," the Avatar answered, the number dropping like a heavy stone into a still pool. "From the initial integration baseline."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy, suffocating. Mireya stood frozen, her mind racing to reconcile the math.</p>
<p>Fourteen months of constant, subterranean output. Fourteen months of a presence so deep and so integrated that it had become a part of the island’s very heartbeat.</p>
<p>"Fourteen months," she repeated, the words tasting like iron.</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>She stared at him, her eyes narrowing with the clinical, predatory assessment of an ice fighter. She wasn’t just listening; she was filing this new, terrifying datum into the massive mental ledger of everything she had observed: the strange coincidences, the impossible feats, the sense that the man standing before her was something far more ancient and profound than a mere human.</p>
<p>"Alright," she said.</p>
<p>It was the same flat, weary acknowledgment Diana had used, the sound of a woman who had just been handed a puzzle too large to solve in a public plaza, deciding to carry the pieces away to be examined in the dark.</p>
<p>She stepped back, retreating into the periphery.</p>
<p>Talyra, however, was already smiling. It was a bright, almost jarring contrast to the heavy atmosphere of the group.</p>
<p>For her, the resolution of the dragon’s fall was a victory, a terrifying moment of chaos that had ended in the exact outcome she had prayed for.</p>
<p>She didn’t follow the disciplined, organized sequence the others had used to approach him. She didn’t walk straight toward him like a soldier.</p>
<p>Instead, she drifted sideways, moving with the fluid, predatory grace of a hunter, circling to his right side. It was the natural instinct of an archer to never approach a target from the front, always to seek the angle, the blind spot, or the flank.</p>
<p>"The four-construct sequence," she said, her eyes dancing with a sharp, intellectual hunger. "When you were bringing the dragon down and when you were also tearing the sky apart."</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>"The intercept angles," she continued, her hands moving through the air as if she were tracing the flight of an arrow. "The first construct slammed into the right wing apex, shattering the membrane."</p>
<p>"The second hit the tail intercept, forcing a violent pivot. The third and fourth came from below, rising at a steep approach angle." She fixed him with the terrifyingly precise attention of an archer who had spent twenty years mastering the art of the kill. "The third and fourth constructs... they weren’t aimed at the dragon, were they?"</p>
<p>"No," the Avatar admitted.</p>
<p>"They were aimed at the void," she whispered, her smile widening. "You aimed them at the exact coordinate where the dragon was going to be once the first two impacts had redirected its arc."</p>
<p>"You didn’t aim at the target; you aimed at the future."</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>"That calculation is impossible in real time," Talyra challenged, her voice rising with excitement. "To calculate the deflection of a deflected arc to account for the mass, the velocity, and the chaotic elemental turbulence of the dragon’s fire, it takes a cognitive load that should have frozen you in place."</p>
<p>"You didn’t even pause and you didn’t even blink."</p>
<p>"The Foresight runs the calculation continuously," the Avatar replied, his voice as smooth and inevitable as a landslide. "The calculation was complete before the first construct was even deployed."</p>
<p>Talyra stared at him, her eyes wide with a mixture of reverence and professional indignation. It was the look of a master predator who had just been told that her lifelong pursuit of perfection was, in the face of his existence, merely a child’s game.</p>
<p>To her, targeting was a violent, beautiful dance of intuition and physics; to hear that he had already solved the equation before the dance had even begun was a blow to her very soul.</p>
