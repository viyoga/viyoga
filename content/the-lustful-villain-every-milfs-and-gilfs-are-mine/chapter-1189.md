<p>Xerollion reached down, his movements fluid and utterly devoid of exertion. His gloved fingers closed around what remained of Apollo’s collarbone, the fabric now fused with congealed blood and torn flesh.</p>
<p>With a casual jerk that made Apollo’s shattered vertebrae protest with a wet CRUNCH, Rex hauled the ruined body off the floor. Apollo’s head lolled backward, his neck limp, his mouth agape in a silent, unconscious plea for an end that would not come.</p>
<p>With his free hand, Rex reached into the very fabric of the failing reality. He didn’t just move through space; he tore it.</p>
<p>His claws raked across the air, leaving glowing violet gashes that bled raw chronal energy.</p>
<p>SHHHHH VROOOOOM</p>
<p>The spiral tore open with the sound of rending metal. The violet-black vortex spun into existence, a hungry mouth of swirling void that demanded to be fed.</p>
<p>Rex stepped into the abyss, dragging Apollo’s broken corpse behind him like a trophy of war. As they crossed the threshold, the portal snapped shut behind them with the force of a collapsing star, swallowing the last gasps of the dying domain.</p>
<p>Behind them, unseen by any living eye, the final remnants of that reality imploded into absolute nothingness, an experiment concluded, its purpose served, its existence erased.</p>
<p>BOOM.</p>
<p>The return to the northern corridor was not a gentle landing. They materialized in a violent burst of dark energy that cracked the stone floor beneath them.</p>
<p>Rex and Apollo dropped onto the solid granite in the exact coordinates where they had vanished, appearing to the assembled soldiers as if they had never left, only worse.</p>
<p>What had felt like hours of agony in the void passed in what seemed to the witnesses like mere seconds of supernatural displacement.</p>
<p>The effect was instantaneous. The cacophony of war, the clash of steel, the screams of dying men, and the thunder of magic died in an instant.</p>
<p>Every fighter engaged in that stretch of the corridor froze mid-swing. The news of their reappearance rippled through the ranks like a physical shockwave, a wave of turning heads and dropped weapons that spread from the center outward.</p>
<p>Ten thousand soldiers, both divine and demonic, stood in a terrifying, unnatural silence. It was the kind of silence that precedes a catastrophe, the held breath of an army about to witness something that would sear itself into their collective memory forever.</p>
<p>Then, they saw him.</p>
<p>Apollo Brightsoul, the Apostle of Life, the golden beacon of Aethelgard, the hero whose name had been whispered in prayers for generations, hung from Xerollion’s grip like something discarded, something broken beyond recognition.</p>
<p>He was no longer a man; he was a crime scene.</p>
<p>His body was a visceral ruin from head to foot. Blood, thick and arterial crimson, soaked every visible inch of his tan skin, dripping steadily onto the pristine stone floor with a rhythmic DRIP... DRIP... DRIP.</p>
<p>One arm hung useless at his side, severed at the shoulder by the grinding force of Rex’s earlier torment, the muscle tissue frayed like old rope.</p>
<p>His legs were bent at grotesque, impossible angles, with shattered femurs and twisted tibias that made it clear neither would ever carry the weight of a hero again.</p>
<p>Most devastating of all was the absence of light. There was no golden aura.</p>
<p>No defiant spark of divine essence flickered in his eyes.</p>
<p>No last stand.</p>
<p>There was nothing left but the raw, wet, bleeding evidence of complete and total annihilation. The hero was dead; only the meat remained.</p>
<p>The silence that swallowed the northern corridor in that first heartbeat had a texture—something visceral and suffocating—that no survivor would ever be able to articulate.</p>
<p>It was not the silence of exhaustion, though thousands of battle-worn soldiers stood within it. It was the silence of a collective reality fracturing.</p>
<p>It was the quiet, hollow interval between the moment humanity believes its strongest champion is invincible and the moment they understand, with bone-deep certainty, exactly how wrong that belief had been.</p>
<p>Valentina Von Starlight stood frozen at the precipice of her crumbling formation. Her face, usually a mask of regal composure, had drained of every drop of color, leaving her skin as pale as marble.</p>
<p>Her hands, which had commanded armies through fifty years of relentless crisis, now trembled visibly at her sides, a betrayal of her body that she couldn’t suppress.</p>
<p>The unshakable confidence she had carried into this corridor—the belief that she stood among the apex of Aethelgard’s power—had been pulverized before ten thousand witnesses.</p>
<p>She was watching the second most powerful being in the entire city reduced to a sack of broken meat and arterial spray.</p>
<p>She had personally taught Apollo advanced ward theory when he was seventeen.</p>
<p>She had mentored him.</p>
<p>She had seen his potential bloom into the finest Apostle of Life this generation had produced.</p>
<p>Now, staring at the twisted, mangled ruin of his limbs and the hollow emptiness in his eyes, she was confronted with a truth that defied her entire life’s work: all that divine training had meant nothing against the monster standing before them.</p>
<p>Beside her, Morwenna Nightwing had gone rigid with a primal horror. One hand rose unconsciously to cover her mouth as her breath came in shallow, ragged hitches.</p>
<p>The complex magical calculations that had been racing through her mind and the theories she had been testing since her private conversation with Rex days earlier simply stopped.</p>
<p>They were incinerated, replaced by a single, overwhelming animal recognition: she had been studying a predator whose scale she had catastrophically underestimated. This wasn’t a rival power; this was an apex catastrophe.</p>
<p>"APOLLO!"</p>
<p>The scream tore through the silence as Elliot Von Starlight lunged forward, his face a contorted mask of grief and fury. He didn’t even make it two steps before three armored soldiers threw themselves across his path, catching him bodily.</p>
<p>They dragged him back, their boots skidding on the stone as he thrashed with inhuman strength.</p>
<p>"Let me go!" Elliot shrieked, his voice cracking and breaking into raw sobs. "That’s my friend!"</p>
<p>"Let me go; I have to get to him. I have to help him—!"</p>
<p>"You’ll die!" one of the soldiers barked, his voice low, urgent, and terrified.</p>
<p>He dug his fingers into Elliot’s armor to anchor him. "Look at him, kid!"</p>
<p>"Look at what’s holding him!"</p>
<p>"You won’t even take five steps before you’re gutted like the rest of us!"</p>
<p>Evelyn Astralight stood locked in place beside the struggling Elliot, her earlier suspicions burned away by the cold fire of raw terror. The realization hit her with the force of a physical blow: whatever power had been moving beneath Aethelgard’s foundations for the past year existed on a scale that made every previous war look like a child’s skirmish.</p>
<p>For the last hour, she had been trying to place a familiar cadence in a masked stranger’s movements, a pattern she couldn’t quite name.</p>
<p>Now, staring at the man holding Apollo aloft like a discarded, blood-soaked doll, that familiarity no longer mattered.</p>
<p>Whatever she had suspected, the reality had already outgrown her comprehension. Her theories could not contain the truth, which was too big, too cruel, and too violent.</p>
<p>And further up the corridor, among the front ranks of the Aethelgard soldiers still struggling to maintain formation, the final blow to their morale landed.</p>
<p>"APOLLO! NO! APOLLO—"</p>
<p>Rex Rexilion screamed Apollo’s name with a raw, cracking desperation that vibrated through the stone floor. He threw himself forward with such ferocity that two soldiers had to brace themselves against each other to hold him back, their faces strained with the effort of restraining his agonized grief.</p>
<p>His face was a perfect, devastating mask of helpless loss, a performance of such exquisite pain that not a single witness in that corridor, not one of the ten thousand souls watching, thought to question the sincerity of his tears.</p>
