<p>They walked together through the echoing stone corridors toward the council chamber, their footsteps falling in a synchronized rhythm that spoke of their shared purpose.</p>
<p>But as they approached the massive, ornate doors of the chamber, Rex deliberately slowed his pace. The air here felt different, heavier, charged with the anticipatory energy of the war council waiting inside.</p>
<p>He stopped fractionally before the entrance, turning his head just enough to catch Elizabeth’s gaze. In that moment, the mask of the partner slipped, revealing something colder, something mechanical.</p>
<p>"I need to be somewhere else," he said. His voice was quiet, but it carried the absolute finality of a death sentence.</p>
<p>Elizabeth didn’t flinch. She didn’t ask where he was going, nor did she question the unnatural timing of his absence.</p>
<p>She had learned, through months of unspoken complicity, that there were parts of Rex that belonged to no one, not even to her.</p>
<p>She simply gave a single, almost imperceptible nod, a silent acknowledgment of the transactional nature of their bond. Then, stepping slightly ahead to shield him from any curious eyes, she pushed open the heavy doors and entered the chamber.</p>
<p>Behind the closed doors, Rex allowed himself a single moment of true vulnerability. He closed his eyes and drew a breath that felt like inhaling liquid nitrogen.</p>
<p>Then, he triggered it.</p>
<p>The activation of the Avatar was not a loud process; it was a silent, violent restructuring of his very essence. Internally, Rex felt the familiar, sickening sensation of his consciousness being shoved into a corner, compressed into a small, watchful pocket of his own mind.</p>
<p>In its place, something else rushed in a construct of pure mimicry, a construct built from data, muscle memory, and flawless behavioral algorithms. It was a ghost wearing his skin.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, his eyes lost focus, becoming dull and hollow like glass beads. His breathing stuttered, a microsecond gap in the rhythm of a living man.</p>
<p>Then, the transformation snapped into place.</p>
<p>His spine straightened with unnatural precision. His facial muscles reconfigured, smoothing out the genuine exhaustion from the laboratory into the practiced, reassuring warmth of Rex Rexilion.</p>
<p>The entity that stepped through those council chamber doors moments later carried every outward marker of the man everyone believed them to be.</p>
<p>The Avatar crossed the room with a gait that was perfect—not too hurried, not too casual. It inclined its head toward Valentina with the exact degree of deference she expected, and as it passed Apollo, it offered a small, measured nod of solidarity, a gesture Rex had performed hundreds of times, replicated here with terrifying accuracy.</p>
<p>It settled into its chair with movements calibrated to ninety-seven point three percent accuracy against the original man, leaving only a three percent margin of error that no human eye could possibly detect.</p>
<p>’I am watching,’ Rex’s true consciousness whispered from the darkness of the Avatar’s mind. ’I am watching them plan their own demise.’</p>
<p>Only Elizabeth, as she slid into the seat beside the construct, felt the hair on the back of her neck stand up. She looked at the man beside her, the man she had just shared sweat and intimacy with, and saw something wrong.</p>
<p>There was a fraction less warmth behind the eyes. There was a half-second delay before a smile reached the corners of the mouth, a minute lag between stimulus and reaction.</p>
<p>It was the uncanny valley rendered in flesh and bone.</p>
<p>Her heart hammered once against her ribs, a primal warning that she was sitting next to a puppet, but she forced herself to remain still. She said nothing.</p>
<p>She couldn’t risk exposing the truth, not when the Key was finished and the trap was set. She simply opened her notes and let the meeting begin, sitting beside a perfect imitation while the real Rex Rexilion watched the world burn from behind a curtain of stolen flesh.</p>
<p>Far beneath the suspended majesty of Aethelgard, beyond the crushing weight of stone and shadow that separated the floating islands from the abyssal foundations of the world, Castle Nocturna breathed with a different kind of life.</p>
<p>Here, light didn’t shine; it bled. Deep violet-black tones pulsed through the air of the throne room like the slow heartbeat of a dying god.</p>
<p>A rift tore open at the center of the chamber. No sound accompanied it, only a sudden, violent displacement of air that smelled of ozone and old blood.</p>
<p>A shape folded itself out of the darkness with the smooth, terrifying efficiency of high-tier teleportation.</p>
<p>Lord Xerollion straightened to his full height, his masked visage catching the sickly glow of the Underlayer’s atmosphere, standing before the throne that Mordecai still nominally occupied, a throne that served as a constant reminder of whose power truly governed these depths.</p>
<p>The reaction across the room was instantaneous, a collective spasm of deference. Every high-ranking official present rose as one, a wave of submission rippling outward from the point of Rex’s arrival.</p>
<p>Mordecai, perched on the throne with the perpetual, twitching nervous energy of a demon lord who had been shattered and reforged as a subordinate, bolted upright. His claws scraped audibly against the armrests.</p>
<p>He moved with the desperate alertness of a man who knew that a single misstep in protocol could result in his immediate, agonizing execution. Near the base of the dais, Pavellia remained an island of unnerving calm, her spine straight, a sheaf of reports pressed against her chest like a shield.</p>
<p>Near the eastern wall, Cassandra and Gorvasha shifted in perfect unison, their postures bleeding from casual wariness into the coiled readiness of apex predators who had sensed a shift in the wind.</p>
<p>And then there was Lilith. Half hidden behind a massive obsidian support column at the rear, her face split into a delighted, hungry grin.</p>
<p>She fought to suppress it, forcing her features into something resembling professional interest as several council members glanced her way, but the fire in her eyes was unmistakable. She didn’t fear the storm; she wanted to drown in it.</p>
<p>"Sit down," Rex commanded.</p>
<p>The words weren’t shouted, but they carried the crushing authority of gravitational force. The room fell silent.</p>
<p>The rustle of silk and the scraping of chairs ceased instantly as every powerful entity in the chamber settled back into their seats.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t bother with pleasantries. He didn’t need to perform for them.</p>
<p>"The moment we have been bleeding for has arrived," he began, his voice cutting through the stillness like a blade through flesh. "Aethelgard has stopped speculating."</p>
<p>"They have stopped posturing... They have committed to annihilation."</p>
<p>He paced a slow, deliberate line before the throne, each step echoing like a hammer blow. "Apollo Brightsoul has been formally appointed as their High Commander of Defense."</p>
<p>"As of this afternoon, he stood before the entire remaining Apostle network and delivered the kind of sanctimonious rhetoric that guarantees nothing less than total war."</p>
<p>"They aren’t fighting for territory anymore; they are fighting for vengeance."</p>
<p>Rex paused, letting the implication of ’vengeance’ hang in the air like poison gas. A cold smile touched the corners of his mouth beneath the mask.</p>
<p>"More importantly, the new key, the one they believe is their salvation, is complete."</p>
<p>"I have personally overseen its final calibration through channels they believe are their own... The dimensional anchor is set."</p>
<p>"When they turn that key, it will not open to their dreams of victory... It will tear open directly into the northern corridor, exactly as Gorvasha identified months ago."</p>
<p>A heavy, suffocating silence descended. The trap was not merely set; it was primed, waiting for the enemy to walk willingly into the teeth of the machine.</p>
<p>Mordecai shifted forward, his breathing shallow, his voice carrying that familiar, frantic edge. "How long... how long do we have to prepare our defenses, My Lord?"</p>
<p>Rex turned his head toward him, his gaze so cold it seemed to physically pin Mordecai to the throne. "Days, Mordecai."</p>
<p>"We have days, not weeks."</p>
<p>His voice dropped an octave, becoming something darker, something that promised violence. "Aethelgard is moving with the frantic urgency of the righteous."</p>
<p>"Grief makes men fast, and conviction makes them reckless."</p>
<p>"They will strike while their hearts are hot, hoping to overwhelm us before their courage wavers."</p>
<p>"Every asset, every blade, every drop of blood we possess must be positioned before that window closes."</p>
<p>"When that key turns, I want them to find nothing but a slaughterhouse waiting for them."</p>
