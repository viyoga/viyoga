<p>Theo stared at Rex, his chest heaving, blood dripping from his chin onto the shattered pavement below. The raw, unhinged fury in his eyes flickered for a moment, replaced by something more desperate: a need for validation, for someone to acknowledge the horror of what they were witnessing.</p>
<p>"Then what is he?" Theo demanded, his voice cracking, raw with a plea that he refused to voice. "If he’s not what I say he is, then what the fuck is he?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t answer immediately. He hung suspended in the air, backlit by the flickering, unstable reality of the temporal storm.</p>
<p>He looked at Theo with the flat, patient attention of a professor observing a student who had failed a fundamental theorem, the kind of look that wasn’t angry but worse: it was disappointed in the student’s lack of comprehension.</p>
<p>"He is the most effective person you have encountered in your life," Rex said.</p>
<p>The words were delivered with a terrifying, clinical calm. "Which is not the same thing as being good."</p>
<p>"You are confusing effectiveness for virtue, Theo."</p>
<p>"You see him achieving results and you assume there is a moral compass guiding him."</p>
<p>"You are disoriented because you are seeing effectiveness stripped of its traditional moral packaging."</p>
<p>Theo flinched as if struck. "He is not your ally, Rex!"</p>
<p>"Can’t you see?! He is using you! He is using all of us as tools for whatever game he’s playing."</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said.</p>
<p>The simplicity of the answer seemed to physically wound Theo. His expression twisted a nauseating cascade of betrayal, rage, and confusion.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex repeated, his register unchanged. "I have known from the beginning."</p>
<p>"The people who are effective at the level I am observing do not act out of altruism."</p>
<p>"They have reasons for their effectiveness that they rarely present to those they intend to use."</p>
<p>"Their motives are not their methods."</p>
<p>Rex drifted closer, his shadow stretching across the distorted ground. "The question isn’t whether he is using us, Theo."</p>
<p>"That is a settled fact. The question is whether the usage produces outcomes that are preferable to the alternatives."</p>
<p>"And so far, the outcomes have been preferable to the alternatives. In the calculus of survival, utility outweighs intent."</p>
<p>This was the most dangerous version of Rex. He wasn’t the warm, social mask he wore to navigate human spaces, nor was he merely the cold weapon of combat.</p>
<p>This was the Philosopher Predator, the version that spoke the most brutal truths with the same casual detachment as he did the weather. He made the truth indistinguishable from a performance, which meant you could never truly know where the man ended and the machine began.</p>
<p>Theo turned away, unable to meet that gaze. His eyes wandered past the immediate crater of their battle, looking down at the city below.</p>
<p>The area affected by the temporal distortion was still hemorrhaging power at its edges. Beyond the immediate devastation of the Nightwing household, the city streets were caught in a nauseating, low-intensity flicker.</p>
<p>Buildings seemed to shudder, their surfaces blurring between new stone and ancient decay. The civilians in those buildings weren’t dead, but they were trapped in a state of profound existential nausea, their minds unable to anchor themselves to a single moment as their timelines wavered.</p>
<p>And then, Theo saw them.</p>
<p>The bodies.</p>
<p>People were collapsing in the streets, clutching their heads as their equilibrium shattered. This was the unintended consequence of a god-tier power being wielded by an untrained hand.</p>
<p>Theo had been focused on Rex on the immediate threat and had failed to manage the secondary hemorrhaging of his own power. He hadn’t intended the collateral damage.</p>
<p>He hadn’t intended for the disoriented screams to occur.</p>
<p>Rex watched Theo’s face crumble as he realized the scale of his failure.</p>
<p>The distortion was affecting children.</p>
<p>Rex’s Soul Manipulation allowed him to see what the naked eye could not. In the outer radius, where the temporal field was weaker, the children weren’t aging backward visibly, but their very essence was being frayed.</p>
<p>Their cellular timelines not yet fully established, still in the vulnerable process of forging a consistent identity, were being caught in the temporal eddies.</p>
<p>Their soul signatures showed a specific, haunting dimming: the mark of a child who had lost precious, irreplaceable moments of development to a random act of cosmic violence.</p>
<p>They were losing time they would never get back.</p>
<p>Rex did not say this to Theo. He did not offer comfort or condemnation.</p>
<p>He simply watched.</p>
<p>He let Theo stare at the collapsing civilians. He let Theo process the horrifying reality that his quest for justice had become an engine of random, senseless suffering.</p>
<p>He watched the realization sink in that his power was not a scalpel but a sledgehammer that could not distinguish between villain and innocent.</p>
<p>And then Rex watched Morwenna.</p>
<p>She had recovered with a terrifying speed, her system designation allowing her to reassemble her biological coherence faster than any normal human.</p>
<p>She pushed herself up from the rubble, blood still staining her clothes, and turned her gaze toward the city. She saw the flickering buildings.</p>
<p>She saw the people collapsing in the distance. She saw the horror of the uncontrolled field.</p>
<p>Rex watched her realize exactly what Theo had done.</p>
<p>Morwenna pushed herself upright, the movement sending fresh waves of agony through her healing tissues. Her muscles screamed as they reknitted, each cellular repair a painful reminder of the violence she had just endured.</p>
<p>Her gaze swept past the cratered earth, past the wreckage of the Nightwing household, and locked onto the city below.</p>
<p>She saw the flickering. She saw the nauseating stutter of reality.</p>
<p>And then she saw the bodies of the civilians who had simply folded like marionettes with their strings cut.</p>
<p>Her eyes moved to Theo.</p>
<p>He was suspended in the air, a broken god caught in the aftermath of his own catastrophe. The amber-gold aura that surrounded him, once a crisp, terrifying manifestation of absolute control, had begun to hemorrhage.</p>
<p>The edges of his power were no longer sharp; they had become diffuse and frayed, bleeding into the atmosphere like ink in water. It was the unmistakable signature of a mind that had broken someone whose attention had been violently severed from their focus, leaving their power to run on hollow momentum rather than conscious direction.</p>
<p>"Theo," Morwenna said.</p>
<p>Her voice wasn’t the warm authority of the matriarch, nor was it the soothing cadence she used to calm the wounded. It was something sharper, colder.</p>
<p>It was the voice of someone speaking to the raw, terrified human beneath the divine designation, the man who had spent twenty-nine years trying to hold back the tide of his own nature.</p>
<p>"Theo, look at what you are doing."</p>
<p>Theo didn’t move. His gaze remained fixed on the distant streets where the innocent were collapsing, his eyes reflecting the same horror she felt blooming in her own chest.</p>
<p>He looked like a man watching his own hands commit a murder he couldn’t stop.</p>
<p>He was still staring into that abyss when the air itself seemed to solidify.</p>
<p>Apollo arrived.</p>
<p>There was no thunderclap, no dramatic shockwave that would have marked a full-scale engagement. This was different.</p>
<p>This was a controlled descent, the arrival of a predator who had assessed the situation from miles away and chosen the most efficient method of approach. He came from the direction of the Academy, his energy signature humming at a measured, disciplined frequency.</p>
<p>He wasn’t here to destroy; he was here to manage.</p>
<p>He touched down on the cracked earth with the silent grace of a predator, his eyes immediately scanning the devastation. His gaze swept over the temporal distortion field, still flickering like a dying lightbulb.</p>
<p>Then his eyes moved to Theo, suspended and shattered.</p>
<p>Then to Rex, standing in the eye of the storm with unsettling stillness.</p>
<p>Finally, Apollo’s gaze landed on the outer radius of the people collapsed in the streets, the victims of a power that had outgrown its master.</p>
<p>"What happened to those people?" Apollo asked.</p>
<p>His voice wasn’t directed at Theo. He knew Theo was beyond answering.</p>
<p>Instead, Apollo turned his piercing stare toward Rex. He recognized Rex as the constant, the one standing at the epicenter of the information, the one who remained functional while everything else spiraled into chaos.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. His expression remained as impassive as marble.</p>
<p>"The temporal distortion’s field application at scale has secondary effects on biological systems," Rex said, his voice carrying the clinical detachment of an autopsy report. "The effects are severity graded by proximity and by the biological system’s developmental state."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy, suffocating.</p>
<p>"The children," Apollo said, his voice dropping an octave, laced with something that sounded dangerously like restrained fury.</p>
<p>"The children at the outer radius have experienced some temporal rollback," Rex answered without hesitation. "Not large enough for visible physical change."</p>
<p>"The field at that distance lacks the concentration required for macro-scale aging."</p>
<p>"However, the effect has manifested at the cellular development timeline level... Their biological clocks have been disrupted."</p>
<p>Apollo’s eyes locked onto Rex’s. In that moment, the air between them grew cold, charged with the unspoken implications of Rex’s words: that this wasn’t just an accident but a mathematical inevitability of the power Theo had unleashed.</p>
