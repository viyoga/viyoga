<p>Above, in the sun-drenched sanctum of the council chamber, the meeting drew toward its inevitable, fateful close. Valentina stood at the center of the dais, her voice carrying the weight of divine mandate as she finalized the timeline.</p>
<p>"Final preparations will be concluded within days," she announced, her eyes scanning the assembled commanders with a steel-hard resolve. "The offensive will launch the moment our preparations are complete."</p>
<p>"Consider yourselves formally on notice: mobilize every asset, ready every blade, and steel every soul."</p>
<p>"We march to reclaim what has been stolen."</p>
<p>The chamber responded not with cheers, but with something far more chilling: the quiet, measured applause of zealots. These were people who believed they were the instruments of justice, the righteous fist rising to correct a long-standing injustice.</p>
<p>In their minds, they were walking toward salvation, the glorious retribution for Veylor, for Calivara, for Eryndra, and for every sacred name that had been desecrated by the ’evil’ of the Underlayer.</p>
<p>They believed their coming bloodshed would finally provide the answer to the tragedies that had haunted them for years.</p>
<p>They were utterly unaware that the man among them, the one nodding solemnly, the one whose face mirrored their own grim determination, was the very architect of their suffering.</p>
<p>Rex, inhabiting the Avatar’s form, watched them through a mask of flawless piety.</p>
<p>’Look at them,’ he thought, a cold, mocking amusement swirling in the depths of his consciousness. ’So eager to die for ghosts...’</p>
<p>’So ready to throw their lives into the meat grinder I’ve built for them...’</p>
<p>’They think they’re marching toward vengeance, but they’re just marching toward the slaughterhouse...’</p>
<p>’I’ll give them their answer... I’ll give them the answer of breaking ribs, the answer of punctured lungs, the answer of blood filling their mouths until they choke on their own righteousness.’</p>
<p>As the formal session dissolved, the room erupted into the controlled chaos of urgent, whispered conversations. Apostles huddled in small clusters, their faces grim with purpose as they discussed troop movements and supply lines.</p>
<p>The Avatar rose from its seat with deliberate grace, the movement fluid and imposing.</p>
<p>It moved through the crowd like a living icon, offering brief, measured words of encouragement to the nearest apostles. Each word was carefully chosen, each nod calculated to reinforce their faith in the mission.</p>
<p>"Stay focused," the Avatar whispered to a young lieutenant whose hand trembled slightly on his sword hilt. "Our purpose is righteous."</p>
<p>"The cost will be high, but it will be worth it."</p>
<p>The lieutenant looked up, eyes bright with fanatical hope. "For the fallen, oh savior."</p>
<p>"For the fallen," the Avatar echoed, the words tasting like ash in his mouth.</p>
<p>Finally, the Avatar turned and began to stride toward the exit, Elizabeth falling into step beside him with the silent synchronicity of two predators moving in tandem.</p>
<p>As they passed through the heavy chamber doors and left the sanctum behind, the false light of the council room faded. The moment the doors clicked shut behind them, the Avatar’s expression underwent its final transformation.</p>
<p>The heroic resolve, the compassionate commander, and the righteous symbol all evaporated. What remained was something hollow, something cold, something that looked exactly like the face of a man who had already seen the end of the world and found it satisfying.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The assembled leadership of the Underlayer began to disperse in a throne room far beneath the island’s foundation, where sunlight was a forgotten myth and the only illumination came from the sickly, violet black shadows that bled from the walls.</p>
<p>The air still vibrated with the residual energy of Rex’s commands, leaving a charged, electric stillness in its wake.</p>
<p>Cassandra and Gorvasha moved off in perfect unison, their cloaks whispering against the stone floor. Even as they walked away, their conversation remained low and urgent, not about strategy, but about the artistry of the kill.</p>
<p>"The third choke point," Gorvasha murmured, her voice thick with a hunter’s hunger, "we need to ensure the debris falls at an angle that pins them against the eastern wall."</p>
<p>"I want them trapped against the stone before the ceiling drops..."</p>
<p>"I want them to feel the cold granite against their spines before it crushes their lungs. " Cassandra’s answering chuckle was dry and hungry. "Agreed."</p>
<p>"We won’t give them the mercy of a clean death."</p>
<p>"We’ll make it slow."</p>
<p>Pavellia moved with the brisk, mechanical efficiency of a clockwork automaton, her fingers dancing across leather-bound reports as she organized the final logistical contingencies.</p>
<p>Mordecai, meanwhile, retreated toward his quarters with the hurried, stumbling gait of a man who had narrowly avoided execution.</p>
<p>His breathing was shallow; every time he closed his eyes, he could still see the cold, inhuman certainty in Rex’s gaze, and it made his skin crawl with the instinctual terror of prey recognizing its predator.</p>
<p>Lilith lingered by the throne room’s entrance for a heartbeat too long. Her fingers traced the carved obsidian of the doorway, her pulse racing with a volatile mix of terror and intoxicating excitement.</p>
<p>She could almost smell it already—the copper tang of blood, the scorched smell of magic failing, and the sweet stench of panic.</p>
<p>Catching herself before her expression betrayed her, she straightened her posture and hurried after the others, her footsteps echoing like rapid heartbeats in the cavernous hall.</p>
<p>Lord Xerollion remained alone.</p>
<p>He stood in the center of the throne room as the deep, oppressive quiet settled over Castle Nocturna like a burial shroud.</p>
<p>Beneath the ornate mask that concealed his true features from every soul in this domain save for the few who already knew the terrifying truth, his lips curled into something that was not quite a smile but something far more dangerous: pure, unadulterated satisfaction.</p>
<p>The duality of the situation was a masterpiece of psychological warfare. Two sides of a war that had not yet drawn blood.</p>
<p>One side was preparing to march forward with hymns on their lips, believing themselves the righteous instruments of long overdue justice, their hearts full of noble purpose and vengeful fire.</p>
<p>The other side was coiled in the dark, preparing to receive them with a trap forged from months of patient, invisible labor, bloodless planning, and calculated deception.</p>
<p>Every soldier, every strategist, and every grieving mother and father who had spent the afternoon nodding solemnly to Apollo’s righteous speeches were all moving forward as puppets on invisible strings.</p>
<p>They were all marching toward their doom according to the singular, absolute will of one mind. A mind that occupied both rooms simultaneously; a mind that belonged to neither side of the conflict but stood above it as its sole arbiter.</p>
<p>Rex allowed himself a long moment to simply exist in that silence. He savored the exquisite tension of the moment, the precise instant when a plan ceases to be an idea and becomes an inevitability.</p>
<p>It was the stillness before the avalanche, the indrawn breath before the scream.</p>
<p>’Let them march,’ he thought, his consciousness expanding through the darkness. ’Let them bring their righteousness, their courage, and their sacred vows.’</p>
<p>’All they are bringing is fuel for the pyre...’</p>
<p>’All they are bringing is more meat for the grinder...’</p>
<p>Turning away from the empty throne, his mind already pivoting toward the final, granular details of the massacre, Rex began to mentally rehearse the coming slaughter.</p>
<p>The corridor’s mouth was preparing to open wide, and he would make sure it was hungry enough to swallow everything that dared to walk into it.</p>
