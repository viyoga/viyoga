<p>The atmosphere in the training hall on the second morning carried a different charge than the day before, a charged, static tension that made the fine hairs on Rex’s arms stand on end. Yesterday had been an explosion of violence; today was the uncomfortable, suffocating aftermath.</p>
<p>Word had spread through the Nightwing household overnight with the insidious speed of a plague. It didn’t travel through official channels or formal decrees; it moved through the whispered conversations of chambermaids polishing silver, the knowing glances exchanged between armored guards in the corridors, and the hushed tones of servants passing trays.</p>
<p>By the time Rex arrived at the sixth hour, the hall was already unnaturally crowded.</p>
<p>The twelve elite fighters were no longer the sole occupants of the floor. Junior guards, whose duty called them elsewhere, had suddenly discovered a desperate need to practice their footwork in this specific hall.</p>
<p>Two administrative staff members had positioned themselves near the arched entrance with the transparent excuse of delivering urgent messages—messages that Rex knew intimately could have waited until sunset. Even the hangers-on, distant cousins, and in-laws who typically avoided anything that involved genuine exertion or sweat had slunk into the shadows along the walls, their eyes sharp with morbid curiosity.</p>
<p>They weren’t here for training. They were here to witness the aftermath of a spectacle.</p>
<p>They wanted to see if the demon who had systematically dismantled the heir was real or if yesterday had been some fever dream of blood and broken bones.</p>
<p>Aurelia noticed the gathering. Her lips pressed into a thin, firm line, but she made no move to disperse them.</p>
<p>Rex understood this silence as a concession; she was allowing them their morbid fascination, as long as it didn’t interfere with the lesson.</p>
<p>"They want to see if yesterday was real," Aurelia said quietly as they crossed the polished stone floor together.</p>
<p>Her voice was low enough that only Rex could hear, but it carried the weary authority of a woman who had spent the night managing both physical injuries and political fallout. "I told them it was."</p>
<p>"They did not fully believe me until they saw the twelve limping through breakfast this morning."</p>
<p>Rex’s expression remained impassive, though his mind briefly cataloged the memory of the previous day: the wet crunch of ribs under his boot, the way Theo’s nose had given way under his fist, the crimson spray that had painted the floor.</p>
<p>"I did not leave anyone limping badly enough to matter," Rex countered.</p>
<p>"You left Rilena favoring her left ankle," Aurelia corrected, though there was no venom in her tone. "It is not a complaint..."</p>
<p>"She earned that injury honestly, and she has already told me twice this morning that she wants to run the sequence again as soon as the ache subsides."</p>
<p>"That is what a good result looks like in this household, Rex."</p>
<p>"Pain that produces determination instead of resentment. And pain that makes them hungry rather than broken."</p>
<p>Rex nodded grimly. That was the core of his philosophy: violence as a tool for refinement, not merely destruction.</p>
<p>As they approached the center of the hall, Rex spotted Diana. She was already on the floor, moving through a series of fluid, lethal stretches.</p>
<p>She was dressed in practical training gear designed to move as an extension of her own body, revealing the corded muscle and lean grace that made her such a formidable asset. When she sensed his approach, she caught his eye across the space.</p>
<p>Her gaze held that particular expression she had cultivated over the months, a look that wasn’t quite a smile but held the unspoken promise of one if he proved himself worthy of the effort.</p>
<p>"You are late," Diana called out as he closed the distance.</p>
<p>Her voice was clear, carrying across the silent hall, drawing the attention of the spectators in the shadows. It was a blatant lie, but it was the specific kind of playful falsehood Diana used as an opening rather than a reprimand.</p>
<p>Rex stopped at the designated training mark, his stance relaxed but his presence commanding.</p>
<p>"I am exactly on time," he replied smoothly. "You simply arrived early because you wanted a front-row seat to my warm-up."</p>
<p>Diana’s lips quirked upward.</p>
<p>"I arrived early because Aurelia asked me to help demonstrate the morning drills," she said, but the slight tremor in her voice and the way the corner of her mouth twitched betrayed her.</p>
<p>The first reason hadn’t been entirely false, but it certainly wasn’t the only one.</p>
<p>Aurelia raised her hands and delivered a single, sharp clap. The sound was not merely a noise; it was an auditory command that reverberated off the vaulted stone ceilings like a crack of thunder.</p>
<p>In an instant, the loose, murmuring crowd along the walls stiffened. The casual posture of the junior guards vanished, replaced by the rigid alertness of soldiers who knew that when a Nightwing matriarch spoke, the world tended to stop turning.</p>
<p>"Today is different from yesterday," Aurelia said, her voice cutting through the remaining silence like a honed blade. "Yesterday was a demonstration of capability."</p>
<p>A pause followed, heavy with the memory of the previous day’s violence.</p>
<p>"Today is instruction." Her gaze swept across the room, pausing briefly on the spectators before locking onto the twelve elite fighters. "Rex Rexilion holds the acting director’s authority in this hall until Leonidas returns."</p>
<p>"Every person standing on this floor is to treat his corrections with the same reverence you would afford mine."</p>
<p>Her eyes shifted to the periphery, narrowing as they landed on the extended family members and the administrative staff who had lingered too long. "That includes the family members who wandered in to watch."</p>
<p>"If you intend to sit against that wall, you will sit in absolute silence."</p>
<p>"If you cannot respect the sanctity of this training hall, you will be put to work. And I promise you, the labor I assign will be far less pleasant than watching."</p>
<p>One of the cousins, a young man who had been leaning smugly against a pillar, bolted upright, his face flushing crimson as he scrambled to find a more unobtrusive position.</p>
<p>Rex began to move. He paced the floor with the same deliberate, predatory economy he had shown the day before, but his internal focus had shifted.</p>
<p>Yesterday, he had been reading the room as a battlefield, evaluating exits, measuring distances, and assessing threats.</p>
<p>Today, he was reading the room like a forge. He wasn’t looking at the floor; he was looking through the people.</p>
<p>He saw the twelve elites: they were bruised, yes, but their eyes held something more dangerous than pain. They held the raw, unvarnished hunger of those who had been broken and had decided they would never be broken again.</p>
<p>He saw the junior guards: a patchwork of potential and dangerous inconsistency, their stances revealing the sloppy habits that inexperience breeds.</p>
<p>He saw Diana: a blade already sharp, but one that needed the careful whetstone of refinement to reach a level of lethality the House of Nightwing had not witnessed in generations.</p>
<p>And then, there was Theo.</p>
<p>Near the back of the formation, standing like a specter in the shadows, was the heir. His arms were crossed tightly over his chest, his face still bearing the faint, yellowish bruises of yesterday’s assault.</p>
<p>He was watching Rex with that same unnerving, flat expression, the expression of a man who was not frustrated but who was calculating. Rex noted his position, the silent challenge in the boy’s stare, and deliberately said nothing. To acknowledge the provocation would be to grant it power.</p>
<p>"We start with the fundamentals," Rex announced, his voice carrying to every corner of the hall. "Not because any of you lack them."</p>
<p>"But because fundamentals are the first things to erode when the blood starts flowing and the lungs begin to burn."</p>
<p>"I would rather find your flaws here, in the controlled safety of this hall, than find them in a real engagement where erosion costs you your life or the lives of those beside you."</p>
<p>The lesson began with brutal honesty. Rex moved through the space like a ghost of discipline.</p>
<p>He demonstrated the perfect stance: the precise distribution of weight that allowed for instantaneous redirection, the subtle angle of the hips that turned a defensive posture into a coiled spring.</p>
<p>He moved from person to person, his touch clinical and uncompromising. He would step behind a junior guard, his hand applying firm, corrective pressure to an elbow that was turned half an inch too far outward.</p>
<p>"Your balance is compromised," Rex would murmur, his voice quiet but carrying the weight of absolute truth. "Fix it, or you’ll be dead before you can draw your sword."</p>
<p>He moved to another, adjusting the angle of a shoulder. "Stop watching their hands."</p>
<p>"The hands lie. Watch their hips, their eyes, and their breathing."</p>
<p>"The body cannot hide its intent."</p>
<p>The air in the hall grew thick with effort. There was no shouting, no grand speeches, only the rhythmic sound of breathing, the occasional scrape of boots on stone, and the quiet, deadly precision of Rex Rexilion systematically rebuilding the unit from the bone up.</p>
