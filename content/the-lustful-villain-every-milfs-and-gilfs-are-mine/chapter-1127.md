<p>The Grand Academy’s main hall was a cathedral of controlled chaos. The air was thick with the smell of parchment, melted candle wax, and the metallic tang of sharpened steel.</p>
<p>Maps were spread across massive oak tables like battlefields waiting to be bled upon, and the constant murmur of officers discussing troop movements created a low-grade hum of anxiety that vibrated through the stone floor.</p>
<p>Valentina stood at the center of this storm, her face a mask of strategic calculation. When Elliot and Evelyn appeared in the doorway, the mask cracked.</p>
<p>For a fleeting second, her composed features softened with a relief so unguarded it was almost painful to witness.</p>
<p>"You’ve returned," Valentina said, rising from her seat, her voice carrying across the hall. "I had wondered if the darkness swallowing Aethelgard would reach you before you could make it home."</p>
<p>"We heard enough on the road to know that ’darkness’ is an understatement," Elliot said, his voice cutting through the ambient noise like a blade.</p>
<p>He stepped forward, the exhaustion of their journey replaced by a rigid, militant posture. "We aren’t here for comfort or debriefings, Lady Valentina..."</p>
<p>"We want in. We want to be part of the forces marching into the Underlayer."</p>
<p>Valentina didn’t flinch at his intensity. Instead, she studied them both.</p>
<p>Her eyes, sharp, ancient, and weighing, traveled from Elliot’s clenched fists to the unnerving stillness in Evelyn’s gaze. She wasn’t just looking at them; she was assessing their combat readiness, their mental state, and whether they would break when the first bone snapped.</p>
<p>"Your timing is either miraculous or cursed," Valentina said finally, a grim smile touching her lips. "But we cannot afford to be picky..."</p>
<p>"We depart in three days. I’ll have you both formally registered with the appropriate divisions before the evening’s bells toll."</p>
<p>In the far corner of the hall, seated at a smaller table littered with supply manifests and casualty projections, Rex Rexilion allowed himself a glance toward the doorway.</p>
<p>To any observer, his expression was the picture of genuine warmth, the easy, welcoming smile of a comrade who had missed his friends.</p>
<p>’Perfect,’ Rex thought, his mind operating on a plane entirely divorced from the warmth of his expression. ’The pieces have landed exactly where they belong.’</p>
<p>Behind the easy smile, Rex’s mind was already rewriting the calculus of the slaughter to come. He wasn’t seeing friends; he was seeing assets.</p>
<p>Two additional reincarnators, both battle hardened, both fueled by a desperate need for redemption. Their arrival was a gift.</p>
<p>Elliot’s Heroic Strike would provide the precise kind of blunt force momentum needed to drive the front line deep enough into the northern corridor to spring the trap to ensure that when the Underlayer’s horrors rushed forth, they would be met not with hesitation, but with a crushing wave of righteous fury that would seal their fate.</p>
<p>But as his gaze lingered on Evelyn, the warmth in his mind cooled to something analytical and cold.</p>
<p>Evelyn.</p>
<p>He remembered the quiet way she had spoken to Helena months ago and the subtle, dangerous perceptiveness she carried beneath her pleasant exterior. She was a creature of patterns.</p>
<p>She was a creature who noticed the seams in the story. Her presence didn’t threaten the trap itself; the massacre in the corridor was inevitable, designed to be too overwhelming for any single mind to unravel in real time, but she represented a variable of awareness.</p>
<p>She was one more set of watchful eyes that might notice the trap before it snapped shut.</p>
<p>One more mind capable of seeing the hand that pulled the strings.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t fear her; he simply catalogued her. He filed her name into a private, dangerous list of variables that required constant monitoring.</p>
<p>The war would proceed as planned, the blood would flow as intended, and Evelyn Astralight would be watched, measured, and neutralized as part of the inevitable cost of his victory.</p>
<p>Elliot found Apollo in one of the smaller training yards adjacent to the main hall, a secluded space where the air tasted of pulverized stone and sweat.</p>
<p>Apollo was alone, lost in a trance of violent motion.</p>
<p>He was running through a series of controlled, devastating movements meant to test the current limits of his Life affinity’s reach. With every strike of his palm against the air, green sparks of vitality flickered, leaving behind faint, shimmering trails of energy that hummed like a disturbed hive of bees.</p>
<p>The moment Apollo caught sight of Elliot standing at the yard’s edge, a lone silhouette against the torchlit corridor, the rhythm of his training shattered. He abandoned the exercise entirely, his movements breaking from fluid grace into something raw and urgent.</p>
<p>He closed the distance between them at something close to a sprint, his boots skidding against the packed earth.</p>
<p>The embrace that followed was not a gentle greeting; it was a collision. It carried the desperate, bone-deep relief of two people who had been hollowed out by each other’s absence, unaware of the void until the moment they finally touched again.</p>
<p>Apollo gripped Elliot’s shoulders with enough force to bruise, his breath coming in ragged gasps.</p>
<p>When Apollo finally pulled back, Elliot felt a cold shock run through him. Apollo looked different than the boy he had left behind.</p>
<p>The boyish enthusiasm, that bright, almost naive fire that had once burned in his eyes, had been tempered, smothered, perhaps by something far heavier.</p>
<p>He looked older, his jawline sharper, his eyes carrying the haunted stillness of someone who had stood in the center of a slaughterhouse and survived.</p>
<p>He bore the visible weight of someone who had shouldered genuine loss and had been forged in its fires, emerging not broken, but irrevocably changed.</p>
<p>"You look like you’ve aged five years in five months," Elliot said, his voice rough as he searched his friend’s face, looking for any trace of the old Apollo beneath the new, hardened exterior.</p>
<p>"Feels like a lifetime, sometimes," Apollo admitted.</p>
<p>His smile was worn, a fragile thing that didn’t quite reach the shadows beneath his eyes. "Some days, the weight of it all makes it hard to even draw breath."</p>
<p>"But I’m glad you’re back, Elliot... Genuinely glad."</p>
<p>"This city... it’s been bleeding for weeks."</p>
<p>"We’ve needed every capable hand, every spark of real power, and yours has been missing for longer than I could bear to admit."</p>
<p>The admission cut through Elliot like a serrated blade. The guilt he had been trying to channel into resolve earlier resurfaced immediately, flooding his chest with a nauseating heat.</p>
<p>This time, he didn’t try to mask it. He didn’t try to be the stoic warrior.</p>
<p>He let the shame show in the way his shoulders slumped and his voice cracked.</p>
<p>"I know. And I’m sorry..."</p>
<p>"God, Apollo, I’m so sorry... I wasn’t here." Elliot’s fingers clenched into fists at his sides. "I was out there chasing phantoms and monster nests in distant lands, trying to prove to myself that I was worth something."</p>
<p>"While you were here, holding this place together while it burned."</p>
<p>He stepped closer, his gaze locking onto Apollo’s with an intensity that bordered on desperation. "Don’t spare me, Apollo."</p>
<p>"Don’t give me the sanitized version or the official report from Lady Valentina."</p>
<p>"Tell me everything... Every scream, every atrocity, every death that didn’t make the papers..."</p>
<p>"I need to understand what actually happened while I was busy being selfish... I need to know what kind of hell I’m walking back into."</p>
