<p>Lustia’s eyes didn’t just light up; they ignited with a hungry, predatory luminescence that made the amber void beyond the chamber seem dull by comparison. This was the moment she had been waiting for the moment the student stopped asking how to survive and started asking how to dominate.</p>
<p>"That depends on the shape of the monster you’re preparing to kill, Rex," she purred, stepping closer until the heat from her body rivaled the ambient glow of the room. "Are you building for individual combat superiority, becoming a walking catastrophe that can tear through any single opponent?"</p>
<p>"Are you building for administrative omnipotence the ability to project your will across two territories simultaneously without your attention fracturing?"</p>
<p>"Or are you building for survival against the inevitable, preparing for the moment the framework sends a divine class entity to erase your very existence?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. "All three... Ideally, I want all three."</p>
<p>"But I’m not interested in hypothetical perfection... I want practical dominance."</p>
<p>"If I have to choose a priority, the single thread that unravels everything else, tell me what actually matters most given the position I’m in right now."</p>
<p>Lustia rose from the couch, her movements feline and deliberate. She wandered toward one of the massive golden pillars, trailing a claw-tipped finger along its spiraling surface.</p>
<p>The sound of her nail dragging against the metal was like a blade scraping bone—sharp, intimate, and unsettling.</p>
<p>"There are categories of power you haven’t even touched yet, Rex."</p>
<p>"You’ve mastered the physical, you’ve learned to bend the elemental, and you’ve begun to grasp the esoteric."</p>
<p>"But those are still playing with the toys of the universe." Her voice dropped an octave, becoming something darker, more profound. "You need to consider Conceptual Manipulation."</p>
<p>Rex’s inner mind immediately began to process the term. ’Concepts... Not the force itself, but the meaning behind it.’</p>
<p>"Instead of controlling fire as a chemical reaction or gravity as a dimensional force, you would be reaching for control over the very idea of heat or the concept of weight," Lustia explained, turning to face him. "It is the difference between bending a river to your will and rewriting what ’water’ fundamentally means within a bounded space."</p>
<p>"You could make a blade that doesn’t just cut flesh but cuts the concept of ’durability’ from whatever it touches."</p>
<p>"It is extraordinarily difficult to acquire, staggeringly expensive, and terrifyingly effective."</p>
<p>"Most gods still operate through physical or dimensional mediums; they throw lightning, and they warp space."</p>
<p>"But if you control the concept? Their lightning becomes meaningless because you’ve rewritten what ’energy’ is allowed to do in your presence."</p>
<p>"What’s the risk?" Rex asked flatly.</p>
<p>He knew there was always a price, and with Lustia, the price was usually paid in blood or exposure.</p>
<p>"The risk is that conceptual manipulation, even at a low potency, is exactly what the framework monitors with religious fervor." Her expression grew grim. "Concepts are the language of creation."</p>
<p>"They are the territory only true gods are supposed to hold... If you acquire this power and use it carelessly, you won’t just be noticed."</p>
<p>"You will be screamed about... You will become a beacon that the Goddess of Justice cannot ignore."</p>
<p>Rex processed this, his mind already weighing the tradeoff: absolute power versus absolute exposure. "And the alternative? The other path you hinted at?"</p>
<p>"Domain Creation," Lustia said, and this time, a note of genuine, almost cruel amusement returned to her voice. "The classic path for ambitious mortals who have grown tired of living in someone else’s house."</p>
<p>She gestured to the opulence surrounding them. "A personal domain."</p>
<p>"A pocket of reality that you author entirely... A space where your own rules supersede the world’s default physics, exactly as mine do here."</p>
<p>"It would give you an unassailable stronghold, a fortress of the mind where no enemy, divine or otherwise, could touch you without your explicit permission."</p>
<p>"More importantly, it would give you a laboratory—a place to experiment with dangerous workings, to evolve without the prying eyes of the heavens."</p>
<p>"But understand the stakes: the cost is astronomical, both in energy and in mental fortitude. A domain without a perfect foundation is not a fortress; it’s a trap."</p>
<p>"If it destabilizes, it won’t just vanish; it will collapse catastrophically, crushing its creator beneath the weight of their own failed reality."</p>
<p>Rex stared at her, the implications of both choices warring in his mind. One was a weapon of mass destruction that would announce his arrival to the universe; the other was a sanctuary that could become his tomb.</p>
<p>He needed to know how a predator would think.</p>
<p>"Which would you choose," Rex asked, his voice steady despite the cosmic implications of the question, "if you were standing where I am?"</p>
<p>Lustia turned back to face him, and for the first time since he had known her, the seductive mask didn’t just slip—it dissolved entirely.</p>
<p>The playful goddess, the teasing lover, and the flirtatious companion—all of it vanished, leaving behind something cold, ancient, and terrifyingly sharp.</p>
<p>Her eyes weren’t those of a woman; they were the eyes of a strategist who had watched civilizations burn to ash and had orchestrated half of them.</p>
<p>"Right now?" she said, her voice carrying the flat, unyielding weight of iron. "With the war between Aethelgard and the Underlayer poised to tear the sky apart?"</p>
<p>"I would tell you to abandon the idea of conceptual manipulation like it’s poison."</p>
<p>She stepped closer, her presence suddenly overwhelming, each footfall echoing like a funeral knell. "It is too loud, Rex... Too bright."</p>
<p>"Trying to grasp at concepts now would be like setting off a flare in the middle of a stealth operation."</p>
<p>"You don’t need to rewrite the meaning of existence to win the war that’s coming; you just need to survive it."</p>
<p>"And to survive it, you need more than just power... You need space."</p>
<p>Rex watched her intently. His mind was racing, calculating the tactical implications of her shift in tone.</p>
<p>’She’s not talking about winning a fight anymore,’ he realized. ’She’s talking about survival in the face of inevitable retaliation.’</p>
<p>"A Domain," Lustia continued, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial murmur that sent a chill down his spine. "That is your priority."</p>
<p>"Not as a weapon to wield against demons or angels, but as insurance."</p>
<p>"Think of it, Rex. A place where your growing collection of your maximum bonds, their children, and the things you have bled for and fought to protect can exist in absolute, inviolable safety."</p>
<p>"A sanctuary where the politics of the surface and the brutality of the Underlayer cannot reach."</p>
<p>"A place where you can retreat, bleed, heal, and plan without the prying eyes of the framework or the claws of your enemies finding you."</p>
<p>She leaned down, her face inches from his, her breath hot against his skin. "Build your foundation before you reach for the tools that draw a god’s eye."</p>
<p>"Secure your kingdom before you try to rewrite the laws of reality."</p>
<p>Rex nodded slowly. The logic was flawless.</p>
<p>He could almost see it as a fortress of his own design, a private reality where he was the sole arbiter of truth. The temptation was intoxicating, but he kept his expression impassive. He needed to know the price of the blueprints.</p>
<p>"How much energy?" Rex asked, his voice rough. "For a stable domain, at a scale that actually matters?"</p>
<p>"I’m not interested in a closet; I need a world."</p>
<p>Lustia gave him a blunt, humorless smile. "More than you currently hold."</p>
<p>"All forty-nine thousand points would be a mere down payment on the foundation alone." She straightened up, pacing again. "You will need to keep accumulating."</p>
<p>"Every kill, every territory seized, every drop of essence harvested from the coming war—it all has to feed the creation of this space."</p>
<p>"But do not wait until you have the energy to start... Begin laying the conceptual groundwork now. Study the architecture of reality."</p>
<p>"Understand what a domain requires so that when you finally have the power to ignite it, you aren’t fumbling in the dark like a mortal playing with divine fire."</p>
<p>Rex remained seated, his fingers drumming against the armrest of the couch. His mind was already reconfiguring itself, dismantling his old priorities and rebuilding them around this new objective.</p>
<p>The war was no longer just about conquest; it was about resources for his future fortress.</p>
<p>"Understood." Rex’s voice was steady, but beneath the surface, his thoughts were churning like a storm. ’A sanctuary. A laboratory. A tomb if I fail.’</p>
<p>He looked up at her, his gaze piercing. "Tell me more about what a domain actually requires."</p>
<p>"Forget the energy cost for a moment; I know I’m not there yet."</p>
<p>"Tell me about the structural principles... What separates a stable domain from one that collapses and crushes its creator?"</p>
