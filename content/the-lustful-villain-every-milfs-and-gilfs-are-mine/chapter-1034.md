<p>The space Theo now occupied defied every law of physics his analytical mind had spent three years cataloging. There was no floor in any conventional sense—no stone, no wood, no solid matter—yet he stood.</p>
<p>It was a surface that presented itself so perfectly, so absolute in its solidity, that the distinction between ’floor’ and ’void’ became purely semantic. He was standing on the concept of stability itself.</p>
<p>The light was wrong. It wasn’t sunlight, nor was it firelight; it was an amber gold radiance that felt neither warm nor cold but was simply present.</p>
<p>It was pervasive, structured, and omnidirectional, as if the very fabric of reality had decided to become luminous. There was no source to point to, no sun on the horizon, and no lamp in the corner.</p>
<p>The light simply was, pouring out of the nothingness.</p>
<p>And then, there were the symbols.</p>
<p>They weren’t merely written; they were woven into the architecture of existence. Theo stared, his mind instinctively trying to process them, but his cognitive faculties recoiled.</p>
<p>They occupied a visual register that bypassed language entirely; his brain recognized them as profoundly meaningful before his conscious mind could even begin to articulate what they were.</p>
<p>Massive, circular arrangements of interconnected glyphs spiraled through the amber haze, some so vast they seemed to form the horizon itself. Others were microscopic, tiny inscriptions etched into the very quality of the air, shimmering like dust motes made of pure information.</p>
<p>All of the glyphs were in motion, rotating slowly and eternally, swirling in patterns that indicated a dynamic nature, as the concepts they represented were never static.</p>
<p>’Time.’</p>
<p>The realization hit him not as a thought, but as a visceral shock. These weren’t metaphors for passing seconds or changing seasons.</p>
<p>These were the literal, mathematical notations of time itself. This was the cartography of the fourth dimension.</p>
<p>He was standing inside the blueprint of causality, surrounded by the written language of a framework that mapped temporal properties, dimensions, and behaviors with the same casual precision a sailor might use to map the stars.</p>
<p>This was a system developed by something that had been watching the universe unfold for eons before the first human tongue had ever formed a syllable.</p>
<p>’I should be dead,’ his inner analyst screamed, the voice cracking with panic. ’The impact of the fall is physics; there is no logic here.’</p>
<p>’There is no data to support this existence.’</p>
<p>’I am standing in the middle of a contradiction.’</p>
<p>"Theo Morr Nightwing."</p>
<p>The voice didn’t come from a direction. It didn’t travel through the air. It simply manifested.</p>
<p>It wasn’t loud, but it possessed a terrifying authority that made the concept of ’volume’ seem trivial. It was the voice of something that did not need to raise itself above ambient noise because ambient noise was merely a property of its domain.</p>
<p>It was as if the space itself had opened its mouth to speak his name. The sound resonated not in his ears but in his bones, vibrating through his teeth and settling in the hollow of his chest.</p>
<p>It was a summons. A recognition.</p>
<p>Theo’s heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird, the only familiar, mortal thing in this impossible expanse.</p>
<p>His training in years of analyzing threats, reading intentions, and calculating outcomes felt utterly useless here.</p>
<p>How do you analyze something that predates the concept of analysis?</p>
<p>How do you prepare for a confrontation with the fundamental mechanics of reality?</p>
<p>Trembling, his muscles locked in a primal fight or flight response that had nowhere to run, Theo turned toward the source of the sound.</p>
<p>The presence that now occupied the space behind him defied every instinctual categorization of the human mind. It was not anthropomorphic in any conventional sense; there was no flesh, no bone, and no skin, yet it held the unmistakable shape of a person standing upright.</p>
<p>It possessed the outline and proportions that the brain defaults to when it desperately needs to make sense of a foreign presence: two legs, a torso, and the suggestion of shoulders.</p>
<p>But there was no substance to the form. The figure was a silhouette carved out of the very fabric of this dimension.</p>
<p>Within that outline, the same amber gold symbols that flooded the horizon ran in a continuous, violent torrent. The symbols did not merely decorate the figure; they constituted the figure itself.</p>
<p>The entity was composed of temporal notation itself, a walking lattice of causality and consequence, swirling in patterns so complex they made Theo’s vision swim.</p>
<p>"You were falling," the voice stated.</p>
<p>The words weren’t an observation; they were a statement of fact, as undeniable as gravity.</p>
<p>Theo swallowed hard, his throat feeling like it was lined with glass. "Yes."</p>
<p>"You intended to be falling," the voice said.</p>
<p>The cadence was flat, devoid of judgment, yet heavy with the implication of absolute understanding.</p>
<p>"Yes," Theo replied, his voice steadier than he felt.</p>
<p>He was forcing the analyst in his mind to take control, to stop the screaming panic and start gathering data, even if that data was impossible.</p>
<p>"You are not falling now."</p>
<p>Theo turned his head slightly, scanning the impossible expanse around him. There was no wind, no terminal velocity, and no sensation of rushing air. Just the silent, eternal rotation of the symbols.</p>
<p>"No," he said quietly.</p>
<p>The figure moved. ’Moved’ was a pathetic word for it; there was no shift in weight, no displacement of air, and no sound of footsteps. It was simply a relocation of existence.</p>
<p>One moment the entity was several paces away; the next, it was closer, its presence pressing against Theo’s consciousness like a physical weight.</p>
<p>Theo felt a spike of adrenaline, not the hopeful kind, but the cold, sharp terror of a prey animal recognizing an apex predator. But beneath that terror, something else flickered: a bitter, defensive recognition.</p>
<p>"I know who you are," Theo said, his voice gaining strength as he leaned into his own understanding of the world’s mechanics. "Or at least, I know what you are."</p>
<p>"The symbols. The domain. The control over the fundamental flow."</p>
<p>"You are a god of time."</p>
<p>"I am the God of Time," the voice corrected. The correction was absolute. "The designation is singular."</p>
<p>Theo let out a breath that was halfway between a laugh and a sob. "Then I know who you are... And I know exactly what this is."</p>
<p>"Do you?" The question wasn’t challenging; it was curious, like a scientist asking a specimen if it understood its own cage.</p>
<p>"This is the moment in the story where someone offers me power," Theo said.</p>
<p>His voice had gone flat, drained of all emotion, adopting the hollow tone of someone who had read too many books, lived too many lives, and seen too many patterns. He was past the stage of awe; he was in the stage of cynical recognition.</p>
<p>"This is the trope... The hero has hit rock bottom."</p>
<p>"The world has stripped him of everything: his family, his status, and his dignity."</p>
<p>"And now, a cosmic entity appears in the void to offer him the one thing that will allow him to claw his way back up."</p>
<p>He looked directly into the swirling vortex of symbols that served as the entity’s face. "This is where you tell me that the lowest possible point is actually the beginning of something."</p>
<p>"That this despair is just the fuel for a new arc."</p>
<p>A silence followed a silence so profound it felt as though the symbols themselves had stopped moving.</p>
<p>"And you do not believe that," the voice said.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question. It was an observation of Theo’s deepest wound.</p>
<p>"I believe it is what people say to the person at the lowest possible point," Theo said, each word dripping with the venom of three years of accumulated failure. "Whether it is true or not is a different question entirely."</p>
