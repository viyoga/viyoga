<p>He stood alone in the narrow corridor of the agricultural district, letting the sensation of total victory wash over him. It was a profound, heavy satisfaction, the kind that only comes when every variable has resolved exactly as calculated, and a few have even exceeded the projection.</p>
<p>He hadn’t actually planned for Ignivara to reach a 100/100 bond. His main plan was centered on the Legion’s arrival, the need for a successful operation, and the exact display of skills needed to make the Celestina communication happen.</p>
<p>The maximum bond in the tree line had been an unplanned variable, a beautiful, violent accident. The system had identified a condition he had inadvertently forged, and the system was never wrong.</p>
<p>He had discovered a fundamental truth of human psychology in Aethelgard: the most effective mechanism for building absolute, soul-crushing desire was to tell a person the complete, unvarnished truth about how thoroughly you had dismantled their entire reality. To show them the cage and then show them that you were the only thing worth loving inside it.</p>
<p>He would file that under ’Tactical Psychological Warfare,’ he noted internally.</p>
<p>"Lilith... secure Ignivara for me... I’ll need her," Rex said it to no one, but he knew that Lilith could probably hear him.</p>
<p>"Yes, my master..."</p>
<p>And then he takes over his Avatar’s body... just for a second to check what’s up and then his real body slowly walked through the shadow.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>As he began the walk back toward the plaza, the transformation began. The predatory glint in his eyes smoothed over; the cold, calculating god retreated behind a mask of human frailty.</p>
<p>By the time he reached the first row of intact buildings, his expression had settled into a masterfully crafted persona: the warm, slightly weary countenance of a man who had been through the trenches, a man who had faced hardship and returned to find his people safe.</p>
<p>The 2,000 points, the memory of Ignivara’s surrender, the broken spirit of Zane, and the terrifying scope of his morning’s work were all buried deep beneath layers of artificial empathy. They were entirely, perfectly invisible.</p>
<p>He emerged from the side street at the edge of the plaza.</p>
<p>The crowd caught sight of him, the weary hero, the survivor, the protector. The roar that erupted was visceral, a tidal wave of sound that caused the very stones beneath his feet to vibrate.</p>
<p>The cheering didn’t just increase; it doubled, a frantic, desperate outpouring of love for a man who didn’t possess a single shred of it.</p>
<p>Rex stood at the edge of the plaza, his eyes scanning the spectacle with a cold, detached precision. He watched the Avatar, that magnificent, hollow geological construct standing at the center of the square.</p>
<p>He saw Lily’s arm looped tightly, affectionately, through the Avatar’s, and Apollo’s hand resting firmly on its shoulder, a gesture of camaraderie and shared triumph.</p>
<p>To any onlooker, it was a scene of profound unity. To Rex, it was a logistical nightmare of identity management.</p>
<p>He was currently processing the challenge of reclaiming his own life while his very essence was being convincingly performed by a mountain of sentient stone thirty meters away.</p>
<p>He had planned for this. He always planned for the friction.</p>
<p>He had choreographed the avatar’s movement to drift toward the Academy gate approximately forty seconds before his own consciousness re-anchored. It was a calculated repositioning, mimicking the natural ebb of a hero transitioning from the roar of public adoration to the quiet of private recovery.</p>
<p>He had waited for the crowd’s collective gaze to pivot, to follow the movement of the "hero," creating a momentary blind spot in the social fabric. By the time Rex stepped from the shadows of the side street, the Avatar’s back was turned, and the crowd’s primary orientation had shifted.</p>
<p>The stage was set.</p>
<p>He stepped into the plaza, moving with a measured, rhythmic stride that suggested fatigue rather than the predatory grace he actually possessed.</p>
<p>Elizabeth saw him first.</p>
<p>She didn’t just "notice" him; she perceived him. Her analytical awareness operated on a frequency that bypassed the mundane, a register of perception that most humans couldn’t even fathom.</p>
<p>As he approached, he saw the exact moment her eyes locked onto him.</p>
<p>She didn’t look at him with the joy of a friend seeing a survivor; she looked at him with the flat, terrifying intensity of an investigator who had just found a glaring, impossible anomaly in a closed system.</p>
<p>She was building a model in real time, and he was the data point that refused to fit.</p>
<p>Rex walked directly into her orbit, ignoring the cheering masses, his focus narrowing solely on the woman who was far too smart for her own good.</p>
<p>"You are over there," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>Her voice was a low, razor-thin thread, intended only for his ears. It wasn’t a question; it was a statement of a perceived impossibility.</p>
<p>"I needed to handle something," Rex replied, his voice matching her controlled volume, devoid of any performative warmth.</p>
<p>"Something," Elizabeth repeated.</p>
<p>The word was heavy, laden with the unspoken suspicion that ’something’ was a euphemism for a grand deception.</p>
<p>She stared at him, her gaze dissecting him. This was not the expression of a student receiving a lecture or a friend offering comfort.</p>
<p>This was the analyst’s stare, the cold, unblinking focus of a predator examining a strange new specimen. She wasn’t just seeing Rex; she was weighing the probability of his presence against the known location of the Avatar.</p>
<p>’She knows,’ Rex thought, a dark flicker of amusement dancing in his mind. She knows the magic wasn’t just a trick. ’She knows the signature of the soul is too distinct to be faked by mere stone.’</p>
<p>’She’s just deciding how much of the truth she can handle before she breaks.’</p>
<p>"How long have you been standing at the side street’s entrance?" she asked, her eyes never wavering from his.</p>
<p>"Approximately forty seconds," Rex answered, his tone maddeningly casual.</p>
<p>"The Avatar moved toward the Academy gate approximately forty seconds ago," Elizabeth stated, the logic snapping into place like the teeth of a trap. "The timing is too precise to be coincidental."</p>
<p>"It means you were watching, waiting for the Avatar to clear the crowd’s primary field of attention before you made your entrance."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. He didn’t offer a defensive lie. He simply presented the logic of a master manipulator.</p>
<p>"The crowd’s orientation follows the subject," Rex said, his voice smooth and clinical. "Entering from the side while the crowd’s primary orientation was toward the center would have produced a specific, messy problem: two Rex Rexilions in the same visual field."</p>
<p>"A paradox the common mind isn’t equipped to resolve."</p>
<p>A silence stretched between them, thick with the tension of a high-stakes interrogation. Elizabeth’s eyes narrowed, searching for a crack in his composure, a hint of the monster beneath the mask of the weary hero.</p>
<p>"So you waited," she said, her voice a quiet accusation.</p>
<p>Rex allowed a ghost of a smirk to touch his lips, a small, arrogant tilt of the mouth that signaled his total lack of remorse for the deception.</p>
<p>"I positioned," Rex corrected, his eyes gleaming with a cold, superior light. "There is a difference."</p>
<p>Elizabeth stared at him, her gaze a silent, razor-sharp acknowledgment of his distinction. She found his choice of words, "positioning versus waiting," to be exactly as meaningful as it actually was: a semantic veil used to mask a calculated lie.</p>
<p>To her, it was a distinction without a difference, a mere way for a predator to dress up his tracks.</p>
<p>"The Tremor situation has been fully addressed," Rex said, his voice dropping into a professional, report-like cadence.</p>
<p>He didn’t care for the nuance of her suspicion; he only cared for the efficiency of the data transfer. "Ignivara Ashenwyrm is at the plaza’s east edge."</p>
<p>"She has agreed to provide information about the Legion’s surface network in exchange for not being detained."</p>
<p>"The Tremor situation," Elizabeth repeated.</p>
<p>She didn’t just say the words; she dissected them. Her voice carried the weight of a scientist weighing a volatile isotope, testing the structural integrity of the phrase to see how much truth it was actually concealing.</p>
<p>"Is resolved," Rex added, his tone final, a hammer blow intended to close the subject.</p>
<p>"And you came from the direction of the agricultural district," she said, her eyes tracking the invisible line between his current position and the carnage he had just left behind. "Rather than from anywhere a person who had simply arrived at the conclusion of the engagement would have emerged from."</p>
<p>Rex met her gaze, his eyes as cold and unyielding as the stone Avatar he had left behind. He didn’t offer a lie; he offered a challenge.</p>
<p>"How long?" she asked.</p>
<p>The question wasn’t about the distance or the time spent walking. It was a probe into the depth of his deception.</p>
<p>"Long enough," Rex replied.</p>
<p>It was a perfect, infuriating answer. It was a truth that functioned as a lie.</p>
<p>Elizabeth fell silent. The air between them grew heavy, thick with the unspoken realization of the magnitude of his fraud.</p>
<p>She looked toward the Academy gate, where the Avatar was now nearly invisible to the retreating crowd. She looked at the broken, blood-stained cobblestones of the plaza, then back to the specific, unnatural geometry of Rex’s position.</p>
<p>In the span of a few heartbeats, her mind constructed a terrifying, flawless model of the last hour.</p>
<p>"The Avatar," she began, her voice trembling with the sheer weight of the logic she was assembling, "has been holding Lily’s arm for the last twelve minutes."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, his expression a mask of bored indifference.</p>
<p>"Lily has not noticed," Elizabeth whispered, the realization hitting her like a physical blow.</p>
<p>"The Avatar is accurate," Rex countered, his voice devoid of any guilt.</p>
