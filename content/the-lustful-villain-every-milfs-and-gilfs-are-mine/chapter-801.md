<p>The roar of the plaza was a cacophony of mindless adoration, a sea of voices screaming for a savior that didn’t actually feel the warmth of their praise. To the Avatar, the cheering was nothing more than a frequency to be processed, a data point in a chaotic environment.</p>
<p>It was a geological construct, a masterpiece of stone and logic operating at a staggering 97.3% behavioral accuracy. It didn’t feel the triumph; it merely simulated the microexpressions of a man who had just survived a cataclysm.</p>
<p>It was a performance of humanity so precise that the distinction between soul and stone was invisible to the masses.</p>
<p>The tension in the air was thick, heavy with the scent of pulverized rock and ozone, when the equilibrium was violently shattered.</p>
<p>THUD.</p>
<p>Lily didn’t just walk into the Avatar; she collided with it like a projectile. She had been moving with the frantic, desperate energy of someone who had spent the last hour trapped in a gravity prison, her mind spiraling with every second she spent watching the destruction through the unyielding stone walls. She was a whirlwind of bruised pride and raw, unadulterated fury.</p>
<p>The Avatar’s reaction was instantaneous. Its arms swept out, catching her with a calibrated tension—not the frantic grip of a man terrified of her anger, but the steady, grounding hold Rex used to anchor her.</p>
<p>It was a physical acknowledgment of her existence that bypassed the need for words.</p>
<p>"You’re late," Lily hissed, her voice muffled against the fabric of the Avatar’s jacket, her body trembling with the residual adrenaline of her ordeal.</p>
<p>"I was occupied," the Avatar replied.</p>
<p>The voice was a perfect sonic match for Rex’s—that specific, infuriating register he used when he was telling her something that was technically a truth, but a truth stripped of its most vital context.</p>
<p>Lily wrenched herself back, her eyes burning as she forced herself to look up into that face. It was a hauntingly accurate reconstruction.</p>
<p>The Avatar possessed the subtle, almost imperceptible asymmetry of Rex’s jaw; it held the exact warmth in its eyes that Rex reserved for her, a stark contrast to the cold, predatory calculation he displayed when the world wasn’t watching. Even the respiration was a lie of perfection, the slight, deepened intake of breath that signaled intimacy rather than mere oxygen consumption.</p>
<p>For a moment, the silence between them was more violent than the tremor itself.</p>
<p>"Occupied," she repeated, the word dripping with skepticism and a growing, painful ache in her chest.</p>
<p>"The tremor situation required direct handling," the Avatar stated, its tone as steady as the bedrock it was composed of.</p>
<p>"You were in there!" Lily erupted, her voice cracking as the frustration of the last hour finally breached the dam.</p>
<p>She gestured wildly at the shattered landscape, the jagged meteorites, and the pulverized earth that had served as her prison. "I was pressed against those walls, feeling the vibration of every impact, feeling the ground scream under my hands, and at no point did you tell me where you were, what you were doing, or if you were even alive!"</p>
<p>The Avatar didn’t flinch. It simply watched her, its eyes reflecting a calm that felt almost insulting given her state of emotional wreckage.</p>
<p>"You knew I was going to be fine," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>"I knew you thought you were going to be fine!" Lily screamed, her eyes glistening with the onset of tears. "Those are two entirely different things, and you know it! You play this game of stoic certainty while I’m left drowning in the uncertainty of your silence!"</p>
<p>The air between them felt heavy, charged with the kind of emotional friction that could shatter stone. The Avatar paused, its internal processors reconciling her outburst with its behavioral parameters.</p>
<p>It settled into the expression Rex used when he was accepting a correction, a look of quiet, defeated validation that signaled he had no intention of fighting a battle he knew he had already lost.</p>
<p>"You are right," it said, the simulation of humility flawless. "I should have communicated more clearly."</p>
<p>Lily held him for one more heartbeat, her eyes searching the Avatar’s face for a flicker of something more than just a perfectly rendered imitation. She was looking for the soul behind the stone, assessing the depth of the response to see if there was a lie buried in the precision.</p>
<p>Finding nothing but the steady, unwavering presence of the man she loved, she let out a ragged sigh and collapsed back into his arms, hugging him with a desperate, quiet ferocity.</p>
<p>The Avatar, maintaining the perfect physical calibration to support her weight, looked over her shoulder. His gaze swept across the plaza, past the cheering masses, to the figures standing in the wake of the devastation.</p>
<p>Diana was positioned three meters back. Her bow was slung across her back, a silent testament to the violence that had just transpired.</p>
<p>Her expression was a fortress; she was performing that familiar, silent calculation weighing a dozen different truths and deciding which ones were safe to utter in front of a crowd of sixty witnesses. She fixed the Avatar with a flat, piercing gaze, the kind of look that could strip a man of his secrets.</p>
<p>She inclined her head once, a sharp, tactical nod. The Avatar returned the gesture with the exact, measured angle Rex would have used to signal mutual respect without unnecessary flourish.</p>
<p>As Lily finally stepped back, breathing more steadily, Diana moved in. She stopped at the two-meter mark, the precise distance she maintained when she was about to deliver a critique she had been sharpening in her mind since the first tremor hit.</p>
<p>"The dragon," Diana said, her voice cutting through the lingering hum of the crowd like a blade.</p>
<p>"Contained," the Avatar replied.</p>
<p>"I know it is contained," Diana snapped, her eyes narrowing. "I was there for the containment."</p>
<p>"What I am asking about is the specific moment where you dropped it into the northern rubble from a height of sixty meters instead of letting it land on the eastern agricultural district, which was its natural fall trajectory."</p>
<p>"The eastern agricultural district is structurally intact," the Avatar countered.</p>
<p>The logic was unassailable, delivered with the cool efficiency of a machine.</p>
<p>"The northern rubble is not," Diana countered, her voice dropping an octave, heavy with the weight of the destruction. "That was the variable you chose to ignore."</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said simply.</p>
<p>Diana stared at him, her eyes scanning the construct with the clinical, unrelenting attention of her designation. She wasn’t just looking at him; she was looking through him.</p>
<p>"You were tracking the fall trajectory while you were fighting Ignivara," she accused, her voice a low lash. "Simultaneously... you were calculating physics while dodging dragonfire and bone-crushing impacts."</p>
<p>"The Foresight window runs continuously," the Avatar explained.</p>
<p>He used the exact register Rex employed when explaining something he considered fundamentally obvious, a tone that was neither arrogant nor defensive, merely factual.</p>
<p>Diana held his gaze for a long, tense moment. The Avatar did not blink.</p>
<p>He maintained the steady, unshakable ease of Rex’s social training and the uncanny ability to endure intense scrutiny without the micro fidgets or defensive shifts that usually signaled a man was hiding a mistake.</p>
<p>"Alright," Diana finally said.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a concession. It was the flat, weary acknowledgment of someone who had extracted the maximum amount of truth the current situation allowed and had decided to take her remaining interrogations to a place with better acoustics and less audience.</p>
<p>She stepped back, her eyes lingering on him for a second longer than necessary.</p>
<p>Behind her, Aisella moved forward. The elf healer remained in her habitual assessment posture, her eyes scanning the Avatar’s frame with the reflexive instinct of a professional.</p>
<p>To any onlooker, the Avatar appeared in peak physical condition, a masterpiece of geological perfection. But Aisella saw the nuance.</p>
<p>She saw the "post-engagement profile" the avatar was intentionally projecting: the slightly depleted energy signature, the subtle, calculated tension in the jaw, and the specific tightness in the shoulders that followed a massive, high-output exertion of Rex’s abilities.</p>
<p>The Avatar had been programmed to run this profile, and he was executing the lie with terrifying accuracy.</p>
<p>Aisella stepped to his left without waiting for an invitation. It was the characteristic behavior of an elf healer who had worked alongside Rex long enough to know that an assessment was not a request but a biological necessity.</p>
<p>Her fingers reached out, her hand closing firmly around his wrist to gauge the pulse of his energy.</p>
<p>The Avatar allowed the intrusion, remaining as still as a mountain.</p>
<p>"The energy signature is at seventy-three percent," Aisella reported, her eyes fixed on his wrist rather than his face.</p>
<p>She was reading his internal architecture with the intense focus required for a subject of such complexity. "The geological layer is stable..."</p>
<p>"The biological baseline is slightly elevated, but within acceptable parameters." She finally looked up, her brow furrowing in professional skepticism. "You should not have been able to pull off that maneuver at sixty percent reserve."</p>
<p>"The strain should have broken your coordination."</p>
<p>"My geological anchor that surpasses the earthen authority’s does not draw from the standard reserve," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>He used the precise, instructional cadence Rex used when explaining a distinction in power that most people were too uneducated to understand. "The geological substrate itself provides a portion of the output."</p>
<p>"The personal reserve is merely used to coordinate the expression, not to fuel the raw force."</p>
