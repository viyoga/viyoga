<p>In the devastated silence of the plaza, something impossible began to happen.</p>

<p>The gaping, cauterized crater where Rex’s heart had been ceased to be a wound and became a wound in motion. The edges of the flesh didn’t just heal; they writhed.</p>

<p>The torn muscle fibers, charred by divine lightning, began to crawl toward one another like living worms. A wet, sickening sound—SLOP, SQUELCH, RIPPLERIPPLE—echoed off the broken walls as layers of tissue knotted together in a grotesque display of biological reconstruction.</p>

<p>Then came the sound of the spine.</p>

<p>CRACK CRACK CRACK CRACK.</p>

<p>It was the sound of wet timber breaking under immense pressure as each vertebrae fused back into alignment, knitting itself together with unnatural speed.</p>

<p>The crowd of watchers gasped in unison, some in horror, some in disbelief, as they watched the corpse reassemble itself.</p>

<p>Then came the heartbeat.</p>

<p>THUMP.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just a sound; it was a seismic event. A single, thunderous pulse that shook Rex’s entire frame and sent a physical tremor through the cracked stone, vibrating through the soles of the feet of everyone standing at the plaza’s edge.</p>

<p>THUMP.</p>

<p>A second pulse, stronger, more deliberate.</p>

<p>THUMP.</p>

<p>A third pulse that didn’t fade. It stayed. It claimed the space.</p>

<p>The violet mark at his wrist suddenly flared with a violent, amethyst light, pulsing in time with his new heartbeat.</p>

<p>Simultaneously, the crimson mark above his chest kindled, burning like a fresh wound against his skin. Rex’s chest suddenly lurched upward as he drew a massive, ragged breath, a sound like a man breaching the surface after drowning in the deepest trench of the ocean.</p>

<p>His lungs burned, his throat screamed, but he breathed.</p>

<p>And when he opened his eyes, the madness was gone. The bloodshot terror and the dying agony had vanished.</p>

<p>His gaze was unnervingly clear, sharp as a surgeon’s scalpel.</p>

<p>He laughed.</p>

<p>It wasn’t the mocking chuckle of a villain or the hysterical cackle of a madman. It was a loud, cracked laugh shot through with something terrifyingly genuine: plain, honest delight.</p>

<p>It was the laugh of someone who had just survived the impossible and found the experience exhilarating. The sound made the hair rise on the necks of every witness; it was the sound of a predator who had learned something new about the hunt.</p>

<p>Rex rolled onto his knees. His movements were fluid despite the fresh scars crisscrossing his torso.</p>

<p>He pressed a blood-slicked hand against his sternum, feeling the solid rhythm beneath his palm. He sat there for several seconds, his shoulders heaving, his body shaking with the aftershocks of resurrection.</p>

<p>His face, though scarred and streaked with dried blood, was lit up with a terrifyingly boyish grin.</p>

<p>"That was... brilliant," he wheezed between ragged breaths, wiping a smear of blood from his lip. "Truly... That was the first time I’ve actually died since I woke up in this hellish world."</p>

<p>He looked up at Evelyn, his clear eyes locking onto hers with unsettling intensity. "And I’ll admit it, Evelyn... I found it absolutely fascinating."</p>

<p>Evelyn flinched. She took half a step back, her boots scraping against the stone.</p>

<p>Her breathing was shallow, her eyes wide with a primal terror she couldn’t suppress. She had just executed a god, only to watch that god stand back up and ask for seconds.</p>

<p>Rex rose to his feet. He didn’t rush. He didn’t charge.</p>

<p>He stood slowly, testing the weight of his limbs, the solidity of his bones. Then, he began to walk toward her.</p>

<p>He walked with the calm, deliberate pace of a man who had already seen the end of the story.</p>

<p>He walked like a reader who had just turned the final page of a book he had loved, satisfied, thoughtful, and already wondering what the sequel would hold.</p>

<p>"Let me spare you the exhausting effort of working it out," Rex said.</p>

<p>His voice wasn’t a roar this time; it was something far worse. It was conversational.</p>

<p>It was intimate. And it carried across the ruined plaza with the unnatural clarity of a predator speaking to prey.</p>

<p>"I can’t die while they’re alive, Evelyn."</p>

<p>The words hit the assembled crowd like a physical blow. Rex’s gaze swept past her, toward the edges of the crater, lingering on the faces of those who had watched him die.</p>

<p>"Every woman I’ve bound to me is an anchor."</p>

<p>"A lifeline stitched into my very soul." His grin widened, revealing teeth stained pink with residual blood. "You can kill me as many times as you like."</p>

<p>"You can burn me to ash, crush me under mountains, or tear my heart out again and again..."</p>

<p>"But I’ll come back... Every single time."</p>

<p>"Until the last thread snaps."</p>

<p>He began to count, each name a death sentence delivered with terrifying casualness.</p>

<p>"Start with Lily and Diana, standing right there, trembling in the dirt."</p>

<p>"Then Elizabeth... Valentina..."</p>

<p>"Mara and Marceline, who are both carrying my children through this nightmare."</p>

<p>"Iris, Aurelia, Elaris, Morwenna, Amelia..." His voice dropped to a whisper that somehow reached every ear in Aethelgard. "And so on, down the list, until there is nothing left but silence."</p>

<p>Evelyn heard it like a blow delivered by an invisible hand. Her knees buckled slightly, not from physical weakness, but from the sheer, corrosive horror of the revelation.</p>

<p>Everything she had been given, every ounce of her training, every drop of divine blood in her veins, rested on a single, sacred pillar: she must act for what was Just.</p>

<p>But Rex had just rewritten the rules of her universe. It was not just to kill the innocent.</p>

<p>It was not just to slaughter pregnant women or execute the daughters of the family that had raised her.</p>

<p>Rex had just demonstrated his ultimate cruelty: he had weaponized her own morality against her. He had told her, in front of the entire city, that the only road to stopping him ran directly through her becoming every monster she had sworn to destroy.</p>

<p>The golden light in her palm stuttered. It flickered weakly, casting dancing, sickly shadows across her terrified face.</p>

<p>For a heartbeat, the divine fire nearly died entirely.</p>

<p>Then Rex moved. And there was no more play.</p>

<p>No more testing.</p>

<p>No more games.</p>

<p>While his body had been knitting itself back together, his mind had been working at a speed that defied comprehension.</p>

<p>[Full Potential Adaptation] had been running in the background like a furnace, consuming every scrap of data from their duel.</p>

<p>It had processed the crushing weight of the Scales of Retribution.</p>

<p>It had analyzed the molecular composition of Judgment Lightning.</p>

<p>It had learned the frequency of her soul.</p>

<p>[ADAPTATION COMPLETE: DIVINE COUNTERMEASURES ENGAGED]</p>

<p>[WRATH: 100%]</p>

<p>[LUST: 100%]</p>

<p>Rex vanished.</p>

<p>There was no sound of movement, no displaced air, just a sudden, violent absence where he had stood. He reappeared inches from Evelyn’s face.</p>

<p>CRACK SHATTER!</p>

<p>His first punch connected with her [Shield of Verdict]. The divine barrier, which had withstood the weight of the heavens moments before, didn’t just break; it disintegrated like a pane of cheap glass hit by a sledgehammer.</p>

<p>The feedback sent a shockwave through Evelyn’s arms that made her bones groan.</p>

<p>Before she could even scream, his second strike landed. A brutal, piston-like fist buried itself into her solar plexus.</p>

<p>THUD SQUELCH.</p>

<p>The air exploded from her lungs in a spray of blood as the force lifted her off her feet, sending her hurtling backward through the air.</p>

<p>She was a human projectile.</p>

<p>She smashed through the first building, ribs splintering with a wet CRUNCH as she tore through plaster and oak. She tore through a second.</p>

<p>Rex was already there.</p>

<p>His third strike caught her under the jaw mid-flight.</p>

<p>KRAK.</p>

<p>The sound of her mandible fracturing was audible even over the ringing in the air. The force of the blow sent her spinning wildly through a third structure, propelling her across the plaza until she slammed into the crumbling foundation of the ancient clock tower.</p>

<p>BOOM.</p>

<p>The tower groaned, its ancient stones protesting the impact, before the entire structure gave way. With a thunderous roar of collapsing masonry, the tower came crashing down directly on top of her.</p>

<p>Buried beneath tons of rubble, Evelyn’s consciousness flickered. Desperate, she reached for the divine mechanism she had used before.</p>

<p>She summoned the Scales. She reached into the metaphysical reservoir of every wound Rex had inflicted upon her during their forty-minute dance of death, intending to reverse the flow to send all that accumulated agony back down the line to crush him.</p>

<p>She pushed. She screamed internally for the retribution to manifest.</p>

<p>Nothing happened.</p>

<p>The wounds she had inflicted on him stayed where they were. The pain she had dealt him remained his alone.</p>

<p>He hadn’t just survived her power; he had learned how to become immune to its consequences.</p>

<p>He had adapted to Justice itself.</p>
