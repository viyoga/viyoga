<p>Rex met Elizabeth’s gaze with a stillness that was more unsettling than any outburst of anger could have been. His eyes were hollowed out by exhaustion and something far colder—the calculation of a man who had already weighed lives on a scale and found most of them wanting.</p>
<p>"That is the question I am building the answer to," Rex said, his voice dropping to a register that felt like grinding gravel. "The answer I am engineering is as many as possible."</p>
<p>He leaned forward slightly, the lamplight catching the hard angles of his face. "You need to understand that ’as many as possible’ is not the same as ’all of them,’ Elizabeth."</p>
<p>"I will not lie to you with false hope."</p>
<p>"I am not promising salvation for every soul that steps through that threshold."</p>
<p>"I am promising survival for the species."</p>
<p>Elizabeth didn’t flinch, but he saw the minute tremor in her fingers as she pulled them away from the research table. Her eyes drifted to the Key, the incomplete skeleton of a dimensional nightmare.</p>
<p>It sat there, pulsing with an unstable, sickly light that seemed to drink the shadows around it. For weeks, she had poured her intellect into its design, treating it as a scientific marvel, a breakthrough in quantum mechanics.</p>
<p>Now, seeing Rex’s face, she realized she wasn’t building a bridge; she was building a meat grinder that might just let some of the meat through intact.</p>
<p>"Yes," she whispered, the word tasting like copper in her mouth. "I understand the distinction."</p>
<p>"I understand what you’re asking me to do."</p>
<p>’I’m asking you to help me build a sieve for human souls,’ Rex thought grimly. ’And I’ll forgive you for the guilt, as long as the sieve works.’</p>
<p>The emergency meeting commenced at the fourth hour of the evening. The air in the High Council chamber was thick with unspoken terror and the acrid smell of burnt oil from the sconces.</p>
<p>Outside, the city of Aethelgard was silent, but it was the silence of a held breath; the city was waiting for the other shoe to drop.</p>
<p>Valentina presided over the chamber with a terrifying, glacial efficiency. She moved through the proceedings like a blade through silk, her voice carrying the flat, unyielding authority of someone who had spent forty years navigating political minefields and emergency crises.</p>
<p>She refused to let the raw horror of their situation dissolve the structure of the meeting; she knew that if they allowed emotion to override protocol, they would descend into chaos before the night was through.</p>
<p>But even her composure couldn’t mask the gravity of the report. The facts were laid bare on the obsidian table like corpses on an altar.</p>
<p>The briefing was clinical, which made it all the more nauseating. The report detailed how Theo Morr Nightwing, a boy they had known, a boy who had been part of their fabric, had been touched by something beyond comprehension.</p>
<p>He had received an Apostle designation from Kaeleth, the God of Time. The report didn’t mince words about the aftermath: the temporal authority unleashed by that designation had not just killed; it had unmade.</p>
<p>It had fractured seconds into centuries, shattered bodies into fragments of meat and bone that existed in multiple moments at once, leaving behind wounds that bled time itself.</p>
<p>The casualty lists were not just numbers; they were accounts of people who had simply ceased to have ever been, leaving only bloodstains that refused to dry.</p>
<p>Rex sat in the shadows at the edge of the council circle, his arms crossed, his face a mask of impassive granite. His mind was elsewhere, replaying the wet, tearing sounds of the reality warping violence, the way the air had screamed when time itself was flayed open.</p>
<p>He could almost feel the phantom ache in his own bones, the instinctual terror of a predator realizing it was being hunted by something that could erase its past.</p>
<p>He waited. He let the council members whisper their panicked reassurances and their horrified accusations.</p>
<p>He waited until the factual presentation of the cold, bleeding reality of their situation was complete.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for the murmurs of panic to die down; he cut through them like a scalpel through diseased flesh. When he spoke, his voice carried the weight of a funeral bell, commanding a silence that felt like a physical pressure against the eardrums.</p>
<p>"The God of Time," Rex began, his eyes scanning the horrified faces around the obsidian table, "is not some abstract myth or a distant whisper in the void."</p>
<p>"Kaeleth is recognized as a patron deity within the Underlayer’s own organizational hierarchy."</p>
<p>"While reverence isn’t universal, some of those bastards down there have no patience for worship. Structured, ritualized devotion is woven into the very fabric of several subcommunities we documented during the reconstruction."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the implication sink in. This wasn’t a random act of divine wrath; it was a bureaucratic deployment of celestial power.</p>
<p>"The designation Kaeleth granted to Theo," Rex continued, his voice growing colder, more dangerous, "was not the impulsive act of a god who decided to play with the surface world on a whim."</p>
<p>"That is naive, and in this room, naivety is a death sentence."</p>
<p>"That designation was an operational deployment... It was connected to the Underlayer’s strategic interests through a pre-existing, binding contractual relationship between Kaeleth and Celestina Von Starlight."</p>
<p>The room went ice cold. Apollo, who had been sitting in contemplative stillness, finally moved.</p>
<p>He didn’t look shocked; he looked like a grandmaster seeing three moves ahead, his mind rapidly assembling the shattered pieces of a terrifying mosaic.</p>
<p>"Celestina contracted with Kaeleth," Apollo stated. It wasn’t a question; it was a confirmation of a hypothesis that made his jaw tighten.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex replied, his gaze unflinching.</p>
<p>"The Balance Keeper’s designation and the God of Time’s authority," Apollo mused, his voice low and dangerous, "the Legion’s surface operations and the Underlayer’s divine connections... they aren’t separate phenomena."</p>
<p>"Precisely," Rex said, leaning forward into the circle of lamplight, his shadow stretching across the table like a predator’s claw. "The picture is the same picture, Apollo."</p>
<p>"The Legion, the Underlayer’s divine network, and Celestina’s operations are not three different threats."</p>
<p>"They are the same operation, viewed from three different angles."</p>
<p>"We aren’t fighting a war against multiple enemies; we are fighting a single, multi-dimensional machine."</p>
<p>Rex swept his gaze across the room, meeting the eyes of every council member, forcing them to confront the reality he was laying bare.</p>
<p>"The engagement with the Underlayer that we have been delaying—the cautious, diplomatic approach we’ve clung to like a child’s security blanket—can no longer be delayed," Rex declared, his words echoing off the vaulted ceiling. "The Underlayer has already breached our reality."</p>
<p>"They have used divine connections to deploy temporal authority against Aethelgard."</p>
<p>"They have demonstrated that they will not negotiate; they will simply overwrite our existence."</p>
<p>"If we wait for them to make the next move, we aren’t choosing patience; we are choosing suicide."</p>
<p>"Every second we hesitate costs us more lives than any delay could ever save."</p>
<p>"The Key," Apollo said, his voice sharp, cutting through the tension.</p>
<p>"Elizabeth’s reconstruction project is nearing completion," Rex answered immediately. "The final components, the materials extracted from the wreckage of what was once Tremor, will be integrated within the next several days."</p>
<p>"When the Key is functional, we will finally have something more than a shield."</p>
<p>"We will have a mechanism for direct, aggressive engagement with the Underlayer."</p>
<p>"We will stop reacting and start striking."</p>
<p>A new voice cut through the room, brittle and edged with suspicion. Morwenna, seated at the far end of the table where the shadows pooled deepest, spoke up.</p>
<p>Her voice carried the unmistakable quality of someone who had been holding a blade at her throat since the day the original Key was destroyed during the island assessment.</p>
<p>"The New Key," Morwenna said, her eyes narrowing as she fixed Rex with a stare that demanded honesty, "opens to where exactly?"</p>
<p>"Where are we sending our people?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. He didn’t offer comfort.</p>
<p>He gave her the tactical truth.</p>
<p>"A site in the Underlayer’s northern geological zone," Rex said. "The location has been selected for its tactical advantages: isolated approach vectors that prevent flanking and a single, controllable entry point."</p>
<p>"Furthermore, the specific geological composition of that zone will amplify the Earth Sovereign’s authority, the authority I carry from the Sable Island integration."</p>
<p>"We won’t just be stepping into their world; we will be bringing the power to reshape it."</p>
