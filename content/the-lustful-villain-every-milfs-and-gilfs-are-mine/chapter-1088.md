<p>Morwenna turned her attention back to the documents, her eyes scanning the columns of figures and casualty reports with a mechanical intensity that bordered on madness.</p>
<p>The lamplight flickered, casting dancing shadows across the ink, making the numbers seem to writhe like insects on the page.</p>
<p>"I have been doing the arithmetic for the last two hours," she said, her voice dropping to a dangerous, brittle whisper. "I have run every variable."</p>
<p>"I have accounted for the loss of revenue from the merchant guilds, the frozen assets from the Council’s investigation, and the projected costs of the immediate military mobilization you’re demanding."</p>
<p>She paused, her pen hovering over a ledger like a dagger over a throat. "The arithmetic does not produce a version of the next twelve months that is acceptable, Rex."</p>
<p>"There is no path forward where the Nightwing household remains a significant institution in this city."</p>
<p>"We are not just bleeding; we are hemorrhaging."</p>
<p>"If we continue on this trajectory, we won’t be facing an enemy from the Underlayer; we will be facing internal dissolution."</p>
<p>"We will be hollowed out from within before the first shot is even fired."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was suffocating. Morwenna finally lifted her head, her gaze locking onto Rex’s with the force of a physical blow.</p>
<p>"Without assistance," she said, the words hanging in the air like a confession.</p>
<p>"We both know that’s the only reality left," Rex replied.</p>
<p>His voice was gravelly, devoid of comfort, acknowledging the brutal truth they both inhabited.</p>
<p>A heavy, glacial quiet settled over the room. This wasn’t the silence of contemplation; it was the silence of a surrender.</p>
<p>It was the sound of someone who had weighed the cost of their pride against the cost of their survival and found that pride was a luxury they could no longer afford.</p>
<p>Morwenna looked at him with the flat, unflinching stare of a woman who had spent forty years climbing to the apex of Aethelgard’s power, only to realize she was standing on a crumbling cliffside.</p>
<p>Her jaw tightened, the muscles working visibly beneath her pale skin. She looked at him not as a subordinate, not even as a commander, but as a desperate ally someone she had spent the last month viewing with suspicion, even hostility, but whom she now had to invoke.</p>
<p>"The household’s position," she said, her voice gaining a steadier, more desperate strength. "Our reputation..."</p>
<p>"The specific, carefully cultivated political standing that the Nightwing family has maintained for four generations built through blood, alliance, and careful calculation."</p>
<p>Her eyes dropped to the documents again, her fingers tracing the jagged lines of their decline. "I cannot restore it alone."</p>
<p>"He damage isn’t just surface level; it’s structural."</p>
<p>"It’s deep, it’s specific, and it’s poisoned at the root..."</p>
<p>"The timeline is too compressed, Rex. The wound is too fresh, the blood loss too rapid."</p>
<p>"The standard approaches—the diplomatic maneuvering, the controlled public statements, the financial restructuring—are useless against a catastrophe of this magnitude."</p>
<p>"We cannot fix this with ink and parchment."</p>
<p>Morwenna lifted her head, her gaze narrowing as she studied Rex. She didn’t look at him with gratitude or relief; she looked at him like a strategist examining a volatile weapon—one that could either save her or detonate in her face.</p>
<p>"You," she said, the word carrying the weight of a grim acknowledgment. "Are apparently very effective at this specific category of problem."</p>
<p>"Problems that cannot be solved with diplomacy."</p>
<p>"Problems that require... a more decisive application of force."</p>
<p>Rex met her stare with unblinking neutrality. "I specialize in outcomes, Morwenna... Not methods."</p>
<p>"And you have," she continued, her voice lowering, each syllable measured as if she were walking through a minefield, "interests that are served by the Nightwing household remaining a significant institution."</p>
<p>"You need our legitimacy, our resources, and our seat at the table."</p>
<p>"You cannot afford to watch us decline into the specific, pathetic irrelevance that our current trajectory produces."</p>
<p>"My interests align with the survival of this house," Rex said.</p>
<p>It was a half-truth, but in their world, half-truths were the only currency that held value.</p>
<p>She held his gaze for a long time, the silence stretching between them until it became painful. The lamplight cast deep hollows beneath her eyes, making her look older, more desperate, and more dangerous.</p>
<p>She was a woman standing at the edge of an abyss, deciding whether to jump or to burn the bridge behind her.</p>
<p>"Then," she said, and when she spoke again, her voice possessed that terrifying, crystalline clarity of someone who has crossed the Rubicon. "I am asking for your assistance... Formally."</p>
<p>"With the understanding that this assistance comes with your specific involvement in the household’s affairs in ways that I have spent years resisting."</p>
<p>"In ways that violate every protocol I have ever upheld."</p>
<p>She leaned forward, the firelight catching the wetness in her eyes before she blinked it away, hardening them into flint. "I’ll do anything, Rex."</p>
<p>"Anything at all, if it means preserving what is left of my family’s name."</p>
<p>Rex watched her. Inside his mind, there was no surprise.</p>
<p>There was no shock. Instead, there was a cold, clinical satisfaction—the precise, mechanical hum of a plan clicking into its final gear. This was the moment he had engineered.</p>
<p>Every brutal training session, every calculated display of power, and every moment of controlled chaos over the last month had been a hammer blow against the walls of her resistance. He hadn’t been waiting for her to break; he had been waiting for her to realize that breaking was the only way to rebuild.</p>
<p>The arithmetic she had been doing—the blood, the debt, the disgrace—had led her exactly where he needed her to go. She was offering him the keys to the kingdom, thinking she was making a desperate bargain, unaware that he had already designed the locks.</p>
<p>He looked at the documents spread across her desk: the evidence of their ruin, the maps of their inevitable decline. Then he looked back at her.</p>
<p>Morwenna didn’t flinch. She held his stare with the absolute, terrifying commitment of a soldier who has accepted a suicide mission.</p>
<p>She had made her choice.</p>
<p>Rex remained silent for several heartbeats. He let the tension coil between them, letting her sit in the raw vulnerability of her own proposition.</p>
<p>He let her feel the full weight of what she was handing over.</p>
<p>Finally, he moved. He didn’t stand to leave.</p>
<p>Instead, he pulled out the chair opposite her and sat down, leaning forward until he invaded her space, his presence overwhelming the small office. His eyes locked onto hers, dark and unreadable.</p>
<p>"Tell me," he said, his voice dropping to a low, commanding register that brooked no evasion. "Tell me exactly what you think you are offering me."</p>
<p>Morwenna swallowed hard, her throat working against the dryness that had rooted itself there. She felt exposed, stripped bare by his question, but she pressed on.</p>
<p>The desperation in her chest was a physical weight, pushing the words past her lips.</p>
<p>"I am offering you access, Rex."</p>
<p>"Full administrative transparency. The authority to override household protocols when you deem them... inefficient."</p>
<p>"I will grant you a seat on the inner council, a voice that carries the weight of my signature."</p>
<p>"You will have control over the training regimens, over the deployment of our private security, and over the allocation of resources that have historically been off-limits to anyone but blood Nightwings."</p>
<p>She leaned closer, her voice cracking slightly. "I am offering you the power to reshape this house in your image."</p>
<p>"The resources to fund whatever operations you require, and the political cover to act without the scrutiny of the Council."</p>
<p>"I am giving you the tools to rebuild Aethelgard’s strength, using whatever methods you think necessary."</p>
<p>Rex listened, his face a mask of impassive calm. He watched her the way her fingers trembled when she spoke of power, the way her eyes darted toward the door as if seeking an escape that didn’t exist.</p>
<p>He let her finish, let her exhaust her offerings, and let her believe she was the one negotiating from a position of desperate strength.</p>
<p>When she finally fell silent, panting softly from the exertion of her own confession, Rex didn’t speak immediately. The silence stretched, growing heavier, more oppressive, until the air in the room felt like liquid mercury.</p>
<p>Then, the mask slipped.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a dramatic change. There was no sudden sneer or theatrical laugh.</p>
<p>Instead, the neutrality simply drained out of his features, replaced by something far more terrifying: a cold, predatory amusement. His eyes didn’t soften; they sharpened, turning into something glacial and ancient.</p>
<p>The ’soldier’ disappeared, and something far more calculating, something darker, emerged.</p>
<p>Morwenna froze.</p>
<p>"Rex?" she whispered, the name catching in her throat. "What... what is that expression?"</p>
<p>"You’re offering me paperwork, Morwenna," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice had changed. The gravelly soldier was gone, replaced by a smooth, velvet tone that carried the edge of a razor blade.</p>
<p>"You’re offering me signatures, seats on committees, and the right to move numbers on a ledger."</p>
<p>"You think that’s what power looks like?"</p>
<p>He leaned back in the chair, crossing one leg over the other with deliberate casualness. "I didn’t spend weeks dismantling your defenses just to become another bureaucrat in your household."</p>
<p>"I didn’t orchestrate this entire collapse just to help you file reports."</p>
