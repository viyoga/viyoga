<p>For several heart-stopping seconds, the order hung suspended in the air like a guillotine blade frozen at its apex. No one moved.</p>
<p>The soldiers’ minds seemed to lag behind their bodies, trapped in a cognitive dissonance where their hands still gripped their hilts with white-knuckled instinct even as their spirits had already collapsed.</p>
<p>They were waiting for the trap. They were waiting for the laughter, for the sudden burst of violet magic that would incinerate them all for their perceived weakness.</p>
<p>Then, the first surrender broke.</p>
<p>CLANG.</p>
<p>A spear struck the stone somewhere near the front of the line, the sound unnaturally sharp, cutting through the heavy atmosphere like a gunshot.</p>
<p>Then came another.</p>
<p>THUD. CLATTER. SHRING.</p>
<p>The sound spread down the ranks in an uneven, stuttering rhythm, a broken percussion of defeat. Metal meeting stone became the only language spoken in the corridor, a series of small, hollow echoes that announced the death of Aethelgard’s defiance.</p>
<p>In the middle of the formation, a young recruit barely eighteen, his face still smooth despite the blood spattered across his cheeks, stared down at his sword as if it had betrayed him.</p>
<p>His hands shook with such violent tremors that when he finally let go, the blade didn’t just fall; it danced.</p>
<p>CLINK CLINK CLINK.</p>
<p>It bounced off the rubble, skidding across the floor in a pathetic display of weakness before coming to rest in the shadows. The boy’s breathing came in shallow, terrified gasps.</p>
<p>Huuu... huuu... huuu...</p>
<p>Beside him, a scarred veteran who had survived three border skirmishes and a dozen siege attempts did not shake. He simply looked at his blade with hollow, dead eyes.</p>
<p>With a grim, practiced calm that was more terrifying than the boy’s fear, he lowered his sword.</p>
<p>His movements were mechanical, the actions of a man who understood the mathematics of war: he knew that laying down a blade was not the same as laying down a life, but it was the only way to preserve what little remained of either.</p>
<p>Further back, an officer, a man whose voice had been hoarse from screaming orders that had fallen on deaf ears all day, simply reached for his belt.</p>
<p>With a metallic CLICK, he unbuckled his sword belt entirely. The weapon and its sheath fell as one heavy mass, disappearing beneath the general wreckage with a muted THUMP.</p>
<p>He didn’t look at the enemy; he simply stared at the floor, his jaw set in a line of silent, bitter humiliation.</p>
<p>Xerollion watched this cascade of surrender with a quiet, predatory satisfaction. He no longer felt the need to mask his triumph behind a veneer of detached neutrality.</p>
<p>Every blade that struck the stone was a thread in his tapestry finally being pulled taut. Every clatter of steel was a note in his symphony of conquest.</p>
<p>’How naive they are,’ he thought, his inner mind savoring the exquisite irony. ’They think this surrender buys them safety.’</p>
<p>’They think they have traded their pride for their lives.’</p>
<p>’They do not realize that by laying down their weapons, they have only made it easier for me to dismantle them.’</p>
<p>He hadn’t needed to slaughter every last soul in Aethelgard. He hadn’t needed to turn this city into a charnel house of bone and viscera.</p>
<p>He had only needed to kill the symbols to break the heroes, to shatter the constructs, to wound the leaders and let the corrosive power of fear finish the work that violence alone could never have accomplished so cleanly.</p>
<p>Fear was a more efficient weapon than any spell; it killed the will before it ever touched the flesh.</p>
<p>But the silence of the surrender was not to last.</p>
<p>On the opposite side of the corridor, beyond the broken lines of the defenders and the watchful gaze of the conquerors, the Underlayer’s formation suddenly erupted.</p>
<p>The word "surrender" had barely finished settling into the demon ranks before the dam broke.</p>
<p>What followed was not merely noise; it was an auditory tsunami. A wave of sound erupted from the front lines of the Underlayer, a primal, guttural roar that rolled backward through the entire army in an unstoppable surge of adrenaline and relief.</p>
<p>For months, these soldiers had been coiled springs of nervous tension, living in the shadow of Rex Rexilion’s absolute dominance, waiting for the moment they could finally stop being prey. That moment had arrived.</p>
<p>The celebration was chaotic, violent, and beautiful in its madness. Cheering broke out in a dozen different languages and guttural demonic dialects simultaneously, creating a wall of sound that made the very air vibrate.</p>
<p>ROOOOOOAAAAAARRR.</p>
<p>Weapons that had been raised in murderous intent were now thrust toward the cracked ceiling, clashing together in celebration rather than threat.</p>
<p>CLANG. CLANG. CLANG.</p>
<p>Armor plates banged against ribs as soldiers threw themselves at one another, embracing with bone-crushing force. Rank and formation were abandoned in favor of pure, unadulterated release.</p>
<p>In the center of this maelstrom stood Mordecai Vrael.</p>
<p>For months, Mordecai had been a ghost haunting his own throne room. Since Rex had first seized control, Mordecai had existed in a state of perpetual, twitchy anxiety, a man who walked on eggshells made of glass, terrified that any wrong word would trigger another catastrophe.</p>
<p>He had been a shell of a king, hovering at the edges of every engagement with a nervous energy that made his subordinates recoil.</p>
<p>But now, something snapped inside him.</p>
<p>With a sound that was half war cry and half sob, Mordecai leapt into the air. His arms were flung wide as if to embrace the entire collapsing kingdom.</p>
<p>"YES! YES!" he screamed, his voice cracking with an unrestrained joy that stunned every council member present.</p>
<p>He descended like a madman, crashing into two of his closest advisors.</p>
<p>OOF. CRUNCH.</p>
<p>The force of his embrace nearly knocked one of them off his feet, the advisor stumbling backward with a startled grunt. Mordecai didn’t care.</p>
<p>He clung to them, his fingers digging into their ceremonial robes with desperate strength. Tears of pure, unfiltered happiness streaked through the soot and blood on his face.</p>
<p>’I am alive,’ his mind screamed, a frantic, joyous chant that drowned out the tactical assessment of the battlefield. ’I am not just surviving...!’</p>
<p>’I am winning. We are winning!’</p>
<p>For the first time since Rex had shattered his illusions of power and reduced his sovereignty to a cruel joke, Mordecai felt a spark of genuine agency.</p>
<p>The gulf between merely surviving a massacre and participating in a victory felt wider than the abyss itself. The terror that had been his constant companion for months was suddenly incinerated by the heat of triumph.</p>
<p>"We did it," Mordecai gasped between ragged breaths, his voice dissolving into a hysterical laugh that bordered on madness.</p>
<p>He pulled away from his advisors, spinning in a circle, his eyes bloodshot and wild as he looked at the carnage, the surrender, and the conquerors. "By the old gods... by everything sacred... we actually did it!"</p>
<p>He turned to a nearby lieutenant, grabbing the man by his breastplate with both hands, shaking him violently. "Did you hear her? Did you hear the headmaster?!"</p>
<p>"She yielded! The Starlight line has bent the knee!"</p>
<p>His laughter turned into a wet, choking sob. "We did it! We actually fucking did it!"</p>
<p>Around him, the demon army continued to howl, a chorus of victors who had forgotten the taste of defeat, oblivious to the fact that their victory was purchased with the very soul of the city they now claimed to own.</p>
