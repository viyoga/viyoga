<p>He took a step closer, his boots crunching on the pulverized remains of a high-tier entity. He leaned down slightly, his mask’s vision burning with a terrifying, golden intensity that seemed to pierce right through Mordecai’s exhausted soul.</p>
<p>"Where’s that bravado now, huh?" Rex sneered, a cruel, mocking grin pulling at his lips. "Where’s that big, brave talk from an hour ago?"</p>
<p>"You said that you wanted to stop becoming a fucking bum and showed me the real Demon Lord, but that was all bullshit..." Rex laughed. "Even though I respected that you sounded so fucking impressive when you were sitting in the back, safe and sound, dreaming up grand strategies!"</p>
<p>Rex let out a sharp, condescending bark of laughter. "But the moment the sky actually opened up, the moment the real weight hit the scale, you folded like a cheap suit!"</p>
<p>"You threw your summons out like a child throwing tantrums, wasting every bit of energy or even gacha you had on a front line that was already drowning!"</p>
<p>"You didn’t fight a war, Mordecai; you just made a loud, expensive mess that I had to clean up!"</p>
<p>Mordecai tried to open his mouth. He wanted to defend himself, to say that he had done his best, that the sheer scale of the SSS classes was beyond anyone’s control.</p>
<p>But as he looked up into Rex’s face, the words died in his throat. Rex didn’t look like an ally right now.</p>
<p>He looked like the apex predator that had just finished a feast, his eyes gleaming with a predatory, restless energy that suggested he might decide to turn his attention to the only thing left in the courtyard.</p>
<p>Mordecai’s breath hitched. A cold, primal fear gripped his chest. He saw the way Rex’s hands were still glowing with residual elemental power, the way his muscles were coiled like a spring.</p>
<p>He realized, with a sickening jolt of terror, that Rex wasn’t just angry, but he was dominant. And in this moment, in this graveyard of gods, Rex could end him just as easily as he had ended the titan.</p>
<p>"You were all talk, bum," Rex continued, his voice dropping to a whisper that felt louder than a scream. "All noise and no substance."</p>
<p>"You wanted to play at being a true demon lord, but you couldn’t even handle the pressure of being a spectator!"</p>
<p>"You’re lucky the world is too broken for you to be a nuisance anymore."</p>
<p>Rex straightened up, looking down at the man with a mixture of pity and pure, unadulterated contempt. He didn’t wait for a response, because he knew there wouldn’t be one.</p>
<p>Mordecai just sat there, paralyzed, his eyes wide and watering, staring up at the man who had just rewritten reality, terrified that one more word of arrogance would be the one that sent Rex’s wrath crashing down on his neck.</p>
<p>"Don’t bother trying to answer," Rex said, turning his back on him as if Mordecai were nothing more than a piece of debris. "You wouldn’t know what to say if you had the breath left to say it."</p>
<p>Rex turned his back on the trembling Mordecai, his gaze sweeping across the desolate landscape of the Underlayer. The horizon was a jagged line of smoke and settling dust, lit by the dying embers of his own elemental fury.</p>
<p>Around the perimeter of the courtyard, shadows began to move. The survivors, the few warriors and mages who hadn’t been pulverized by the rifts or drained by the sheer pressure of the engagement, emerged from the ruins.</p>
<p>They were battered, bleeding, and broken, but as their eyes landed on Rex, a collective, silent reverence took hold. They didn’t approach him as a comrade; they approached him as a monument.</p>
<p>They stood at a distance, watching him with the wide-eyed, fearful respect one reserves for a storm that has finally passed.</p>
<p>Rex felt their stares, and he drank them in. The ego that had driven him through the madness of the SSS class fights surged back, stronger than ever.</p>
<p>He felt the weight of the entire district, the entire underlayer, pressing against the soles of his boots.</p>
<p>"Look at this, Mordecai," Rex said, his voice carrying across the silent courtyard, loud enough for the survivors to hear. He didn’t even need to look back to know the man was listening. "Look at what’s left..."</p>
<p>"The rifts are gone... The monsters are meat... and the sky is quiet again."</p>
<p>He spread his arms wide, a gesture of grotesque ownership. "You spent your whole life worrying about the balance, about the ’cost’ of power, and about the ’danger’ of pushing too far."</p>
<p>"But look around you! The Underlayer didn’t break because of the monsters!"</p>
<p>"It broke because it couldn’t contain me!"</p>
<p>He began to walk back toward Mordecai, his stride slow, deliberate, and predatory. The survivors held their breath.</p>
<p>The air felt thick, charged with a tension that was almost physical.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer hasn’t just survived tonight," Rex whispered, his shadow falling over Mordecai once more, eclipsing the man entirely. "It has fallen... and it has fallen under my foot."</p>
<p>"Everything you see, the rubble, the blood, the very ground you’re kneeling on, it all belongs to the man who was strong enough to take it."</p>
<p>Mordecai’s eyes went wide, a single tear tracing a path through the grime on his cheek. He wanted to scream, to protest, to remind Rex that they were supposed to be a team, but his throat was a desert.</p>
<p>He was a man staring into the eyes of a god who had grown bored of his worshippers.</p>
<p>Before Mordecai could even draw a breath to plead, Rex moved.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a clumsy strike; it was a sudden, violent blur of motion. Rex’s hand shot forward, his fingers wrapping around Mordecai’s throat with the crushing precision of a tectonic shift.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of Rex’s hand closing was the only thing that broke the silence. He hauled Mordecai upward, forcing the exhausted man to lurch off his knees and into the air, dangling precariously by his neck.</p>
<p>Rex’s grip was absolute, his thumb pressing deep into the windpipe, his knuckles white with the sheer force of his dominance.</p>
<p>"You wanted to be part of this, didn’t you?" Rex hissed, his face inches from Mordecai’s, his eyes burning with a terrifying, beautiful madness.</p>
<p>He squeezed, his muscles rippling with the effort, watching the light begin to flicker in Mordecai’s terrified eyes. "You wanted to stand in the center of the storm?"</p>
<p>"Well, here you are, Mordecai... Right in the middle of it. And the storm is starting to get hungry."</p>
<p>Rex’s fingers tightened. The air in Mordecai’s lungs was gone, and the steady, rhythmic drumbeat of panic began to override the man’s thoughts.</p>
<p>His feet kicked uselessly against the air, his hands clawing feebly at Rex’s wrist, but the grip was like a vise. Rex’s expression was cold, devoid of the camaraderie they had shared hours before.</p>
<p>He wasn’t looking at Mordecai; he was looking through him, as if calculating the exact moment when the vertebrae would snap.</p>
<p>"Maybe you’re right," Rex murmured, his voice a low, rhythmic rumble. "Maybe it’s time for a change in leadership."</p>
<p>Just as Mordecai’s vision began to haze into darkness, his internal system, the gacha architecture buried deep in his soul, triggered an emergency fail-safe. It was a last-ditch survival protocol, a desperate automatic draw that triggered when the user’s life signs flatlined.</p>
<p>[CRITICAL ALERT: LIFE SIGNS DECLINING]</p>
<p>[EMERGENCY GACHA PROTOCOL ACTIVATED]</p>
<p>[SPENDING ALL REMAINING RESERVES...]</p>
<p>THOOM!</p>
<p>A burst of violent, violet lightning erupted from Mordecai’s chest, a kinetic shockwave that slammed into Rex’s arm. The force was so sudden that Rex’s grip broke, sending Mordecai crashing back into the dirt, gasping for air.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t pursue the kill. He stepped back, eyes narrowing, his curiosity piqued by the sudden surge of energy.</p>
<p>From the glimmering vortex of the gacha draw, something emerged. It wasn’t a hero or a soldier.</p>
<p>It was a pale, gaunt thing with elongated limbs and a maw that seemed to unhinge, revealing rows of needle-like teeth.</p>
<p>A Void Eater.</p>
<p>"What the hell is this?" Rex muttered, his hand hovering near his own weapon, ready to annihilate the new threat.</p>
<p>But the Void Eater didn’t attack. Instead, it threw its head back and released a sound that tore through the silence of the ruins.</p>
<p>"SCREEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE!!!"</p>
<p>The sound was a sonic laceration, a high-pitched, mind-wrenching wail that made the survivors cover their ears in agony. But as the creature screamed, its body began to ripple and warp. It wasn’t just screaming; it was inhaling.</p>
<p>From all around the courtyard, the remains of Mordecai’s fallen, the shattered stone golems, the scorched husks of fire spirits, and the half-melted remnants of metal guardians began to dissolve.</p>
<p>They turned into swirling ribbons of blue and silver energy, attracted by the Void Eater’s cry like moths to a flame.</p>
<p>Swoosh. Whoosh. Vrrrrm.</p>
<p>The remains flew toward the creature, spiraling up its body and being absorbed into its flesh. The Void Eater’s pale skin began to harden into black obsidian. Its limbs grew massive, muscle fiber weaving itself out of the stolen essence of its dead comrades.</p>
<p>The silver energy of the fallen summons fused with its bones, creating a shimmering, metallic skeletal structure that was visible beneath its skin.</p>
<p>Mordecai lay on the ground, coughing and wheezing, watching in a daze as his failed army rebuilt itself into a single, monstrous entity.</p>
<p>Rex watched the transformation, his lips curling into a smirk of genuine interest. He didn’t look afraid; he looked delighted.</p>
<p>The creature had grown to ten feet in height, a towering monstrosity of fused materials and stolen energy, its eyes glowing with a cold, spectral light. It stood between Rex and Mordecai, a wall of raw, evolved power.</p>
<p>"Now that," Rex said, his voice cutting through the creature’s fading shriek, "is something worth fighting."</p>
<p>He raised his hand, elemental static dancing between his fingers, his posture radiating an arrogant, irresistible confidence. "You’ve got one last trick, don’t you, Mordecai?!"</p>
<p>"Let’s see if it’s enough to keep you breathing."</p>
