<p>The Support Layer was trying to play a game of chess, but Rex was playing a game of slaughter. They were trying to minimize risk, but they were also minimizing the very force required to actually penetrate his defense.</p>
<p>They were pulling their punches before they had even thrown them.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for them to find their courage. He didn’t wait for them to test his guard.</p>
<p>With a predator’s smile that never reached his eyes, Rex began to advance. He didn’t circle; he walked directly into the heart of their caution, forcing them to react to his approach, forcing them to break their careful distance, and forcing them to choose between maintaining their formation or defending themselves.</p>
<p>He walked straight into the trap they were trying to set, knowing full well he was the one who would spring it.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for them to close the distance; he invaded it.</p>
<p>By advancing aggressively, he shattered the very foundation of their caution. Caution requires control over distance, control over timing, and control over the engagement’s tempo.</p>
<p>By walking directly into their space, Rex stripped them of those luxuries, forcing them to react to him on his terms rather than theirs. He turned their measured approach into a panicked scramble for survival.</p>
<p>The second wave adapted. They were elite for a reason; they didn’t freeze.</p>
<p>Instead, they tightened their formation, their movements becoming more economical, more coiled.</p>
<p>But they were trying to adapt to a human. Rex was adapting to a mathematical certainty.</p>
<p>His martial arts mastery didn’t just predict their movements; it rendered them obsolete. The system didn’t see a sequence of punches or kicks; it saw a cascade of probability, a pattern that had already committed to its next three seconds of existence before the fighters themselves had even consciously decided on them.</p>
<p>Rex wasn’t reacting to what they were doing; he was occupying the space where they would be doing it.</p>
<p>The first fighter of this wave launched a calibrated four-strike combination: three rapid jabs building momentum toward a devastating fourth blow designed to land while Rex was still reeling from the initial assault.</p>
<p>Rex saw the fourth strike before the first had even left the fighter’s shoulder.</p>
<p>Instead of blocking, which would have required absorbing the energy, Rex moved to interrupt the rhythm. He stepped into the microscopic gap between the second and third strikes, a moment of perfect vulnerability where the fighter’s weight was suspended between movements.</p>
<p>The interruption was brutal. Rex’s shoulder collided with the fighter’s sternum with the force of a battering ram.</p>
<p>The fighter’s ribs gave way with a wet, sickening crack, the sound echoing through the hall like breaking timber. The man’s own momentum betrayed him, sending him stumbling through his own unfinished combination, his lungs seizing as Rex guided the broken stumble all the way to the floor.</p>
<p>Immediately, the second and third fighters synchronized. This was the pincer, a coordinated high-low attack designed to overwhelm Rex’s guard by attacking two different planes simultaneously.</p>
<p>Rex read both trajectories in the same millisecond. He chose to address the low attack first not because it carried more threat, but because the mechanics of the countermove placed his body in a position that made the high attack physically impossible to land.</p>
<p>As he swept the low fighter’s leg, his body rotated with such violent precision that the high fighter’s strike found nothing but empty air where Rex’s skull had been moments before.</p>
<p>The two fighters hit the floor in rapid succession, one crashing down from a leg sweep, the other tumbling from the loss of balance.</p>
<p>"Two more down," Aurelia whispered, her eyes tracking the crimson spray that had just painted a section of the hardwood. "But they’re learning..."</p>
<p>"They’re starting to see the gaps."</p>
<p>The third wave, the Reserves remained motionless. They were the most dangerous predators in the room because they were the most patient.</p>
<p>They watched with cold, analytical detachment, their minds cataloging every microexpression, every shift in Rex’s stance. They were waiting for the data, gathering the intelligence that would allow them to execute the perfect response to whatever the first eight had failed to accomplish.</p>
<p>Rex turned his attention back to the remaining two fighters of the second wave.</p>
<p>He allowed them to see what they wanted to see. He let them believe they had found the flaw in his defense, the perfect angle to exploit.</p>
<p>He deliberately shifted his stance, exposing a sliver of his flank, making the opening look tantalizingly vulnerable.</p>
<p>’Go ahead,’ Rex thought, his inner voice as cold as a winter grave. ’Take the bait...’</p>
<p>’Walk into the slaughter.</p>
<p>He had read that ’opening’ six seconds before they had even noticed it. He had been maneuvering toward that exact moment, sculpting the battlefield to ensure that when they exploited the angle, they would be walking directly into a meat grinder.</p>
<p>They saw the opening. They saw their chance to salvage the unit’s honor.</p>
<p>And with desperate, hungry eyes, they exploited it.</p>
<p>At full potential, Rex’s Martial Arts Mastery ceased to be a library of techniques and became something far more terrifying: it became a living equation of violence. It wasn’t just knowing how to punch; it was knowing the exact molecular consequence of every possible movement.</p>
<p>When the two remaining fighters of the second wave lunged into the trap he had meticulously laid, they weren’t just attacking; they were delivering themselves into a meat grinder.</p>
<p>The response to a coordinated two-person exploitation, when the target has already anticipated the trap, is a single, devastatingly precise maneuver: using the first attacker’s committed momentum to become a physical barrier against the second.</p>
<p>Rex spun, his body a blur of controlled violence. He caught the first fighter’s wrist while simultaneously driving his elbow into the second’s ribs.</p>
<p>The first fighter’s momentum was redirected into his partner’s chest with the force of a sledgehammer. There was a wet, crunching sound as ribs splintered like dry kindling, followed by the sickening gurgle of punctured lungs.</p>
<p>Both fighters collapsed, their bodies convulsing as they hit the floor in a tangle of broken limbs and labored breaths.</p>
<p>Eight fighters down. The hardwood was no longer pristine; it was slick with sweat and splatters of crimson.</p>
<p>Diana, watching from the periphery, had fallen into a deathly silence. She wasn’t just observing a sparring match anymore; she was witnessing something primal, something that defied her understanding of the man she loved.</p>
<p>Her pupils were dilated, her breath shallow, as she watched Rex move through the carnage with the cold, mechanical precision of an executioner. She was recalibrating her entire reality.</p>
<p>Then, the third wave arrived.</p>
<p>Rex looked up at them over the broken forms of the eight who lay scattered across the floor, some gasping for air, others clutching shattered bones. His gaze was flat, devoid of malice or triumph.</p>
<p>He looked at the final four with the detached curiosity of a scientist observing a new strain of virus. He was genuinely interested to see if they had learned anything from the blood spilled.</p>
<p>They didn’t come in a spread. They came in a phalanx.</p>
<p>This was not the unit’s standard tactical approach. These four moved in a tight, overlapping formation, shoulders nearly touching, moving as a single, multi-limbed organism rather than four individuals.</p>
<p>Rex’s assessment flashed across his mind: ’The smartest four...’</p>
<p>’They saw that individual approach equals failure...’</p>
<p>’They have chosen mass over maneuverability.’</p>
<p>A tight formation solved the problem of individual exploitation, but it birthed a new vulnerability: catastrophic commitment. Their combined momentum was immense, but their ability to adapt to sudden changes in direction was severely compromised by the need to maintain their structural cohesion.</p>
<p>They were a juggernaut, powerful but linear.</p>
<p>"Good," Aurelia whispered, her voice barely audible over the ragged breathing of the fallen. "That is the right idea."</p>
<p>"Let us see if the idea has teeth."</p>
<p>Rex waited. He waited until they were two steps from contact, until the air between them hummed with the displaced pressure of their approach.</p>
<p>Then, he stepped aside.</p>
<p>It was a single, economical movement, the bare minimum displacement required to vacate the kill zone. The formation’s combined momentum carried all four past him like a tidal wave passing a boulder.</p>
<p>They were skilled enough to avoid crashing into each other, but their trajectory was now committed. They had sailed past their target.</p>
<p>For four seconds, they were caught in the deadly limbo of reorientation—the time it takes a formation to pivot, reverse, and reengage.</p>
<p>To a normal human, four seconds is a lifetime of hesitation. To Rex, four seconds was an eternity of opportunity.</p>
<p>He struck before they had even completed half their arc.</p>
<p>His first target was the lead fighter of the formation. Rex’s strike wasn’t aimed at the face or chest; it was a surgical strike to the shoulder joint.</p>
<p>With a sharp, cracking pop, the joint dislocated, the tendons tearing like old rope. The fighter’s arm went limp, instantly destroying the structural stability needed to maintain the formation’s momentum.</p>
<p>The phalanx began to fray at the point of contact, the integrity of the unit unraveling like a rope with its primary strand severed.</p>
<p>The second fighter reacted instinctively, attempting to close the gap left by her collapsing comrade. It was a correct tactical instinct, but it was executed half a second too late.</p>
