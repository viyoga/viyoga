<p>The anger cracked for a moment. His expression changed into something worse, and it was something helpless.</p>
<p>"No... no, she wouldn’t..." But even as he said it, he already sounded unconvinced.</p>
<p>Rex remained silent. Gelion looked at him with shaking eyes.</p>
<p>"WHY?!" The question came out broken.</p>
<p>"WHY HIM?!"</p>
<p>No answer.</p>
<p>Gelion’s breathing became unstable again. "I BUILT EVERYTHING FOR HER!"</p>
<p>His voice cracked hard. "EVERYTHING I DID WAS FOR HER!"</p>
<p>He struggled again instinctively. The telekinesis crushed the movement flat instantly.</p>
<p>His knees nearly buckled. "I GAVE HER A KINGDOM—!"</p>
<p>Rex finally spoke. "You gave her fourteen months of lies."</p>
<p>The impact of his words was more profound than any telekinetic force. Gelion stopped moving completely.</p>
<p>For several seconds the room became silent except for his breathing. Then the breathing itself began breaking apart.</p>
<p>He suddenly looked down, his jaw clenched tightly enough to tremble. The rage still simmered within him.</p>
<p>But now it had nowhere left to go. He could neither fight nor move, nor could he deny what he had come to understand. And worst of all, he could not shake the image of her being with Rex.</p>
<p>The child. The pregnancy. The choice. All of it.</p>
<p>His face contorted in turmoil. A sound escaped him that was dangerously close to a sob before he crushed it down instantly.</p>
<p>"...Fuck." Another breath, shaking now. "...Fuck."</p>
<p>Then finally, his eyes watered despite everything he did to stop it.</p>
<p>"YOU FUCKING RUINED MY LIFE!" he screamed.</p>
<p>This time the anger sounded genuinely destroyed.</p>
<p>Rex watched him without expression. The telekinesis kept him in place with the same indifferent pressure as before.</p>
<p>"She loved me," Gelion said.</p>
<p>His voice had dropped from the scream to something quieter and worse. "She loved me before any of this... before the Underlayer... before the network... before any of it!"</p>
<p>"She came here with me because she chose to."</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Then how—" Gelion’s voice broke on the word and he stopped and rebuilt it. "How is she—"</p>
<p>"Because you were busy building a kingdom for her," Rex said, "and forgot to ask whether she wanted the version of you that was doing the building or the version of you that actually talked to her."</p>
<p>Gelion looked at him with the expression of someone who has been given an accurate answer to a question they did not want the answer to.</p>
<p>"You don’t know anything about us," he said.</p>
<p>"I know she made a decision," Rex said. "And she made it without hesitation, which tells you something about the state of the version of yourself you left her with while you were doing everything else."</p>
<p>Gelion went quiet, and the quality of his quiet was different from before. The rage was still there but it had stopped looking for an exit and had settled into something heavier.</p>
<p>"You took everything from me," he said, not screaming now, but just a statement.</p>
<p>"You were building it on ground you’d already sold," Rex said. "That’s not the same thing as me taking it."</p>
<p>Mordecai, who had been watching from the head of the table with the expression of someone observing a fire that was burning in a direction they had not anticipated, said nothing.</p>
<p>Pavellia said nothing. Cassandra, who had entered the room partway through and was standing near the door with her arms crossed and her expression carefully neutral, also said nothing.</p>
<p>The room had developed the quality of a space in which everyone present understood that the relevant conversation was between two people and that interrupting it would be a mistake.</p>
<p>"I’ll FUCKING kill you," Gelion said.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a tactical threat. It was a visceral declaration from someone who had reached this point with their entire being rather than through rational thought.</p>
<p>"You can try," Rex said, and his voice was entirely level and entirely without performance. "But you are currently suspended thirty centimeters off the ground by a mechanism you cannot break, in a room full of people who are waiting to see what I decide to do next, and your ability to kill me is approximately the same as your ability to have made different choices fourteen months ago."</p>
<p>He paused.</p>
<p>"Which is to say you had the capacity and squandered it at the point of decision," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Enjoy the child," Gelion said.</p>
<p>It came out with the specific quality of someone using the only thing left available to them as a weapon, knowing it will not do what they need it to do. "I hope it’s worth it."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him.</p>
<p>"It will be," he said simply. "But I ain’t paying child support because a slime queen like her... she could handle it herself."</p>
<p>Gelion’s face moved through several things in quick succession and ended at a place that had run out of options.</p>
<p>Mordecai, who had been watching this exchange with the expression of someone observing something they will need to think about for a long time afterward, said, quietly, "Is the child actually—"</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, without looking at him.</p>
<p>Mordecai closed his mouth.</p>
<p>The room was quiet for a moment. The specific quiet of a space that has absorbed something significant and is waiting for the next thing.</p>
<p>"Listen closely," Rex said, and his voice shifted into the register he used when the preliminary work was finished and the actual conversation was beginning.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer needs a restructuring," Rex said, which was the statement he had been building to, and everyone in the room felt the weight of it arriving.</p>
<p>"A comprehensive one," he said. "Not a targeted adjustment, not a specific personnel change. A full operational review with the authority to remove anything that is functioning as a vulnerability rather than as an asset."</p>
<p>He looked at Mordecai.</p>
<p>"And Mordecai," Rex said. "Before you tell me this is excessive or that the council won’t accept it or that there are political considerations I’m not accounting for, I want you to look at what is currently suspended thirty centimeters off the floor in the center of your secured council chamber and tell me that your current system of oversight is working."</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at Gelion.</p>
<p>Gelion looked at Mordecai with the specific expression of a man who had been useful to everyone in the room at some point and had arrived at the moment when that usefulness was over.</p>
<p>Mordecai looked back at Rex.</p>
<p>"What do you need from me?" he said.</p>
<p>"Your full authorization," Rex said. "In front of the city."</p>
<p>"You want me to announce it publicly," Mordecai said.</p>
<p>"I want the Underlayer to know that the authority for what happens next comes from you," Rex said. "Everything I do in the next several days carries your endorsement."</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at him for a long moment. "And if I object to something you intend to do?"</p>
<p>"Tell me now," Rex said. "Before we announce it."</p>
<p>Mordecai was quiet. He was doing the specific calculation that he always did when the available options were all ones he did not entirely prefer.</p>
<p>Rex had seen Mordecai perform this calculation several times; it always looked the same and consistently led to the same conclusion: among the available imperfect options, the one Rex was presenting was the least bad.</p>
<p>"The reincarnators," Mordecai said carefully. "The ones who are here and are not Legion contacts."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"What happens to them?"</p>
<p>"The ones who are not Legion contacts," Rex said, "will have an opportunity to demonstrate it."</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at him. "That’s not... a specific answer."</p>
<p>"It’s the answer I have right now," Rex said. "If you want specifics, they depend on what the review finds."</p>
<p>"What I can tell you is that the people in this city who chose to be here and have not been selling information about their neighbors to an organization that wants them dead are not the target of this restructuring."</p>
<p>"And the ones who have," Mordecai said.</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at Gelion again.</p>
<p>"How many?" he said.</p>
<p>"Eleven," Rex said. "Including Gelion."</p>
<p>"The others are still in the population and are currently unaware that the network they were operating inside has been identified."</p>
<p>"Eleven," Mordecai repeated, and the number sat in the room with the specific weight of something that was both more and less than expected. More because eleven meant the problem was distributed throughout the city. Less because eleven out of two hundred thousand was a number that could be addressed.</p>
<p>"Eleven is manageable," Mordecai said, and he was thinking out loud rather than asking for confirmation.</p>
<p>"Eleven is what I’ve found," Rex said. "The restructuring is partly to confirm there are no others."</p>
<p>Mordecai absorbed this. "You don’t know for certain."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said. "Which is why the review has to be comprehensive."</p>
<p>"A targeted response to eleven known contacts tells the ones we haven’t found that we’re working from incomplete information."</p>
<p>"A full restructuring tells everyone in the city that the baseline has changed and the old assumptions no longer apply."</p>
<p>Cassandra, who had been standing near the door with her arms crossed, said, "He’s asking you to turn the Underlayer upside down because of eleven people."</p>
<p>"He’s asking me to turn the Underlayer upside down because of what those eleven people represent," Mordecai said, and it was the first time in the conversation that he had answered one of Cassandra’s objections himself rather than looking to Rex to do it.</p>
<p>He appeared to be somewhat surprised by his own response.</p>
<p>Cassandra glanced at Mordecai before shifting her attention to Rex.</p>
<p>"The transition period," she said. "While the review is running, the monitoring network will be in a state of partial uncertainty."</p>
<p>"Any external threat that moves on the Underlayer during that window finds us less coordinated than usual."</p>
<p>"That’s a real concern," Rex said.</p>
