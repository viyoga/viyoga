<p>"That pressure will manifest as restrictions and limitations on what he can do, how much he can influence, and how openly he can exert his will for a significant period of time."</p>
<p>She paused, watching the fury boil in his eyes. "It is not punishment in the way you are picturing it, Rex."</p>
<p>"It is not a lashing or a fall from grace. It is politics."</p>
<p>"Politics," Rex repeated.</p>
<p>The word came out of him with a flat, hollow resonance, dripping with a contempt so thick it was almost palpable. He let out a short, bitter bark of a laugh that sounded more like a snarl.</p>
<p>"He tried to erase my children before they even had a chance to draw breath."</p>
<p>"He tried to unmake my very soul and my entire legacy... and the consequence is politics?"</p>
<p>"He gets a reprimand in a divine boardroom while we’re left to bleed in the dirt?"</p>
<p>"The consequence from his peers is politics," Lustia said, her voice sharpening, her eyes locking onto his with a sudden, piercing intensity that made the air between them hum. "But do not mistake the cosmic scale for the final scale, Rex."</p>
<p>"I did not say that was the only consequence available."</p>
<p>Rex fell into a silence that was heavy, suffocating, and thick with the scent of impending storm. For a long time, he didn’t move; he simply stood there, a predator frozen in the moment before the lunge.</p>
<p>The anger he had been nursing, that jagged, directionless fury that had been tearing at his insides since Lustia first spoke the name of the God of Time, began to undergo a terrifying metamorphosis.</p>
<p>The chaotic, screaming rage of a victim transformed into something else. It shed its heat, losing its frantic, burning energy, and distilled itself into something far more dangerous: a cold, surgical, and absolute focus.</p>
<p>It was the anger of a man who had stopped asking ’Why?’ and had begun asking ’Where?’ He wasn’t just a man who had been harmed anymore; he was a man who had identified a target, and he was already beginning to map out the most efficient way to destroy it.</p>
<p>His eyes, sharp and predatory, locked onto Lustia.</p>
<p>"The Balance Keeper designation," Rex said, his voice unnervingly calm, like the stillness before a landslide. "Celestina’s connection to the God of Time..."</p>
<p>"The contract they formed..."</p>
<p>"Connect the dots for me, Lustia... Don’t leave anything out."</p>
<p>"Yes," Lustia replied, sensing the shift in the air.</p>
<p>"The Balance Keeper’s god and the God of Time," Rex continued, his mind working with the ruthless efficiency of a machine, "they aren’t just allies."</p>
<p>"They are the same god... aren’t they?"</p>
<p>Lustia did not answer immediately. The silence stretched, taut and vibrating with tension, until it became an answer in itself.</p>
<p>"They are allied," Lustia finally said, choosing her words with divine precision. "The specific divine designations are not identical, but the god who granted Celestina the Balance Keeper authority and the God of Time are operating from the same strategic position."</p>
<p>"They share a singular, terrifying objective: the preservation of what they call the world’s fundamental equilibrium."</p>
<p>"And reincarnators," Rex said, a dark, knowing smirk ghosting his lips, "are what they define as the ’disruption’ to that equilibrium."</p>
<p>"We are the grit in the gears..."</p>
<p>"We are the anomaly that needs to be sanded down."</p>
<p>"Correct," Lustia said.</p>
<p>Rex lifted his wrist, staring down at the Mark. It looked innocent enough, a mere brand of divinity, but to him, it felt like a bullseye painted in blood.</p>
<p>"He didn’t just cast a wide net over Aethelgard," Rex said, his voice dropping to a low, guttural growl. "He targeted me. Specifically."</p>
<p>"The anchor point you described, the one that the pregnancy disrupted, wasn’t a general temporal working."</p>
<p>"That was a precision strike... It was a working that had been meticulously tuned to my specific divine anchor."</p>
<p>"Yes," Lustia said, her voice uncharacteristically quiet.</p>
<p>"How?" Rex demanded, the calm in his voice cracking just enough to reveal the violence beneath. "How does a god tune a cosmic working to a single, specific anchor?"</p>
<p>"Walk me through the mechanics, Lustia."</p>
<p>"Don’t give me the poetic, ’divine will’ bullshit."</p>
<p>"Give me the mechanics."</p>
<p>"How does a god find a single, microscopic thread inside an entire island and pull it out for special attention?"</p>
<p>Lustia stood tall, meeting his intensity. "He would have needed a sample, Rex."</p>
<p>"A piece of your essence, or something that had been in prolonged, intimate contact with your divine signature before you learned to use Presence Manipulation to mask it."</p>
<p>"The Earthen Authority’s geological anchor... it was broadcasting."</p>
<p>"It was screaming your signature at a scale that a temporal god with sufficient attention could have read, intercepted, and copied without ever needing to stand in the same room as you."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes darkened. The realization hit him like a physical weight, a cold dread settling in his marrow.</p>
<p>"So that fucking pussy has been watching me because of that power..." Rex gritted his teeth. "He has been studying me since the moment I integrated the gauntlets."</p>
<p>"Since some point after that," Lustia clarified. "The exact date is obscured; the same temporal distortions that made your night so difficult to measure also make his observation window a blur."</p>
<p>"But a working this surgically, this devastatingly specific, does not happen from a single glance."</p>
<p>"He wasn’t just watching you, Rex... He was dissecting you."</p>
<p>"He was reading the rhythm of your soul for a long time before he ever began building the anchor into the reset architecture."</p>
<p>"He was trying to erase me," Rex said. It wasn’t a question; it was a grim, undeniable fact.</p>
<p>"He was trying to erase everything you had built," she corrected. "The structures, the alliances, the power... the entire footprint of your existence."</p>
<p>"It is not quite the same thing as erasing you, but in operational terms, the difference is marginal."</p>
<p>"Marginal?" Rex snapped, the word exploding from him.</p>
<p>He stepped into her space, his presence overwhelming, his eyes burning with a terrifying, singular purpose. "Do not tell me the difference is marginal!"</p>
<p>"There is a version of tonight where you are standing here describing this to a version of me that doesn’t even exist!"</p>
<p>"A version of me that was unmade, wiped clean, replaced by a ghost in a world of strangers!"</p>
<p>"The reset took the part of me that would have been standing here to hear these words!"</p>
<p>"To you, it’s a distinction in a manual, but to me..."</p>
<p>He leaned in closer, his voice dropping to a whisper that carried the weight of a death sentence.</p>
<p>"To me, the difference is total."</p>
<p>Lustia met his gaze, and for a moment, the air in the domain seemed to solidify, becoming heavy and pressurized, as if the very atmosphere were reacting to the sheer gravity of Rex’s conviction. She didn’t flinch at his outburst.</p>
<p>She didn’t offer the hollow comfort of a goddess trying to soothe a mortal’s wounded pride. She simply let the weight of his truth settle between them.</p>
<p>"It is total," she said, her voice resonant and absolute, cutting through the lingering tension like a clarion call. "You are right."</p>
<p>"I chose the wrong word... To lose the self is to lose the universe."</p>
<p>"There is no margin for error when the error is your very existence."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t respond immediately. He turned his head, his eyes sweeping over the vast, ethereal expanse of the domain.</p>
<p>He looked at the warm, amber-hued light that bathed the space, the infinite, unstated reaches of the void that belonged to Lustia and, by the strength of their bond, to him as well. It was a sanctuary, a place of power and respite, yet all he could see was the shadow of the god who had tried to turn this sanctuary into a tomb.</p>
<p>"I am... going to fucking torture and kill him," Rex said.</p>
<p>The declaration didn’t carry the theatrical swell of a hero’s vow. There was no roar, no dramatic flare of energy, no trembling in his voice.</p>
<p>It was delivered with a terrifying, pragmatic flatness, the way a man might state that he intended to eat or that he intended to sleep. It was a logistical fact.</p>
<p>A settled debt. A line item on a list of inevitable conclusions.</p>
<p>Lustia watched him, her eyes tracing the hard lines of his face, the way his muscles were coiled like a spring under tension, ready to snap.</p>
<p>"Yes," she said, her voice a calm anchor in the storm of his intent. "I know."</p>
<p>Rex turned back to her, his eyes narrowing, his gaze becoming a probe, searching for the one thing he hated most in a partner: hesitation.</p>
<p>"And you aren’t going to tell me it’s impossible," Rex stated, his voice dropping into a low, commanding growl. "You aren’t going to stand there with that divine poise and tell me he is too old, or too established, or too far above the station of a reincarnator with a ’lust’ designation."</p>
<p>"You aren’t going to tell me that a mortal, even one with a mark, shouldn’t be reaching for the throat of time itself."</p>
<p>He stepped closer, his presence filling the space between them, demanding an answer that would dictate his next thousand moves. "I want to know right now, Lustia."</p>
<p>"Before I spend a single goddamn hour planning, before I bleed a single drop of sweat or a liter of blood on this path, tell me whether you are going to be the kind of patron who tells me to be reasonable."</p>
