<p>Morwenna turned her head slowly, her gaze sweeping across the table until it locked onto Valentina.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was deafening. It was a look forged in the fires of a forty-year history, a look that carried the crushing weight of their earlier conversation, the unspoken grievances, and the shared burdens of leadership.</p>
<p>But in this new, poisoned context, the look had transformed. It wasn’t an offering of forgiveness, nor was it a demand for absolution.</p>
<p>It was something far more profound and far more unsettling: it was the look of a woman who had finally seen the architecture of a lie, a reconfiguration of the soul that occurs when a long-held truth is suddenly revealed to be a beautifully crafted deception.</p>
<p>Morwenna was seeing Valentina clearly for the first time, and the clarity was terrifying.</p>
<p>Valentina held the gaze. She did not blink, did not flinch, and did not offer the mercy of a downward glance.</p>
<p>She sat in the center of the storm, an obsidian queen accepting the verdict of her closest confidante.</p>
<p>"The Legion’s structure is compartmentalized," Ignivara said, her voice cutting through the heavy, emotional tension like a scalpel.</p>
<p>She forced the room’s focus back to the tactical reality, knowing that if they dwelled too long on the betrayal, they would drown in it. "It is designed to prevent the very thing we are experiencing now: the spread of truth."</p>
<p>"Each operative is a cell in a larger organism, knowing only their immediate neighbors."</p>
<p>"There are seven operational tiers of isolation between a standard field operative and Celestina herself."</p>
<p>"I operated at tier three... This means my world was limited to my field contacts, my tier two supervisor, and the tier four coordinator who managed our entire region’s operations."</p>
<p>"Kregg was tier four," Rex interjected, his voice a low, grounding rumble.</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara confirmed, her eyes sharpening. "Kregg was the lynchpin.’</p>
<p>"He coordinated the underlayer insertion network and the surface monitoring operations for the territories adjacent to Aethelgard."</p>
<p>"By eliminating him, Rex didn’t just kill a man; he severed the specific neural node that connected those two massive operations."</p>
<p>"He blinded the Legion’s left hand to what its right hand was doing."</p>
<p>"When Rex eliminated him..." the knowledge system holder began. The tone was different now.</p>
<p>Two hours ago, the mention of Rex’s violence would have been a challenge, a probe for weakness. Now, it was the clinical observation of a scholar recording a pivotal event in a grand history.</p>
<p>...he struck the heart of the coordination."</p>
<p>"When Rex eliminated him," Ignivara repeated, her voice hardening, "in the canyon."</p>
<p>"Amidst the blood and the broken stone."</p>
<p>"What is the delta?" the divination representative asked, leaning forward, her eyes searching for the tactical gap. "What does a tier four know that a tier three is denied?"</p>
<p>"Resource routing," Ignivara answered instantly. "The Legion’s lifeblood."</p>
<p>"The funding networks, the communication crystal relay structures, the precise, hardened safe house locations within their operational territories."</p>
<p>"Kregg held the map to the entire machine. He knew the locations of four major safe houses, and I only know two."</p>
<p>"You will provide those locations," Morwenna commanded. It wasn’t a request; it was a decree, the voice of a commander seizing a weapon from the wreckage of a battlefield.</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara said, her posture straightening. "And the communication crystal relay structure for the territories Kregg coordinated."</p>
<p>"Because I spent two years under his direct supervision, I have the relay sequences the ’language’ the Legion uses to speak to itself across the stars."</p>
<p>Morwenna’s eyes narrowed, her mind already calculating the variables of war. "How current is that information? How much of it is already decaying?"</p>
<p>"The relay structure was last updated eight months ago," Ignivara said, her voice grim. "The Legion is meticulous; they cycle their entire communication infrastructure on a fourteen-month basis to prevent interception."</p>
<p>"The current structure has exactly six months of operational validity remaining."</p>
<p>"Six months," Morwenna whispered, the number hanging in the air like a ticking time bomb.</p>
<p>"Six months to strike," Ignivara said, her eyes meeting Morwenna’s with a fierce, desperate intensity. "Six months to use the crystal locations and the relay sequences before the Legion rotates its entire network."</p>
<p>"After that, the intelligence I give you will be nothing more than a graveyard of historical data ghosts of locations that no longer exist."</p>
<p>Morwenna turned her sharp, calculating gaze toward Rex. She was looking for a reaction, a flicker of hesitation, a sign of fatigue, or perhaps a grim confirmation of the blood he had spilled to get them to this moment.</p>
<p>Rex said nothing. His silence was a heavy, ironclad thing. It was the quintessential Rex behavior: he had already processed the six-month deadline, filed it into a mental tactical folder, and moved past the shock of the timeline.</p>
<p>To him, a ticking clock wasn’t a source of anxiety; it was simply a parameter of the mission.</p>
<p>"The origin," Apollo said, breaking the heavy silence.</p>
<p>He leaned forward, his eyes burning with the need for a more profound understanding of the enemy’s soul. "You said Celestina built this because she hates reincarnators."</p>
<p>"That’s a simple emotion for such a complex machine."</p>
<p>"I need to understand the why... What is the specific nature of that hatred?"</p>
<p>Ignivara turned her gaze to him, her eyes reflecting a profound, almost existential dread.</p>
<p>"It isn’t hatred in the way a mortal feels it," she began, her voice dropping into a solemn, reverent register. "And it isn’t petty, or jealous, or vengeful either."</p>
<p>"Celestina Von Starlight is not a reincarnator... she was born of this world, tied to its very soil and sky."</p>
<p>"This means she possesses a native system designation rather than a reincarnator’s foreign architecture."</p>
<p>"Her designation is the Balance Keeper, a divine class mantle bestowed by a god whose entire existence is dedicated to the maintenance of this world’s fundamental, unyielding equilibrium."</p>
<p>She paused, letting the weight of that divinity sink into the room.</p>
<p>"The Balance Keeper’s perception is not a choice; it is a biological and spiritual mandate," Ignivara continued. "Her designation is specifically attuned to anything the god considers a ’destabilizing element. ’ And in the eyes of that god’s framework, reincarnators are the ultimate instability."</p>
<p>"All of you are anomalies. You bring memories, knowledge, and system capabilities from a foreign reality and graft them onto the architecture of this one."</p>
<p>"You are foreign code running on native hardware."</p>
<p>"The god considers us contaminants," the knowledge system holder whispered.</p>
<p>There was a trace of something like existential vertigo in his voice, the realization that to the very universe they inhabited, they were a virus.</p>
<p>"The god’s framework views unrestricted reincarnator presence as a long-term existential risk, a slow-motion collapse of the world’s stability," Ignivara clarified. "Celestina built the Legion because her very soul screams at her every time she encounters a reincarnator operating at a significant level."</p>
<p>"The Balance Keeper’s perceptive function doesn’t just ’see’ you; it shows her the exact, mathematical way your presence is warping the world’s equilibrium, like a crack spreading through a foundation."</p>
<p>"What does that look like to her?" Aurelia asked, her voice tight, as if she were trying to visualize a world being torn apart by its own inhabitants.</p>
<p>"I am not a Balance Keeper," Ignivara said, a small, grim smile touching her lips. "I don’t see the math. But I remember the founding session."</p>
<p>"I was a fresh recruit, brought in by Kregg... She spoke to us with a terrifying, calm conviction."</p>
<p>"She said she sees the world’s architecture the way a master mason sees a cathedral. To her, the world is a perfect, finished structure."</p>
<p>"And reincarnators..." she paused, searching for the right words, "...reincarnators look like load-bearing pillars that have been shoved into a wall after the building was already finished."</p>
<p>"They are placed in the wrong locations, at the wrong angles, changing the stress distribution in ways the structure was never designed to accommodate."</p>
<p>"To her, we aren’t people; we are structural errors waiting to cause a collapse."</p>
<p>"She genuinely believes she is the hero," Elizabeth said, her voice laced with a mixture of pity and awe. "She thinks she’s the architect repairing a flawed design."</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara said, her eyes locking onto the group. "And that is the most vital thing you must understand about Celestina von Starlight."</p>
<p>"She is neither a tyrant seeking power nor a conqueror seeking glory."</p>
<p>"She’s just a woman driven by a divine, infallible perception."</p>
<p>"She does what she does because she is convinced, by the very gods themselves, that her violence is a necessity."</p>
<p>"She isn’t killing people; she is ’correcting’ the world."</p>
<p>Rex, who had been listening with the stillness of a predator, finally spoke. His voice was low, cold, and carried the finality of a tombstone.</p>
<p>"Which makes her," Rex said, his eyes narrowing with a lethal intensity, "significantly more dangerous than someone who is doing it for reasons that can be negotiated with."</p>
<p>"You can bargain with greed or you can fight against malice. But how do you fight someone who believes their cruelty is a divine duty?"</p>
