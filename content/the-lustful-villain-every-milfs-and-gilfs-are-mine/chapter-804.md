<p>Seven meters away, Apollo emerged from the Academy gates, Kaelira trailing close behind him.</p>
<p>Apollo was a mess. He was still vibrating with the aftershocks of the engagement, his energy signature flickering like a dying candle, only partially stabilized by Aisella’s restorative work.</p>
<p>His clothes were shredded, his skin bruised, and his face was a map of exhaustion and dried blood. He looked like a man who had spent twenty minutes trading blows with a geological god and another forty minutes wrestling with the very fabric of space itself.</p>
<p>Objectively, he looked a thousand times more battered than the Avatar.</p>
<p>He limped toward the Avatar, his boots crunching on the debris, and stopped directly in front of him.</p>
<p>Without a word, Apollo swung his arm and hit the Avatar on the shoulder.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a punch, and it wasn’t a strike of aggression. It was the heavy, unrefined impact of a brotherhood forged in fire, the kind of physical shorthand used by men who didn’t need to say "we survived" because the bruise on their ribs said it for them.</p>
<p>It was an acknowledgment of trust, a silent tether between two souls who had walked through hell together.</p>
<p>The Avatar responded instantly, his movement a perfect, precise mirror of Rex’s soul. He returned the impact with the exact same force, the same angle, and a small, knowing smile that flickered across his lips—a silent promise that they were still standing.</p>
<p>"I knew you’d show up," Apollo said, his voice raspy, flavored by the grit of pulverized stone and the metallic tang of his own blood.</p>
<p>He stood there, swaying slightly, the adrenaline finally beginning to ebb, leaving behind a hollow, aching exhaustion that seemed to settle deep in his marrow.</p>
<p>"You were managing fine," the Avatar replied.</p>
<p>It was a quintessential Rex response, a compliment delivered with such indirectness that it almost felt like a dismissal, implying that while Apollo had been fighting for his life, his survival was a foregone conclusion that didn’t require much praise.</p>
<p>"Managing fine?" Apollo let out a short, jagged laugh that ended in a wince as his bruised ribs protested. "I wasn’t ’managing,’ Rex. I was losing..."</p>
<p>"I was being dismantled by a void-working entity while Aisella was essentially stitching my soul back together with nothing but sheer, professional stubbornness and a prayer."</p>
<p>"My reserves were screaming." He leveled a hard, searching gaze at the Avatar. "Where the hell were you?"</p>
<p>"Dealing with the Tremor situation," the Avatar said, his voice steady, a stark contrast to Apollo’s ragged breathing. "Which is a conversation far too long and heavy for the chaos of this plaza."</p>
<p>Apollo’s eyes drifted. He looked at the screaming, jubilant crowd, then down at the jagged, blood-stained fissures in the earth, and finally toward the north, where the dragon lay like a mountain of dead meat, its massive carcass leaking ichor into the ruins.</p>
<p>The scale of the devastation was staggering; the air still felt heavy with the scent of scorched earth and the visceral, iron smell of a god’s death.</p>
<p>"Yes," Apollo said, his voice dropping an octave. "It is."</p>
<p>He turned back to the Avatar, his expression shifting into that guarded, internal mask he wore when a thought was too heavy to let slip into the open air. He was holding something back, a grief so sharp it threatened to break through his stoic veneer.</p>
<p>"Eryndra and Calivara," he said.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question. It was a heavy, solemn placement of names, a way of anchoring the conversation to the reality of the fallen before the moment passed and the names were lost in the noise of the crowd.</p>
<p>The Avatar fell silent. It was the specific, heavy silence. Rex used when he was absorbing news that didn’t need a rebuttal, only a profound, internal acknowledgment of the weight it carried.</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said, the word a soft, somber confirmation.</p>
<p>"They were good," Apollo said.</p>
<p>His voice was flat, stripped of all ornament, the way a man’s voice sounds when he is stating a fundamental truth that the universe has no right to challenge. "Calivara... her diagnostic work in the middle of that hellscape... it was inhuman."</p>
<p>"I’ve only seen that kind of precision in theoretical designation catalogs..."</p>
<p>"She was mapping the armor’s vulnerability architecture in real time, under full output, while the world was literally tearing itself apart around her."</p>
<p>"I know," the Avatar said, his gaze momentarily drifting to the horizon, as if seeing the ghosts of their brilliance.</p>
<p>"I want it noted," Apollo pressed, his eyes flashing with a sudden, fierce intensity. "Not just buried in a casualty report."</p>
<p>"Not just a line in a ledger of the dead... I want it in the designation advancement records."</p>
<p>"Whatever they were aiming for, whatever mountain they were climbing... I want the Academy to know they didn’t just reach it."</p>
<p>"They conquered it."</p>
<p>"I will speak with Valentina tonight," the Avatar promised, the commitment absolute.</p>
<p>Apollo searched the Avatar’s eyes for a moment, finding the steady, reliable presence he needed.</p>
<p>"Thank you," he said, the words a quiet exhale of relief.</p>
<p>The Avatar looked at him, the connection between them solidifying in the wreckage.</p>
<p>"Yes," it said.</p>
<p>From the shadows of the Academy gate, Kaelira stepped forward. She had remained two meters behind Apollo, a silent sentinel throughout his emergence.</p>
<p>She wasn’t bleeding, and she hadn’t faced the bone-crushing pressure of the dragon’s wrath, but she bore the specific, hollowed-out exhaustion of a support operative. She had been the tether, the one managing the logistics of survival while the warriors danced with death, and the mental toll of maintaining that stability was etched in the weary set of her shoulders.</p>
<p>She approached the Avatar, her gaze direct, piercing, and devoid of any hesitation. She was a woman who measured her words as carefully as her strikes, and she looked as though she intended to say exactly what needed to be said and not a syllable more.</p>
<p>"The healing work you requested from Aisella at the start of the engagement," she began, her voice cutting through the distant roar of the crowd like a blade.</p>
<p>The Avatar turned his gaze toward Kaelira, his eyes steady and unblinking, absorbing her presence with the unnerving stillness of a predator or a monument.</p>
<p>"When you told Apollo that Aisella needed to restore him," Kaelira began, her voice low but sharp, cutting through the distant, chaotic roar of the celebrating masses, "you also gave her a secondary directive."</p>
<p>"You told her to address the broader plaza second, in whatever order her reserve allowed."</p>
<p>"The phrasing wasn’t just a suggestion; it was a tactical command wrapped in a polite suggestion. It was deliberate."</p>
<p>She stepped closer, her eyes searching the Avatar’s face for a crack in the mask. "You were telling her to prioritize her own reserve management over the sheer number of people she addressed."</p>
<p>"You were protecting the asset."</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar replied.</p>
<p>The word was a simple, unadorned fact.</p>
<p>"You were telling her," Kaelira pressed, her tone gaining a sharp, analytical edge, "that her continued capacity was more vital than the immediate relief of the crowd."</p>
<p>"You knew that a depleted healer is a net loss to any extended engagement."</p>
<p>"You weren’t just managing a crisis; you were calculating the possibility of a second one."</p>
<p>"You were betting that this engagement wasn’t actually finished."</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said again.</p>
<p>The repetition was not a lack of vocabulary but a refusal to offer more than the truth required.</p>
<p>"And you delivered that directive," Kaelira continued, her gaze intensifying, "while simultaneously informing Apollo that the Tremor situation required immediate attention."</p>
<p>"You played both sides of the coin..."</p>
<p>"You implied the engagement was concluded for the sake of the soldier but remained active for the sake of the healer."</p>
<p>’You gave them two different realities based on what each one needed to survive."</p>
<p>The Avatar did not flinch. He held her gaze, the silence between them stretching, thick with the tension of a woman trying to peel back the skin of a god to see the clockwork beneath.</p>
<p>"The specific phrase was ’in whatever order your reserve allows,’" the Avatar stated, his voice cool and precise. "The phrase was mathematically accurate."</p>
<p>"The order Aisella chose to execute was hers to determine. The variables were hers to manage."</p>
<p>Kaelira stared at him, her expression shifting into that particular, weary look of someone who had received an answer that was technically, undeniably complete but was currently deciding if ’technically complete’ was actually the register she had been looking for. She wasn’t looking for a logic puzzle; she was searching for a man.</p>
<p>"You are cautious with words," she said, her voice tinged with a hint of skepticism.</p>
<p>"I am careful with words," the Avatar corrected.</p>
<p>He said it with that unmistakable, razor-sharp quality Rex used when he was drawing a line in the sand, a distinction that was real, unarguable, and intended to stand entirely on its own.</p>
<p>Kaelira searched his eyes for one more second, searching for the man behind the precision, but found only the perfect reflection of a hero.</p>
<p>"Yes," she finally conceded, a small, almost imperceptible nod. "You are... I’ve noticed that since the dungeon."</p>
