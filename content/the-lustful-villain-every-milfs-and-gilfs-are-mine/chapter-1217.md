<p>Morwenna staggered back into the world of the living, her consciousness returning in nauseating waves of pain and disorientation.</p>
<p>She had been unconscious for what felt like an eternity, her last memory a kaleidoscopic blur of magical feedback and the bone-jarring impact of a spell that had sent her spiraling into darkness.</p>
<p>Now, she was being half carried upright by two Nightwing elite soldiers, their armored arms providing the only thing keeping her from collapsing into the gore-slicked stone.</p>
<p>Gasp. Pant. Wheeze.</p>
<p>Her breathing was shallow, each inhale sending a spike of agony through her lungs. Her eyes, bloodshot and unfocused, surveyed the carnage.</p>
<p>She saw the twisted metal, the severed limbs, and the smoking craters where divine power had struck, but she saw something else too.</p>
<p>Beneath the surface-level horror that mirrored every other face in the corridor, there was something darker festering in her expression. A private, corrosive knowledge.</p>
<p>’How could this be happening?’ she thought, her mind spinning in a vicious circle. ’Just days ago... we were planning for a future.’</p>
<p>’We were laughing. We were certain.’</p>
<p>The irony was a physical weight, a cruel joke played by fate that she could share with no one. She carried that secret like a second wound, a deeper laceration that no healing salve could reach, layered directly over the purple bruises already blooming along her ribs.</p>
<p>She remained silent, knowing that to speak would be to shatter the fragile illusion of dignity remaining in the Aethelgard ranks. Her silence was her armor, but it was armor that felt increasingly like a coffin.</p>
<p>Through the haze of grief and Morwenna’s silent torment, Valentina forced herself to move. Her own body was a map of trauma; her muscles screamed from overuse, her joints felt as though they had been filled with broken glass, and every step was a battle against the vertigo threatening to claim her.</p>
<p>But she could not afford to fall. Not now.</p>
<p>Not when the last vestiges of their identity were crumbling.</p>
<p>She forced herself to stand straight, squaring her shoulders despite the visible tremor running through her frame. She turned to face the wreckage of her army, her gaze sweeping across the broken, the weeping, and the hollow-eyed.</p>
<p>"Listen to me!" Valentina commanded.</p>
<p>Her voice cracked on the first syllable, but she pushed through it, forcing it to carry with enough authority to slice through the ambient noise of sobbing and panicked whispers. "Listen to me!"</p>
<p>"I know this feels like the end of everything," she said, her words rough with emotion but gaining strength as she spoke. "I know we just lost more than any of us can reasonably bear in a single day."</p>
<p>"We have watched our gods fall. We have watched our certainty die."</p>
<p>A few soldiers slowly lifted their heads. Their faces were masks of dried blood and salt-streaked tears, but their eyes, once empty, began to focus.</p>
<p>A flicker of something tentative, something dangerously fragile, began to stir in the space where hope had been brutally extinguished only minutes before.</p>
<p>Even Elliot, who had been lost in the abyss of his own grief, pulled his face away from Evelyn’s shoulder. His eyes were red-rimmed and swollen, but he looked toward Valentina.</p>
<p>For the first time since Apollo’s death, there was a spark of something in his expression that wasn’t pure devastation. It was a desperate, starving need for a reason to keep breathing.</p>
<p>Valentina took a breath, feeling the burn in her chest, and pressed on. Each word felt like she was dragging it out of her own depleted soul.</p>
<p>"This surrender is not the end," she continued, her voice growing steadier even as exhaustion dragged at every syllable like an anchor. "It is not a death sentence."</p>
<p>"It is a chance to survive... It is a chance to retreat, to regroup, to find another way forward when the smoke clears."</p>
<p>She paused, meeting the gaze of a young soldier who was staring at her with terrifying expectation.</p>
<p>"I don’t know what comes next," she admitted, her voice dropping to a lower, more intimate register that commanded attention through sincerity rather than volume. "I don’t know what the darkness holds."</p>
<p>"But I promise every one of you standing here I will not let what happened today become meaningless."</p>
<p>"We will survive this. We will remember. And we will return."</p>
<p>Valentina let her gaze drift slowly across the fractured ranks. She didn’t address them as a faceless legion or an abstract concept of ’the army’; she hunted for individual eyes.</p>
<p>She sought the cracked visors, the blood-streaked cheeks, and the hollow stares of the broken. This was a tactic she had honed over decades in smaller skirmishes, but she had never once had to deploy it at a scale this apocalyptic.</p>
<p>"I have led Aethelgard’s defense for longer than most of you have been alive," she said, her voice cutting through the oppressive atmosphere like a blade through fog. "I have buried students."</p>
<p>"I have made decisions that haunt my dreams every single night."</p>
<p>"I have tasted the copper of blood and the salt of grief more times than I can count."</p>
<p>She paused, letting the weight of her own history settle over them. "I am not going to stand here and lie to you."</p>
<p>"I am not going to tell you this doesn’t feel like the end of everything... Because it does."</p>
<p>"Pretending otherwise would be a betrayal and an insult to every soul we lost today, to every body rotting in the rubble of this corridor."</p>
<p>A low murmur of agreement rippled through the soldiers, a sound born of shared trauma.</p>
<p>"But listen to me," she pressed, her voice gaining a desperate, iron-willed edge. "Feeling like an ending and being one are not the same thing."</p>
<p>"There is a chasm between the two, and I need every single one of you to hold onto that distinction."</p>
<p>"Hold onto it with everything you have left until I can give you a better answer than the one I have right now."</p>
<p>Her words were not a cure; they were not a magical balm that would erase the sight of Apollo’s corpse or the memory of bones snapping under demonic might.</p>
<p>But they carried a gravity that demanded acknowledgment. They planted a microscopic seed of belief in the frozen soil of their despair.</p>
<p>Elliot, still clinging to Evelyn, gave a weak, almost imperceptible nod. Tears continued to stream down his face, but his spine straightened by a fraction of an inch—a tiny rebellion against the gravity of his grief.</p>
<p>Evelyn looked up at Valentina, her own body still wracked with tremors of fear and exhaustion, but her eyes held a spark of something that wasn’t quite hope but was definitely trust.</p>
<p>Around them, the movement began. Slowly, painfully, soldiers began to push themselves up from the blood-slicked stone.</p>
<p>Grrr... shhh...</p>
<p>The sound of armor scraping against debris filled the air. They pulled each other upright with quiet, wordless support, their hands gripping shoulders as broken bodies leaned on functional ones. It was a grim, silent choreography of survival.</p>
<p>The corridor now existed as two parallel universes, divided by an invisible, impenetrable line of fate.</p>
<p>On one side, the Underlayer was a roaring furnace of euphoria. The air was thick with the smell of smoke, sweat, and victory.</p>
<p>Xerollion stood at the epicenter of the storm, the sun around which their new world orbited. Every demon who passed him offered a salute, a roar of approval, or a reverent nod.</p>
<p>He was no longer just a commander; he was a myth made flesh, the architect of their transcendence. Their laughter echoed off the ceiling like thunder, a triumphant symphony of those who had claimed their destiny.</p>
<p>On the other side, Aethelgard was a tomb of weeping shadows. The air here was cold, heavy with the metallic tang of blood and the suffocating stench of defeat.</p>
<p>The ranks trembled, broken and grieving, their bodies still twitching from the shock of the slaughter. Yet, through the tears and the terror, they attempted to stand.</p>
<p>Behind Valentina, they clung to the last fragile thread of their existence, the desperate, bleeding hope that while they had been broken today, they had not been destroyed.</p>
<p>The war had not ended; it had simply changed shape.</p>
