<p>The third strike was not merely a blow; it was a release of pent-up years of disappointment, a thunderclap of physical condemnation. The impact sounded like wet leather slapping stone, sending a spray of blood from Theo’s split lip across the wall. His legs buckled, but Aurelia was relentless.</p>
<p>She didn’t let him fall; she dragged him. Her fingers locked into the collar of his shirt like iron bands, her knuckles white and bloodless as she hauled his stumbling, broken form backward into the confines of his room.</p>
<p>The doorframe scraped against his shoulder with a sickening grinding noise as she shoved him inside. With a final, guttural snarl of disgust, she threw him.</p>
<p>Theo crashed onto the hardwood floor beside his bed with a bone-jarring thud that seemed to vibrate through the very foundations of the house. He lay there, curled on his side, gasping for air that tasted of copper and bile.</p>
<p>Aurelia stood over him, silhouetted against the dim light of the corridor, her chest heaving in violent, arrhythmic spasms. Her shadow loomed over him like a vengeful deity.</p>
<p>"I loved you enough to forgive the street," she began, her voice dropping to a low, terrifying register that made the hair on Theo’s neck stand on end. "I loved you enough to forgive the blood you spilled in those alleys, the chaos you sowed."</p>
<p>"I loved you enough to forgive the blade you turned on innocent people... God help me, I still do not know if that forgiveness was earned, or if I simply gave it because the alternative—the alternative of admitting my own son was a monster—was more than I could bear to face."</p>
<p>She took a step closer, the hem of her gown whispering against the floor like a serpent in the grass. "I do not know how much more of you I have left to forgive, Theo."</p>
<p>"I have given until there is nothing left in my own heart but scars. And I am beginning to be terrified that the answer is none..."</p>
<p>"That there is nothing left inside that hollow shell of yours worth the effort of saving... That you have finally become the void you always feared."</p>
<p>"Mother..." Theo croaked from the floor. The fire had gone out of him.</p>
<p>The manic conviction that had fueled his screaming was gone, replaced by a raw, childlike vulnerability that made him look smaller than he ever had. Blood leaked from the corner of his mouth, dripping steadily onto the floorboards.</p>
<p>"Mother, please... you have to... you have to believe me... Please."</p>
<p>Aurelia looked down at him, and for a moment, the mask of fury shattered. Beneath the rage, something cracked a fissure of grief so profound, so absolute, that it seemed to darken the very air between them.</p>
<p>It was the look of a woman watching something precious burn to ash in real time, knowing she was the one holding the torch.</p>
<p>"It is too late," she said, and the words were colder than any blade he had ever wielded. "Do you understand that, Theo? "</p>
<p>"Truly understand?"</p>
<p>Her voice trembled with the weight of her final judgment. "It has been too late since the moment you raised that blade over their bed."</p>
<p>’Since the moment you chose violence over truth... You speak of conspiracies, of visions, of Rex Rexilion being some ancient demon."</p>
<p>"But you are wrong."</p>
<p>She leaned down, her face inches from his pain-streaked features, her eyes twin pools of frozen sorrow. "Rex has already won, Theo. "</p>
<p>"Not because of some grand scheme. Not because of some mystical prophecy that only your broken mind could perceive."</p>
<p>"He won because you handed him the crown... He won the moment you decided that your wounded pride was worth more than this family’s trust."</p>
<p>"You handed him victory the night you decided a sword was the answer to your own inadequacy."</p>
<p>She straightened up, pulling away from him as if his very presence contaminated her. "There is nothing left to fight for here."</p>
<p>"There is no enemy to vanquish, no monster to slay..."</p>
<p>"There is only the wreckage that remains to be managed. And I am the one who has to clean up the blood, because apparently no one else in this family is capable of stopping before the damage becomes permanent."</p>
<p>Aurelia retreated from his broken form, each step backward feeling like she was withdrawing from a battlefield where the enemy was her own blood.</p>
<p>She stopped at the threshold of the doorway, the shadow of the frame cutting across her face like a scar. The fire in her eyes had dimmed, leaving behind something far more devastating: the hollow, ashen glow of total exhaustion.</p>
<p>"This is my fault," she said.</p>
<p>The admission was quiet, almost fragile, but it carried more destructive power than all her screaming. It was the sound of a dam finally giving way.</p>
<p>"I should never have struck you that first time... Not in front of them. Not in front of the household."</p>
<p>She closed her eyes for a heartbeat, a shudder wracking her shoulders. "I told myself it was justice..."</p>
<p>"I told myself you deserved every blow for what you nearly did for the blood you almost spilled in that room."</p>
<p>"And perhaps you did deserve it. Perhaps every bruise you carry tonight is a debt paid." Her voice cracked, raw and bleeding. "But striking you did not fix whatever was already shattered inside you, Theo."</p>
<p>"It didn’t mend the cracks... All it did was confirm your darkest fears."</p>
<p>"It taught you that violence is the only language this family speaks when words fail..."</p>
<p>"I taught you that lesson, and you learned it with terrifying precision."</p>
<p>A single tear escaped her closed lids, tracking through the pale dust of her makeup. "Everything since then has simply been the two of us proving that lesson correct, over and over again, until there is nothing left between us but bruises, broken bones, and accusations that neither of us can ever take back."</p>
<p>"Then let me explain!" Theo screamed, the sound tearing from his lungs as he fought to push himself upright.</p>
<p>His muscles spasmed against the pain; his fingers scraped uselessly against the hardwood as he tried to haul his battered body off the floor. Blood smeared across the planks as he dragged himself up, his breathing a wet, desperate rattle.</p>
<p>"Mother, please! Just listen to the truth for just one minute of—"</p>
<p>"No."</p>
<p>The word was not a shout. It was a guillotine blade. It cut through his plea, severing his hope mid-sentence.</p>
<p>Aurelia reached for the handle and pulled the door shut. The click of the latch sounded like a gunshot in the sudden silence of the room.</p>
<p>Outside in the corridor, the household staff servants and guards who had gathered like carrion birds at the sound of the violence stood frozen. They watched with wide, terrified eyes as Aurelia turned the key in the lock from the outside, sealing her son in his own private hell.</p>
<p>Her hands were shaking so violently that the brass key rattled against the lock mechanism.</p>
<p>The fury was gone now. The rage had burned itself out, leaving behind nothing but the cold, dead embers of a mother who had lost everything.</p>
<p>She stood there for three seconds, a statue of grief in the dim hallway, before she turned her back on the door. She did not look at the staff. She did not acknowledge the whispered conversations or the frightened glances of those who witnessed her humiliation.</p>
<p>She simply walked away, her footsteps echoing down the hall like the steady, inevitable march of a funeral procession.</p>
