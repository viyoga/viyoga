<p>The courtyard had become a theater of the impossible. Around the perimeter, the sky continued to vomit monsters, a chaotic symphony of shrieks, crashes, and elemental roars, but at the center, there was a pocket of terrifying, absolute stillness.</p>
<p>Rex was no longer just a fighter; he was an architect of reality, fighting a war on two fronts: the visible chaos of the monsters and the invisible war against the Nothing.</p>
<p>His body was a temple of strain. Every muscle was locked in a permanent state of high-tension vibration.</p>
<p>His skin was flushed, his eyes bloodshot from the sheer mental load of maintaining the Earthen Apostle’s delicate, surgical extraction while simultaneously slamming geological barriers into the path of a dozen SSR-class entities.</p>
<p>It was a cognitive load that would have caused a normal man’s brain to hemorrhage, but Rex lived for the challenge. He thrived on the razor’s edge between total control and total collapse.</p>
<p>"Don’t you dare close your eyes, Mordecai!" Rex barked, his voice a jagged whip of command.</p>
<p>He didn’t look back, but he could feel the man’s presence, a fading warmth in the center of the cold. "If you pass out now, you’re missing the best part!"</p>
<p>"You’re finally seeing what the world actually looks like when the lights go out!"</p>
<p>Mordecai let out a sound that was half sob, half laugh. "It’s... it’s too much..."</p>
<p>"The scale... it’s not just a fight... It feels like the universe is trying to erase itself!"</p>
<p>"Then be the eraser!" Rex roared, his hands glowing with a violent, novel frequency that hummed with a sound no ear was meant to hear. "Don’t let it erase you!"</p>
<p>"Look at me! Look at what we’re doing!"</p>
<p>Rex felt the entity react. It was a subtle shift in the ’nothingness,’ a slight tightening of the void, but it was there.</p>
<p>By feeding it the novel frequencies, he had given the void a sense of direction. He had given the absence a target.</p>
<p>He was the most captivating thing in a universe of static.</p>
<p>’There,’ Rex thought, his mind a cold, calculating machine even as his heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. ’The interface... It’s not heat... It’s not energy... It’s the tension between existence and nonexistence.’</p>
<p>’It’s the... why... of its shape.’</p>
<p>He pushed the earthen apostle to its absolute limit. He wasn’t just pulling energy; he was performing a cosmic heist, stealing the very concept of ’being’ from the entity to fuel his extraction.</p>
<p>The strain was immense. It felt like trying to pull a thread of silk from a mountain of lead.</p>
<p>Forty minutes... forty minutes of hell.</p>
<p>And it’s also forty minutes of managing the screaming, biting, crashing reality of the SR and SSR classes while his primary focus was a microscopic, delicate tug of war with a god-sized hole in the world.</p>
<p>He felt the entity’s coherence begin to fray. It was like watching a shadow lose its edges, the sharp silhouette of the void beginning to blur and bleed into the ambient air.</p>
<p>"It’s working..." Mordecai whispered, his voice gaining a sudden, desperate strength as he watched the impossible happen. "Lustful Villain... you’re actually... you’re breaking it!"</p>
<p>Rex let out a sharp, triumphant bark of laughter, though it sounded more like a snarl of pure, unadulterated ego. "Breaking it? Mordecai, I’m rewriting the damn rules!"</p>
<p>"This thing thinks it’s the end of everything, but it hasn’t met me yet!"</p>
<p>He felt a surge of pure, intoxicating arrogance. This was why he was Rex.</p>
<p>This wasn’t just survival; this was the ultimate flex. He was standing in the middle of a dimensional apocalypse, sweating, bleeding, and exhausted, and he was winning.</p>
<p>He was playing a game with a being that shouldn’t exist, and he was finding it engrossing.</p>
<p>"Watch closely, you bum!" Rex shouted, his eyes burning with a terrifying, golden light as he pushed the extraction to its absolute zenith. "Watch how a real man handles the void!"</p>
<p>The entity shuddered. The ’nothingness’ rippled, a wave of existential nausea washing over the courtyard, but Rex held his ground.</p>
<p>He was the anchor, the unwavering constant in a chaotic storm. And this was only the beginning.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The silence that followed the disappearance of the Absence was not a peaceful one; it was the ringing, hollow silence of a vacuum. The entity hadn’t died; it had simply ceased to be a thing.</p>
<p>It had dissolved into the background of the universe, leaving behind a void that felt even more terrifying than the entity itself.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t allow Mordecai a second to mourn the existential dread. He didn’t even give him a second to breathe.</p>
<p>"Lustful Villain... stop! Just stop!" Mordecai’s voice was a desperate, ragged scream.</p>
<p>He was clutching his chest, his face pale as death, his eyes darting wildly between the rifts and the man who seemed to be treating an apocalypse like a playground. "The SSS class... they’re coming!"</p>
<p>"We have to regroup, we have to find a way to stabilize the rifts, we have to..."</p>
<p>"We have to what, Mordecai?" Rex interrupted, his voice smooth, almost bored, although his muscles were screaming and his energy was a flickering ember.</p>
<p>He didn’t even turn around. He was too busy watching the sky as the second SSS class began to tear its way into reality.</p>
<p>"Find a way to hide? Find a way to wait for the end? Look at you, shaking like a leaf because the world is finally showing its teeth!"</p>
<p>"You’re still thinking like a survivor. Start thinking like a conqueror."</p>
<p>"A conqueror?!" Mordecai practically choked on the word. "There is a mountain-sized god of stone and flesh coming out of the sky!"</p>
<p>"It’s not a game or even a reconstruction anymore!"</p>
<p>"You’re going to burn yourself out!"</p>
<p>"You’re going to die just to prove a point!"</p>
<p>"If I die, it’ll be the most interesting thing to happen to this city in a thousand years," Rex shot back, a sharp, arrogant grin cutting through the grime on his face.</p>
<p>He turned his head just enough to catch Mordecai’s gaze, his eyes glowing with a predatory, manic light. "Don’t worry about me..."</p>
<p>"Worry about whether you can keep up when the real heavy lifting starts."</p>
<p>Then, the sky broke.</p>
<p>The second SSS class entity didn’t just arrive; it occupied the atmosphere. It was a titan of lithic biology, a creature of such immense geological scale that its movements felt like the shifting of tectonic plates.</p>
<p>It was a living mountain, a fusion of ancient, mineralized marrow and sentient, pulsing flesh. It was a creature that had existed for eons, and it looked at Rex not as a threat, but as a momentary irritation in its eternal existence.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t hesitate, and of course, he didn’t regroup. He launched an attack.</p>
<p>The next hour and twenty minutes were a masterclass in brutal, surgical violence. Rex was a whirlwind of elemental precision.</p>
<p>He wasn’t just smashing the titan; he was deconstructing it. Using the Earthen Apostle’s authority, he reached into the very marrow of the beast, finding the microscopic seams where biology met geology.</p>
<p>’There... The calcium silicate interface... Break it!’</p>
<p>He would slam a tectonic pulse into a limb, not to crush it, but to vibrate the mineral bonds until they shattered, leaving the biological tissue exposed and vulnerable. The titan roared a sound that wasn’t a sound but a low-frequency vibration that rattled the very teeth in Rex’s skull.</p>
<p>It lashed out with a tail of solid granite, a strike that would have leveled a skyscraper.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t dodge. He met it straight on.</p>
<p>He wove a lattice of compressed kinetic energy and geological reinforcement, catching the blow with a grunt of effort that sounded more like a challenge than a struggle.</p>
<p>"Is that all?" Rex hissed through clenched teeth, his eyes locked on the titanic eye of the creature. "You’ve been around for a million years?"</p>
<p>"Well, guess what? Your time just ran out!"</p>
<p>He was fighting a god of the earth, and he was treating it like a puzzle to be solved. Rex was already three steps ahead, his mind a frantic, brilliant engine of adaptation, as the creature adapted each time, reinforcing a seam in its biology or hardening its minerals in response to his touch.</p>
<p>He was learning the creature’s rhythm, its ancient, slow-motion heartbeat, and he was breaking it piece by piece.</p>
<p>Mordecai watched from the ground, a helpless spectator to a madness he couldn’t comprehend. He saw Rex bloody and exhausted, pushing himself past the point of human endurance, and he saw a man who looked more alive in the middle of a slaughter than he ever had in the quiet moments of peace.</p>
<p>"Lustful Villain, please!" Mordecai yelled as a massive shockwave from a successful extraction sent a cloud of dust and pulverized stone billowing across the courtyard. "It’s too much! Just rest for a second!"</p>
<p>"Rest is for the dead, Mordecai!" Rex’s voice drifted back through the roar of the battle, sounding distant, almost transcendent. "And I’m far from finished!"</p>
<p>Finally, with a sound like a continent snapping in half, the titan’s structural coherence failed. The massive, integrated systems of stone and flesh could no longer hold the tension Rex had introduced.</p>
<p>The creature began to slump, its gargantuan form dissolving into a mountain of disconnected rubble and lifeless organic matter.</p>
<p>The silence returned, heavier than before.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the center of the wreckage, the dust settling around him like gray snow. His breathing was deep, ragged, and heavy, but he didn’t collapse.</p>
<p>He stood tall, his shoulders squared, his head held high. He looked at the carnage he had wrought, the broken god at his feet, and the still-tearing rifts above.</p>
<p>He wiped a mixture of blood and stone dust from his forehead, a slow, weary, but utterly triumphant smirk spreading across his face. He looked at Mordecai, who was staring at him in a mixture of awe and pure, unadulterated terror.</p>
<p>"See?" Rex whispered, his voice a low, gravelly rasp of pure, unshakeable confidence. "Told you... it was engrossing."</p>
