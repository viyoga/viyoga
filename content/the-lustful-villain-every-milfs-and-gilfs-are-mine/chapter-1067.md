<p>The aftermath in Kaeleth’s domain was not the silent, hollow vacuum that a normal engagement produced. When a god loses a piece, there is usually a moment of divine shock, a stutter in the heartbeat of reality, but Rex knew better than to expect mercy from the architect of time.</p>
<p>A cold, sharp dread began to crystallize in the pit of his stomach. He knew this before it manifested as a physical threat, because he had learned a lesson in the blood-soaked halls of Lustia’s domain.</p>
<p>’Knowing what is coming is not the same as being ready for it.’</p>
<p>Knowledge was data; readiness was survival. And Kaeleth was not an entity that accepted the theft of its own power without attempting to reassert control through sheer, devastating recalibration.</p>
<p>Rex felt the shift first in his marrow. The air didn’t just change temperature; it changed logic.</p>
<p>The fundamental rules governing his own movement began to feel viscous, like he was suddenly wading through cooling mercury.</p>
<p>Then, the amber-gold sky of the domain began to warp.</p>
<p>This was not the gradual, organic shift of an environment responding to changed atmospheric conditions. This was not the soft sunset of a passing day.</p>
<p>This was the violent, deliberate, and controlled recalibration of a reality that was being forcibly rewritten by its owner. The amber light didn’t fade; it twisted, twisting like a gut being wrung out by invisible hands.</p>
<p>The very geometry of the space began to buckle, walls of light folding in on themselves with the grinding screech of tectonic plates made of pure energy.</p>
<p>Rex’s inner monologue screamed a single, undeniable truth as the horizon began to curve inward, threatening to crush him: ’I didn’t break the cage... I just provoked the jailer.’</p>
<p>The spatial coordinates around him began to hemorrhage light. He could feel the pressure building behind his eyes, the sickening sensation of his own temporal signature being forcibly synchronized with the domain’s new, hostile configuration.</p>
<p>The exits to the dimensional wounds he had torn open to escape were being stitched shut with threads of golden causality. Every path he had mapped out for his retreat was being overwritten, erased, and replaced with dead ends of infinite loops.</p>
<p>The realization hit him with the force of a physical blow: he wasn’t just facing a wounded god. He was standing inside a closing trap.</p>
<p>The domain wasn’t just a setting anymore; it had become a living, breathing mechanism designed to compress, to grind, and to erase the anomaly that was Rex.</p>
<p>He was trapped. The walls were moving.</p>
<p>The floor was dissolving into a kaleidoscope of hostile temporal distortions.</p>
<p>Rex closed his eyes.</p>
<p>Through the cold lens of his Divine Perception’s passive read, Rex felt the mechanism of the trap snap shut. This wasn’t a targeted attack; there was no projectile to dodge, no blade to parry.</p>
<p>This was something far more insidious: a domain-level temporal working.</p>
<p>It was the God of Time’s equivalent of a sealed tomb. Kaeleth wasn’t attacking Rex’s body; he was attacking the very concept of ’now’ that Rex occupied.</p>
<p>The domain was being forcibly dragged out of the standard physical plane’s relationship with time and shoved into a native framework where time was no longer a constant; it was whatever Kaeleth decided it would be.</p>
<p>The working reached Rex like a wave of nauseating vertigo. The continuous thread of the present moment began to fray, stretching into impossible lengths and compressing into razor-thin slices of instantaneity.</p>
<p>Reality became a strobing nightmare. One moment he was standing; the next, he was experiencing three separate moments simultaneously, each one conflicting with the other.</p>
<p>’Fuck,’ Rex thought, his teeth grinding together as his brain struggled to process the sensory overload. ’This isn’t a fight.’</p>
<p>’This is an erasure...’</p>
<p>His full potential adaptation screamed at him. It had already mapped, analyzed, and countered every mechanism Theo’s designation had ever produced.</p>
<p>But this was raw, unfiltered divine authority. The adaptation’s analysis engines whirred with the frantic urgency of a first contact mechanism encountering alien mathematics.</p>
<p>It was trying to categorize a force that didn’t follow the rules of the universe Rex knew.</p>
<p>The system flashed a warning in his mind, crimson and stark: [CRITICAL THRESHOLD APPROACHING]</p>
<p>[Seven point three seconds.]</p>
<p>That was the countdown. In seven point three seconds, Kaeleth’s temporal framework would have rendered Rex’s existence so fundamentally uncertain that his biological coherence would shatter.</p>
<p>His neurons would fire at different times than his synapses; his blood would flow before his heart could pump it; his consciousness would fragment into a thousand disconnected echoes. He would become a smear of disorganized matter, a human glitch in a divine program.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes snapped open, burning with a desperate, violent clarity.</p>
<p>’You want to rewrite the rules?’</p>
<p>’Then I’ll break the paper you’re writing on.’</p>
<p>He didn’t aim at Kaeleth. He didn’t aim at the shifting amber sky.</p>
<p>Instead, he turned his telekinesis inward, not on a target, but at the very fabric of the domain itself. He pushed his power with absolute, terrifying molecular precision, driving a raw, kinetic force through the medium that Kaeleth was trying to manipulate.</p>
<p>He wasn’t fighting the god; he was attacking the structural foundation of the god’s stage.</p>
<p>He poured everything into it, every ounce of his will, every drop of his accumulated power, forcing the telekinetic pressure to interface with the domain at the subatomic level, beneath the level of the temporal manipulation itself.</p>
<p>The effect was instantaneous and catastrophic.</p>
<p>The amber gold sky didn’t just dim; it shattered.</p>
<p>It wasn’t the god who broke, but the reality he had constructed. The temporal framework, meeting the telekinetic’s full spectrum output at the molecular level of its physical implementation, reached its breaking point.</p>
<p>The domain’s structural foundation buckled under the impossible stress. There was a sound like a thousand glass cathedrals collapsing at once—a high-pitched, bone-shaking screech that tore through the air.</p>
<p>The reality of the domain fractured like ice under a sledgehammer. Cracks of pure white void spiderwebbed across the amber sky, spreading with lethal speed.</p>
<p>The very concept of the space began to splinter into jagged, lethal shards of crystallized time and broken physics.</p>
<p>The pieces flew.</p>
<p>The shards that tore through the air were not physical in any way a human mind could comprehend. They weren’t stone, metal, or glass; they were jagged, screaming splinters of fractured causality.</p>
<p>These were fragments of the working’s raw, bleeding chunks of implemented temporal authority that had been violently severed from Kaeleth’s unified framework.</p>
<p>Without the god’s will to hold them together, they had become rogue variables: crystallized moments of time that had lost their purpose and gained only momentum and malice.</p>
<p>They didn’t just fly toward Rex; they hunted him.</p>
<p>Each fragment was a weapon of divine origin, carrying within its razor-sharp edges a concentrated dose of Kaeleth’s domain-level authority.</p>
<p>To touch one was not merely to be cut, but to have one’s personal timeline assaulted. A single shard could age a limb to dust in a heartbeat or freeze a heart mid-beat while the rest of the body continued to scream.</p>
<p>The air screamed with them, a chorus of grinding reality as these temporal anomalies tore through the space between them.</p>
<p>’Goddammit, move!’ Rex’s mind roared as his reflexes pushed him to the absolute brink of human and superhuman capability.</p>
<p>His vision blurred as the fragments streaked past, leaving trails of distorted light that looked like wounds in reality itself.</p>
<p>One shard, larger than the rest and pulsing with a sickly, unstable violet hue, tore through the space inches from his face.</p>
<p>Rex felt the temporal wake of it, a nauseating lurch that made his stomach turn as his perception of the last three seconds began to overlap with the next three. His inner monologue became a fractured mess of overlapping voices.</p>
<p>’Move, don’t move left or right... I’m already dead... or... I’m still alive.’</p>
<p>A fragment clipped his shoulder. There was no pain at first, only a terrifying, hollow wrongness.</p>
<p>He watched in horror as the skin where the shard struck didn’t bleed red; it simply ceased. A perfect circle of his flesh vanished, not torn away, but erased from the timeline.</p>
<p>For a split second, he saw the bone beneath, white, clean, and impossibly old, before the temporal feedback caught up and the wound began to hemorrhage reality itself, blood spurting out in staccato bursts that seemed to freeze in midair before splashing down seconds too late.</p>
<p>"Fuck you, Kaeleth!" Rex spat, blood frothing at the corners of his mouth as he gritted his teeth against the agony.</p>
<p>His bones felt like they were being ground between two millstones as the proximity to the shards disrupted the very atomic bonds holding his skeleton together. "Is this all you’ve got? Playing with broken toys?"</p>
<p>But the warning from his Full Potential Adaptation was screaming red. The fragments were converging.</p>
<p>They weren’t random debris; they were forming a storm, a cyclone of broken time designed to shred anything caught in its center.</p>
<p>He could hear the wet, grinding sound of his own muscles tearing as the temporal pressure intensified. His left arm felt like it was being pulled in two different directions at once, one half of it accelerating toward the future, the other half dragged backward into the past.</p>
<p>The pain was beyond anything he had ever endured; it was a metaphysical violation, a violation of his very right to exist in a linear sequence.</p>
<p>The shards closed in. A wall of glittering, lethal paradoxes.</p>
<p>Rex braced himself, his muscles coiling like steel springs, his consciousness narrowing down to a single, desperate point of defiance. He couldn’t dodge them all.</p>
<p>He couldn’t outrun the end of time.</p>
<p>Rex received them.</p>
