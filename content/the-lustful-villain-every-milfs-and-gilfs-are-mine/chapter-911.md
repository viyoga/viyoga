<p>Theo had been watching. He had been watching since the first bell of the sixth hour, a silent specter at the periphery of the training hall.</p>
<p>He had constructed a fortress of rationalizations to protect himself from the truth: that he was watching with the obsessive focus of a man who had been usurped. He told himself it was professional duty that a Nightwing heir must closely monitor the methods of the acting director.</p>
<p>He told himself he was studying Rex’s techniques to better understand the threat. He told himself he was simply assessing the new power dynamic of the household.</p>
<p>Each lie was thinner than the last, crumbling under the weight of the undeniable reality unfolding before him.</p>
<p>When the kiss ended and the hall began to fracture back into its normal rhythms, Theo moved. He crossed the stone floor without conscious intent, his boots making no sound, drawn by a gravitational pull he couldn’t resist.</p>
<p>He found himself standing near the wall where Diana had left her belongings. His hand reached out, almost involuntarily, and gripped the rough fabric of her towel.</p>
<p>It was a pathetic, automatic gesture, the action of a man whose hands needed a purpose because his mind was drowning in something he refused to name.</p>
<p>Diana was still standing near Rex, her chest still rising and falling from the exertion, when she sensed his approach. She turned, wiping sweat from her brow, and her expression shifted instantly.</p>
<p>The warmth she had shown Rex vanished, replaced by the polished, impenetrable neutrality of a trained operative.</p>
<p>"Thank you," she said, reaching for the towel.</p>
<p>Her voice was flat, stripped of inflection, carrying nothing but the hollow courtesy she would extend to a stranger.</p>
<p>Theo didn’t retreat.</p>
<p>"You have improved," he said, his voice carrying a strained edge that he couldn’t quite suppress. "Since yesterday... the change is... not subtle."</p>
<p>"He is a good teacher," Diana replied.</p>
<p>She didn’t look at him. She began wiping the salt from her neck, her body language already angling away from him in the precise, dismissive posture of someone who had already decided that this interaction was a tactical waste of time.</p>
<p>"I could see that," Theo said, his jaw tightening.</p>
<p>He stood there for a moment, the silence between them stretching uncomfortably long, filled only by the distant sounds of other fighters resuming their drills. Then, driven by some desperate, ill-advised impulse, the kind that makes men ruin themselves, he pushed further.</p>
<p>"We used to spar together... before all of this and before the House became... this. You remember."</p>
<p>Diana paused mid-motion. For a heartbeat, something flickered behind her eyes, a ghost of a memory, but it was gone before it could take root.</p>
<p>"I remember," she said coldly. "We were fifteen..."</p>
<p>"You broke my wrist because you didn’t know how to control your own momentum, and I forgave you because we were children who knew nothing of consequence."</p>
<p>The memory hung between them like a blade. Theo flinched almost imperceptibly at the reminder of his own past weakness, his fingers curling tighter around the towel.</p>
<p>"I have thought about that sometimes," Theo said, his voice dropping to something raw and almost vulnerable, though it still tasted of bitter regret. "I’ve thought about how different everything might have been."</p>
<p>"How different we might have been if we had kept training together instead of drifting into separate circles."</p>
<p>Diana finally turned to face him fully. The mask of polite neutrality didn’t just slip; it shattered.</p>
<p>The patience she had been extending that fragile, customary courtesy withered and died in her eyes.</p>
<p>"Theo," she said.</p>
<p>Her voice wasn’t loud, but it possessed a terrifying, razor-edged clarity that seemed to suck the air out of the immediate space around them. "I am going to say this once, and I am going to say it clearly, because I think you are trapped in some delusion that if you say enough small things in enough quiet moments, something will eventually land differently than the last one did."</p>
<p>"You think you’re planting seeds... You’re not."</p>
<p>Theo opened his mouth to speak, but she cut through him like a blade through silk.</p>
<p>"It will not happen... I am not drifting back toward you."</p>
<p>"There is no version of the last several months, no timeline, no alternative reality where that happens."</p>
<p>"I did not choose Rex because you were unavailable, or because the timing was wrong, or because I was looking for an escape."</p>
<p>"I chose him because I love him... plainly, violently, and with a certainty I have never felt for anyone else." Her gaze hardened, becoming something cold and unforgiving. "And every time you find some pathetic excuse to stand near me and invoke memories from when we were fifteen, it doesn’t make me nostalgic, Theo."</p>
<p>"It doesn’t make me remember a lost connection... It makes me uncomfortable and it makes me want to walk away from you."</p>
<p>Theo’s jaw tightened so hard the muscles along his temple visibly pulsed. His hands clenched into fists at his sides, the knuckles whitening.</p>
<p>The humiliation was visible in the way his shoulders tensed, a predator caught in a trap of his own making.</p>
<p>"I was not trying to make you uncomfortable," he said, his voice strangled, defensive, and dangerously thin.</p>
<p>"I know you weren’t trying to," Diana said. Her expression shifted then.</p>
<p>The anger ebbed away, leaving behind something far more devastating: pity. It was a soft, hollow sound in her voice that cut deeper than any insult. "That is actually the worst part of this, Theo."</p>
<p>’You aren’t doing this maliciously... You don’t even realize you’re doing it."</p>
<p>"Which means you don’t know how to stop, which means this cycle will continue until you finally develop the spine to actually let me go..."</p>
<p>"I cannot make that decision for you, and I cannot grieve the ghost of our childhood for you."</p>
<p>She didn’t wait for his rebuttal. She didn’t give him the satisfaction of watching him break.</p>
<p>She simply turned her back on him, a dismissal more absolute than any physical blow, and walked back toward the center of the training floor.</p>
<p>Her destination was Rex. He was standing there, framed by the afternoon light filtering through the high windows, engaged in a low-voiced conversation with Aurelia about the afternoon’s tactical schedule.</p>
<p>He hadn’t moved, hadn’t flinched, and hadn’t broken eye contact with the matriarch, but Rex was not a man who missed details. He had heard every syllable of the exchange, every inflection of Diana’s pain and Theo’s desperation, yet he remained as impassive as carved granite.</p>
<p>Theo remained frozen at the wall. His fingers curled reflexively around the empty air where the towel had been, his hand grasping at nothing.</p>
<p>He stood there, a disgraced prince in his own kingdom, watching the woman he wanted walk toward the man who had already won.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The training session dragged on for another hour, stretching long past the point where most of the observers had drifted away to their respective obligations. The hall gradually emptied, the echoing shouts and the rhythmic clatter of weapons fading until the space held only those with a genuine reason or obsession to remain.</p>
<p>Aurelia moved through the remaining crowd like a queen surveying her domain, dismissing the last of the junior guards with crisp, unyielding instructions for the afternoon’s duties.</p>
<p>Iris departed with a subtle alertness in her step, leaving to investigate something at the household’s outer perimeter that had been gnawing at her assassin instincts since sunrise.</p>
<p>Elaris left last, carrying the unmistakable, buoyant energy of someone who had gained far more entertainment from watching the morning’s drama than she had anticipated.</p>
<p>Rex remained. He began a final, solitary drill, the kind of brutal, punishing conditioning work that Martial Arts Mastery did not strictly require for combat efficiency but that his body demanded for survival.</p>
<p>It was the raw discipline of maintaining the foundation upon which his lethal abilities rested. His movements were mechanical yet explosive; he moved through shadow boxing sequences that looked more like controlled strikes against invisible throats, followed by explosive bursts of footwork that left scuff marks on the stone.</p>
<p>By the time he finally ceased, the hall felt cavernous and still. His shirt was soaked through, clinging to the hard planes of his chest like a second skin, and his breathing came in deep, ragged draws.</p>
<p>This was not the performed exhaustion of a demonstration; it was the honest, bone-deep fatigue of a body that had been pushed to its absolute limit for three consecutive hours.</p>
<p>Diana was already there, waiting at the edge of the floor. She didn’t greet him with words; she simply existed there, a steady presence in the quiet hall.</p>
<p>As he approached, she handed him a cup of water. He took it without ceremony, drinking half of it in desperate, greedy gulps, feeling the cold liquid cut through the furnace of his throat. When he finished, she took the empty cup and reached for a fresh towel.</p>
<p>"Sit," she commanded. Her voice was quiet, but it carried the weight of an order he wouldn’t or couldn’t refuse. "You have been standing since before dawn, Rex."</p>
<p>"I am fine," he said.</p>
<p>The words were true; his body was built for this, forged in fires far hotter than a morning drill, but he knew it wasn’t the point. He could see it in the way her jaw set, the way her eyes traced the tension in his shoulders.</p>
<p>Diana’s expression didn’t waver. She didn’t argue, because she didn’t need to.</p>
<p>She simply stared at him with that terrifyingly perceptive understanding that saw past his discipline straight to the exhaustion he refused to acknowledge.</p>
<p>"Sit anyway," she said, her voice softening but retaining its iron core. "Let me do this..."</p>
<p>"I want to."</p>
