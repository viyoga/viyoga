<p>Dinner was quieter than the previous night, not because anything was wrong, but because the house occasionally held that quality—where the day had been long, not difficult, but full, and people arrived at the table exhausted in a comfortable way, needing no words to fill the silence.</p>
<p>Lily did most of the talking. Her conversation was easy and warm, the kind that required nothing from those who were silent.</p>
<p>Diana contributed when it felt necessary. Rex ate with the unhurried patience he brought to everything, responding only to questions posed to him and remaining silent otherwise.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was present in the way she had learned to be in spaces where she managed something internal; she was there, attentive, entirely composed, and careful not to let her emotions show on her face.</p>
<p>After dinner, Rex helped clear the dishes as he had begun to do, while Elizabeth remained at the table with her wine, chatting with Lily and Diana.</p>
<p>At one point, Rex passed through the dining room doorway on his way somewhere else, and Elizabeth’s gaze followed him in a way that suggested she was tracking something she shouldn’t have been, a realization that prompted her to redirect her attention.</p>
<p>Diana noticed this subtle shift. Her expression remained unchanged, which was her particular way of responding to something unspoken.</p>
<p>Later, Lily and Diana went upstairs, moving with the natural timing of people who have decided to return a room to its remaining occupants, while Elizabeth and Rex sat at the table with the last of the wine, embodying the specific quality of an evening that had reached its quiet point.</p>
<p>"You didn’t come by this afternoon," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>"You didn’t need me this afternoon," Rex said.</p>
<p>"There was a gap in the timeline that I couldn’t fill without the interior knowledge," she said. "You said you’d come back to it."</p>
<p>"You worked around it," Rex said. "You left the space and kept going."</p>
<p>"That was the right call."</p>
<p>She looked at him. "You could see the ledger from the sitting room."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said. "But I know how you work."</p>
<p>Elizabeth held her wine and was quiet for a moment. "You..."</p>
<p>"You spent the morning with Lily and Diana," she said, delivering the statement with flat precision rather than as a question.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said. "Nothing’s wrong with that, right?"</p>
<p>"But... you weren’t working."</p>
<p>"Nope," Rex said. "I think the professor could handle it, and besides we still have six days left."</p>
<p>"Or maybe... this one week isn’t really useful at all because I bet Valentina is going to forget about the key and the legion anyway."</p>
<p>She looked at her wine.</p>
<p>"That’s fine," she said. "That’s entirely your time to use as you choose..."</p>
<p>"The analysis doesn’t require you all day."</p>
<p>"Elizabeth," Rex said.</p>
<p>She looked up.</p>
<p>"Just say it," he said.</p>
<p>She looked at him for a moment, her expression resembling that of someone who had been holding something tightly in a closed hand and was now being asked to open it, revealing a thing that was not what she would have chosen to present to another person.</p>
<p>"I don’t have a right to it," she said. "I want to be clear about that first."</p>
<p>"I know what the arrangement is and I know what it isn’t, and I don’t have a claim on your time or your attention outside of the work."</p>
<p>"But," Rex said.</p>
<p>"But," she said, and then she stopped and looked at the wall for a moment, and then she looked back at him. "I watched you with them today... well, it’s not because I was watching."</p>
<p>"I just—I passed the doorway a few times, and I saw it, and I kept working, which was the correct thing to do." She paused. "And I couldn’t focus."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>"I sat at that table for six hours and did good work," she said, "and I also could not stop being aware of what was happening in the sitting room, which is not something I have experienced before in a professional context and which I do not know what to do with."</p>
<p>"It’s not a professional context," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I know what it is," she said. "That’s the part I don’t know what to do with."</p>
<p>Rex stood up, and she watched him come around the table, and she did not move, and he sat in the chair beside hers, which was closer than across the table, and he looked at her with the attention she had been thinking about intermittently all day.</p>
<p>"You could have come in," he said. "This morning."</p>
<p>"I wasn’t occupied with anything that required a closed door."</p>
<p>"I know," she said.</p>
<p>"But you didn’t," he said.</p>
<p>"No," she said.</p>
<p>"Why," he said.</p>
<p>She looked at him, and she said, with the specific honesty of someone who had been choosing carefully what to say and had run out of careful choices, "Because I would have wanted to stay."</p>
<p>The quiet that followed had the weight of something that had been said that couldn’t be unsaid, and Elizabeth was very still in the way of someone who had made themselves that still deliberately and was aware of every inch of the distance between her and the person beside her.</p>
<p>Rex reached out and took her hand, not the way you took someone’s hand to hold it but the way you did it when you were turning it over to look at it, and she let him, and he turned it over and looked at her palm and then looked at her.</p>
<p>"Then stay," he said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at him for one long second, and then she did something unexpected—something she had neither planned nor predicted. It emerged from a part of her that had been building an argument against it for two days, an argument she hadn’t consciously noticed had already been lost.</p>
<p>She leaned forward, closing the remaining distance between them, and kissed him. The kiss was neither tentative nor practiced; it was direct, reflecting the specific decisiveness of someone who had chosen to stop managing the decision and simply act.</p>
<p>He kissed her back, which was what she had known he would do and which was still different from anticipating it.</p>
<p>When she pulled back, she looked at him with the unmistakable expression of someone who had just crossed a line they could not uncross, caught in the moment of deciding whether they truly wanted to.</p>
<p>"I don’t know how to do this," she admitted. "Not like they do—Lily and Diana. I’m not sure what that even looks like."</p>
<p>"You don’t have to do it the way they do," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I know that," she said. "I mean, I don’t know how to do it at all."</p>
<p>"Not—not this part." She paused. "I know how to work with you."</p>
<p>"I know how to conduct a professional arrangement with specific terms..."</p>
<p>"I don’t know how to—" She looked at his hand holding hers.</p>
<p>"Be jealous," she said. "And act on it..."</p>
<p>"And feel like this is something I’m allowed to want."</p>
<p>"You’re allowed to want it," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Alexander—"</p>
<p>"Is not here," Rex said, not unkindly.</p>
<p>Elizabeth fell silent for a moment.</p>
<p>Then she said, "No, he isn’t."</p>
<p>She looked at their hands and then at him, and then she stood, which he let her do, and she picked up the wine glass and the ledger from the table, and she said, "I’m going upstairs," in a tone that was not closing something but opening a question.</p>
<p>She walked to the door, paused, and turned back toward him, her expression revealing that she had made a decision she was ready to commit to before she had a chance to reconsider.</p>
<p>"Give me twenty minutes," she said.</p>
<p>Rex looked at her.</p>
<p>"And then come up," she said.</p>
<p>She turned and went upstairs.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The guest room corridor was quiet by the time Rex reached it. Lily’s door was closed and the light under it was off, which meant she was asleep or near enough.</p>
<p>Diana’s door was closed and the light under it was still on, because Diana read late.</p>
<p>He stopped at Diana’s door and knocked once, which was the specific knock she would recognize.</p>
<p>The light shifted slightly under the door, and then Diana opened it, and she looked at him in the way she looked at him when she already knew something and was waiting to see what he was going to do with it.</p>
<p>"She’s waiting," Rex said.</p>
<p>Diana looked at him for a moment. "Is she all right."</p>
<p>"She will be," Rex said.</p>
<p>Diana nodded, the specific nod she used for things she had assessed and accepted. "She’s been carrying it all day," she said. "I noticed at lunch. I noticed again at dinner." She paused. "It was only a matter of time before she stopped managing it and just felt it."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I’m jealous too, you know~!" Diana whistles. "I haven’t gotten any sex from you for a long time."</p>
<p>"You’ll have to wait for that."</p>
<p>Diana laughed and looked at him with the steady attention she had when she was about to say something she meant entirely.</p>
<p>"Take care of her," she said. "She acts like she doesn’t need it."</p>
<p>"That’s not the same thing as not needing it."</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said.</p>
<p>Diana held his gaze for a moment longer before planting a kiss on his cheek and stepping back from the door.</p>
<p>"Goodnight," she said.</p>
<p>"Goodnight," Rex said.</p>
<p>He turned and continued down the corridor.</p>
<p>Elizabeth’s door was unlocked, which answered the unasked question. He opened it and stepped inside.</p>
<p>She was by the window, standing as she often did when she was waiting and trying not to appear as such. She turned when she heard the door open.</p>
<p>She said, "I thought about what you mentioned—about not having to do it the way they do."</p>
<p>"And," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And I think I want to find out what my version of it looks like," she said, which was the most honest sentence she had said to him and possibly the most honest sentence she had said to anyone in a long time.</p>
<p>Rex crossed the room.</p>
