<p>Valentina said nothing.</p>
<p>The silence that followed Morwenna’s demand was not the hollow, ringing silence of a shock; it was something far more predatory. The previous silence had been the sound of a room catching its breath after a blow.</p>
<p>This silence was the sound of a predator coiled, muscles tensed, deciding exactly where to sink its teeth. It was the heavy, suffocating stillness of a woman who had already absorbed the impact, measured the depth of the wound, and was now calculating the exact cost of her next words.</p>
<p>The air in the room felt thick, almost viscous, as if the sheer weight of the unspoken truth were turning the oxygen into something heavy and hard to swallow.</p>
<p>"Valentina," Morwenna said again.</p>
<p>This time, the formal veneer of the High Council had cracked. Her voice had shifted, shedding the polished, diplomatic edges of a network member addressing its headmaster. It had dropped into a lower, more intimate, and far more dangerous register, the raw, unvarnished tone of two women who had shared decades of secrets, wars, and triumphs.</p>
<p>It was the voice of a comrade, a confidante, and a judge, all wrapped into one.</p>
<p>"I am asking you directly," Morwenna pressed, her eyes boring into Valentina’s with a ferocity that seemed to demand the truth bleed out of her. "As someone who has stood at your side for forty years... as someone who has bled in the same trenches as you... tell me..."</p>
<p>"Is the name a coincidence?"</p>
<p>The question hung in the air like a guillotine blade suspended by a single, fraying thread.</p>
<p>The room were no longer just watching; they were vibrating with a collective, frantic attention. Every person at that table understood, with a primal, gut-wrenching certainty, that the next few seconds would dictate the fate of the Grand Academy.</p>
<p>The answer wouldn’t just be information; it would be a declaration of war, a eulogy, or a salvation. They were all leaning in, unconsciously, as if the answer itself might physically strike them.</p>
<p>’Look at her,’ Rex thought, his eyes narrowed, his mind dissecting the minute tremors in the room. ’The tension is so high it’s a physical ache.’</p>
<p>’You could hear a pin drop, or a bone snap, and it would sound like a cannon blast.’</p>
<p>’They are all waiting for the verdict.’</p>
<p>’They are waiting to see if the woman they follow is a saint or a butcher.’</p>
<p>Valentina’s hands remained flat on the obsidian table. They were motionless, yet Rex could see the immense, invisible effort required to keep them there.</p>
<p>It was as if she were physically pinning her own soul to the table to keep it from leaping out of her chest.</p>
<p>The silence stretched, agonizingly long, pushing the limits of human endurance. It felt as though the very walls of the academy were leaning in, listening, waiting for the foundation to finally give way.</p>
<p>"I need a moment," Valentina said.</p>
<p>Her voice was a miracle of engineering. It was controlled, steady, and calm, but it was a control that had a visible cost.</p>
<p>It was the voice of a woman walking a tightrope over an abyss of fire, her every syllable a desperate attempt to keep from falling. There was a microscopic tremor in the cadence, a hint of the tectonic plates of her life grinding against one another, revealing the sheer, brutal effort it took to remain upright.</p>
<p>She wasn’t just asking for time to think; she was asking for time to rebuild her mask before the world saw the wreckage beneath.</p>
<p>"We don’t have a moment!" the divination representative burst out, her voice cracking like a whip.</p>
<p>The composure she had maintained all afternoon finally splintered, replaced by a raw, jagged desperation. "The world isn’t waiting for your contemplation, Headmaster!"</p>
<p>"The network has already lost fifty percent of its Aethelgard-adjacent capacity."</p>
<p>"The underlayer has reorganized itself under a new, shadow governance."</p>
<p>"The Legion’s founder has made an active, violent move against this city " she slammed a hand onto the table, the sound echoing like a gunshot, "and if the Headmaster has information relevant to any of these existential threats that she has been hoarding like a secret treasure "</p>
<p>"I am not withholding information!"</p>
<p>Valentina’s voice cut through the representative’s outburst with the sudden, lethal precision of a guillotine. The control was no longer just a mask; it had become a weapon.</p>
<p>The sharpness in her tone was the specific, defensive edge of a commander who had just been accused of treason and was deciding whether to forgive or to execute.</p>
<p>"I am processing information in a room full of people who are treating a revelation like a battlefield," Valentina continued, her eyes flashing with a cold, ancient fire. "And you are demanding that I perform a miracle of speed as if accuracy were a luxury we can afford to discard!"</p>
<p>"The network deserves--" the representative began, her face flushing a deep, indignant red.</p>
<p>"The network deserves accurate information," Valentina snapped, her voice dropping to a low, dangerous growl that seemed to vibrate the very obsidian of the table, "delivered at the speed that accuracy requires."</p>
<p>"Not information delivered at the speed that panic prefers!"</p>
<p>The word "panic" hit the room like a physical blow.</p>
<p>"Panic," Morwenna repeated.</p>
<p>She said the word softly, but the way she uttered it made every head at the table snap toward her. It wasn’t a question; it was a verdict.</p>
<p>Morwenna leaned forward, her shadow stretching long across the table, looming over the center of the conflict. Her eyes were no longer searching; they were fixed, predatory, and terrifyingly certain.</p>
<p>"No one here is panicking, Valentina. We are asking a question that your reaction has already answered."</p>
<p>The room went deathly still again. This wasn’t the silence of shock, nor the silence of a countdown.</p>
<p>This was the heavy, suffocating stillness of a tomb just before the lid is sealed. It was the silence of a predator that has finally cornered its prey and is simply waiting for the final struggle to end.</p>
<p>Rex watched the color drain from Aurelia’s face. He watched the way Apollo’s hand drifted, almost imperceptibly, toward the hilt of the blade at his hip.</p>
<p>The air was so thick with tension that it felt as though a single heavy breath might cause the entire room to implode, crushing them all under the weight of the truth.</p>
<p>’There it is,’ Rex thought, a dark, visceral thrill running down his spine. ’The mask is slipping.’</p>
<p>’The cracks are widening.’</p>
<p>’She’s trying to fight the truth with logic, but you can’t fight a landslide with a shield.’</p>
<p>’Morwenna isn’t just asking anymore... She’s hunting...’</p>
<p>"Your reaction," Morwenna continued, her voice gaining a terrifying, rhythmic momentum, like the steady, heavy thud of a drum preceding a massacre. "When Apollo spoke that name, the air didn’t just leave your lungs; it left your soul."</p>
<p>"It was not the reaction of a woman hearing a new truth. It was not the shock of a scholar discovering a lost, dusty lineage in a forgotten tome."</p>
<p>"It was the visceral, bone-deep shudder of a woman hearing a secret she had spent thirty years trying to bury in the dark, beneath layers of lies and the blood of the fallen."</p>
<p>The momentum of her words was relentless, stripping the room of its remaining air. Morwenna wasn’t just speaking; she was deconstructing Valentina, piece by agonizing piece.</p>
<p>"Morwenna—" Valentina started.</p>
<p>The command was there, the old authority trying to claw its way back to the surface, but it was failing. Her voice was losing its razor-sharp edge, softening into something fragile, something that looked dangerously like dread.</p>
<p>It was the sound of a fortress realizing its walls were made of nothing but sand.</p>
<p>"You knew," Morwenna whispered.</p>
<p>The whisper was more devastating than a scream. It landed with the sickening, final thud of a hammer driving a nail into a coffin lid.</p>
<p>The sound seemed to echo in the silence, a heavy, metallic vibration that settled in the marrow of everyone present.</p>
<p>Morwenna leaned in closer, her shadow swallowing the headmaster’s hands. Her eyes were cold, unblinking, and merciless, stripping away every layer of Valentina’s dignity, every shred of the respect she had spent a lifetime cultivating.</p>
<p>She was looking past the headmaster, past the leader, and staring directly into the heart of a traitor.</p>
<p>"You knew the name," Morwenna hissed, her voice dripping with a cocktail of betrayal and pure, unadulterated fury. "You knew the bloodline."</p>
<p>"You knew the exact weight of the shadow the Legion cast over this world."</p>
<p>"You sat in this very chair, wearing that very crown, watching us bleed, watching our people die, watching the reincarnators fall one by one like wheat before a scythe... and you sat there in silence."</p>
<p>"You sat there, knowing the enemy was not a stranger but your own kin."</p>
<p>"You didn’t just keep a secret, Valentina."</p>
<p>"You nurtured a lie while we paid for it in blood."</p>
<p>The accusation hung in the air, thick and suffocating, a heavy shroud of treason that seemed to stain the very obsidian of the table. The tension had reached a breaking point; it was no longer a psychological war but a spiritual execution.</p>
<p>The room waited, breathless, for the moment the headmaster would either shatter or strike back.</p>
