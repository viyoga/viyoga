<p>Something in her tone shifted, a subtle tightening of the air between them, a change in frequency that Rex caught instantly. His instincts, honed by countless near-death encounters and the brutal lessons of Kaeleth’s domain, flared to life.</p>
<p>"Talked about where?" Rex asked, his voice flat but dangerous.</p>
<p>His mind was already cataloguing the implications. In his world, ’talk’ was never just conversation; it was reconnaissance.</p>
<p>It was the prelude to an assassination attempt.</p>
<p>Lustia’s smile remained fixed on her lips, but her eyes sharpened, losing their playful sheen and replacing it with something guarded, something ancient. "Among circles that pay attention to this sort of thing."</p>
<p>"The kind of circles where existence itself is a currency." She paused, letting the weight of her words settle like ash. "The divine framework doesn’t move quickly, Rex, and it doesn’t move loudly; it moves with the inevitability of a landslide, but it does move."</p>
<p>"Whispers have begun to circulate that Kaeleth’s operation over Aethelgard has collapsed."</p>
<p>"Nothing official has been announced, of course; Kaeleth is too wounded in pride to admit he was bested by a mortal who bled him."</p>
<p>Her fingers drummed absently on the crimson silk. "But entities talk."</p>
<p>"They always talk. And the story of a forbidden god’s chosen apostle beating a two thousand-year-old time deity into submission?"</p>
<p>"That is a story that travels faster than any plague."</p>
<p>"Is that a problem?" Rex asked.</p>
<p>He felt the familiar coldness settling in his gut, the pragmatic calculation of a predator who knew he had just made too much noise.</p>
<p>"It’s a variable," Lustia corrected sharply, her eyes locking onto his. "One I need you to weigh with extreme caution."</p>
<p>"You are no longer operating in the shadows, Rex..."</p>
<p>"The shadows were comfortable for you once, but you’ve grown too large for them."</p>
<p>"You’ve outpaced the ordinary machinery of this world."</p>
<p>"Your combined abilities now sit well above what any single Apostle on this plane could hope to match." Her voice dropped to a conspiratorial murmur. "And depending on how you continue to adapt, you’re approaching territory that draws attention from higher up the hierarchy than city-level gods and their local puppets."</p>
<p>Rex finally lowered himself onto the couch across from her. He didn’t relax; he simply shifted his weight, turning the information over in his mind like a piece of intelligence that might contain a hidden blade.</p>
<p>’I’ve traded stealth for raw power,’ he thought. ’The cost of victory is visibility.’</p>
<p>"How much attention, exactly?" he demanded. "Give me numbers, Lustia."</p>
<p>"Give me threats."</p>
<p>"Not the kind that gets you struck down in your sleep not yet, anyway." She reached out, her fingertips tracing the line of his jaw with an almost maternal gentleness that felt grotesquely out of place given the subject matter.</p>
<p>"Most of the higher framework doesn’t concern itself with the petty squabbles of the mortal plane unless a Forbidden God is directly and visibly violating the operational code." Her touch turned cold. "But understand the shape of the risk, Rex."</p>
<p>"I am not acknowledged by the framework."</p>
<p>"My apostles operate outside its normal oversight..."</p>
<p>"That is why I could give you a system most gods would consider heresy a system of infinite adaptation."</p>
<p>"That freedom cuts both ways."</p>
<p>She leaned closer, her face inches from his, her breath ghosting over his lips. "If your name becomes loud enough in the wrong circles, if your power becomes an undeniable anomaly, there are entities who will not send an assassin."</p>
<p>"They will send an auditor... They will see an unacknowledged god’s champion accumulating this much power as something that requires... direct investigation."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He stared into those ancient eyes, his own expression unreadable.</p>
<p>"Which entities?"</p>
<p>Lustia’s expression didn’t just sober; it calcified. The playful, seductive goddess vanished, replaced by something ancient and terrifyingly pragmatic.</p>
<p>"The Goddess of Justice," she said, the name carrying a weight that seemed to physically press down on the room. "She is the one I would watch most closely."</p>
<p>"She is not like Kaeleth... she doesn’t act out of wounded pride or petty rage."</p>
<p>"She acts out of inevitability."</p>
<p>Lustia leaned back, her eyes distant as if reading invisible text written across the fabric of reality. "She still has Xavier Xenworth’s file open, Rex."</p>
<p>"His soul’s departure from his Apostle designation was... ambiguous... and even messy."</p>
<p>"She never formally closed the case because she never accepted the explanation."</p>
<p>"If she ever connects the dots, if she sees the thread connecting Xavier’s death to the reincarnator who inherited his signature, to now to a fight against a rogue time god that ended with an unacknowledged Forbidden God’s champion walking away with a piece of the divine prize, she will act."</p>
<p>Her voice dropped to a whisper that carried more menace than any shout. "She is old, Rex... and patient."</p>
<p>"And she is thorough in a way that makes Kaeleth look like a reckless child playing with matches."</p>
<p>"When she moves, she doesn’t just strike; she erases."</p>
<p>Rex absorbed the warning with the cold, detached efficiency of a machine. Inside his mind, he was already constructing a contingency map.</p>
<p>’The Goddess of Justice.’</p>
<p>’An auditor of souls.’</p>
<p>’A predator of anomalies.’</p>
<p>He filed the threat away in the same mental compartment where he kept the blueprints for every assassination and every political coup he had orchestrated. He didn’t fear her; fear was a luxury for the weak, but he respected her as a force of nature.</p>
<p>"So what’s the play?" Rex asked, his voice cutting through the tension like a blade. "Do I slow down?"</p>
<p>"Do I stop expanding my influence?"</p>
<p>"Absolutely not." Lustia’s grin returned, but it wasn’t warm anymore. It was sharp, hungry, and dangerous. "That would be cowardice, and cowardice gets you killed faster than ambition does."</p>
<p>"The plan is to be surgical about how loudly you expand."</p>
<p>She stood up, pacing the room with renewed energy. "Finish what you’ve started with the Underlayer engagement."</p>
<p>"That conflict is entirely contained within the mortal plane—demons squabbling over scraps, a floating city trying to maintain its grip."</p>
<p>"No divine oversight watches internal territorial wars closely enough to care who emerges victorious."</p>
<p>"Let them bleed each other dry while you consolidate power."</p>
<p>Her finger pointed toward him accusingly. "But keep your temporal authority quiet."</p>
<p>"Use the overlay you’ve built for Celestina to mask the aftershocks of your fight with Kaeleth."</p>
<p>"Don’t let his name reach the framework’s formal attention... Every day that story remains a rumor instead of a confirmed incident is another day you get to grow without a divine target painted on your back."</p>
<p>"And if the rumor becomes a fact?" Rex pressed, his eyes narrowing. "If she finds me anyway?"</p>
<p>Lustia stopped pacing and looked at him with a terrifying certainty. "Then you’ll have already grown strong enough that even the Goddess of Justice thinks twice before acting alone."</p>
<p>"You won’t be an anomaly to be corrected; you’ll be a power to be negotiated with."</p>
<p>"That is exactly why we’re having this conversation, Rex... I’m not teaching you how to hide; I’m teaching you how to become inevitable."</p>
<p>Rex leaned back against the silk cushions, the weight of her words settling into his bones. He found himself appreciating Lustia’s brutal honesty.</p>
<p>Other gods spoke in riddles and divine metaphors designed to keep mortals off balance. Lustia spoke in terms of variables, risks, and outcomes.</p>
<p>She gave him intelligence he could weaponize.</p>
<p>His mind shifted from strategy to resources. He could feel the raw power coursing through his veins, the accumulated essence of his conquests, the stolen authority of a god, and the sheer momentum of his own evolution.</p>
<p>"Let’s talk about the energy, then," Rex said, his voice hardening with intent. "Forty nine thousand points."</p>
<p>"That’s more than I’ve ever held at once, and I refuse to let it sit idle while I plan."</p>
<p>"I intend to burn through as much as possible before the Underlayer war truly ignites... I need to be at peak capacity."</p>
<p>He met her gaze directly, his eyes burning with a cold, calculating fire. "What should I be prioritizing?"</p>
<p>"Strength, versatility, or something more... fundamental?"</p>
