<p>Rex was already occupying the space she was trying to fill, his body moving through the opening she had created. He met her mid-adjustment with a brutal palm strike to the sternum that sent her staggering backward, blood speckling her lips as her breath failed her.</p>
<p>The third fighter, desperate to salvage the unit’s cohesion, made the fatal mistake of committing to a full-force strike rather than focusing on reorientation. She threw everything she had into a desperate haymaker.</p>
<p>Rex met that commitment with the ultimate cunning of a predator who knows everything. He didn’t block; blocking would have wasted energy. Instead, he caught the arm and redirected the strike’s own terrifying force back into the fighter’s own body.</p>
<p>The momentum of her own attack tore through her shoulder socket, sending her spinning violently toward the floor in a spray of sweat and blood.</p>
<p>In less than two seconds, Rex had neutralized three of them before they could even finish turning around.</p>
<p>The fourth fighter, the final remaining threat, did not panic. As her three companions collapsed into the growing pool of crimson and broken bone, she completed her reorientation in two seconds instead of four.</p>
<p>She was exceptional.</p>
<p>Rilena did not fall into the trap of desperation. Unlike the others who had committed to singular, explosive techniques that Rex could easily dismantle, she maintained a fluid, predatory circling.</p>
<p>She threw shallow, probing strikes, ghostly jabs that weren’t meant to connect but to test the tension in Rex’s muscles, to gauge the exact millisecond his reflexes triggered. When Rex responded with minimal movement, a deliberate, calculated economy of motion, she didn’t misinterpret it as an opening.</p>
<p>She read his restraint as data. She understood that a predator who doesn’t flinch is a predator who has already won.</p>
<p>"That is Rilena," Aurelia said, her voice carrying a rare note of genuine academic fascination. "She has been with the unit longer than anyone else standing."</p>
<p>"Watch her... Watch what she does when she stops probing."</p>
<p>Rilena struck. She exploded forward, changing levels twice in a single, seamless approach, a terrifying display of biomechanical control.</p>
<p>Most fighters would have lost their balance, their center of gravity shattered by the rapid shift from high- to low- to mid-level. To execute it cleanly required a level of trust in one’s own body that bordered on the supernatural.</p>
<p>She executed it with flawless precision.</p>
<p>Rex’s inner systems hummed with acknowledgment. His martial arts mastery flashed a warning: ’High tier threat detected...’</p>
<p>’Probability of successful engagement: 98%.’</p>
<p>For the first time in the session, Rex gave an opponent time. He allowed her six seconds of sustained engagement the longest any single fighter had lasted.</p>
<p>In those six seconds, the training hall became a blur of lethal motion. Rilena’s strikes were precise, aimed at nerve clusters and joints, forcing Rex to move with genuine purpose.</p>
<p>She landed two techniques: a vicious spinning heel kick that Rex had to catch mid-air and a lightning-fast elbow aimed at his temple that would have shattered the skull of any other man in the household.</p>
<p>But Rex was not any other man.</p>
<p>The resolution was not a grand explosion of violence, but something far more chilling: a quiet, surgical conclusion. After six seconds of confirming Rilena’s terrifying skill, Rex simply decided the test was over.</p>
<p>As she committed to a final, desperate strike, Rex didn’t block; he flowed. He caught her momentum, pivoted his hips with devastating torque, and redirected her entire kinetic force downward.</p>
<p>There was no bone breaking this time, only the wet thud of a body being placed precisely where it needed to be. Rilena hit the hardwood in a controlled roll, gasping for air, her eyes wide with the shock of having been read like an open book.</p>
<p>Rex stood alone in the center of the hall. Twelve fighters lay scattered around him like debris after a hurricane, some unconscious, some clutching broken ribs, some convulsing from the shock of impact.</p>
<p>His chest rose and fell in a measured, elevated rhythm, the specific breathing pattern of a man who had engaged in genuine combat, not the composed stillness of someone who had merely been playing.</p>
<p>Then, he raised his fist.</p>
<p>A sound tore from his throat, not a shout of anger, not a rehearsed victory cry, but something primal and involuntary. It was the raw, guttural roar of a predator who had asserted dominance over the pack.</p>
<p>The sound was visceral, echoing off the high ceilings and vibrating through the bones of everyone present.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy enough to suffocate.</p>
<p>Diana made a sound from the sidelines, a small, broken whimper that she couldn’t suppress. She was caught between terror and an overwhelming, terrifying awe at the monster she loved.</p>
<p>Iris stood frozen, her mask of neutrality shattered; she was staring at Rex with a raw, exposed intensity that she could no longer hide. Elaris remained silent, her eyes darting between Rex and Aurelia, sensing the seismic shift in the room’s power dynamic.</p>
<p>Aurelia stood at the hall’s edge, arms crossed. Her expression wasn’t one of surprise but of profound recalibration.</p>
<p>She had expected to see talent; she had witnessed a force of nature.</p>
<p>"The dragon," Aurelia said finally.</p>
<p>Her voice was flat and direct, the tone she used when she was dismantling her own previous conclusions. "And Tremor... Yesterday..."</p>
<p>"I said those might have been situations where you were fortunate. That you had simply survived by chance."</p>
<p>Rex wiped a streak of someone else’s blood from his jaw, his eyes locking onto hers with terrifying clarity. "You said that."</p>
<p>Aurelia took a breath, the weight of her admission hanging in the blood-scented air.</p>
<p>"I was wrong," she said.</p>
<p>Rex met Aurelia’s gaze, and for a moment, the predator vanished. In its place was something almost human: a warm, faintly pleased expression, the kind one reserves for receiving a truth that costs the giver something precious.</p>
<p>"You were assessing with limited information," Rex said, his voice carrying through the blood-scented silence like a calm blade. "You were calculating variables while missing the fundamental constant."</p>
<p>"I was assessing with bias," Aurelia corrected immediately.</p>
<p>There was no defensiveness in her tone, only the clinical honesty of a scientist admitting a contaminated sample. "The bias produced an incomplete assessment."</p>
<p>"I saw a survivor where I should have seen a force."</p>
<p>Her eyes swept over the carnage; the twelve fighters were scattered across the hardwood like broken dolls. Some were still whimpering, fingers pressed against shattered ribs; others were staring at the ceiling with the glassy-eyed shock of those who had just touched death.</p>
<p>"Get up," Aurelia commanded.</p>
<p>Her voice wasn’t raised, but it carried the absolute authority of a queen addressing her subjects. "All of you!"</p>
<p>"Look at your wounds... Feel the failure in your bones." A pause. "You were outclassed."</p>
<p>"That is a fact... But what you do in the next sixty seconds determines whether you are victims of circumstance or students of war."</p>
<p>"Get the fuck up."</p>
<p>One by one, through gritted teeth and labored breaths, they began to rise. The sound of their movement, the shuffling of limbs, the wet slap of bloodied palms on wood, was the only soundtrack to their humiliation.</p>
<p>Aurelia turned her attention to the standard setter. "Rilena."</p>
<p>Rilena straightened, though her chest still heaved as she fought to reclaim her oxygen. "Yes, Mistress."</p>
<p>"Six seconds," Aurelia said, stepping closer to the woman who had pushed Rex further than anyone else that morning. "That is longer than anyone else received."</p>
<p>"What does that data point tell you?"</p>
<p>Rilena wiped a smear of blood from her split lip, her eyes bright with the adrenaline of a near miss. "That I was the closest to actually presenting a problem..."</p>
<p>"That I forced him to engage rather than simply react."</p>
<p>"Yes." Aurelia’s expression sharpened, cutting through any potential pride. "But it also tells you exactly how far you still have to go before six seconds becomes six minutes."</p>
<p>"Do not mistake a temporary reprieve for mastery..."</p>
<p>"I want you obsessing over that gap, the terrifying distance between where you are and where he is, not comforting yourself with the compliment hidden inside it."</p>
<p>The tension in the room shifted from the aftermath of violence to the weight of expectation.</p>
<p>Elaris stepped forward from the shadows of the observer’s position, her movements fluid and deliberate. She broke the circle of fallen warriors, walking toward the two powerhouses at the center of the hall.</p>
<p>"Aurelia," Elaris said, her voice cutting through the lingering hum of adrenaline.</p>
<p>Aurelia turned her head slightly, meeting Elaris’s steady stare. "I know what you’re thinking, Mother."</p>
<p>"You know what I am going to say," Elaris countered with a faint, knowing smile.</p>
<p>"Yes," Aurelia said, though she didn’t move to concede. "And I am not going to say it yet."</p>
<p>Elaris turned to Rex, speaking conversationally, as if they were discussing something as mundane as the weather rather than the fact that he had just systematically dismantled an elite unit. "She is going to say it eventually, Rex."</p>
<p>"She simply prefers to arrive at obvious conclusions through her own internal process rather than accepting them when they are handed to her on a silver platter."</p>
<p>Rex watched them, his breathing finally returning to a resting state, though his body still hummed with the residual electricity of the fight. He saw the unspoken words hanging between the two women: the acknowledgment of what he truly represented and what he truly was.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said quietly.</p>
