<p>The silence that followed was absolute.</p>
<p>Morwenna searched Valentina’s face, looking for the lie, looking for the crack in the armor, and looking for the shadow of the enemy. She found nothing but the woman she had known for forty years, a woman who was as terrifyingly honest as she was formidable.</p>
<p>Slowly, Morwenna lowered her gaze. She looked down at the obsidian table, her eyes tracing the dark, reflective surface.</p>
<p>She said nothing. And in that silence, in the heavy, unsaid acknowledgment of the truth, the council felt the first real breath of stability return to the room.</p>
<p>Rex swept his gaze across the room, his eyes moving like a general surveying a battlefield after the smoke had cleared, looking for the wounds that hadn’t yet begun to bleed.</p>
<p>"This network," he began, his voice cutting through the heavy silence like a scalpel, "is the very thing the Legion has been trying to dismantle for thirty years."</p>
<p>"They haven’t succeeded through direct, frontal assaults, because they aren’t fools."</p>
<p>"A direct attack against a coordinated apostle network backed by Aethelgard’s defensive infrastructure and a mage class of Lady Valentina’s caliber at the helm is a suicide mission."</p>
<p>"It’s a waste of resources."</p>
<p>He paused, his eyes narrowing as he leaned into the table, the tension in his shoulders radiating outward.</p>
<p>"No, they don’t want to break our walls."</p>
<p>"They want to break our minds... They do it through this..."</p>
<p>"Through the precise, surgical introduction of a single, poisoned piece of information into a controlled environment and then simply sitting back to watch as the environment fractures itself around the toxin."</p>
<p>He turned his focus to Apollo, whose face was a mask of growing realization.</p>
<p>"The name was in the canyon’s intelligence," Rex said, the words landing with the weight of a death sentence. "But the canyon intelligence was obtained under conditions the Legion was intimately aware of."</p>
<p>"Their operatives were there and they were watching."</p>
<p>"They were breathing the same dust we were."</p>
<p>"That information wasn’t a discovery, Apollo. It was a delivery."</p>
<p>Apollo’s eyes widened, his breath hitching in a way that betrayed the sudden, violent reorganization of his thoughts.</p>
<p>"You are saying..." he started, his voice a low rasp, "...that they let the name be found?"</p>
<p>"I am saying the name was placed in a position where it was guaranteed to be found," Rex corrected, his tone clinical, almost brutal. "It was planted at a specific depth, in a specific format, designed to mimic the texture of authentic, hard-won intelligence."</p>
<p>"It was engineered to look like a discovery so that we would lower our guard..."</p>
<p>"The difference between a discovery and a delivery is often invisible to the person holding the prize."</p>
<p>Apollo looked down at the table, his hands clenching into fists. He was experiencing the mental equivalent of a bone snapping: the sudden, sickening realization that the foundation of his recent work was not solid ground but a trapdoor.</p>
<p>He was reassessing everything, stripping away the layers of his own certainty to find the hollow truth beneath.</p>
<p>"If you are right," Apollo whispered, the words sounding like they were being pulled from a wound, "then every step of my investigation... every moment of pride in finding that name... I was merely delivering exactly what they wanted me to deliver."</p>
<p>"You delivered the truth," Rex said, and for the first time, there was a flicker of grim solidarity in his voice. "The delivery method was theirs."</p>
<p>"The decision to bring that truth to this room was yours, and it was the correct one... The problem isn’t the information itself."</p>
<p>"The problem is the package it was wrapped in and the timing of the unwrapping."</p>
<p>"They didn’t give us a gift; they gave us a grenade and waited for us to pull the pin."</p>
<p>Apollo sat back, his expression flattening into a cold, unyielding pragmatism. He was a man who had just been handed a more lethal weapon: a better perspective.</p>
<p>He accepted the reframe, discarding his old, shattered logic to make room for the brutal reality Rex had laid bare.</p>
<p>"They gave us the name," Rex summarized, his voice a low, commanding growl. "They gave it to us knowingly."</p>
<p>"They calculated that delivering the name in a room filled with this much tension and history would produce exactly this result."</p>
<p>"They didn’t want to kill us; they wanted us to kill each other."</p>
<p>The room were listening now, not just with their ears, but with their very souls. The air felt electric, charged with the terrifying possibility that they were all currently dancing to a Legion tune.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at the dark surface of the table, his gaze piercing.</p>
<p>"The question we should be asking," he said, "is not what Valentina knows, or when she knew it. That is a distraction."</p>
<p>"The real question is: what information did the Legion want us to have, and what does that tell us about the much larger, much more dangerous truths they are still keeping in the dark?"</p>
<p>He looked up, his eyes locking onto Valentina one last time, not as a suspect, but as a comrade in arms.</p>
<p>"She is not the enemy in this room," Rex declared, his voice final and absolute. "She is the primary target of the same operation we are all currently falling victim to.’</p>
<p>"And the most effective thing we can do to spite the Legion right now is to stop doing exactly what they expect us to do."</p>

<p>It was the silence of a group of soldiers who had realized they were walking through a minefield and were now listening, with terrifying intensity, for the next click.</p>
<p>Apollo leaned back, his eyes distant, calculating.</p>
<p>Morwenna stared at the table, her jaw set in a line of grim determination.</p>
<p>Aurelia let out a long, slow breath, the tension leaving her lungs in a weary hiss.</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder remained silent, his intellect finally catching up to the gravity of the situation.</p>
<p>Rex sat down, the movement quiet but heavy, leaving the council to sit in the shadow of the truth they had finally, painfully, begun to grasp.</p>
<p>The silence that followed Rex’s declaration was not merely an absence of sound; it was a physical weight, a suffocating pressure that seemed to squeeze the very air from the lungs of everyone present.</p>
<p>It was the kind of silence that follows a lightning strike: the ringing in the ears, the smell of ozone, and the terrifying realization that the world has fundamentally changed and cannot be unchanged. It lasted a moment too long, stretching into an agonizing, uncomfortable void where every heartbeat felt like a hammer blow against the stillness.</p>
<p>Finally, the divination representative broke the spell. Her voice was barely a whisper, thin and fragile, like the cracking of ice on a frozen lake.</p>
<p>"Alright," she said, her eyes searching Rex’s face for any sign of a bluff, any tremor of uncertainty. "If we are to stop tearing ourselves apart... if we are to stop dancing to the Legion’s tune... then what do you suggest we do instead?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t answer immediately. He let the question hang in the air, letting the desperation of the room settle into a focused, hungry anticipation.</p>
<p>He didn’t look like a man offering a suggestion; he looked like a man presenting a weapon.</p>
<p>He leaned forward, the movement slow and deliberate, his eyes locking onto hers with a predatory intensity that demanded her total attention.</p>
<p>"We stop reacting to the scraps they throw us," Rex said, his voice dropping into a low, resonant tone that commanded the room. "And we start taking the heart of the beast."</p>
<p>A ripple of unease moved through the council, a collective intake of breath, but Rex pressed on, his words cutting through their hesitation like a hot blade through fat.</p>
<p>"I have Ignivara Ashenwyrm," he revealed.</p>
<p>The name hit the room like a physical blow. A few members of the council visibly recoiled, a murmur of shocked recognition rippling through the ranks.</p>
<p>Ignivara. The name was synonymous with devastation, a legend of tactical cruelty.</p>
<p>"She was the primary Legion operative who designed the foundational attack on this island," Rex continued, his voice hardening, stripping away any lingering doubt. "She is the architect of the very scars we still carry."</p>
<p>"She is the one who calculated the trajectories of the fire and the timing of the collapses, and she is also the shadow behind the Legion’s most successful deconstructions."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the sheer audacity of his claim sink in. He could see the doubt fighting with necessity in their eyes.</p>
<p>"She is currently in the city’s eastern district," Rex said, his gaze sweeping the room, pinning each of them to their seats. "Under Elizabeth’s research supervision."</p>
<p>"She is not a prisoner in a cage, but she is no longer a free agent."</p>
<p>"She has seen the math of the Legion... she also has seen the architecture of their lies. And most importantly..."</p>
<p>He leaned in even closer, his voice dropping to a lethal, conspiratorial hush that forced the entire room to lean in with him.</p>
<p>"...she has agreed to provide full intelligence. Not just rumors or scraps of reconnaissance, but the complete operational structure of the Legion."</p>
<p>"The entire network architecture. The deep layer protocols of their communication and, most critically, the specific, terrifying capabilities that Celestina Von Starlight has been meticulously building in the dark for the last thirty years."</p>
