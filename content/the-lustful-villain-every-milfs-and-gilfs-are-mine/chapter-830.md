<p>The room fell into a silence so profound it felt as though the very air had turned to stone. No one moved.</p>
<p>No one breathed. The tension was a physical cord stretched to the point of snapping, leaving them all suspended in the terrifying moment before the first blow was struck.</p>
<p>"He is right," Morwenna said.</p>
<p>The words were a soft, heavy thud in the center of the room, like a stone dropped into a deep, still well.</p>
<p>Every head turned toward her. The collective gaze of the council was a physical weight, a suffocating pressure that seemed to demand an explanation for her sudden, uncharacteristic surrender.</p>
<p>Morwenna didn’t look up. She remained anchored to the table, her eyes fixed on the dark, polished obsidian as if she were reading the very grain of the stone.</p>
<p>Her posture was not one of defeat but of a grim, weary clarity, the stance of a veteran who had finally stopped fighting a storm and had simply accepted that the rain was coming. She held her conclusion with the flat, cold acceptance of someone who had spent a lifetime weighing truths and had finally found one that was too heavy to deny.</p>
<p>"He is right that the timing is deliberate," she said, her voice steady despite the tremor of emotion beneath the surface. "And he is right that I reacted to the surname before I had the intelligence to back it up."</p>
<p>"I let the name strike me like a blade before I even knew where the wound was."</p>
<p>Slowly, she lifted her gaze. It wasn’t a look of accusation but one of profound, aching expectation. She looked directly at Valentina.</p>
<p>"But," Morwenna added, her voice sharpening, cutting through the sudden wave of sympathy in the room, "that does not change the fact that you knew."</p>
<p>"It does not change the fact that you held this secret while we all bled for the cause."</p>
<p>"This room deserves to hear from you, Valentina."</p>
<p>"Not because the Legion orchestrated this moment to tear us apart, and not because we are playing into their hands."</p>
<p>"We deserve the truth because our forty-year relationship, which I believed was built on absolute honesty, I believed was built on absolute honesty... deserves an honest account."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was no longer just a lack of noise; it was a living thing. It was a predator, coiled and hungry, waiting at the head of the table.</p>
<p>Rex stepped back from the obsidian surface. The movement was fluid, almost ghostly. He sat down, his chair scraping the floor with a sound like a dying breath.</p>
<p>He didn’t look at Valentina. He didn’t need to.</p>
<p>Through his geological awareness, he could feel the room’s tectonic shifts. He could sense the temperature of the collective attention rising, the way the air grew thick and ionized with the sheer, concentrated focus of every person present.</p>
<p>He could feel the weight of the silence as it pressed against Valentina, a crushing, invisible force waiting for her to break or to bloom.</p>
<p>’The floor is yours, Valentina,’ Rex thought, his eyes closing halfway as he felt the tension vibrate in his very marrow. ’Show us the truth, or let the lie bury us all.’</p>
<p>He had given her the space. He had cleared the wreckage of the argument to leave her standing alone in the clearing’s center.</p>
<p>Valentina finally moved. She looked down at the table, her fingers tracing a microscopic scratch in the stone.</p>
<p>Then, she looked at Morwenna, her eyes reflecting a lifetime of unspoken burdens. Finally, she swept her gaze across the room, meeting the eyes of the men and women who had been her shield, her sword, and her family.</p>
<p>"I knew the name," she said. The confession was quiet, but it carried the finality of a gavel. "I have known it for eleven years."</p>
<p>A collective, sharp intake of breath hissed through the room.</p>
<p>Eleven years of silence.</p>
<p>Eleven years of a lie, sitting in the dark, growing like a tumor.</p>
<p>"I did not know it as the name of the founder of an organization," Valentina continued, her voice gaining a hard, defensive edge as she felt the room’s judgment pressing in. "To me, it was a ghost."</p>
<p>"A name that appeared in three separate, disconnected intelligence threads that I was monitoring over the course of our operations."</p>
<p>"Threads that seemed unrelated, drifting through the noise of a thousand different reports."</p>
<p>"It wasn’t until the threads began to tighten, until the data points began to bleed into one another, that I was able to see where they intersected."</p>
<p>She paused, her jaw tightening, the muscles in her neck corded with the effort of holding her composure.</p>
<p>"When they intersected," she said, her voice dropping to a low, haunting register, "the picture they produced was not something a leader could simply act upon."</p>
<p>"It was a fractured, terrifying image. It was incomplete. And the completion of that picture required a level of information that did not exist within our standard intelligence channels."</p>
<p>"It required a truth that was hidden even from our own sensors."</p>
<p>Aurelia leaned forward, her eyes like flint, her expression devoid of the pity the others were beginning to show. She wanted the marrow of the truth, not the skin.</p>
<p>"What kind of information, Lady Valentina?" Aurelia demanded, her voice cutting through the tension like a razor. "What was so dangerous that you chose to sit on it for a decade?"</p>
<p>Valentina turned her gaze toward Aurelia. The look wasn’t one of a leader defending a policy but of a woman exposing a raw, unhealed nerve.</p>
<p>"Personal information," Valentina said.</p>
<p>The two words didn’t just fall into the silence; they crushed it. The weight of that statement was the sound of a massive, ancient door finally being unlatched, the sound of someone who had been bracing their entire body against a tidal wave for eleven years and had finally, exhausted, stepped aside to let the water rush in.</p>
<p>The room became unnervingly still. It was the kind of stillness that precedes a structural collapse, where the air feels too heavy to breathe and the slightest movement feels like a sacrilege.</p>
<p>"The name Celestina Von Starlight," Valentina began, her voice gaining a hollow, haunting resonance, "is not merely a coincidence of surnames."</p>
<p>"It is not a shadow connection or a strategic link..." Valentina takes a deep breath. "It is the name of my father’s second daughter."</p>
<p>She paused, and for a moment, the only sound was the frantic, rhythmic thudding of Rex’s own heart, which he could feel vibrating in his ears like a war drum.</p>
<p>"It was a child born of a relationship that preceded his marriage to my mother by approximately four years," Valentina continued, her eyes glassing over with a cold, distant light. "A relationship that my family... a relationship that my entire lineage chose to bury, to unacknowledge, and to strike from the records during his lifetime."</p>
<p>The silence shifted. It transformed from a heavy weight into something jagged and sharp.</p>
<p>It was the specific, sickening quality of silence that arrives when a room has finally received the truth it was screaming for, only to find that the truth is far more monstrous and intimate than the question had ever prepared them to handle. They had expected a conspiracy of nations; they had received a conspiracy of blood.</p>
<p>"You have a sister," Morwenna whispered.</p>
<p>She said it with a fragile, devastating quiet, the sound of a woman who had stood by Valentina’s side through decades of war and loss, only to realize that the person she thought she knew was a stranger wrapped in a familiar skin.</p>
<p>The realization seemed to physically ache in the air, as if the very history of their friendship were being rewritten in real time.</p>
<p>"I have a half sister," Valentina corrected, her voice hardening, a defensive armor snapping into place to protect the wound. "A woman whom I have never met."</p>
<p>"A woman whose existence was a ghost in the machine until the intelligence threads began to converge into a pattern that the existing data could not reconcile."</p>
<p>"The math didn’t work unless she was the missing variable."</p>
<p>She looked down at the obsidian table, her reflection staring back at her: a leader, a commander, a legend. But beneath the surface, she saw only a daughter lost in the dark.</p>
<p>"When the pattern finally became undeniable," Valentina said, her voice dropping to a jagged, pained murmur, "when the picture was finally complete, I did what any person would do."</p>
<p>"I reached out... I sent a communication to the private address the intelligence indicated she used."</p>
<p>"I waited for a sign. A word. A single breath of acknowledgement."</p>
<p>She gripped the edge of the table, her knuckles turning a ghostly, bloodless white.</p>
<p>"I have sent three communications over the course of eleven years," she said, and the sheer, agonizing loneliness of that statement seemed to vibrate through the floorboards. "And in eleven years... I have received nothing."</p>
<p>"No response. No rejection. Just a silence as vast and as cold as the one sitting in this room right now."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was no longer a void; it was a pressurized chamber, thick with the scent of ozone and the unspoken grief of a decade of secrets. The air felt viscous, as if the very atmosphere were struggling to contain the emotional fallout of Valentina’s confession.</p>
<p>"You were trying to reach her," Apollo said.</p>
<p>His voice wasn’t an accusation; it was a quiet observation, a steady hand thrown into a churning sea.</p>
