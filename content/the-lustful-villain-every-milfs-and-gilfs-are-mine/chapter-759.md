<p>Elizabeth had been positioned in the analytical shadow of the engagement since the moment Lily’s barrier first flared to life. She wasn’t fighting with muscle or instinct; she was fighting with a cold, systematic taxonomy of magical architecture.</p>
<p>While the others felt the heat of the battle, Elizabeth was reading the dragon’s energy as if it were a complex, decaying equation.</p>
<p>She moved with a rhythmic, unhurried grace through the narrow gap in the root barrier, a passage Nerith had held open with agonizing precision, a prenegotiated corridor carved out of living wood just for this moment.</p>
<p>"The bond link is running on a maintenance frequency," Elizabeth announced, her voice cutting through the heavy, ozone-scented air like a scalpel. "The rider has lost active command."</p>
<p>"The dragon is no longer being steered by a tactical mind; it is currently operating on pure, unadulterated base instinct."</p>
<p>"Is that better or worse?" Mireya yelled from the left, her boots skidding on the blood-slicked ice as she fought to keep her blades buried deep in the dragon’s frozen shoulder.</p>
<p>CRUNCH!</p>
<p>The ice groaned as the dragon’s massive weight shifted.</p>
<p>"For us... better," Elizabeth replied, her eyes glowing with the faint, blue light of her analytical trance. "A tactically directed dragon would have seen our coordination and broken the rhythm by now."</p>
<p>"This beast is just reacting to pain and stimuli... It’s a cornered animal, not a soldier."</p>
<p>"But don’t be fooled; the instinct of a wounded, mature dragon is territorial aggression!"</p>
<p>"It’s going to stop fighting the roots and start trying to obliterate them in approximately forty seconds."</p>
<p>"Which is exactly when the root hold degrades!" Lily shouted, her hands trembling as the golden light of her barrier began to sputter and hiss like a dying flame.</p>
<p>FZZT!</p>
<p>"Yes," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>Her tone wasn’t urgent; it was devastatingly calm, the flat, terrifyingly precise delivery of a scholar who had already seen the end of the experiment. "I know."</p>
<p>"What is the actual working, Elizabeth?" Diana demanded. She was a statue of tension, a fresh arrow nocked and drawn, her eyes darting between the dragon’s wounded left side and its still powerful right shoulder.</p>
<p>"The bond link is a standing wave," Elizabeth explained, stepping into the precise focal point of her prepared spell.</p>
<p>The air around her began to hum with a low, vibrating frequency that made the teeth of everyone nearby ache. "Every dragon rider bond relies on a frequency pair."</p>
<p>"The rider sends a signal; the dragon responds..."</p>
<p>"Without the rider’s active input, the dragon’s response frequency drifts into a baseline maintenance pulse, a ’heartbeat’ that keeps the bond from snapping."</p>
<p>"I have been dissecting that pulse for the last two minutes."</p>
<p>"Can you shatter it?" Nerith asked, her voice strained as she poured her mana into the secondary root network to prevent a total collapse.</p>
<p>CREAK... SNAP!</p>
<p>A root the size of a tree trunk splintered under the dragon’s lashing tail.</p>
<p>"I won’t shatter the bond; that would be too much energy, but I will generate a counter frequency," Elizabeth said, her hands beginning to weave intricate, glowing geometric patterns in the air.</p>
<p>VROOOOOOM!</p>
<p>The hum grew louder, more aggressive. "I will create destructive interference."</p>
<p>"A magical ’noise’ that cancels out the maintenance pulse..."</p>
<p>"For a moment, the dragon will be a god without a tether. It will lose its behavioral anchor."</p>
<p>"How much confusion are we talking about?" Mireya asked, her teeth gritted as she felt the dragon’s heat trying to melt her ice lock.</p>
<p>"Ninety seconds of total disorientation," Elizabeth said. "After that, the dragon’s biological system will compensate, and its instinct will reassert itself with even more ferocity."</p>
<p>"But ninety seconds is our window to build a containment structure that doesn’t rely on the bond."</p>
<p>"Nerith!" Lily called out, her eyes fixed on the crumbling barrier. "If the dragon stops thrashing, can the roots hold a passive containment for ninety seconds?"</p>
<p>"If it stays still, yes!" Nerith grunted, sweat pouring down her face. "A stationary mass is a different kind of load."</p>
<p>"If it doesn’t fight the roots, the roots can hold the weight!"</p>
<p>"Then we have a plan," Lily declared, her voice hardening with resolve. "Root hold is nine seconds."</p>
<p>"Elizabeth disrupts the bond. We use those ninety seconds of confusion to lock it down for good!"</p>
<p>"The ice on the left wing will hold," Mireya added, her voice a low growl. "The joint is frozen solid."</p>
<p>"As long as the temperature stays low, that wing isn’t moving."</p>
<p>"The right wing is the nightmare," Diana countered, her bowstring taut enough to snap.</p>
<p>She was staring at the dragon’s dominant side, the side that was still pulsing with terrifying, rhythmic strength. "If that confusion causes even one massive, panicked lunge, the right wing will be the first thing to move!"</p>
<p>"It’s the strongest limb... The first instinct of a disoriented beast is to strike with its most powerful weapon."</p>
<p>"How big is the movement, Diana?" Lily asked, the tension in the air becoming almost suffocating.</p>
<p>"Big enough to shatter the root barrier," Diana warned, her eyes narrowing. "And big enough to reach us here on the meteor."</p>
<p>Lily’s mind raced, her thoughts a frantic calculation of energy reserves, kinetic force, and the terrifying variables of a disoriented apex predator. The air was thick with the smell of scorched earth, ozone, and the metallic, iron-heavy scent of dragon blood.</p>
<p>"I will hold the barrier through the confusion window!" Lily commanded, her voice cracking slightly from the sheer mental strain of maintaining the light affinity shield.</p>
<p>FZZT... CRACKLE!</p>
<p>The golden veil before her shuddered, a visible ripple of instability passing through the light. "The reserve is dangerously low, but if I drop the density to a single-layer shell, it’s manageable..."</p>
<p>"If that right wing lunges toward the meteor, the barrier will act as a kinetic redirector... We’ll slide the impact away rather than absorbing it!"</p>
<p>Diana’s eyes flickered toward Lily, her expression grim. "A redirect works against a targeted strike, Lily!"</p>
<p>"A lunge from a disoriented beast isn’t a calculated blow; it’s a chaotic, uncoordinated thrash!"</p>
<p>"The force distribution will be erratic... It could shatter your shell like an eggshell if the angle is wrong!"</p>
<p>"I know the risks!" Lily snapped back, her eyes flashing with a desperate, fierce light. "But it’s the only way we survive the ninety seconds!"</p>
<p>"I’m going to try it!"</p>
<p>The silence that followed lasted only a heartbeat, but it felt like an eternity of suspended animation. Diana stared at her, measuring the resolve in Lily’s eyes against the dwindling golden glow of the shield.</p>
<p>Finally, Diana gave a sharp, singular nod.</p>
<p>"Alright," Diana said, her voice dropping into a lethal, low register as she turned her gaze back to the dragon’s right shoulder.</p>
<p>TWANG!</p>
<p>She pulled her string back even further, the wood of the bow groaning under the tension. "But if it breaks, don’t expect me to catch you."</p>
<p>"Mireya!" Lily called out, her eyes scanning the carnage. "When Elizabeth’s frequency hits, you move!"</p>
<p>"Get off the left side immediately!"</p>
<p>"The confusion state won’t distinguish between the roots and you; it’s going to lunge at anything that feels like a threat!"</p>
<p>"I don’t want you at contact range when the orientation loss hits!"</p>
<p>Mireya, still standing amidst the jagged, blood-stained ice, tightened her grip on her blades.</p>
<p>SKREEEE!</p>
<p>The ice groaned as she braced herself.</p>
<p>"I can maintain the freeze from three meters!" she shouted over the dragon’s heavy, labored breathing. "I don’t need to be touching the flesh to lock the joint!"</p>
<p>"Three meters then!" Lily ordered. "Stay back! Do not let that beast catch you in the lunge!"</p>
<p>"Three meters! Got it!" Mireya yelled, already shifting her weight, preparing to leap back as the magical storm approached.</p>
<p>"Nerith!" Lily’s voice was a whip, lashing through the chaos. "As soon as that bond disruption registers, you start building the containment structure!"</p>
<p>"Do not wait for the dragon to start acting crazy!"</p>
<p>"The window starts the microsecond Elizabeth’s working lands, not when the beast starts screaming! Move on the signal!"</p>
<p>"Understood!" Nerith grunted, her hands buried in the dirt, her mana pulsing through the earth in rhythmic, heavy thumps.</p>
<p>THOOM... THOOM...</p>
<p>The ground itself seemed to be breathing with her.</p>
<p>"Elizabeth!" Lily turned her focus to the scholar.</p>
<p>"When you give the word, Lily..." Elizabeth replied, her voice a hauntingly calm anchor in the storm.</p>
<p>The air around her was now screaming with a high-pitched, dissonant hum, the geometric patterns of her spell glowing with a blinding, predatory intensity.</p>
<p>VREEEEEEEEE!</p>
<p>Lily braced herself. She felt the roots beneath her feet beginning to fray, the tension in the air reaching a breaking point.</p>
<p>She watched the dragon, a mountain of scales, blood, and fury, and felt the countdown in her very marrow.</p>
<p>The numbers were aligned.</p>
<p>The variables were set.</p>
<p>The trap was about to spring.</p>
<p>"NOW!" Lily roared.</p>
<p>Elizabeth didn’t throw a fireball or a bolt of lightning; she threw a mathematical catastrophe.</p>
<p>With a sharp, violent motion of her hands, she launched the invisible working directly into the heart of the dragon’s bond link. To an observer, it looked like she was throwing nothing at all.</p>
<p>There was no flash of light, no roar of energy, only a sudden, sickening ripple in the air, like heat rising off a sun-scorched road.</p>
<p>VREEEEEEEEE THUM!</p>
<p>The disruption hit the maintenance frequency with the force of a silent hammer. The change in the dragon was more terrifying than its rage.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a reaction to pain; it was a total, fundamental erasure of self. The purposeful, predatory intelligence that resided in the beast’s eyes, the terrifying awareness of a creature that knew its place in the world, simply vanished.</p>
<p>In two seconds, the dragon went from a god of the sky to a hollow vessel.</p>
