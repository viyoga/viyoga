<p>Rex stood over her, the predator admiring the kill, though the kill refused to die. He raised a hand to his face, wiping a thick smear of crimson from his chin with the back of his knuckles.</p>

<p>A dark, satisfied smile tugged at his lips.</p>

<p>He had dismantled a god.</p>

<p>He had broken the lightning.</p>

<p>He bent his head low, leaning into her space, wanting to see the moment the last spark of defiance finally flickered out.</p>

<p>Then, his smile faltered. His brows drew together.</p>

<p>Something had shifted. The air around them didn’t just grow cold; it grew heavy, as if the atmosphere itself had suddenly gained mass.</p>

<p>Rex felt it before his eyes could process it. Using the same supernatural sensitivity he employed to read the intricate registers of human souls, he felt a tremor.</p>

<p>It wasn’t a physical vibration; it was a psychic pulse deep beneath the surface of Evelyn’s soul register, buried beneath layers of system code, beneath the fundamental laws of Aethelgard, and beneath the ancient Veil of Anonymity that had kept her existence a secret from every scanner in the realm.</p>

<p>The veil, that impenetrable shroud of divine silence, hadn’t just been pierced. It had shivered.</p>

<p>And beneath that shiver, something ancient, something primal, something that had been slumbering in the abyss of her subconscious for eons, had stirred.</p>

<p>THUMP.</p>

<p>It beat once. A single, thunderous pulse that felt less like a heartbeat and more like the striking of a war drum against the fabric of reality.</p>

<p>The third stage of the Tempest Vanguard System had begun to call out. It wasn’t a request; it was a summoning.</p>

<p>Rex’s expression transformed. The satisfaction of the victor died, replaced by something far more dangerous: the hunger of a scholar who had just found a forbidden text.</p>

<p>His smile returned, but it was different now—slow, wide, and wholly delighted. This was the true objective.</p>

<p>This was the prize he had been hunting since the moment he decided to burn this world down.</p>

<p>"There you are," he whispered, his voice thick with anticipation. "Show me what you really are."</p>

<p>Evelyn’s eyes closed slowly. There was no dramatic gasp, no final surge of power.</p>

<p>She simply looked... tired. Exhausted by the sheer burden of existing.</p>

<p>One by one, the last blue sparks died on her fingertips. The crackling energy that had defined her vanished, leaving behind only the smell of burnt ozone and ionized air.</p>

<p>The entire plaza seemed to exhale, a collective release of tension as she lay motionless among the jagged teeth of broken stone.</p>

<p>Her head was turned to one side, pale against the rubble, and a thin, steady thread of blood leaked from the corner of her mouth, staining the grey dust crimson.</p>

<p>Rex was no longer smiling.</p>

<p>His entire body went rigid as Divine Perception flooded his consciousness. His mind laid out a vision that defied every law of magic he understood.</p>

<p>The Veil of Anonymity wasn’t being torn down by an external force; it was cracking from the inside.</p>

<p>A web of fine, hairline fractures was spreading across the surface of her soul register like frost spreading across a frozen pond in midwinter.</p>

<p>But there was no light spilling through those cracks.</p>

<p>No holy radiance, no elemental fury.</p>

<p>Instead, something worse emerged. A thin, impossibly bright line of pure, concentrated energy began to bleed through the fractures.</p>

<p>It didn’t spread outward; it shot straight upward, piercing through the ceiling of the sky itself. It was a silver-white thread pulled impossibly taut by a hand he could not see, a lifeline or a noose stretched between her broken body and something waiting in the void above.</p>

<p>The thread began to vibrate. It began to tighten.</p>

<p>Rex felt the hairs on his arms stand up. He felt the raw, terrifying pull of something celestial and hungry tugging at the girl’s corpse.</p>

<p>He knew that whatever was holding that thread was coming to claim what was theirs.</p>

<p>He didn’t wait to see what was on the other end.</p>

<p>Rex didn’t hesitate. He was a pragmatist who understood that some miracles were better left unborn.</p>

<p>He saw the silver thread tightening and saw the fractures spreading, and he knew that if that process was completed, the Evelyn he had been breaking would be gone forever, replaced by something he couldn’t control.</p>

<p>He raised his right hand, fingers curling into a claw. He reached past her skin, past her shattered ribs, and wrapped his Telekinesis around her heart.</p>

<p>He made the grip tight, clean, and utterly deliberate, a psychic stranglehold designed to stop the engine of her soul before the passenger could take control.</p>

<p>"Not today," he growled through gritted teeth. "You don’t get to ascend."</p>

<p>The air above her chest began to warp and ripple as his power condensed, seeking to crush the muscle beneath her ribs into nothingness.</p>

<p>DING.</p>

<p>The sound wasn’t a crash; it was a chime. A perfect, resonant, low-frequency note that sounded like a massive temple bell struck in the heart of a vacuum.</p>

<p>A wall of golden light, impossibly thin as spun glass, erupted from Evelyn’s skin, forming a perfect sphere around her broken form. Rex’s telekinetic assault struck the barrier and simply... bounced.</p>

<p>BOOM.</p>

<p>The recoil was catastrophic. The force of the rejection slammed into Rex like a physical freight train, hurling him backward ten meters across the plaza.</p>

<p>His boots skidded across the broken stone, sending sparks flying as he fought to maintain his balance, finally coming to a halt in a crouch, breathing hard.</p>

<p>Far across the square, the atmosphere changed. Cassandra straightened instantly, her hand flying to the hilt of her sword with the practiced reflex of a soldier sensing an existential threat.</p>

<p>Lilith stopped breathing entirely, her eyes fixed on the epicenter. All over the city, the mundane world froze.</p>

<p>People who had been crouching in doorways, terrified by the violence, now raised their heads in unison.</p>

<p>A column of white gold light had descended from the heavens. It hadn’t fallen like a meteor; it had settled with impossible grace, a silent, unmoving pillar of divine radiance that pierced through the clouds and anchored itself in the center of the plaza.</p>

<p>It looked less like an attack and more like a pillar holding up the sky itself.</p>

<p>...</p>

<p>Evelyn opened her eyes.</p>

<p>The pain the screaming agony of broken bones, charred flesh, and punctured lungs was gone. The sensation of being crushed and burned had vanished as if it had never existed.</p>

<p>She was no longer in the plaza. She was in a room that possessed no walls, no corners, and no boundaries.</p>

<p>The floor beneath her was made of white marble, polished to such a mirror-like sheen that it felt as smooth and level as the surface of still water.</p>

<p>It stretched away from her in every direction, endlessly perfect, until it dissolved into a soft, pearlescent haze.</p>

<p>There was no ceiling to speak of, only an infinite expanse of pale light.</p>

<p>Suspended above her head hung a great golden scale. It defied every law of physics, floating freely in the open air without chains or supports.</p>

<p>One pan was heaped with a warm, pulsating light that felt like summer noon; the other was filled with a patient, velvety darkness that felt like the space between stars.</p>

<p>Evelyn tried to move, instinctively reaching for her Swift Step to test her positioning.</p>

<p>But her body did not so much as twitch. Her supernatural reflexes, her lightning, her very essence—they were gone.</p>

<p>She looked down at herself. Her torn, blood-soaked clothes were whole again.</p>

<p>Her skin was unblemished. Her ribs, which she had felt splintering moments ago, felt solid and untouched.</p>

<p>"Where..." she started to ask, but her voice felt strange in this place.</p>

<p>"You are where you are meant to be."</p>

<p>A woman stood before the scale.</p>

<p>She was tall, her posture impossibly straight, wrapped in a plain robe of grey and white that lacked any embroidery or ornament. A simple band of white cloth covered her eyes, yet Evelyn was struck by a sudden, terrifying certainty: the woman was looking at her.</p>

<p>She wasn’t just looking at her body; she was looking through her, past her flesh, past her memories, seeing her entire existence laid bare like a clerk examining a ledger.</p>

<p>The woman held no weapon. She had no need for one.</p>

<p>When she spoke, her voice was level and quiet, devoid of warmth, yet equally free of cruelty.</p>

<p>It was the voice of inevitability.</p>
