<p>Elizabeth’s posture changed slightly, indicating a subtle shift that Rex had learned to recognize as the specific adjustment she made when a topic emerged that she had been approaching but would have preferred to discuss on her own terms.</p>
<p>"She’s going to want the full intelligence review," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>"The document, the relay structure, the name Solmordia, all of them," she said, setting the document on the table. "And when she gets to the section about the Balance Keeper, she’s going to ask the obvious question."</p>
<p>"She will ask whether we have a name," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Yes," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>"And she will also inquire about the circumstances under which the name was shared," she said carefully. "Kregg gave it at the end of a sustained interrogation."</p>
<p>"If she asks how far that interrogation went, and she will ask, I have to either answer accurately or construct a version that holds up under scrutiny." She paused. "I’m not built for the second one."</p>
<p>"I can decline to answer, but I can’t give her a version that isn’t true."</p>
<p>"Then decline," Rex said. "Tell her the full methodology is in a separate handling file pending sensitivity review."</p>
<p>"That buys time," Elizabeth said. "It doesn’t solve the problem."</p>
<p>"And what are you going to tell her?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"That’s what I’m trying to work out," Elizabeth said. "If I tell her we have a name, she will want it immediately, along with details about the source, the circumstances, and the reliability of the source; however, the answers to all three questions lead back to the canyon in a way that I am unsure how to navigate."</p>
<p>"Leave the name with me for now," he said.</p>
<p>"What...?" Elizabeth looked at him. "Please explain what you mean by that."</p>
<p>"There’s a reason the name matters beyond identifying the Balance Keeper," Rex said. "There’s a family connection to this city and to people in this institution, and I need to understand the shape of that connection before it goes into a formal intelligence review."</p>
<p>"If it goes into the review without that context, the consequences are unpredictable in a way that I don’t want to be unpredictable."</p>
<p>Elizabeth studied him with the evaluating attention she used for things she was categorizing. "You’re telling me you know something about the name that I don’t."</p>
<p>"I’m telling you I know something about the name that requires careful handling," Rex said. "The careful handling is something I can do more effectively than the formal review process can at this stage."</p>
<p>"Because the formal review process would trigger responses before the context is established," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>She was quiet for a moment, working through this with the visible thoroughness that was her natural mode for decisions she would have to live with.</p>
<p>"And if the family connection doesn’t clarify things?" Elizabeth said. "If what you find is worse than what the review would have produced on its own?"</p>
<p>"Then I handle it," Rex said. "That’s still better than if the review were to handle it without understanding the situation."</p>
<p>"You keep saying that," Elizabeth said. "Handle it, as if that’s a complete answer and not just a way of describing what you intend to do without committing to how."</p>
<p>Rex considered this.</p>
<p>"About half the time the shape of something turns out to be what I expected," he said. "The other half is usually worse."</p>
<p>"But by that point, I’ve had enough time to understand what ’worse’ actually means, which makes it more manageable than it would have been if I’d handed it over immediately without context."</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at him for a moment with the expression of someone deciding whether to push further on a point she already knows she can’t move.</p>
<p>"You’ve sat on things before," she said. "Significant things."</p>
<p>"I have," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And the people who trusted you with those things," she said. "Did they feel, afterward, that you’d made the right call?"</p>
<p>"Some of them," Rex said. "They were the ones who were still around to have an opinion."</p>
<p>It was a frank answer, and Elizabeth received it as one. She didn’t look away from him.</p>
<p>"You’re asking me to withhold potentially significant intelligence from the senior review," she said.</p>
<p>"I’m asking you to let me handle the most volatile element of it in a way that reduces the risk of unintended consequences," Rex said. "The rest of the intelligence is complete and ready for review."</p>
<p>"You can present everything except the name and the family connection, and that’s still substantially more than the network had yesterday."</p>
<p>Elizabeth pressed her lips together briefly. "If this goes wrong—"</p>
<p>"Then the version of this that goes wrong is a version I’ve managed directly rather than one that the review process triggered without full context," Rex said. "Which is the better version of it going wrong."</p>
<p>She looked at him for a long moment. Then she exhaled once, slowly; this sound was neither an agreement nor a refusal, but rather the specific indication of someone accepting a constraint they do not fully endorse because the alternative is worse.</p>
<p>"For now," she said. "But not indefinitely."</p>
<p>"Understood," Rex said.</p>
<p>She looked at the document again. "Alexander doesn’t know any of this."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said.</p>
<p>"He thinks the canyon account is what it appears to be."</p>
<p>"Yes."</p>
<p>She closed the document and put it in her robe’s pocket.</p>
<p>"I need you to understand something," she said. "What I’m doing this morning, and what I agreed to last night, doesn’t change what I know."</p>
<p>"I know what I know, and I’m making a series of pragmatic decisions because the situations you’ve put in front of me don’t have better options available." She looked at him directly. "But I’m not confused about what the situations are or how they were constructed."</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I want you to know I do," she said.</p>
<p>"I got that from the first time you said it," Rex said, "and the second time."</p>
<p>Something shifted in her expression that was very nearly amusement and then settled back into the composure she maintained.</p>
<p>"One more thing," she said.</p>
<p>"Alexander," she said. "He’s going to ask questions about what we’re doing with the intelligence review."</p>
<p>"Where I’m going and who I’m meeting with."</p>
<p>"Tell him what you need to tell him," Rex said. "He trusts you to manage the professional details."</p>
<p>"He does," she said. "He also loves me, which means he notices things he doesn’t ask about directly."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing to this.</p>
<p>"He’s been managing the weight of the key," Elizabeth said. "Every time I suggest he rest, he finds another reason why now isn’t the right moment."</p>
<p>"He thinks if he stops moving, he’ll have to sit with what the Key showed him."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing, which was the appropriate response.</p>
<p>"I’m not using that as a lever," Elizabeth said. "I want to be clear about that."</p>
<p>"I’m describing it because it’s relevant to how he’ll receive the explanation. There’s a difference."</p>
<p>"There is," Rex said. "You don’t have to explain the difference to me."</p>
<p>"I’m going to tell him I’m working with you on the recovery analysis," she said. "That’s accurate enough to hold."</p>
<p>"It is," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And I’m going to tell him that while we’re working on it, I’m staying at the Starlight household rather than our place," she said. "Because the review requires proximity to the academy’s resources and my family’s records."</p>
<p>She paused.</p>
<p>"He’s not going to like it," she said.</p>
<p>"No," Rex said. "But he’s also going to accept it, because the explanation is professional and because he’s still managing the weight of the key."</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at the window for a moment. "He is," she said.</p>
<p>"He’ll accept almost anything right now that frames itself as a reasonable professional requirement, because accepting it feels like contributing to a solution he can’t fix directly." She paused. "I know how he thinks."</p>
<p>"Then use it," Rex said, not unkindly.</p>
<p>She looked at him with the expression of someone who has heard a piece of advice that is accurate and uncomfortable in equal measure. Then she stood up, and Rex stood up, and they got ready for the morning in the practical, unsentimental way of two people who have decided what the day is and are moving toward it.</p>
<p>Rex was pulling on his shirt when Elizabeth said, from across the room, "I need to ask you about something."</p>
<p>"About tonight?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"N-no!"</p>
<p>"I’m listening."</p>
<p>She had her jacket on to cover her robe, and her hands were at the buttons, and she did not look up from the buttons when she said it. "Marceline and Mara."</p>
<p>"What about them?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"Are they pregnant..." Elizabeth said, "...because of you?"</p>
<p>It was not phrased as a question. Rex noticed this and also recognized that she already knew the answer, indicating that the kitchen conversation had explored much more than she had suggested when she said "long enough" and ended the topic.</p>
<p>"How stupid are they to tell you that they’re pregnant," Rex said. "But I guess that’s reasonable... knowing Marceline is friends with your bitchy sister."</p>
<p>"Yes... I fucked them so hard that they ended up pregnant," Rex laughed.</p>
<p>Elizabeth finished buttoning her jacket and smoothed the front with both hands. She looked at him with an expression that conveyed her strong commitment to concealing her thoughts, which was a very distinct kind of demeanor.</p>
<p>"I can’t believe it," she said. "Like... both of them at the same time?"</p>
<p>Elizabeth continued. "Simultaneously."</p>
<p>"Nah... the timing was not coordinated," Rex said. "If that’s what you’re asking."</p>
<p>"I am not asking about the timing," Elizabeth said. "I’m asking about the circumstances!"</p>
<p>"You do know that they have husbands, Rex."</p>
<p>"They do," Rex said. "And what’s wrong with that, huh?"</p>
<p>"Their husbands are not you."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said. "They’re not, detective."</p>
<p>"And this," Elizabeth said, "did not present itself to you as a problem at any point."</p>
