<p>The captain had no answer. The question hung in the air like a guillotine blade suspended by a single fraying thread.</p>
<p>Around him, the silence was absolutely not a supportive silence but a hollow, shameful one. No lieutenant stepped forward to bolster his courage; no sergeant offered a tactical counterargument.</p>
<p>The authority he had spent decades cultivating evaporated in an instant, leaving behind only a frightened man in expensive armor.</p>
<p>After a long, humiliating silence that seemed to stretch into eternity, the captain lowered his head, his shoulders slumping, and retreated into the ranks.</p>
<p>He did not speak again. He simply became another frightened ghost in a corridor full of them.</p>
<p>Standing on the periphery of Valentina’s crumbling forces, Elizabeth Von Starlight watched the carnage unfold with an expression carefully sculpted into one of shock and visceral horror.</p>
<p>To any observer, she was another victim of the moment’s terror. But beneath the mask, her mind was a storm of cold calculation and nauseating guilt.</p>
<p>She was the only person in that entire corridor who knew with absolute, terrifying certainty exactly whose voice was speaking and exactly how every syllable of this psychological slaughter had been rehearsed in the dark.</p>
<p>She had known for weeks in the abstract exactly what Rex was capable of. She had seen the blueprints of his ambition, understood the terrifying scope of his power, and grasped exactly how far he intended to push this war until Aethelgard snapped like dry kindling.</p>
<p>But knowing it in theory was nothing like watching it performed. Watching him dismantle the city’s faith in front of ten thousand souls who trusted her, who had marched into this trap believing her own Key had opened a path to justice, felt like swallowing molten lead.</p>
<p>The betrayal sat like a stone in her chest, crushing her lungs.</p>
<p>Her hand moved instinctively to the hilt of a dagger at her belt. Her knuckles turned white as she gripped the weapon, but there was no plan behind the gesture.</p>
<p>No strike to deliver, no assassination to attempt. It was merely the reflexive spasm of a body that demanded action even when the mind attached to it understood that resistance was not just futile; it was suicidal.</p>
<p>’God help us,’ she thought, her inner voice a faint whisper against the roaring terror of the crowd. ’He isn’t just killing our heroes... He’s killing our reality.’</p>
<p>Xerollion sensed the shift in the air. He could feel the last vestiges of hope bleeding out of the Aethelgard formation.</p>
<p>He turned his attention back to the masses, his presence expanding until he seemed to fill every inch of the stone corridor. When he spoke again, his voice dropped into a cold, quiet register—a register so intimate and controlled that it somehow carried more menace than any scream.</p>
<p>It reached the furthest edges of the corridor like a poisonous vapor.</p>
<p>"Aethelgard will never win against the Underlayer," Xerollion said, each word measured, each syllable a nail in a coffin. "Because you never truly understood the nature of the enemy you were fighting."</p>
<p>"You built your entire campaign on the comforting delusion that the worst thing beneath your city was some ancient demon king with delusions of grandeur, a monster you could identify, name, and eventually slay."</p>
<p>He paused, tilting his head as if listening to the heartbeat of the city above them.</p>
<p>"You never considered that something far worse than any demon might simply be wearing a kind face and standing at your dinner table."</p>
<p>"You never considered that the predator wasn’t waiting in the dark but was already walking among you."</p>
<p>The violet energy pulsing from his boots flared brighter, casting jagged, dancing shadows that looked like grasping claws against the walls.</p>
<p>"Today," Xerollion promised, his voice dropping to a deadly, final whisper, "I am going to give you a lesson you will never forget."</p>
<p>"I am going to show you what happens when your world finally meets its true master."</p>
<p>Xerollion shifted his attention with the slow, deliberate grace of an executioner. His free hand rose, fingers splayed like claws, and positioned itself inches from Apollo’s exposed, blood-slicked throat.</p>
<p>Almost immediately, the air around his fingertips began to warp. Violet black energy thick as tar and crackling with malevolent static began to coil and spiral around his digits.</p>
<p>BZZZZT CRACKLE.</p>
<p>The sound was wrong; it wasn’t the clean hum of holy magic but something hungry, something that sounded like teeth grinding against bone.</p>
<p>The entire corridor seemed to contract. Ten thousand lungs drew one single, collective breath and held it, trapped in the throat of a thousand terrified souls.</p>
<p>Every eye in the chamber—soldier, commander, spy, and monster alike—was fixed on that gathering orb of annihilation. The certainty of the coming atrocity was so absolute that the air itself felt pressurized, as if the world were bracing for an impact that would shatter reality.</p>
<p>"NO! STOP IT! STOP!"</p>
<p>Elliot’s scream tore through the suffocating silence. It wasn’t the battle cry of a prince or the roar of a warrior; it was the raw, guttural shriek of a child witnessing his world burn.</p>
<p>He thrashed against the grip of the three soldiers holding him, his boots scraping uselessly against the stone.</p>
<p>"DON’T DO THIS!" he howled, his voice breaking into wet, desperate sobs. "Please, whatever you are, whatever you want, just stop!"</p>
<p>"He hasn’t done anything to deserve this!"</p>
<p>"Take me instead!"</p>
<p>"Kill me, kill all of us, just don’t...! PLEASE—"</p>
<p>His pleas bounced off the stone walls, hollow and pathetic, swallowed whole by the predatory silence that followed.</p>
<p>Valentina Von Starlight took one involuntary step forward, her hand outstretched as if she could somehow bridge the impossible chasm separating her from the center of the carnage. But she stopped mid-motion, her boot freezing on the stone.</p>
<p>The distance—the sheer, yawning gulf of space between her position and the two figures rendered her authority, her experience, and her very existence utterly meaningless.</p>
<p>For fifty years, she had been the immovable object, the strategist who could solve any crisis with preparation and steel.</p>
<p>Now, standing at the edge of this abyss, she understood for the first time in her long life the true, terrifying meaning of powerlessness. She wasn’t a commander anymore; she was merely a spectator to her own extinction.</p>
<p>Xerollion ignored the chaos. He ignored the screams. His focus was singular, honed to the point of lethal perfection.</p>
<p>He looked down at Apollo not with hatred, which would have required effort, but with the detached curiosity of a man observing an insect before crushing it.</p>
<p>"Goodbye, Apollo Brightsoul," Xerollion said.</p>
<p>His voice had dropped to something final, something hollow, utterly devoid of mercy or hesitation. It was the sound of a door closing forever.</p>
<p>"The hero who failed to protect anything that ever mattered to him..."</p>
<p>"The light that burned out before the dawn."</p>
<p>The violet black energy at his fingertips reached its critical mass. It began to pulse with a rhythmic, deadly intensity—THUM THUM... THUM THUM—matching the terrified heartbeat of everyone present.</p>
<p>The light grew so bright it cast harsh, jagged shadows that danced wildly across Apollo’s broken features, making his death throes look like a grotesque puppet show.</p>
<p>The entire corridor held its breath in an absolute, suffocating silence. Time itself seemed to stretch, thinning out until there was nothing left but the glow at Xerollion’s fingertips and the terrifying expectation of the final second, the moment that would decide whether the Apostle of Life would die as a martyr or be erased as a failure.</p>
