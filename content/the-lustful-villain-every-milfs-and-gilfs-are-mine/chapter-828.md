<p>The room’s stillness underwent a terrifying transformation. It was no longer the absence of sound; it was a dense, pressurized vacuum that accumulated weight with every passing second.</p>
<p>It was the heavy, suffocating stillness of a coiled spring, or the moment of absolute silence before a dam bursts, the suspension of all motion, all breath, all life, waiting for the single, violent word that would trigger the carnage.</p>
<p>The combat-adjacent representative, a man whose very muscles seemed to vibrate with the instinct to draw steel, finally broke the tension. His voice was low, gravelly, and stripped of all pretense.</p>
<p>"How long?"</p>
<p>The question wasn’t a request for a timeline; it was a demand for a reckoning.</p>
<p>Valentina turned her gaze toward him. Her eyes were ancient, weary, and shadowed by a darkness that seemed to pull at the light in the room.</p>
<p>"How long have you known?" he pressed.</p>
<p>His voice wasn’t unkind, but it possessed the unrelenting, crushing weight of a man who had seen enough blood to know that half truths were just a slower way to die. He wasn’t going to allow the conversation to drift; he was pinning her to the moment.</p>
<p>"I have known the name for some time," Valentina said, her voice a thin, brittle thread of composure. "The specifics of what the name meant... the true depth of what was attached to it... I was still mapping the connections."</p>
<p>"Still mapping?" the divination representative interjected, her voice sharp and jagged with rising fury. "Mapping for how long, Headmaster? Days? Months?"</p>
<p>"Years," Valentina said.</p>
<p>The word fell like a heavy stone into a deep well. It cost her something visible: a microscopic tremor in her jaw, a sudden, hollow deepening of her eyes.</p>
<p>It was the sound of a woman admitting she had been drowning in a secret for a lifetime.</p>
<p>The room absorbed the confession like a sponge soaking up blood. The weight of it was almost physical, a crushing pressure on the chests of everyone present.</p>
<p>Apollo leaned forward. The movement was slow, deliberate, and heavy with the gravity of a god descending to earth.</p>
<p>When he spoke, the formal, polished veneer of the Apostle of Life had vanished entirely. He wasn’t speaking as a pillar of the network; he was speaking as a man who had felt the pulse of the world and found it fading.</p>
<p>"Valentina," he said, and his voice was a low, resonant ache. "Whatever you know, we need to know it."</p>
<p>"Not because the network requires a report... Not because of protocol or politics... But because Aethelgard is still smoldering in the wake of the Legion’s advance."</p>
<p>"Because there are people in the northern gardens who were buried in shallow graves today."</p>
<p>"If there is a single shred of information that could have prevented that slaughter, if there is a single truth that could have saved those lives... then the people who trusted this network to be their shield deserve to have someone in this room stand up and say so."</p>
<p>Valentina looked at him, and for the first time, the mask didn’t just crack; it began to dissolve.</p>
<p>Her expression was the look of a titan who had been carrying the weight of the sky on her shoulders for decades, only to be told, with terrifying, quiet clarity, that the sky was about to fall and she could no longer hold it up alone.</p>
<p>She opened her mouth, a breath hitching in her throat, but before the truth could spill out, a new voice sliced through the air like a cold blade.</p>
<p>"This network," the voice came from the far end of the table, from a man Rex had cataloged as a knowledge system holder, a man who had arrived in Aethelgard only twelve months ago.</p>
<p>He didn’t have the history of the others, but he had the cold, calculating eyes of a man who dealt in pure, unadulterated logic. "This network has been operating under leadership that may have possessed the very intelligence required to stop the slaughter of forty-three reincarnators."</p>
<p>"And that leadership may have consciously chosen to hoard that intelligence."</p>
<p>"I want to be direct about the implications."</p>
<p>"If this is true, then every decision made, every life lost, and every drop of blood spilled in the last thirty years has been a consequence of a calculated silence."</p>
<p>The accusation was a physical strike. It was a direct assault on the legitimacy of the entire institution.</p>
<p>"You do not have the full picture," Elizabeth interjected sharply, her voice a defensive shield.</p>
<p>"No one has the full picture!" the man shot back, his eyes flashing with a dangerous, intellectual hunger. "That is precisely the problem!"</p>
<p>"We are navigating a storm with a map that has been intentionally drawn with missing pieces!"</p>
<p>"You have been in this network for twelve months," Elizabeth countered.</p>
<p>She didn’t shout; she didn’t need to. Her voice was flat, precise, and freezing the sound of a veteran warrior dismissing a novice’s bravado.</p>
<p>"The person you are speaking of has spent thirty years carving this network out of chaos."</p>
<p>"Building it, defending it, and bleeding for it."</p>
<p>"You are comparing a moment of observation to a lifetime of sacrifice."</p>
<p>"Those are not equivalent positions, and they do not grant you the authority to judge the weight of the secrets she carries."</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder didn’t flinch. He met Elizabeth’s freezing gaze with the unyielding stare of a man who had spent his life dissecting data until the truth was stripped bare and bleeding.</p>
<p>"The tenure of the person in the position," he said, his voice cutting through the tension like a scalpel through skin, "does not determine the legitimacy of holding that position accountable."</p>
<p>"A king who reigns for a century is no less a tyrant if he hides the location of the enemy’s blade."</p>
<p>"Accountability requires evidence!" Elizabeth snapped, her composure finally fraying at the edges.</p>
<p>The air around her seemed to hum with a defensive, electric heat. "You have a surname, a suspicion, and a loud voice."</p>
<p>"That is not the same thing as evidence."</p>
<p>"You are throwing stones at a mountain and claiming you’ve moved it."</p>
<p>"It is the same surname as the founder of the very organization that is currently butchering people like us!" the divination representative shrieked, her voice cracking under the sheer, visceral weight of the injustice. "It is the same blood!"</p>
<p>"The same name that is written in the dirt of the northern gardens!"</p>
<p>"I am acutely aware of what it is," Elizabeth said, her voice dropping to a low, dangerous hiss. She leaned forward, her knuckles white against the obsidian table. "I am making a distinction between what a name is and what a name proves."</p>
<p>"In a court of law, a coincidence is a curiosity..."</p>
<p>"In a war, a coincidence is a death sentence..."</p>
<p>"We must be careful which one we are treating this as."</p>
<p>"Elizabeth."</p>
<p>The name was spoken softly by Morwenna, but it carried the weight of a command. It was the sound of a seasoned general pulling a soldier back from the brink of a foolish charge.</p>
<p>Elizabeth turned her head, her eyes flashing with a momentary, indignant fire, but she fell silent as she met Morwenna’s gaze.</p>
<p>"We are not asking you to prosecute her," Morwenna said.</p>
<p>Her voice had shifted again, adopting the weary, heavy quality of a woman who had sat in a thousand rooms, survived a hundred arguments, and knew exactly when a conversation had ceased to be productive and had begun to turn into a slaughter.</p>
<p>"We are not here to build a gallows, Lady Elizabeth."</p>
<p>"We are here to find the truth!"</p>
<p>"We are asking her to speak!"</p>
<p>The room held its breath. The silence was so absolute that the sound of a heartbeat felt like a drum.</p>
<p>"Let her speak," Aurelia said.</p>
<p>She didn’t look at Valentina. She didn’t look at the chaos of the council.</p>
<p>Instead, her eyes locked onto the knowledge system holder with a flat, terrifyingly direct expression, the look of a predator that had decided the prey had made enough noise and was now ready to be stilled. It was the expression of a woman who had decided that a man had reached the limit of his usefulness.</p>
<p>"Whatever she has to say," Aurelia continued, her voice a low, melodic threat, "she cannot say it while you are occupying the very air she needs to breathe."</p>
<p>"You are crowding the truth, and in doing so, you are choking it."</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder felt the weight of Aurelia’s gaze. It was a heavy, physical pressure, a silent demand for submission.</p>
<p>He looked at her, searching for a crack in her iron resolve, but found only the calm, lethal certainty of a woman who was finished with his interrogation.</p>
<p>He opened his mouth to retort, to offer one more logical syllogism, one more piercing observation... but the words died in his throat.</p>
<p>He stopped talking.</p>
<p>The silence returned, but it was different now. It was no longer a vacuum; it was a stage. All eyes, all breath, and all the crushing weight of the world drifted toward the head of the table, waiting for the first crack in the silence to reveal the ruin beneath.</p>
