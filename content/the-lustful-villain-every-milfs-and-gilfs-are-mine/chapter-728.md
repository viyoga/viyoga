<p>Aurelia was right. Rex was currently working in a very intense and focused way, where subtlety was ignored in favor of straightforward, harsh decisions.</p>
<p>He wasn’t feeling "bloodlust" in the traditional sense; he was executing a sequence of violent equations. He was a machine of geometry and mass, and if they stayed, he might just decide that the "variable" of Iris’s grief needed to be solved as well.</p>
<p>Iris looked at Rex one last time. Her gaze was a searing, silent accusation, a promise of a pain he hadn’t yet calculated.</p>
<p>It was a look of pure, concentrated hatred, wrapped in the velvet of absolute misery.</p>
<p>"Look at what you’ve done," her eyes seemed to scream, even as her lips remained parted in a silent, trembling gasp.</p>
<p>Aurelia didn’t wait for a response. She reached out, her arm hooking firmly around Iris’s shaking shoulders, physically forcing the assassin’s momentum away from the crater of death.</p>
<p>She steered her, a heavy, grounding weight, away from the blood-soaked stones of the plaza and toward the relative safety of the nearest intact street.</p>
<p>Rex watched them go. He didn’t move to follow, nor did he offer a parting word.</p>
<p>He simply stood amidst the ruins of the man he had dismantled, his eyes tracking their retreating forms with the detached interest of a scientist watching a reaction settle. He let them go because Aurelia’s assessment was mathematically sound: the interaction had reached its logical conclusion.</p>
<p>The massacre was over. For now.</p>
<p>Rex turned his back on the retreating shadows of Iris and Aurelia, his gaze shifting with predatory smoothness toward the only threat still standing in his immediate vicinity.</p>
<p>Apollo.</p>
<p>The man was back on his feet, his silhouette trembling against the backdrop of the ruined plaza. The life affinity designation had begun its silent, microscopic work, stitching together the fraying edges of Apollo’s stamina.</p>
<p>It hadn’t restored him to his full, luminous glory, but it had dragged him back from the brink of collapse to a functional, desperate level of combat readiness. He was coming with everything he had left, a lashing, frantic energy born of grief and necessity.</p>
<p>"You didn’t just kill my friends... but you too killed someone close to her," Apollo said.</p>
<p>His voice was low, a serrated whisper that vibrated with a rage so profound it had moved past the stage of shouting and into something much more dangerous: a cold, vibrating stillness.</p>
<p>"Veylor... he wasn’t even fighting you directly..." Apollo gritted his teeth. "He was just... he was just there, and you slaughtered him like a beast in a pen!"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even blink. He stood amidst the gore of Veylor’s remains, the blood of a comrade still wet on his boots.</p>
<p>"He was supporting two people who were fighting me," Rex countered, his voice as flat and unyielding as a tombstone. "In any logical system, that person qualifies as a combatant."</p>
<p>"He was fighting me from a distance." Rex raised both his arms. "The distance is a variable, not an excuse."</p>
<p>"That’s not how the world works!" Apollo roared, his energy flaring in a jagged, golden burst that cracked the stones beneath his feet.</p>
<p>CRACK!</p>
<p>"That’s not how honor works!"</p>
<p>"It is, actually," Rex said, a flicker of genuine, chilling boredom crossing his features. "It’s exactly how it works."</p>
<p>"I don’t understand why everyone is so perpetually surprised by the fundamental nature of conflict," Rex shrugged. "The fighting part was never going to be the gentle part..."</p>
<p>"It was always going to be the part where things break."</p>
<p>"You son of a—" Apollo’s hands began to glow, the light of his life affinity turning a violent, pressurized gold.</p>
<p>"Save your breath," Rex interrupted, his eyes narrowing as his gravitational aura began to hum with a low, terrifying frequency.</p>
<p>VREEEEEEEEE!</p>
<p>"You’re going to need every lungful for what comes next."</p>
<p>Rex lunged. The air screamed as he moved, a blur of concentrated mass.</p>
<p>He met Apollo halfway, and the collision was a deafening explosion of light and pressure.</p>
<p>BOOOOOOM!</p>
<p>The second engagement was a frantic, brutal dance of golden light and crushing darkness, a symphony of clashing energies that tore the very air asunder until the world itself seemed to split open.</p>
<p>SHHHHHHHH LINK!</p>
<p>Valentina arrived. She did not descend from the sky, nor did she run across the dirt.</p>
<p>She manifested through the very fabric of the island’s surface. It was the high-tier projection technique used by the most elite mages, a way to engage the battlefield without the vulnerability of physical transit.</p>
<p>She deployed a spatial separation technique, a masterpiece of impossible physics. In less than two seconds, a yawning, invisible chasm of distorted space slammed between Rex and Apollo, forcing forty meters of "nothingness" to exist between them.</p>
<p>The sudden vacuum caused a violent atmospheric snap.</p>
<p>WHUMP!</p>
<p>Rex skidded to a halt, his boots carving deep grooves into the stone. He stared at the shimmering, distorted void that now separated him from his prey.</p>
<p>He didn’t look frustrated; he looked observant. And then he turned his head, orienting his senses toward the source of the spatial distortion.</p>
<p>Valentina was positioned at the Academy’s main gate. Through the [Foresight], Rex mapped her position and realized she hadn’t sent this as a mere defensive barrier.</p>
<p>She wasn’t a distant operator playing a game of chess; she was a direct opponent, a grandmaster stepping onto the board. The massive mana signature she was weaving at the gate wasn’t a continuation of the spatial separation; it was the opening gambit of a much larger, much more lethal engagement.</p>
<p>Rex began to walk toward her. His stride was heavy and purposeful, each step echoing like a heartbeat in the silent courtyard.</p>
<p>As he moved, the golems scattered across the island fed him a relentless stream of data through the relay network. The tactical picture was grim.</p>
<p>The situation for Aethelgard was catastrophic. The enemy had neutralized, broken, or forcibly redirected sixty percent of the island’s active combat forces.</p>
<p>The golem network’s elevated, systemic output had acted like a tightening noose, creating a pressure that individual warriors simply couldn’t withstand. The outer ring villages were smoldering ruins.</p>
<p>The consolidation sweep was a success, effectively cutting off the reincarnator population from the Apostle network’s protection in four out of the seven populated areas.</p>
<p>The only thing keeping the island from total collapse was the remaining thirty percent of the force; the thirty percent were anchored to Valentina’s telepathic coordination network. She was the spine of the entire defense.</p>
<p>If she broke, the island broke.</p>
<p>Rex looked at Valentina across the shattered, bloodstained courtyard. The distance between them felt heavy, pregnant with the weight of the coming storm.</p>
<p>She looked back at him. Her expression was not one of fear but of terrifying, crystalline clarity.</p>
<p>She was the most powerful mage on the island, a woman who had just witnessed the unthinkable: the fall of Morwenna Nightwing in a mere eight minutes of brutal, lopsided combat. She had processed the implications.</p>
<p>She had seen the math. And she knew exactly what kind of monster was walking toward her.</p>
<p>Rex’s footsteps were slow, rhythmic, and maddeningly casual as he crossed the threshold of the broken courtyard. He walked as if he were strolling through a garden rather than a graveyard of his own making.</p>
<p>A low, dark chuckle bubbled up from his chest, a sound that lacked any warmth; it was the sound of a man who had already seen the end of the world and found it amusing.</p>
<p>"Headmaster," Rex called out, his voice carrying effortlessly across the expanse of shattered stone.</p>
<p>He tilted his head back, a cocky, lopsided grin spreading across his face. "I was hoping we’d get to this eventually."</p>
<p>"Though, I’ll admit, I expected the conversation to go a little differently than this." He gestured vaguely at the smoking ruins and the blood-slicked ground around them.</p>
<p>Valentina stood like a statue of ancient marble, her energy radiating from her in visible, shimmering waves of violet light. Her eyes were cold, but there was a flicker of something, exhaustion perhaps, or a deep, simmering fury behind them.</p>
<p>"Did you?" she countered, her voice a sharp, melodic blade. "And what, exactly, did you expect, Tremor?"</p>
<p>Rex laughed again, a sharp, barking sound that seemed to mock the very gravity of the situation. "HA!"</p>
<p>He wiped a stray droplet of Veylor’s blood from his cheek, looking at it with detached curiosity before flicking it away. "Honestly? I expected to be having a much quieter discussion with you in your office sometime next month."</p>
<p>"Maybe over a glass of something expensive," Rex laughed. "We were talking about a name, remember? A legacy."</p>
<p>He paused, his grin widening into something more predatory. "I’d hoped that conversation would carry more weight than it apparently does right now."</p>
<p>"But then again, you always were a bit too fond of the ’heroic’ struggle, weren’t you?"</p>
<p>For a fraction of a second, something flickered across Valentina’s face, a momentary crack in her impenetrable mask. A shadow of doubt, or perhaps a flash of old respect.</p>
<p>Rex’s [Foresight] caught it instantly, and his grin turned triumphant.</p>
<p>"It does matter," she said, her voice regaining its ironclad composure. "The past matters."</p>
<p>"But that doesn’t change what I am going to do to you in the next five minutes."</p>
<p>"Fair enough!" Rex conceded, though his tone suggested he found her resolve adorable.</p>
<p>He spread his arms wide, his silhouette framed by the chaos of the burning island. "I love the passion! Truly!"</p>
