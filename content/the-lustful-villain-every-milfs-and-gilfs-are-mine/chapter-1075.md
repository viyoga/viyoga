<p>Rex stared through Kaeleth, his eyes not seeing a god but seeing a data stream, a sequence of failures unfolding in real time.</p>
<p>The tension in the sanctum was so thick it felt like breathing liquid mercury. Every ragged breath Kaeleth drew sounded like gravel grinding in a wound, a wet, choking noise that punctuated the terrible silence.</p>
<p>"You made me faster every time you hit me," Rex said, his voice disturbingly calm, almost conversational. "That’s the beauty of it, isn’t it?"</p>
<p>"The Adaptation’s analysis speed isn’t just a passive shield; it’s a learning function."</p>
<p>"It’s a predator that grows stronger with every wound it receives..."</p>
<p>"You weren’t fighting me, Kaeleth... You were training me."</p>
<p>Kaeleth remained silent. His golden blood continued to drip from his chin, spattering against the floor with a rhythmic tap... tap... tap that sounded like a countdown.</p>
<p>His divine composure was gone, replaced by the hollow stare of a man who had just realized he was trapped in a cage of his own making.</p>
<p>"How many times did you hit me?" Rex asked.</p>
<p>He tilted his head, studying the god with clinical detachment.</p>
<p>Kaeleth said nothing. His lips merely trembled, blood frothing at the corners of his mouth.</p>
<p>"Seventeen," Rex stated, each syllable landing like a hammer blow. "I received seventeen distinct first contact mechanisms over the course of this engagement."</p>
<p>"Seventeen separate analysis windows where I had to parse your reality."</p>
<p>"Seventeen coverage extensions where I had to remap my own existence to survive you."</p>
<p>Rex took a step forward. The floor beneath his boot groaned as if protesting his approach.</p>
<p>"And at the end of that seventeenth strike, that last pathetic attempt to fracture my soul, the Adaptation’s analysis speed for temporal class workings was running at approximately forty percent of the initial baseline."</p>
<p>"I’m not learning in increments anymore, Kaeleth..."</p>
<p>"I’m learning in leaps... I’m learning faster than you can think."</p>
<p>Rex began walking toward him. It wasn’t a charge; it was a slow, inexorable march.’</p>
<p>The kind of walk a butcher takes toward a stunned animal.</p>
<p>"You ran out of new things, didn’t you?" Rex’s voice grew darker, more predatory. "The temporal domain’s working set is finite."</p>
<p>"You have a god’s arsenal... you have countless expressions of temporal authority."</p>
<p>"You can slow time, you can loop it, you can fracture it into shards. But they are all just different faces on the same coin."</p>
<p>"They all share one fundamental mechanism."</p>
<p>Rex was ten meters away now. He could see the way Kaeleth’s divine armor was hairline fractured, the way the golden light flickered like a dying candle in a hurricane.</p>
<p>"Once the Adaptation has covered that fundamental mechanism through enough implementations, the coverage extends to the general case," Rex explained, his inner monologue a cold stream of tactical certainty. ’He’s cornered.’</p>
<p>He’s a king with no kingdom left, a scholar with no words left.’</p>
<p>’Now, I break what’s left.’</p>
<p>"It doesn’t matter which flavor of time you throw at me... I’ve already learned the recipe."</p>
<p>The distance closed.</p>
<p>Nine meters.</p>
<p>Eight.</p>
<p>"You cannot produce a new mechanism," Rex said, his voice dropping to a guttural whisper that seemed to vibrate in Kaeleth’s very bones. "You’ve been practicing the same divine arts for two thousand years."</p>
<p>"You’re a master of your domain, yes. But mastery isn’t invention."</p>
<p>"You cannot invent a new class of working on demand, because working classes aren’t things you do."</p>
<p>"They are properties of the domain itself. And your domain? Your domain is exhausted."</p>
<p>Rex stopped five meters away. He stood there, a mountain of scarred muscle and murderous intent, looming over the broken deity.</p>
<p>The air between them crackled with the static of Rex’s unleashed power, a physical pressure that made the very air scream.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes locked onto Kaeleth’s, no mercy, no hesitation, only the cold satisfaction of a predator who has already won.</p>
<p>"You’re out of options," Rex said.</p>
<p>Kaeleth raised his gaze.</p>
<p>There was something profoundly wrong with the way he looked at Rex. When a mortal runs out of options, they collapse into a predictable biological cascade: sweat, dilated pupils, the frantic heaving of lungs, and the primal stench of terror.</p>
<p>But a god? A god’s desperation was something far more alien.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s defeat didn’t manifest as fear; it manifested as a catastrophic shift in the ambient reality around him. The very air grew cold and brittle, the light turning sickly and sharp.</p>
<p>It was the expression of a being who had been the absolute center of all mathematical certainty for eons, suddenly realizing he was nothing more than an error in someone else’s equation.</p>
<p>Desperation flickered in his eyes, and Kaeleth reached for a working. His fingers twitched, beginning to weave the first threads of a temporal severance, a spell that would have unmade Rex’s past to erase his present.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t give him the chance.</p>
<p>"Too slow," Rex growled.</p>
<p>Rex slammed his fist into the air, unleashing Gravity Manipulation at maximum local concentration. He didn’t just increase the weight; he created a localized singularity directly in front of Kaeleth’s hands.</p>
<p>The spell was interrupted mid-formation. In the divine calculus, an interrupted working is worse than a failed one; it becomes a mass of raw, unchanneled potential that refuses to return to its source.</p>
<p>It becomes a bomb.</p>
<p>The discharge exploded outward in a sphere of crushing force and temporal feedback. The sound was not an explosion but a wet, tearing shriek as space itself screamed under the pressure.</p>
<p>The force caught Kaeleth’s center mass. His divine form buckled as the gravity tore at his molecular coherence, sending him hurtling backward like a broken marionette.</p>
<p>He slammed into the domain’s far wall with enough force to shatter stone, the impact producing a sickening crunch as his divine ribs made of solidified light and authority fractured under the trauma.</p>
<p>Kaeleth slid down the wall, coughing up globs of luminous gold blood. His form flickered erratically, his divine coherence struggling to stitch itself back together while his consciousness fought to process the simultaneous trauma of the wall impact and the gravitational discharge.</p>
<p>He was drowning in sensation, his mind a storm of pain and disorientation.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for him to recover. He was already moving.</p>
<p>Rex sprinted toward the throne. The throne wasn’t just furniture; it was the heartbeat of this reality.</p>
<p>It was the domain’s primary architectural structure, the physical manifestation of Kaeleth’s very identity.</p>
<p>It was the anchor around which every law of physics in this space had been spun. To destroy the throne was to lobotomize the domain.</p>
<p>Rex reached it in seconds, his shadow stretching long and menacing across the throne’s surface.</p>
<p>"This ends now," Rex whispered, his eyes burning with the fire of the Forbidden God.</p>
<p>He channeled the Earthen Authority, but he didn’t approach it as a simple geologist. He infused the command with the terrifying molecular precision granted by his Full Potential Telekinesisfull potential telekinesis.</p>
<p>He wasn’t just trying to break stone; he was targeting the fundamental bonds holding the divine substance together.</p>
<p>The throne’s material was alien amber gold temporal notation solidified into a physical form. It was harder than diamond and more complex than any alloy known to man.</p>
<p>But Rex’s power didn’t care about hardness. It cared about structure.</p>
<p>Rex found the seams. He found the microscopic lines where the temporal notation had been fused during the throne’s creation—the spiritual welds that held the god’s authority in place.</p>
<p>Crack.</p>
<p>A sound like a lightning strike echoed through the sanctum. The throne didn’t break like rock; it splintered along those divine seams, the golden light bleeding out of the cracks like molten marrow.</p>
<p>Rex slammed both palms against the surface, his muscles bulging, tendons standing out like cables as he poured every ounce of his devastating will into the structure.</p>
<p>"BREAK!" Rex roared.</p>
<p>With a sound that mimicked the breaking of a world, the throne shattered.</p>
<p>The collapse of the anchor structure triggered an immediate, violent reaction. The domain’s ambient medium went berserk.</p>
<p>The amber gold symbols that had been flowing in graceful, predictable patterns suddenly turned into a maelstrom of chaotic energy. The very air became a turbulent vortex of fractured time, shredding anything caught in its path.</p>
<p>The reference point was gone.</p>
<p>The logic of the space had died.</p>
<p>The floor buckled and heaved like the deck of a ship in a hurricane. The shockwave of the throne’s destruction tore through the ground, sending debris flying like shrapnel.</p>
<p>In the center of the chaos, stripped of his anchor, stripped of his authority, and stripped of his dignity, Kaeleth collapsed.</p>
<p>The god fell to his knees, his form flickering weakly as the world he had built around himself tore itself apart.</p>
