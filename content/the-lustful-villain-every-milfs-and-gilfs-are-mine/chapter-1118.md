<p>Cassandra broke the silence, stepping away from the obsidian wall with the measured grace of a predator. Her voice didn’t just fill the room; it commanded it.</p>
<p>Even beneath Rex’s shadow, she retained the steel of the general who had once commanded Mordecai’s legions, an edge that had only grown sharper and more lethal since she had begun advising the true power in the Underlayer.</p>
<p>"Our forces have been drilling the corridor engagement doctrine for weeks," Cassandra stated, her eyes meeting Rex’s with unflinching respect. "The soldiers are hungry for this."</p>
<p>"They know the stakes... But I would advise against a hasty strike." She leaned forward slightly, the lamplight catching the hard lines of her face. "Let them advance."</p>
<p>"Let their initial wave push past our first defensive positions."</p>
<p>"Let them feel the false hope of progress."</p>
<p>"We need to draw them deep into the throat of that corridor."</p>
<p>"A shallow trap only catches the front line and leaves the rear free to retreat."</p>
<p>"A deep trap catches the entire force and turns their momentum into their own noose."</p>
<p>Rex’s lips curled into something approximating approval. "A massacre requires proper staging, Cassandra."</p>
<p>"I agree." He turned his cold gaze toward the other side of the chamber. "Gorvasha, give me the tactical reality."</p>
<p>"Confirm the corridor’s current state."</p>
<p>Gorvasha stepped into the light, her tusked features twisted into a grim, almost ecstatic satisfaction. To her, this wasn’t just strategy; it was art.</p>
<p>"The corridor remains exactly as I designed it," she growled, her voice like grinding stone. "The geological structure is perfect."</p>
<p>"The stone itself will act as an amplifier for any earth-based workings we deploy..."</p>
<p>"The resonance will be devastating."</p>
<p>She spread her hands as if she could already feel the vibrations. "There is a single entry point from the Key’s anchor location."</p>
<p>"No lateral exits, no hidden tunnels, no escape routes within three hundred meters in any direction."</p>
<p>"Once we seal that rear entrance, the corridor ceases to be a passage and becomes a tomb."</p>
<p>"There will be no path back to the surface except through whatever blood we choose to spill to hold the line." A dark, hungry smile ghosted across her lips. "It is, without exaggeration, the finest kill box I have identified in fourteen months of surveying this wasteland."</p>
<p>’I want to hear the bones snap,’ Gorvasha thought privately, her pulse quickening at the mental image of Apostle knights being crushed against the walls by shifting tectonic plates. ’I want to hear their screams echo off the stone until the air itself tastes like copper.’</p>
<p>Pavellia moved next, stepping forward with the detached, terrifying precision that made her the most dangerous administrator in the Underlayer. Her reports were recited with the cold efficiency of a machine executing a script.</p>
<p>"Infrastructure audit across all seven population centers is complete," she announced. "Monitoring systems are fully operational."</p>
<p>"I have coordinated final security protocols with our Second Stratum contacts through the old Gelion channels."</p>
<p>"They remain compliant and have already delivered additional materials to reinforce the corridor’s structural integrity."</p>
<p>"Should we need to deliberately collapse sections of the ceiling to bury living enemies alive during the engagement, the materials are staged and ready."</p>
<p>"Good," Rex said, his voice clipped.</p>
<p>The room was a well-oiled machine, each component clicking into place for the coming slaughter. "All systems are green... All blades are sharp."</p>
<p>Finally, Rex turned his attention toward the back of the room. Lilith straightened instantly.</p>
<p>The hungry grin she had been fighting to suppress flared briefly before she forced it into an expression of eager, almost desperate professionalism. She practically preened under his direct attention, her body coiled like a spring.</p>
<p>"Lilith," Rex commanded, "you will monitor Morwenna’s surface reserve force."</p>
<p>"You are to maintain a concealed position near the corridor’s surface entrance. I require real-time intelligence."</p>
<p>"If there is any deviation from the plan Valentina and Apollo have laid out, if even a single unauthorized unit moves toward that corridor before we have given the signal, I want to know immediately."</p>
<p>"Understood, my lord," Lilith said, her voice breathless.</p>
<p>She was visibly vibrating with restrained excitement, her fingers twitching against her thighs as if imagining the feel of a blade in her hand. "I won’t miss a single heartbeat!"</p>
<p>"I’ll watch them like a hawk until they’re all screaming."</p>
<p>Cassandra and Gorvasha exchanged a brief, knowing glance, a silent acknowledgment of the girl’s obvious bloodlust, but Rex didn’t acknowledge it. He didn’t need to.</p>
<p>In the coming days, Lilith would get exactly what she wanted.</p>
<p>Rex turned away from the council members, pacing slowly toward the center of the throne room. The violet-black light seemed to cling to his cloak like a living shadow as he addressed the room as a whole.</p>
<p>When he spoke, his voice shifted, dropping into that low, deliberate cadence that felt less like speech and more like the tolling of a funeral bell.</p>
<p>"Understand the principle behind everything we are about to accomplish," Rex began, each word measured, each syllable carrying the crushing weight of absolute certainty. "Aethelgard believes they are the architects of justice."</p>
<p>"They believe they are the righteous flame descending to burn away the corruption that lurks beneath their pretty stones."</p>
<p>"They see the Underlayer as the aggressor, the monster beneath the bed that has orchestrated every tragedy, every loss, every ounce of their grief."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the silence stretch until it became uncomfortable, until the very air seemed to thin. A faint, razor-sharp edge of dark amusement touched his voice.</p>
<p>"And in a sense, they aren’t entirely wrong..."</p>
<p>"They simply have no idea how directly that responsibility traces back to a single source."</p>
<p>His eyes swept across the room over Mordecai’s nervous twitching, over Gorvasha’s hungry stillness, over Lilith’s eager breathing. "They will march into that corridor with their heads held high, their hearts full of sanctimonious fire."</p>
<p>"They will believe they are the hand of God descending upon a den of demons."</p>
<p>"And every step they take, every glorious, righteous stride, will carry them deeper into a throat that has been waiting to swallow them since before they even finished forging the key they think will save them."</p>
<p>The tension in the room was palpable now, a physical pressure that made it hard to breathe. Rex leaned forward slightly, his shadow stretching unnaturally long across the obsidian floor.</p>
<p>His voice dropped lower, colder, and quieter, carrying through the chamber like a blade sliding between ribs.</p>
<p>"They think they have come to punish us," he whispered, the words dripping with venomous irony. "In reality, they are simply walking into a mouth that has been waiting for this meal for a very long time."</p>
<p>A murmur of agreement rippled through the council. This was not the frantic, terrified compliance that had defined Mordecai’s reign.</p>
<p>This was something far more dangerous: the settled, absolute confidence of predators who knew the trap was sprung and were simply waiting for the prey to arrive. They didn’t just trust the plan; they trusted the monster who had engineered it.</p>
<p>’Let them come,’ Gorvasha thought, her claws digging crescents into the stone armrest of her chair. ’Let them bring their armor and their prayers.’</p>
<p>’I want to hear how loudly their bones crack when the earth decides to close around them.’</p>
<p>Far above them, separated by miles of unforgiving rock and a web of lies, the world above remained oblivious.</p>
<p>In a council chamber drenched in the deceptively warm glow of ordinary daylight, an Avatar, a perfect construct with ninety-seven point three percent accuracy, sat among Aethelgard’s remaining leadership.</p>
<p>It nodded with practiced solemnity as they discussed their noble crusade, its movements indistinguishable from the man whose authority it mimicked. It played its part in their delusion perfectly, a wolf wearing the skin of a shepherd.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, beneath their feet, the true Rex Rexilion stood in the darkness, orchestrating the precise, bloody geometry of their inevitable extinction.</p>
