<p>Morwenna had spoken to Theo across many distances in her life, each gulf defined by the shifting architecture of their shared history.</p>
<p>There was the intimate distance of childhood, the physical span that required her to crouch, bringing her eye level to his, offering a sanctuary of shared breath.</p>
<p>Then there was the growing chasm of adolescence: the distance between two bodies occupying the same house but inhabiting separate, warring territories of self.</p>
<p>Eventually, that grew into the sterile, political distance of a household head addressing a chosen heir, a formal, measured space where every word was weighed for its impact on legacy.</p>
<p>She had navigated the spaces of grief, the heights of celebration, and that treacherous, careful neutrality required when speaking truth to a young person who would inevitably recoil if the medicine tasted too bitter.</p>
<p>But she had never spoken to him across this distance.</p>
<p>Forty meters of yawning, impossible void separated them. Theo did not stand; he hovered, suspended in the amber gold residue of a temporal designation gone catastrophically wrong.</p>
<p>The air around him screamed with the violence of its own existence. It was a disturbed, hemorrhaging atmosphere, bucking and convulsing as it was forced to sustain two conflicting temporal states simultaneously.</p>
<p>The very fabric of reality seemed to fray at his edges, sparking with lethal, chronological static.</p>
<p>"Theo," she said.</p>
<p>Her voice cut through the howling distortion, carrying the precise register she had used when he was a child, the tone that bypassed anger to deliver the cold, unvarnished weight of consequence.</p>
<p>Not punishment, but reality.</p>
<p>Theo turned.</p>
<p>His eyes were no longer the eyes of the boy she had raised; they were twin craters of molten hatred, burning with an insanity that transcended mere rage. His jaw was set in a grotesque snarl, muscles twitching with a manic energy that seemed to threaten tearing his own skin apart.</p>
<p>Inside his mind, there was no longer any room for memory, only the rhythmic, pounding crescendo of vengeance.</p>
<p>’Break it,’ something primal and shattered whispered behind his ribs. ’Break the world that made you.’</p>
<p>’Break her. Break everything until there is nothing left to hurt.’</p>
<p>"You think you can still speak to me like that?" His voice was a fractured ruin, wet with the copper tang of blood that leaked from his split lips. "Like I’m still your precious heir?"</p>
<p>"Like I’m still something you can shape with your pretty little lessons?"</p>
<p>A vein throbbed violently in his temple, nearly bursting beneath the strain of his transformation. The golden energy around him surged, a shockwave of temporal displacement that cracked the ground beneath Morwenna’s feet.</p>
<p>The sound was like bone grinding against stone, a wet, splintering noise that echoed in the hollow of her chest.</p>
<p>"I am the consequence you never prepared for, Morwenna," he spat, his fingers curling into claws as the energy began to calcify around his knuckles like armor made of dying seconds. "I am the reality that eats its own creator."</p>
<p>He looked at her.</p>
<p>Blood continued its slow, obscene journey down Theo’s face. The gash across his brow was deep, a jagged wound that wept crimson steadily enough to coat his jawline and drip onto his collar in hot, rhythmic splatters.</p>
<p>His right eye was squeezed half shut to shield it from the stinging salt of his own blood, but his left eye remained fixed on Morwenna wide, unblinking, and terrifyingly analytical.</p>
<p>It was the gaze of a predator weighing whether the prey before him was worth the effort of the kill.</p>
<p>"You can come down," Morwenna said, her voice steady despite the temporal static tearing at the air around her.</p>
<p>"No," he said.</p>
<p>The word was clipped, vibrating with the unstable energy coursing through his veins.</p>
<p>"Then I can come up."</p>
<p>It wasn’t a threat. It was the flat, terrifyingly calm statement of someone who had already accepted the cost of the ascent.</p>
<p>"Don’t," Theo warned.</p>
<p>His fingers flexed, and the sound of tendons snapping under the strain of his power echoed like breaking twigs.</p>
<p>’Stay back,’ his mind screamed, even as his body prepared to launch. ’If you come closer, I will have to destroy you too.’</p>
<p>’I will have to tear the sky apart to keep you from seeing what I’ve become.’</p>
<p>"Theo," Morwenna said, stepping forward despite the way the ground groaned beneath her. "I have known you since you were three years old."</p>
<p>"I have watched every fracture in your character, every moment of growth... I know what this madness looks like from inside its storm, and I know what it looks like from the safety of the shore."</p>
<p>"I am asking you; I am begging you to let me tell you what I see from the outside before you decide that the darkness inside you is the only truth that exists."</p>
<p>Theo’s jaw worked silently. His teeth ground together with a wet, audible creak.</p>
<p>He wasn’t dismissing her; he was processing her words like venom, deciding whether they were a cure or simply another lie to be purged.</p>
<p>"What happened to you," Morwenna asked, her voice cracking slightly with the first sign of emotion, "in this last month?"</p>
<p>"I need to understand. I need to know the shape of this pain."</p>
<p>"You were there for most of it," Theo snapped.</p>
<p>The temporal distortion around him flared brighter, casting jagged, dancing shadows across his blood-streaked face.</p>
<p>"I was present for what was visible," she countered, refusing to flinch as a shard of debris, flung by the temporal wind, whistled past her ear. "I am asking about what was hidden."</p>
<p>"What happened in the silence?"</p>
<p>He looked at her then, and for a heartbeat, the insanity wavered, revealing a hollow, aching void beneath.</p>
<p>"Everything," he whispered, the word carrying more weight than any scream. "Everything that was mine stopped being mine."</p>
<p>"Bit by bit. Piece by piece. Like someone walking through my life with a sledgehammer, systematically dismantling the walls until there’s nowhere left to stand."</p>
<p>"Rex," she said, naming the wound.</p>
<p>"Yes," he spat, the name tasting like bile. "Rex."</p>
<p>"And you believe," she said, stepping closer even as the golden energy began to sear the edges of her clothes, "that this... this slaughter, this unraveling of reality is the answer to that loss?"</p>
<p>"I believe," Theo said, and his voice shifted.</p>
<p>The flat, numb recitation of his losses ignited into something hot, volatile, and dangerously focused. "I believe there is nothing left to address, Morwenna."</p>
<p>"There is no ’fixing’ this. There is no rebuilding. The only variable left is the equation itself."</p>
<p>"He exists, and I exist. Those two facts are incompatible."</p>
<p>"One of us has to be erased to make the math work again."</p>
<p>"Theo, listen to me... Please."</p>
<p>"I have listened to you for twenty-two years more!" he roared, the sound tearing from his throat as a shockwave of golden light punched outward, cracking the stone floor between them. "I listened to the council, I listened to the tutors, I listened to the gods, and I listened to you!"</p>
<p>"I followed every rule, I bled for every lesson, I performed every duty with perfect precision, and the result is that I am standing here in the ruins of my life, and he is standing there, untouched, breathing, alive."</p>
<p>His chest heaved, his breath coming in ragged, wet gasps. "I did everything I was supposed to do, and none of it mattered!"</p>
<p>"NOTHING!"</p>
<p>"It mattered," she insisted, her eyes glistening with unshed tears. "Every sacrifice, every choice mattered."</p>
<p>"To whom?" he asked.</p>
<p>The question wasn’t angry anymore. It was worse. It was cold, clinical, and utterly devoid of hope. It was the question of a man who had spent his entire life tracking the consequences of his actions, only to find that the universe refused to keep score.</p>
