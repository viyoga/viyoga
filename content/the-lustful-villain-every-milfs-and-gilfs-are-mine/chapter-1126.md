<p>Evelyn reached out, her fingers closing around his hand in a grip that was both desperate and grounding. It was a steadying gesture that mirrored, in its own quieter and more fragile way, the same instinct Rex had shown Lily and Diana in the courtyard not an hour earlier.</p>
<p>But where Rex had offered the unshakable confidence of a mountain, Evelyn’s touch carried the tremor of someone whose own foundation was cracking, the particular uncertainty of someone who wasn’t entirely sure her own guilt had settled, or if it was simply waiting to consume her.</p>
<p>"Then we stop chasing ruins," she said, her voice dropping to a fierce whisper that cut through the ambient noise of the courtyard. "No more..."</p>
<p>"No more proving our worth against corpses in ancient tombs or monsters in distant caves!"</p>
<p>"We’re here now, Elliot... Whatever comes next... whatever this city needs to survive... we are part of it."</p>
<p>"We stop disappearing for months at a time to chase some phantom idea of heroism that this city never asked us to prove."</p>
<p>Elliot turned to face her fully, the wind whipping strands of hair across his face. As he looked into her eyes, the raw, bleeding wound of his regret began to cauterize, hardening into something far more dangerous: resolve.</p>
<p>The guilt was still there, a cold stone in his gut, but it was no longer paralyzing him. He was channeling it, forging it into a weapon of pure determination.</p>
<p>"I need to know everything," he said, his voice dropping to a low, dangerous register. "I want the details they left out of the official reports."</p>
<p>"How exactly Theo died... What Rex actually did to stop him."</p>
<p>"I want to hear about the blood, the screams, the way the air felt when he turned..."</p>
<p>"I want to understand the shape of the nightmare we’re walking into before we step blindly into the mouth of it."</p>
<p>For the better part of the next hour, they became scavengers of truth. They moved through the city like wraiths, gathering fragments of the catastrophe from whoever seemed willing to speak and from those who seemed too broken to lie.</p>
<p>With each retelling, the story grew more visceral, more grotesque, and infinitely more unsettling.</p>
<p>The accounts painted a picture of hell unleashed. They heard of the temporal distortion fields that had swept through the outer districts like a poisoned fog, a nightmare where children’s faces wrinkled into ancient flesh in seconds, only to dissolve into screaming infants moments later as time itself buckled and tore.</p>
<p>They heard of the courtyard confrontation, described not as a noble duel but as a slaughterhouse of reality: the sound of bones cracking not just from physical force but from the sheer stress of time accelerating around them.</p>
<p>They heard whispers of Theo’s final moments, the way he had surrendered his designation with a look of hollow horror before the life was literally wrung out of him.</p>
<p>Then came the darker whispers. The paranoid gossips, those who spoke in hushed tones behind closed doors, insisted there was more, something called Tremor, and something else... something darker that seemed to be the true architect of the chaos.</p>
<p>They couldn’t name it, but they could feel its shadow.</p>
<p>Elliot absorbed every syllable with the ravenous intensity of a starving man, his mind working overtime to reconstruct months of missed context in a single, brutal sitting. He wanted the gore; he wanted the ugly truth; he wanted to feel the weight of what they had missed.</p>
<p>Evelyn, however, listened differently. She was quieter, her attention sharper, almost surgical.</p>
<p>While Elliot focused on the horror, Evelyn focused on the seams. She listened for the pauses that lasted too long, the way certain guards flinched when specific names were mentioned, and the subtle inconsistencies that didn’t align with the polished, heroic narrative being fed to the public.</p>
<p>She saw the gaps where the truth had been surgically removed, replaced by a story that made sense. She catalogued every lie, every omission, every shadow of doubt, and for now, she chose to keep them entirely to herself, letting the poison of the truth simmer beneath her calm exterior.</p>
<p>By the time the last fragment of truth had been scraped from the city’s wounds, a heavy, unspoken understanding settled between them. The air around them felt charged, not with hope, but with the grim electricity of impending violence.</p>
<p>They had stopped being travelers seeking rest; they had become soldiers seeking vengeance against their own negligence.</p>
<p>"We’re going," Elliot said finally.</p>
<p>His voice had shed its exhausted rasp, hardening into that singular, tempered register that Evelyn recognized instantly as the tone of a man who had crossed the Rubicon, the one that meant no plea, no warning, and no argument could move him from his course.</p>
<p>"I’m done with regrets..."</p>
<p>"Whatever this offensive against the Underlayer turns out to be... whether it’s a desperate gambit or a suicide mission I’m in!"</p>
<p>"I refuse to stand on these walls and watch this city bleed another drop while I sit idly by...! Not after what was lost... Not after Theo..."</p>
<p>"I’m with you," Evelyn said.</p>
<p>The words came out instantly, sharp as a blade. There was no hesitation, no weighing of risks.</p>
<p>Yet, for a heartbeat, something flickered deep within her pupils—a flash of something ancient and hungry. It was the ghost of the raw, terrifying speed and devastating power she rarely allowed herself to unleash, the power that marked her as something far more dangerous than a mere Apostle candidate.</p>
<p>"If the Underlayer’s poison has truly reached this far, if they’re the ones pulling the strings behind every tragedy, then we don’t just push them back."</p>
<p>"We tear them out by the roots... completely."</p>
<p>They began their march toward the Grand Academy, their movements possessed by a renewed, almost violent urgency that cut through the bone-deep exhaustion of their months on the road. The exhaustion was still there, but it was buried under a layer of adrenaline and fury.</p>
<p>As they walked, Elliot became a storm of restless energy. He muttered to himself, a low, angry litany of self-recrimation.</p>
<p>"I should have known... The letters from home... the way Diana sounded so tired, the way Lily stopped asking about my travels..."</p>
<p>"I dismissed it all as ordinary household politics, petty noble squabbles..."</p>
<p>"God, I was so blind." His fists clenched at his sides, knuckles whitening. "I read those letters and saw boredom where there was actually terror..."</p>
<p>"I saw a routine where there was actually a massacre in the making."</p>
<p>Evelyn walked beside him, a silent shadow to his tempest. While Elliot externalized his guilt through rage, Evelyn internalized her findings through cold, surgical analysis.</p>
<p>Her mind was a loom, weaving the scattered threads of the day’s conversations into a complex tapestry. She catalogued the timing of the attacks, the specific locations of the temporal distortions, and the way the guards’ eyes avoided certain topics.</p>
<p>And in the center of that tapestry, a single, persistent thread began to glow with suspicion.</p>
<p>Rex Rexilion.</p>
<p>The name had been the heartbeat of every story they had heard. In every account of every catastrophe, the dragon’s fire, Tremor’s madness, and the temporal collapse, Rex Rexilion was there.</p>
<p>He was the hero who arrived at the exact moment the situation became terminal; he was the one who delivered the decisive, bone-crushing blow that saved the day; he was the constant variable in a city defined by chaos.</p>
<p>While there was nothing logically impossible about a capable reincarnator being present for multiple crises in a city as tightly interconnected as Aethelgard, something about the sheer, mathematical consistency of his presence nagged at her.</p>
<p>Her analytical mind, honed by years of learning to distrust patterns that felt too convenient, couldn’t shake the feeling. Real heroism was messy; it was late, it was flawed, and it was often too small to matter.</p>
<p>But Rex’s heroism was... perfect. It was too clean.</p>
<p>It was as if the disasters were calibrated to meet him.</p>
<p>The thought was dangerous. It was a seed of doubt that could undermine everything they believed about the city’s salvation.</p>
<p>She watched Elliot’s profile the way his jaw was set in righteous fury, and she made a silent vow. She would not poison his resolve with her paranoia.</p>
<p>Not yet.</p>
<p>She would let him believe in the hero while she quietly hunted for the truth behind the legend.</p>
