<p>The silence that followed Apollo’s question was not a void; it was a pressurized chamber, a heavy, suffocating weight that seemed to squeeze the very oxygen from the lungs of everyone present. It was the kind of silence that precedes a tectonic shift, the momentary, terrifying stillness before the earth groans and the mountains begin to crack.</p>
<p>Rex counted the seconds.</p>
<p>’One.’ The air felt thick, like breathing liquid lead.</p>
<p>’Two.’ He could hear the frantic, rhythmic thrumming of his own pulse in his ears.</p>
<p>’Three.’ He watched a single bead of sweat trace a slow, agonizing path down the temple of the Apostle representative sitting across from him.</p>
<p>’Four.’</p>
<p>Then, the room broke.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a sudden explosion of noise but a controlled, cascading collapse, the specific, jagged way a structure fails when its foundation has been pulverized. It was a fracture spreading through glass, a series of sharp, overlapping cracks that signaled the end of the world as they knew it.</p>
<p>Aurelia was the first to fracture. She let out a sharp, strangled half-sentence, a gasp of a word that died in her throat, choked off by the sheer impossibility of what she was hearing. It was the sound of a heart breaking in real time.</p>
<p>At the far end of the table, the Apostle network representatives descended into a frantic, hissed communion. Their voices were low, urgent undertones, a staccato of whispered panic as they began to recalculate their loyalties, their allegiances, and their survival in a world where the headmaster might be the very enemy they had been preparing to fight.</p>
<p>Morwenna turned her gaze toward Valentina. The sharp, predatory suspicion she had been honing like a blade against Rex all afternoon vanished, replaced by something far more devastating.</p>
<p>It was an expression of profound, soul-deep disorientation. It was the look of a woman who had spent decades building a cathedral of trust, only to realize she had been worshipping at an altar of lies.</p>
<p>The betrayal wasn’t just political; it was personal, a jagged rip in the fabric of a relationship she thought she understood.</p>
<p>’Look at them,’ Rex thought, his inner voice a cold, detached observer of the carnage. ’The pillars are crumbling.’</p>
<p>’The lies are bleeding out of the cracks, and they’re all drowning in it.’</p>
<p>’They don’t even realize the floor has already fallen away.’</p>
<p>Apollo remained fixed on Valentina, his eyes unblinking, his gaze a heavy, accusatory weight. He was a soldier watching a commander’s first sign of weakness, waiting to see if she would rally or retreat.</p>
<p>Valentina, however, was a fortress under siege. She did not look at her granddaughter’s weeping eyes; she did not look at Morwenna’s wounded stare; she did not look at Apollo’s interrogation.</p>
<p>She was staring at the obsidian surface of the table, her eyes fixed on a point that didn’t exist. Her face was a masterpiece of forced composure, a mask of granite held together by sheer, agonizing willpower.</p>
<p>Inside, he knew, there was a tempest, a swirling, violent chaos of regret, fear, and ancient secrets, but on the outside, she was a woman managing the impossible gap between the wreckage of her soul and the expectations of the room.</p>
<p>’Hold it, Valentina,’ Rex thought, a dark, twisted sense of amusement flickering in his mind. ’Hold the mask just a little longer.’</p>
<p>’Let the tension stretch until it screams.’</p>
<p>’Let them wonder if you’re a god or a ghost.’</p>
<p>The tension in the room was a physical thing now, a taut wire vibrating at a frequency that made the teeth ache. Every breath felt like a struggle; every movement felt like a transgression.</p>
<p>The administrative order of the Grand Academy had been replaced by a primal, instinctive dread.</p>
<p>Finally, the divination representative, a woman whose very profession relied on sensing the unseen, found her voice. It was a thin, trembling sound, cutting through the low hum of the room like a needle through silk.</p>
<p>"Von Starlight," the divination representative whispered, the name sounding like a death knell in the heavy air. "Celestina Von Starlight..."</p>
<p>"That is the name of the Legion’s founder."</p>
<p>The words didn’t just hang in the air; they seemed to vibrate, a low-frequency hum of dread that rattled the very bones of everyone present.</p>
<p>"That is what the intelligence indicates," Apollo replied, his voice a grim anchor in the rising tide of panic.</p>
<p>He didn’t offer comfort; he offered the cold, hard edge of a fact.</p>
<p>The divination representative leaned forward, her eyes wide, reflecting the flickering candlelight like a cornered animal sensing a predator.</p>
<p>"The founder of the organization that has systematically hunted, broken, and eliminated forty-three reincarnators over the last thirty years..." she paused, her voice trembling with the sheer weight of the logic, "...shares a surname with the Headmaster of the Grand Academy."</p>
<p>She spoke with a terrifying, clinical precision. She wasn’t shouting; she wasn’t pointing a finger in a theatrical accusation.</p>
<p>She was simply laying a bloody, severed limb on the table and letting everyone else deal with the stench. She was presenting a fact so massive, so undeniable, that it demanded the room’s total, violent attention.</p>
<p>The room did not just react; it convulsed.</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder at the far end of the table lunged forward, his hands slapping onto the obsidian surface with a dull, heavy thud, his knuckles white as he braced himself as if the table itself might split open.</p>
<p>Aurelia’s hand, trembling with a sudden, frantic energy, reached for her tea glass, but she froze halfway there, her fingers hovering in the air, paralyzed by a sudden, visceral nausea.</p>
<p>Rex watched the micro movements of the room with the detached intensity of a surgeon. He saw the second Apostle representative, a man Rex had already mentally tagged as a high-tier combatant due to the coiled, lethal tension in his shoulders pushing back from the table by a mere four centimeters.</p>
<p>It was a subtle, instinctive retreat, the universal movement of a creature trying to create a sliver of distance between itself and a sudden, lethal threat.</p>
<p>’Look at them,’ Rex thought, his mind a dark, swirling vortex of observation. ’They aren’t just processing information.’</p>
<p>’They are reacting to a predator in their midst.’</p>
<p>’They are looking at Valentina and seeing not a leader, but a monster wearing a familiar face.’</p>
<p>’The air is thick with the scent of a coming slaughter.’</p>
<p>"The intelligence does not establish a direct connection between..." Elizabeth began, her voice desperate, a thin line of defense trying to hold back a landslide.</p>
<p>She was trying to provide the logic, the nuance, the diplomatic shield that would prevent the room from tearing itself apart.</p>
<p>"The surname is the same," Morwenna cut her off.</p>
<p>The interruption was brutal. Morwenna’s voice wasn’t loud, but it had the crushing weight of an iron hammer.</p>
<p>It was the specific, terrifying quiet of a woman who was no longer performing the social graces of restraint but was being held back by the sheer force of her own fury. She was a woman standing on the edge of a precipice, deciding whether to leap or to push someone else over.</p>
<p>She turned her gaze, sharp and unforgiving, directly onto the head of the table.</p>
<p>"Valentina," Morwenna said, the name a demand, a challenge, and a condemnation all at once. "We need to hear from you."</p>
<p>"No more shadows. No more ’suspicions.’ No more managing the tension."</p>
<p>"We need the truth, or we need to know if we are sitting at a table with an enemy."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was different from the others. It wasn’t a vacuum; it was a countdown. The room held its collective breath, the tension so high it felt as though a single loud word would cause someone’s heart to burst or a bone to snap under the sheer atmospheric pressure.</p>
<p>All eyes—Aurelia’s tearful ones, Apollo’s steel ones, Morwenna’s predatory ones—converged on Valentina, waiting for the woman at the center of the web to finally speak.</p>
