<p>The Academy fighters surged back to their feet, their movements jarring and uncoordinated, the sudden absence of the crushing gravity leaving them disoriented and nauseous. They stood in a ragged circle of wheezing breath and trembling limbs, the physical shock of the release rippling through them.</p>
<p>The three flagged reincarnators exchanged a rapid-fire, silent conversation of glances. They were deciphering the situation in real time, their faces etched with a mixture of wariness and growing dread.</p>
<p>The detection type's voice was sharp, cutting through the tension. "Are you going to kill us?"</p>
<p>"That depends on what you do next," Rex replied, his voice like grinding stone. "You aren't Legion contacts."</p>
<p>"Right now... you are not a threat to what I am building. But you are reincarnators on an island that is about to become the epicenter of a war."</p>
<p>"Your response to that war will determine which side of the rubble you end up on."</p>
<p>"What war?" the combat type demanded. He was a mountain of a man, his body a testament to the strength amplification designation, shoulders broad, and jaw set with a stubborn, defensive pride.</p>
<p>He stood his ground, his fists clenched, waiting for an answer that made sense.</p>
<p>"The one that ends the ancient, decaying deadlock between the Underlayer and Aethelgard's Apostle network," Rex said.</p>
<p>"You're declaring war," the detection type stated, her eyes narrowing.</p>
<p>"I am describing reality," Rex countered. "The Underlayer's new governance made its declaration last night."</p>
<p>"The only question left is how long it takes the surface to wake up and notice." He looked at them with a flat, unblinking intensity. "You have choices."</p>
<p>"You can hide behind the apostle network and pray they can protect you."</p>
<p>"You can try to survive in the gaps of the conflict, neither here nor there, hoping the fires don't reach you. Alternatively, you can choose to align yourself with the Underlayer."</p>
<p>"You can choose to be part of what comes next."</p>
<p>The wind affinity caster, who had remained silent and poised, finally spoke. Her voice was cold, calculating.</p>
<p>"The governance you're talking about... the one that's supposed to replace the Legion... that's you..."</p>
<p>"You're the one who shattered the plaza!"</p>
<p>"I am," Rex said.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer's reconstruction," she continued, her voice trembling slightly. "The purge of the Legion's internal network..."</p>
<p>'You did that in a single night?"</p>
<p>"I did," Rex said. "The Legion has spent thirty years thinning the ranks of reincarnators on this planet."</p>
<p>"Their operational infrastructure in the kingdom below no longer exists, and their reach is being severed, one connection at a time."</p>
<p>The combat type looked at the detection type, his confusion turning into a palpable, heavy tension.</p>
<p>"What is the Legion?" he demanded. "What is she talking about?"</p>
<p>The detection type didn't look at him. Her eyes remained locked on Rex, her expression one of grim realization.</p>
<p>"They're the ones who make us disappear," she said. "The ones who hunt us down."</p>
<p>"Only the network knows they're here, and if you're flagged, you're already dead."</p>
<p>Rex watched the combat type's face as the truth sank in. The man's posture shifted, the confidence wavering as the scope of the situation began to settle over him.</p>
<p>"I'm offering you the chance to move from the hunted to the hunter," Rex said. "But that choice has to be yours."</p>
<p>"I won't beg you to join me, and I won't spare you if you get in my way."</p>
<p>He turned his back on them, walking toward the academy gate. The fighters there were now fully recovered, their weapons leveled at him, their faces masked by the rigid, terrified discipline of soldiers facing an unknown enemy.</p>
<p>Rex raised his voice, the sound booming across the plaza, echoing off the walls of the settlement.</p>
<p>"Tell whoever is responsible for this institution that the Underlayer's new governance is here," he shouted, his voice commanding and absolute. "Tell them the window for negotiation is thirty minutes."</p>
<p>"If you want to discuss terms before the clock runs out, send a representative to this plaza."</p>
<p>He paused, his gaze sweeping over the frightened soldiers and the stunned reincarnators.</p>
<p>"If not," he said, his voice dropping to a low, ominous rumble that seemed to vibrate in the very ground beneath their feet, "I will come inside and find you myself."</p>
<p>Rex released the final remnants of the gravity constraint, the invisible pressure snapping away like a broken cord. The Academy fighters slumped, gasping as the sudden lightness made them feel as though they might float away, their eyes darting between the cratered ground and the silent titan standing amidst the wreckage.</p>
<p>The plaza settled into a heavy, suffocating stillness, a landscape of fractured stone and a crowd of terrified witnesses all paralyzed by the same singular question: What just happened?</p>
<p>Rex moved to the center of the devastation, his footsteps echoing in the unnatural quiet. The golem relay was a relentless torrent of data in the back of his mind.</p>
<p>The island was in flux. The sweep was nearly complete: seven unflagged reincarnators had converged on the central market; four flagged engagements had been resolved with surgical finality; three were still locked in combat.</p>
<p>He glanced at the temporal readout in his mind.</p>
<p>'Twenty-seven minutes.'</p>
<p>He looked up at the academy's upper spire, the fifteen-story monolith of magical prestige that Elizabeth had once described as the pinnacle of civilization.</p>
<p>He didn't see an academy; he saw a target. He didn't see an institution; he saw a variable in a much larger equation.</p>
<p>Then, the air changed.</p>
<p>A thermal differential began to bloom above the Academy's summit, a shimmering distortion of heat that made the sky itself seem to warp. It was a signature he knew intimately: the unmistakable, surging buildup of a high-tier designation preparing for full-scale activation.</p>
<p>This wasn't a tactical response; this was a grand arrival.</p>
<p>Apollo descended.</p>
<p>He did not fall, nor did he fly; he descended with the terrifying grace of an apostle of life exerting total control over his essence. The light surrounding him was blinding, a solar corona that turned the air into a shimmering veil of gold.</p>
<p>He hit the plaza with a controlled, thunderous impact, a landing that whispered of a power capable of leveling the city, yet calibrated with the precision of a master surgeon.</p>
<p>As the light stabilized, Rex felt the weight of it. This was the Apollo he had seen in the depths of the Underlayer's canyon—the version of the man who had stood against Kregg and the one that the extraction ring had struggled to suppress.</p>
<p>The restoration the temple healers had promised was complete; the man before him was no longer a fragment of his former self but a perfected vessel.</p>
<p>But the Apollo in the plaza was not the man from the Brightsoul hallway at three in the morning. That Apollo had looked at Rex with the wide, unburdened eyes of unconditional trust. The man standing across the fissure carried a heavy, layered complexity, the look of someone who had spent every waking second since that night wrestling with a truth that threatened to break him.</p>
<p>Rex narrowed his eyes, his perception sweeping past Apollo to the figures emerging from the light behind him. They moved in a tight, tactical formation, a unit that had arrived together, prepared.</p>
<p>To Apollo's left stood Kaelira Ignisvale. Her recovery had been predicted to take weeks, but she stood now with a terrifying, revitalized poise, her energy signature humming with the efficiency of someone who had used her convalescence to sharpen her blade.</p>
<p>To his right was a stranger, a woman whose very presence felt heavy and visceral. Her energy signature was a dense, rhythmic resonance; she was a blood affinity caster who used the essence of life itself as her medium, a blood sorceress named Calivara Crimsonveil.</p>
<p>And hovering in the rear, maintaining a calculated distance, was a third woman. Her signature was unlike the others: diffuse, multi-layered, and hauntingly complex.</p>
<p>She didn't just exist in the physical; she resonated across dimensional planes as an astral summoner named Eryndra Voidstar.</p>
<p>Rex cataloged them with cold, surgical precision. These were the ones who had not been in the canyon.</p>
<p>They had not felt the terror of the Underlayer, nor had they shared the crucible of events that had transformed the expedition members from mere allies into Rex's most unbreakable bonds.</p>
<p>Apollo had brought his remaining circle. He had brought the ones who still belonged to him.</p>
<p>The silence between the two men stretched, taut as a bowstring, vibrating with the unspoken history of the last few weeks.</p>
<p>"You..." Apollo said.</p>
<p>His voice was steady, but there was a tremor of something profound beneath the surface, the sound of a man rehearsing a name to anchor himself to a reality he wasn't yet ready to accept. The light around him pulsed once, a heartbeat of pure energy.</p>
<p>"I think," Apollo said, his gaze locking onto Rex's mask, "that we need to talk."</p>
<p>Rex stared back across the fractured stone through the blinding radiance of the Apostle's activation. He looked at the three warriors standing in grim readiness behind him.</p>
<p>He felt the weight of the impending storm.</p>
<p>'This is it,' Rex thought, his mind turning toward the horizon. ' The beginning of the final phase...'</p>
<p>'Not the war with Aethelgard... but the war for what remains of us.'</p>
