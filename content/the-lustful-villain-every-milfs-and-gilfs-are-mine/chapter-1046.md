<p>Morwenna stared up at her great-grandson, suspended in that hemorrhaging amber sky forty meters above her.</p>
<p>She felt a hollow, sickening sensation in the pit of her stomach, the precise, cold dread that comes when one recognizes a situation that possesses no possible beneficial outcome.</p>
<p>There was no path here that ended in healing; there was only the choice between different flavors of catastrophe.</p>
<p>"To me," she said, her voice cracking like thin ice under the pressure of the temporal howling. "To Elaris... To the household."</p>
<p>"The household," Theo repeated.</p>
<p>The way he said it made the word sound foreign, like a relic from a dead civilization. His voice held the hollow inflection of someone who had watched a sacred concept be stripped of its meaning and replaced with something transactional and cruel.</p>
<p>"The household is currently being directed by the person I just tried to kill."</p>
<p>"You’re speaking of a ghost, Morwenna. A structure that burned down while you were still busy admiring the blueprints."</p>
<p>Morwenna froze. The silence that followed was worse than the temporal screaming.</p>
<p>She had no answer for that. The truth of his words struck her with the force of a physical blow, leaving her momentarily breathless.</p>
<p>That was the problem: the fundamental, unsolvable fracture between them. From approximately two seconds into this confrontation, she knew that this conversation would not be the salvation she had hoped for.</p>
<p>She wasn’t a teacher or a great-grandmother anymore; she was a witness to a massacre in progress.</p>
<p>But she could not stop. She would not let him slip into the abyss without one last fight.</p>
<p>"What you have received from the god who spoke to you to get this such power..." she cried out, stepping forward as the golden energy lashed at her skin, leaving stinging red welts across her cheekbones, "is not what you call true power, Theo!"</p>
<p>"Do you hear me?! It is not power! It is more than a leash!"</p>
<p>"And it is a direction carved into your soul by something that doesn’t understand what it means to bleed, to hurt, or to love."</p>
<p>"That direction was chosen for you, not by you! It was chosen based on what that entity thinks you can be used for—a weapon, a tool, a vessel—not based on who you actually are!"</p>
<p>Theo’s head tilted to the side. The movement was unnervingly smooth, almost mechanical.</p>
<p>Blood from his brow wound trickled into his left eye now, blurring his vision with a crimson haze, but his stare remained unnervingly sharp.</p>
<p>"That is very wise," he said, his tone dripping with a terrifying, mocking sweetness. "It is also exactly what Rex says."</p>
<p>"He says it to the council. He says it to the servants."</p>
<p>"He says it to everyone, about everything, with that same patronizing smile."</p>
<p>Morwenna felt a surge of fury beneath her grief. "You cannot win an argument against me simply by pointing out that your argument was made first by someone else!"</p>
<p>"I can," Theo snapped, his voice suddenly dropping to a guttural snarl that vibrated in Morwenna’s very bones.</p>
<p>The temporal field around him convulsed, sending a spray of molten amber shards downward that tore through the stone floor like shrapnel. "I can when the ’someone else’ is the reason the argument exists in the first place! Rex didn’t just give me a different perspective, Morwenna; he took everything until there was nothing left for me to argue about except his own damn existence!"</p>
<p>A manic grin split his bloody face, revealing teeth stained pink. Inside his mind, the darkness was screaming louder than the storm.</p>
<p>’She’s trying to cage you again,’ the voice hissed. ’She’s trying to put you back in the box.’</p>
<p>’Break the box. Break her. Break the world.’</p>
<p>Theo tore his gaze away from Morwenna, his eyes snapping toward Rex. Below, on the street level between Valentina and the Academy’s outer gate, Rex stood like a monument of unshakeable calm.</p>
<p>Something flickered across Theo’s face, a microscopic fracture in his madness, the involuntary expression of a man who has just been struck by a truth he desperately wants to deny.</p>
<p>Then, the voice arrived.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a scream or a thunderclap; it was the same cold, ancient presence that had haunted him since the cliff’s edge. It had been there, coiled in the background of his consciousness like a sleeping serpent, not intrusive enough to feel alien, but present enough to feel like part of his own shadow.</p>
<p>’Do not let her speak more.’ Kaeleth’s voice was devoid of drama.</p>
<p>It was the flat, surgical delivery of an entity that had been communicating through this channel long enough to know exactly how to twist the knife. ’She is already doing it, Theo.’</p>
<p>’She is already sowing the seeds of doubt.’</p>
<p>’Can you not hear it? Can you not hear the tremor in your own conviction?’</p>
<p>Theo looked back at Morwenna, his pupils dilating despite the blood streaming into his eye.</p>
<p>He could hear it. Kaeleth was right.</p>
<p>There was a foreign texture to his own voice, a crackle of uncertainty he recognized from twenty-nine years of playing political games he didn’t believe in. When he argued, he usually sounded like steel; now, he sounded like breaking glass.</p>
<p>’She will tell you more,’ Kaeleth whispered, the words sliding into his mind like oil. ’And more.’</p>
<p>’And by the end of it, you will have talked yourself into a corner so deep that you will never find the light again.’</p>
<p>’And when you try to back out, when you try to reclaim your fury, the cost will be higher than any blood you could spill today.’</p>
<p>’You will lose yourself entirely.’</p>
<p>Theo’s gaze shifted violently to Rex.</p>
<p>Rex was leaning in toward Valentina, speaking with the measured, hypnotic cadence of a predator who knew exactly how to make his prey agree with him. Theo watched as Valentina’s shoulders relaxed, her posture gradually aligning with Rex’s framing, her resistance melting away like wax near a flame.</p>
<p>Rex wasn’t even looking at him. He was calmly orchestrating a new reality while Theo hung suspended in the air, bleeding from a dozen wounds, and Morwenna offered him a lifeline of logic that he could feel, god help him, actually working.</p>
<p>The realization burned worse than the temporal energy searing his flesh. The rage boiled over, not as passion but as a cold, killing frost.</p>
<p>"You are doing it again," Theo said.</p>
<p>His voice had gone utterly flat, stripped of all inflection. It was the dead register of a man who had stopped considering options and had returned to his original, violent purpose.</p>
<p>"Theo—" Morwenna started, reaching out a hand as if she could physically grasp the fading pieces of his soul.</p>
<p>"You are on his side," Theo interrupted, each word landing like a hammer blow against an anvil. "Not consciously."</p>
<p>"I don’t think you even possess the self-awareness to realize you’ve betrayed me!"</p>
<p>"But he has been in this household for one month, Morwenna... One FUCKING month!"</p>
<p>The temporal field around him bucked violently, a shockwave of gold energy sending debris cascading down like lethal rain. Theo’s breathing was wet and labored, but his words were precise.</p>
<p>"You have been present for every atrocity this month has produced."</p>
<p>"You have watched the foundations crack. And yet, the way you are talking to me right now, the register you’re using, the way you’re carefully curating your points, the way you’re waiting for me to explain myself like some wayward child—it’s not concern!"</p>
<p>"It’s judgment." His teeth bared in a bloody snarl. "You’re talking to me like someone who has already decided what my explanation means!"</p>
<p>"You’ve already written my verdict, haven’t you?"</p>
