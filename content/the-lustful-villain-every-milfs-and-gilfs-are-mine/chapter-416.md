<p>Mireya looked at her.</p>

<p>"The argument is about either the principle or the outcome."</p>
<p>"It’s about the principle," Mireya said. "The outcome being good doesn’t make the method acceptable."</p>
<p>"Yes, I do agree with that in general," Aisella said. "But still... I’m asking whether you’d be applying that principle with the same energy if the outcome had been different."</p>
<p>"I..." Mireya’s jaw tightened.</p>
<p>"Yes," she said. "I would."</p>
<p>"Then I respect that," Aisella said. "I disagree with your conclusion about what the principle requires in this specific situation, but I respect that you’re applying it consistently."</p>
<p>She paused.</p>
<p>"What I’m asking is for the same respect in the other direction. I’ve applied my own assessment consistently too."</p>
<p>"I looked at the situation, and I reached a different conclusion. That doesn’t mean I haven’t thought about it."</p>

<p>"Treating them as the same measure is going to produce the wrong answer either way."
