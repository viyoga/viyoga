<p>The amber gold radiance that had been flickering like a dying candle since the throne’s destruction suddenly exploded. It wasn’t just light; it was a supernova of temporal energy that roared back to life with a brightness that exceeded anything Rex had registered throughout the entire engagement.</p>
<p>The temporal notation—the raw script of reality—began to bleed through the air, the walls, and the shattered floor at such a terrifying density that the domain appeared to be ablaze with something far more lethal than fire. It was a conflagration of pure causality.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s form began to change. He didn’t grow in physical height or mass, but his presence underwent a grotesque expansion.</p>
<p>He began to occupy more of the ambient register, his very existence stretching to fill every crack and crevice of Aethelgard. He had stopped pretending.</p>
<p>The mask of the administrator was gone; the predator had filled the cage.</p>
<p>"You think you understand the mathematics," Kaeleth’s voice boomed.</p>
<p>It no longer came from a single throat; it resonated from the walls, the floor, and the air itself. The domain was no longer his battlefield; the domain was Kaeleth.</p>
<p>"You think seventeen mechanisms constitute the complete set?"</p>
<p>"You think you have mapped the limits of my design?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t answer. His mind was screaming warnings.</p>
<p>His martial arts mastery wasn’t just analyzing a stance; it was screaming a primal alarm, translating the shift in Kaeleth’s divine posture into a single, terrifying truth: ’Distance has become irrelevant.’ Survival probability is plummeting.’</p>
<p>’Move. Now. ’Rex’s inner voice was a blade of focus cutting through the chaos. ’He’s not attacking me.’</p>
<p>’He’s attacking the space I occupy.’</p>
<p>"There is a difference," Kaeleth roared as the reality around them began to warp and scream, "between what I have shown you... and what I AM."</p>
<p>The first worker arrived before Rex could even complete his repositioning.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a spell. It wasn’t a strike. It was a catastrophic collapse.</p>
<p>The entire temporal fabric of the domain imploded toward Kaeleth’s position with the force of a collapsing star, only to rebound outward in a single, violent pulse of absolute annihilation.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was using the domain as a limb, swinging the very concept of ’now’ like a sledgehammer.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a single mechanism. The adaptation couldn’t categorize it.</p>
<p>It was a compound catastrophe: acceleration, deceleration, severance, and fracture all compressed into one impossible moment. Kaeleth had stopped bothering to distinguish between his powers; he was simply unleashing the raw fury of time itself.</p>
<p>Rex took the hit head-on. There was no escape.</p>
<p>When the ground you stand on becomes the weapon, there is no cover.</p>
<p>The adaptation stuttered. Faced with a simultaneous assault of contradictory mechanisms, the system spent two agonizing seconds trying to parse the data, trying to understand how to file a phenomenon that was simultaneously speeding up and slowing down, breaking and mending.</p>
<p>In the calculus of god-tier combat, two seconds was an eternity. Two seconds was long enough to die.</p>
<p>The pulse tore through Rex’s biological timeline with the brutality of a meat grinder. Rex screamed as he felt his own history being shredded.</p>
<p>In a single heartbeat, his hands aged decades, the skin wrinkling and spotting with liver marks, only to reverse in a wet, nauseating stutter back to youth, then age again.</p>
<p>His bones groaned as they calcified and softened in rapid succession. The Infinite Regeneration fought desperately to smooth out the damage, but it was trying to repair a foundation that was constantly shifting.</p>
<p>Underneath the physical agony, a deeper horror took root: a fracture in his consciousness. His sense of continuity shattered.</p>
<p>For a moment, Rex couldn’t remember if he had already been hit, if he was currently being hit, or if he had already died.</p>
<p>The ’now’ became a kaleidoscope of broken moments.</p>
<p>The force of the pulse launched Rex across the domain like a ragdoll. He struck the far wall with a sound like a sledgehammer hitting wet concrete.</p>
<p>The amber gold surface cracked behind him as his body embedded itself into the debris, ribs snapping with audible, wet cracks that echoed through the chamber.</p>
<p>Blood bright, arterial red sprayed across the golden floor, steaming as it touched the charged air.</p>
<p>Rex coughed, tasting copper and something ancient, his vision swimming in a haze of temporal static. His lungs burned, each breath a struggle against the reality that seemed to reject his presence.</p>
<p>But Kaeleth was not finished. The god was not giving him a moment to breathe, let alone to heal.</p>
<p>The golden storm began to gather again, more focused now, more murderous. The hunt had truly begun.</p>
<p>Kaeleth didn’t run; he simply ceased to be in one position and appeared in another, crossing the distance with a speed that shattered the sound barrier before the shockwave could even form.</p>
<p>The blade that coalesced from his robes this time was no longer a weapon; it was a nightmare. A dozen shards of solidified temporal notation orbited his hand like a constellation of razor-sharp stars, each fragment lashing out independently with its own unique severance property.</p>
<p>They moved with a hive mind intelligence, seeking every weakness, every gap in Rex’s guard.</p>
<p>Rex’s reflexes screamed. He threw his forearm up just as the first three shards shrieked through the air.</p>
<p>The impact felt like being struck by lightning; the vibration rattled his teeth and threatened to split his radius. But the fourth shard was faster. It found the gap between his guard and his shoulder, slicing through flesh and bone alike.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t just bleed. The wound screamed with a metaphysical agony.</p>
<p>Instead of crimson blood, the gash leaked moments.</p>
<p>Through the torn muscle and severed artery, Rex could see a visible thinning in reality, glimmering fragments of his own past and future being pulled loose from his body like loose threads from a tapestry, scattering into the air as useless shrapnel of time.</p>
<p>’Focus, dammit!’ Rex’s inner voice roared over the ringing in his ears. ’Don’t think about the wound.’</p>
<p>’Don’t think about the seconds you’re losing.’</p>
<p>’If you hesitate, you’re already dead.’</p>
<p>Instead of retreating into the safety of distance, Rex made a desperate, suicidal calculation: he shoved off the cracked wall, muscles straining against the crushing gravity, and charged directly into the storm.</p>
<p>He knew that retreating would only give Kaeleth the space to continue weaving these devastating compound workings. He had to force this into a brawl.</p>
<p>He had to make it personal.</p>
<p>Kaeleth met him head-on with the fury of a collapsing star.</p>
<p>The fight devolved from a tactical engagement into a primal collision of two unstoppable forces. There was no more finesse, no more measured exchanges.</p>
<p>It was raw, ugly, and devastating. Kaeleth threw everything the domain could conjure: shards of frozen time and waves of accelerated entropy, while Rex absorbed the worst of it, his body becoming a canvas of violence.</p>
<p>The god’s blades became a blur of lethal light. Rex felt the sting of a dozen shallow cuts across his chest and arms. Each one was a theft; each cut stole a fragment of his continuity.</p>
<p>His Infinite Regeneration worked overtime, knitting flesh together as fast as it was torn, but it couldn’t fix the fraying of his soul.</p>
<p>Rex felt a nauseating unsteadiness creeping into his perception; he would swing a fist, only to feel as though he had already swung it three seconds ago or was yet to swing it at all.</p>
<p>’The clock is ticking,’ Rex thought grimly, his mind fighting through the temporal static. ’ My regeneration can fix my bones, but it can’t fix my timeline.’</p>
<p>’If I lose too many seconds, there won’t be enough of me left to regenerate.’</p>
<p>He drove forward anyway, fueled by pure, stubborn defiance.</p>
<p>His earthly authority answered his desperate intent before his conscious mind could even formulate the command. With a guttural roar, he ripped a massive slab of the amber gold floor free, hurling it like a shield into the path of the orbiting blades.</p>
<p>The collision was thunderous stone grinding against temporal energy in a shower of sparks and debris.</p>
<p>That half second of interrupted rhythm was all he needed.</p>
<p>Rex closed the final distance, his boots skidding on blood-slicked stone. He channeled every ounce of his gravity manipulation and telekinesis into his right fist, condensing the weight of a mountain into a single point of impact.</p>
<p>He drove the strike straight into Kaeleth’s chest.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of the impact reverberated through the entire domain. Kaeleth staggered backward, his divine form convulsing as the concentrated force of the blow bypassed his temporal defenses.</p>
<p>It was the first time since Kaeleth had unleashed his full power that he had actually given ground. Rex saw it immediately: the stagger wasn’t just physical; it was systemic.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was burning through his divine reserves at a catastrophic rate. By choosing to unleash everything at once, the god had abandoned the economy of power.</p>
<p>He was trading his eternal essence for immediate, overwhelming destruction.</p>
<p>Rex stood his ground, breathing hard, blood dripping from his chin as his own temporal wounds continued to leak seconds into the air. His voice came out rough, cracked like broken glass.</p>
<p>"You’re spending yourself," Rex rasped, each word a battle against the disorientation clawing at his brain. "Every mechanism you throw at once instead of one at a time is a mechanism you can’t use again for a long time."</p>
<p>"You know the math, Kaeleth. You’re burning your own foundation to kill me."</p>
