<p>Aurelia knocked on Theo’s door for the fourth time that morning, each rap against the heavy oak feeling like a futile heartbeat. When the expected silence followed, it wasn’t just empty; it felt hostile, as if the room itself had been infected by the same decay that had consumed Theo’s spirit.</p>
<p>"Theo," she said, her voice steady despite the fraying edges of her composure.</p>
<p>Her palm pressed flat against the wood, feeling the unnatural coldness seeping through. "The unit is waiting."</p>
<p>"I do not care if you spar today... I only care that you get out of this room and eat something that is not whatever is left over from yesterday’s tray."</p>
<p>Silence. Not even the sound of shifting sheets or a labored breath.</p>
<p>Just that suffocating, pressurized stillness, the kind of silence that exists in the eye of a hurricane.</p>
<p>She had already questioned the household staff. The reports were damning in their subtlety: Theo had been taking his meals at the threshold like a starving animal, refusing to cross into the shared spaces of the home.</p>
<p>He had abandoned the morning briefings, his chair empty at the table where he once sat with a fragile hope. He had vanished from the training halls entirely since the day Rex had dismantled him.</p>
<p>Aurelia could still see it in her mind’s eye: Rex, moving with that terrifying, effortless grace, correcting Theo’s footwork with a casual cruelty that had left Theo broken on the floor in less than sixty seconds.</p>
<p>But it wasn’t just the physical humiliation that had shattered him; it was the memory of the sounds that had bled through the walls of his own home: the wet, obscene squelching of Diana’s surrender; the guttural triumph in Rex’s voice; and the way Diana’s screams had sounded less like pain and more like a twisted form of worship.</p>
<p>That memory was a poison, a corrosive acid that had eaten through Theo’s identity until there was nothing left but this hollow shell.</p>
<p>Aurelia understood grief. She had watched soldiers weep over broken bones and mothers wail over empty cradles.</p>
<p>She knew what mourning looked like when it wore a face of rage or sorrow. But this?</p>
<p>This was different. This was a slow, methodical suicide of the soul.</p>
<p>This was the quiet collapse of a man who had watched his entire moral universe be violated and found himself powerless to stop the desecration.</p>
<p>"I am not going away," she said to the door, her voice dropping to a fierce whisper. "You know that about me."</p>
<p>Even with all the effort with a different kind of talk... it’s still nothing. The door remained a sealed tomb.</p>
<p>She stood there, one hand braced against the wood and the other pressed hard against her forehead, feeling the weight of her own helplessness. That was when Iris appeared in the corridor, her footsteps silent, her presence as sharp and precise as a blade.</p>
<p>"Let him," Iris said.</p>
<p>Aurelia turned, her eyes narrowing. "Let him what?"</p>
<p>"Let him sit in there," Iris said, her gaze fixed on the closed door. "Let him skip the meals, skip the training, and wallow in whatever hell he has constructed for himself."</p>
<p>"You knocking on that door four times every morning isn’t pulling him back to the light, Aurelia."</p>
<p>"It is giving him an audience for his misery... You are providing a stage for his shame, and that is exactly what is keeping it alive."</p>
<p>"He is my son," Aurelia snapped, the maternal instinct rising like a defensive reflex. "I cannot simply abandon him to this darkness."</p>
<p>"I know," Iris said.</p>
<p>Her voice, usually clipped and devoid of inflection, carried an unexpected note of something almost tender beneath the steel. "I am not telling you to stop caring about him."</p>
<p>"I am telling you that some kinds of weakness need to be allowed to run their course before anyone can build strength back on top of them."</p>
<p>"You taught me that yourself, years ago, when I was the one behind a closed door after my first real loss in the field."</p>
<p>"You didn’t knock four times a morning..."</p>
<p>"You didn’t demand I face the sun."</p>
<p>"You told me once that you would be there when I was ready, and then you left me alone until I actually was."</p>
<p>Aurelia remained motionless, suspended in that heavy, ringing silence that follows a truth too sharp to ignore. It was the specific quiet of a woman being handed back her own wisdom at a moment when she had forgotten she possessed it, a bitter mirror held up to her own past.</p>
<p>Her jaw tightened, the muscles working beneath her skin as she struggled to maintain the fiction of her authority.</p>
<p>"That is different," she said finally, though the words lacked their usual steel.</p>
<p>They sounded hollow and defensive, like a shield that had developed hairline fractures. "This isn’t a tactical retreat, Iris... This is... this is him rotting."</p>
<p>"It is not," Iris countered immediately, her voice cutting through Aurelia’s denial like a scalpel through flesh. "He lost something."</p>
<p>"He lost the illusion of safety, the illusion of control, and the illusion of the woman he thought he knew."</p>
<p>"It does not matter that the ’something’—that person, that connection—was never truly his to own."</p>
<p>"The loss is real to him and the violation is real."</p>
<p>"The humiliation he feels every time he closes his eyes and hears Rex’s laughter is real. Let him be weak in there for as long as he needs to be weak."</p>
<p>"Let him feel the full weight of that weakness until it becomes unbearable."</p>
<p>Iris took a step closer, her eyes locking onto Aurelia’s with unsettling intensity. "He will come out when staying in becomes worse than coming out."</p>
<p>"Human nature abhors a vacuum, and it abhors stagnation even more."</p>
<p>"Everyone breaks, Aurelia, but everyone also rebuilds."</p>
<p>"But they can only rebuild when they stop trying to preserve the broken pieces... If you keep knocking, you’re asking him to show you his wounds before they’ve even finished bleeding."</p>
<p>"You’re forcing him to perform his grief for you."</p>
<p>Aurelia looked at the door one last time. In her mind’s eye, she didn’t see a bedroom; she saw a tomb where her son was burying himself alive, surrounded by the ghosts of Diana’s screams and Rex’s mocking triumph.</p>
<p>She could almost feel the phantom vibrations of that night through the wood, the wet, rhythmic sounds of a world being torn apart. She imagined Theo curled in the corner of that room, his eyes bloodshot, his mind replaying the moment Diana’s gaze had shifted from love to that vacant, pervy worship of the man who had destroyed them both.</p>
<p>’I want to break the door down,’ she thought, a violent impulse rising in her chest.</p>
<p>’I want to drag him out by his hair, force him to face the sun, force him to fight until his knuckles bleed and his ribs crack, just so he remembers that he is alive.’ But she knew that violence would only feed the monster Rex had awakened inside Theo.</p>
<p>Slowly, deliberately, she let her hand fall away from the wood. The contact ended with a soft, final thud.</p>
<p>She exhaled a long, shuddering breath that seemed to drain the tension from her shoulders, the sound of a woman choosing to trust someone else’s cold judgment over the raw, bleeding pull of her own instinct.</p>
<p>She turned her back on the room, her spine straightening as she reclaimed her mantle of command, though the wound remained. She began walking down the corridor toward the training hall, each footstep echoing with a new, grim purpose.</p>
<p>"He had better come out on his own within the week," she said, the words directed more at the empty hallway than at Iris. It was a threat, a plea, and a deadline all wrapped into one.</p>
<p>"He will," Iris said, watching Aurelia’s retreating figure with the detached patience of a predator who understood the necessity of patience. "Or he will not."</p>
<p>"And if he doesn’t, then it becomes a different problem, one that requires a different kind of intervention."</p>
<p>"Either way, standing at that door and begging for a ghost to speak is not the answer today."</p>
