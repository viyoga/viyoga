<p>Theo reached the cliff’s edge, where the world simply ended.</p>
<p>The drop was staggering, a vertical abyss that seemed to swallow the very concept of ground. From this height, the Convergence Waters were invisible, hidden beneath the veil of mist and shadow at the island’s base, but their presence was unmistakable—a low-frequency hum that vibrated in his molars.</p>
<p>To his right, the great waterfall that fed from the agricultural district’s irrigation overflow roared with a deafening, relentless fury. It was white, continuous, and utterly indifferent to the broken man standing on its precipice.</p>
<p>Theo stood at the edge and stared into the void.</p>
<p>He had been a reincarnator for three years in this world and a man for twenty-six years in the last. His previous life had been a mediocre tragedy, the story of a man who existed in the perpetual ’almost.’</p>
<p>He had been good enough at enough things to believe the gap between who he was and who he wanted to be was closing, but never good enough to actually bridge it. He had spent twenty-six years chasing a horizon that kept retreating.</p>
<p>When he had woken up in this world, he had harbored the desperate, pathetic hope that this time, the gap would close. That this life would be the one where effort equaled outcome.</p>
<p>But his system had been cruel in its honesty. It hadn’t granted him a combat class or a mage class.</p>
<p>Instead, it had given him a designation built for sensing and analyzing the reincarnator’s equivalent of being told he was destined to be the strategist who understood the battlefield but never the warrior who won it.</p>
<p>He was built to observe the slaughter, not to inflict it.</p>
<p>For years, he had labored to develop those senses, building a web of connections and investments that his system insisted were the keys to his survival.</p>
<p>Diana had been the sun at the center of that web.</p>
<p>She wasn’t just a person; she was a calibrated objective. Diana Von Starlight was the ’heroine,’ the one whose presence was designed by the system to force his maximum growth through the singular, desperate need to protect her.</p>
<p>He had poured all those years of his soul into her safety, into her happiness, into being the foundation she could rely on.</p>
<p>Rex had taken her in six months. Rex hadn’t invested a single moment in planning, strategy, or protection.</p>
<p>Rex had simply existed, and his raw, violent gravity had pulled her in like a collapsing star.</p>
<p>Theo unclenched his hands, and the sting of pain finally registered. His palms were raw, bleeding from where his nails had been gouging into his flesh since he started the climb, leaving crimson rivulets that dried in the mountain wind.</p>
<p>"It is not," he said, his voice cracking against the thunder of the waterfall, "my fault that I am like this."</p>
<p>The water didn’t answer. The wind only tore at his clothes.</p>
<p>"I did not choose to be this pathetic," he screamed at the empty sky, the words tearing at his throat. "I chose to try not to be like this!"</p>
<p>"That is what I did for twenty-six years in that hellhole of a world, and another twenty years more in this one!"</p>
<p>"And the result?! The fucking result is that I am still exactly the same!"</p>
<p>He turned his gaze to the terrifying drop, the black maw of the abyss.</p>
<p>"And everyone who told me that trying was what mattered..." A bitter, hysterical laugh escaped his lips, tasting like copper and bile. "Everyone who said ’just keep trying’ was a liar."</p>
<p>"Because here is what actually happens when you try for a lot of fucking years of accumulated failure!"</p>
<p>"You end up standing at the edge of a cliff, watching everything you bled for being occupied by someone who never even had to try."</p>
<p>He pointed a shaking, blood-stained finger toward the island’s center, toward the Nightwing estate that now felt like a foreign country.</p>
<p>"It’s all gone," he whispered, the rage collapsing into something far more dangerous: hollow despair. "I lost Diana. I watched her eyes change when she looked at him."</p>
<p>"I lost Iris. I lost my grandmother, and... worse of them all... I lost my mother’s respect for that steel foundation I thought was unshakable."</p>
<p>"I lost the household’s respect... I lost my room."</p>
<p>His breathing became ragged, each inhale a struggle against the crushing weight in his chest. The inner monologue that had sustained him as the strategist and the analyst was screaming at him to retreat, to plan, and to analyze his way out of this.</p>
<p>But there was no data left to analyze. There was only loss.</p>
<p>"I lost the heir’s position in operational terms, even if no one has had the courage to say the words out loud yet," he choked out, tears finally mixing with the blood on his cheeks. "Because those words require Grandfather to say them."</p>
<p>"And Grandfather is not here. And when he finally returns from whatever mission he’s on, it will already be over."</p>
<p>"The throne will be occupied. The doors will be locked."</p>
<p>"And my room... my room will already belong to someone else."</p>
<p>Theo turned his gaze away from the distant lights of the Nightwing estate and fixed it on the roaring white fury of the waterfall. The water was a wall of liquid violence, crushing itself against the rocks below with mindless, eternal power.</p>
<p>"I was supposed to be the hero of this story," he said, his voice barely audible over the thunder, yet carrying the sharp edge of a blade. "That is what the system was for."</p>
<p>"That is what reincarnating was supposed to mean."</p>
<p>A bitter smile twisted his features, one that didn’t reach his hollow eyes. "In every story I read back home, before the lights went out and I died in that godforsaken world, this was the moment."</p>
<p>"The hero stands at his lowest, broken and betrayed, staring into the abyss."</p>
<p>"The setbacks were supposed to be the setup."</p>
<p>"The pain was supposed to be the crucible."</p>
<p>"This moment, this absolute fucking devastation, was supposed to be where the arc started rising."</p>
<p>His eyes drifted back to the black maw of the cliff’s edge.</p>
<p>"But I have been at the low point for years," he whispered, the words tasting like ash. "The arc isn’t rising."</p>
<p>"There is no arc... There is no grand redemption waiting around the corner."</p>
<p>"There is no hidden power awakening in my veins."</p>
<p>"There is just this. Just the wind, the water, and the truth."</p>
<p>’You’re pathetic, Theo,’ his own mind hissed at him, the voice of his internal analyst turning cruel, turning venomous. ’You analyzed every variable, you calculated every risk, and you invested every ounce of yourself into a game where you never even had the right pieces.’</p>
<p>’You’re not a protagonist.’</p>
<p>’You’re just an extra who stayed on screen too long.’</p>
<p>He raised his fist, staring at the crimson smear across his palm. The blood was already drying in the biting morning air, flaking into dark, scabbed patterns that looked like cracked earth.</p>
<p>"I am a failure," he declared, the admission feeling strangely liberating, like a final confession before execution. "In two worlds. Across two lifetimes."</p>
<p>"No matter how many times I reset, no matter how many chances I get, this is the immutable truth of my existence."</p>
<p>"Whatever I am, wherever I go, whatever I attempt, this is what happens."</p>
<p>"I reach for everything, and I grasp nothing but air."</p>
<p>He opened his hand slowly, deliberately. The dried blood flakes peeled away from his skin, caught by a gust of wind and scattered into the abyss like worthless confetti.</p>
<p>"I am tired," he said, the volume dropping to a ghostly murmur. "I am so goddamn tired of being this person."</p>
<p>"I’m tired of pretending that ’trying’ matters."</p>
<p>"I’m tired of the hope that kills more effectively than any blade."</p>
<p>He didn’t hesitate. Hesitation was for people who still had something to lose.</p>
<p>Theo took one deliberate step forward.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, the cliff’s edge received his weight, holding him in a precarious balance between existence and nothingness.</p>
<p>Then, it did not.</p>
<p>Gravity claimed him with terrifying suddenness.</p>
<p>The world inverted. The sky was above him and below him simultaneously, a disorienting, nauseating kaleidoscope of blue and gray.</p>
<p>His stomach lurched upward into his throat as the wind screamed past his ears, tearing at his clothes like claws. In that suspended, terrifying moment of freefall, the split second where the body had not yet processed that it was dying, Theo simply closed his eyes.</p>
<p>He welcomed the darkness. He begged for the impact to shatter the failure that was his soul.</p>
<p>’Finally,’ he thought as the rush of air became a roar, it ended.</p>
<p>But the impact he expected—the bone-shattering crunch of stone, the wet explosion of organs against the canyon floor—did not come.</p>
<p>Instead, there was light.</p>
<p>It wasn’t the flickering, chaotic static of a brain shutting down from trauma, nor the tunnel vision of approaching unconsciousness.</p>
<p>This was different.</p>
<p>This was a light that possessed intent. It was steady, absolute, and possessed a terrifyingly precise frequency, as if the universe itself had decided to stop pretending and reveal something that had been hidden behind the veil of his reality.</p>
<p>It was a light that didn’t just illuminate; it commanded.</p>
<p>Theo’s consciousness, pulled from the brink of annihilation, jerked violently back to the surface. His lungs burned as if he had been underwater for hours.</p>
<p>Theo opened his eyes.</p>
