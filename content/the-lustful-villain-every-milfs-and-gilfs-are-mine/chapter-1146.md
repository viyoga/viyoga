<p>The three spears streaked through the smoke-choked air like silver lightning, each aimed at a different vital point: throat, heart, and groin. To any other warrior, it would have been a fatal triangulation, a coordinated execution attempt.</p>
<p>But Rex didn’t move to dodge. He didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>Instead, he extended his right hand. He didn’t grasp the shafts; he seized the space around them.</p>
<p>A micro thin layer of molecular precision telekinesis snapped into existence, creating an invisible cage of force that intercepted all three projectiles mid-flight.</p>
<p>VROOOOM THUD.</p>
<p>The spears stopped dead in midair, millimeters from his mask, frozen in time. They hung there, quivering violently against the unseen barrier, the kinetic energy of the throw still rippling through the wood.</p>
<p>Rex stared at them with the detached, clinical interest of a scientist examining a specimen under a microscope. He analyzed their vectors, their velocity, and the desperation etched into their trajectories.</p>
<p>’Too predictable,’ he thought. ’Too emotional.’</p>
<p>’You’re throwing away your lives like they’re worthless.’</p>
<p>’Let me show you what happens when you actually waste something precious.’</p>
<p>With a flick of his wrist, a motion as casual as shooing away a fly, he reversed the momentum. The telekinetic force didn’t just push them back; it amplified their speed by a factor of five, sending them shrieking back toward their owners with devastating force.</p>
<p>SHRAK THWIP!</p>
<p>The first thrower didn’t even have time to gasp before his own spear punched through his left thigh, splintering the femur with a sound like a gunshot. He collapsed instantly, screaming a wet, ragged sound as blood geysered from the wound, painting the cracked stone crimson.</p>
<p>SQUELCH.</p>
<p>The second spear found its mark with unerring accuracy, driving through his right shoulder and embedding itself three inches deep into the wall behind him.</p>
<p>The man was pinned to the masonry like an insect in a display case, his face draining of color as he hung there, convulsing in shock.</p>
<p>The third soldier, perhaps blessed by some distant deity, managed to twist his torso at the last possible microsecond. The spear missed his sternum but tore through the thick meat of his upper arm, ripping through muscle fibers and tendons.</p>
<p>He fell to his knees, clutching the ruined limb, his breath coming in desperate, sobbing gasps. It was a mercy, a calculated, precise adjustment in Rex’s internal equation.</p>
<p>This wasn’t cruelty. It wasn’t even sadism.</p>
<p>To Rex, this was simply arithmetic.</p>
<p>’Kill enough to break the assault,’ he reasoned, his mind working with terrifying coldness. ’But not enough to create a legend of invincibility that demands a hero.’</p>
<p>’If they think I am a god, they will fight with martyrdom.’</p>
<p>’If they think I am a monster, they will fight with fear.’</p>
<p>’Fear is more useful. Fear makes men run.’</p>
<p>’Men who run can be hunted. Men who die as martyrs become unbreakable.’</p>
<p>He needed to maintain the illusion of being a man, a very dangerous, very lethal man, but not a supernatural inevitability.</p>
<p>Cripple where possible.</p>
<p>Disable where necessary.</p>
<p>Terrify always.</p>
<p>Kill only when the math demanded it.</p>
<p>The difference would matter enormously when this war’s official history was written by the very people who would eventually trust him.</p>
<p>The slaughter was becoming too loud, too messy. It was time to change the texture of the battlefield.</p>
<p>Rex reached into the darkest recesses of his power: Sovereign Necromancy.</p>
<p>He didn’t unleash it in a torrent of bone and shadow; he used it like a scalpel. He drew just enough essence from the recently deceased to twist their lingering echoes back into the world.</p>
<p>Rrrrrip... Shhhhh.</p>
<p>Eleven corpses began to stir from the blood-slicked floor. They rose in a loose, unnatural arc across the front of his advance.</p>
<p>These weren’t the theatrical, snarling zombies of folklore. There were no glowing eyes, no roars of rage.</p>
<p>They rose with the slack, hollow posture of marionettes whose strings had been cut and reattached by an amateur. They stood with empty sockets staring at nothing, their skin a pale, bruised grey.</p>
<p>They did not attack. Rex had not given them that command.</p>
<p>Instead, they simply... walked.</p>
<p>A slow, relentless, shambling advance that cut through the chaos like a cold fog. Each step they took toward the living soldiers was accompanied by the wet slap of dead feet on gore-soaked stone.</p>
<p>"Stay back! Get away from them!" a sergeant screamed, but his voice lacked conviction.</p>
<p>His sword hand was shaking so violently the blade rattled against his shield.</p>
<p>The psychological weight was unbearable. The living soldiers found themselves unable to maintain their formation.</p>
<p>How do you charge into a line when the front rank consists of your own dead friends?</p>
<p>How do you thrust a spear through a man whose face you recognized from the mess hall only yesterday?</p>
<p>"I can’t... I can’t do this..." A young recruit whimpered, dropping his shield with a clang that sounded like a death knell.</p>
<p>He turned and bolted into the darkness behind the lines.</p>
<p>Another soldier, a seasoned veteran who had survived three campaigns, suddenly doubled over, retching violently onto the floor as his stomach rebelled against the sight of his fallen comrade walking toward him with a vacant, eyeless stare. He couldn’t even lift his weapon.</p>
<p>The corridor’s central advance, the backbone of Aethelgard’s desperate push, disintegrated. In under twenty seconds, a disciplined military unit had dissolved into a panicked mass of terrified individuals.</p>
<p>And the most terrifying part? Not one of Rex’s constructs had landed a single blow.</p>
<p>They simply existed as a reminder that even death was now under his command.</p>
<p>Beneath the reinforced soles of Rex’s boots, something far more insidious than brute force was at work. His Earthen Divine Authority ran in a continuous, silent loop, bleeding into the very foundation of the corridor.</p>
<p>To the Aethelgard soldiers, the floor appeared unchanged; the stone was still cold, still solid, still gray. But physics itself was being subtly sabotaged.</p>
<p>Every step Rex took sent an imperceptible harmonic resonance through the substrate of the corridor. Within a fifteen-meter radius of his position, the world became treacherous.</p>
<p>It wasn’t that the ground shook; it was that the ground became uncooperative. A soldier attempting to pivot would find his footing yielding by a fraction of a millimeter too far.</p>
<p>A commander trying to plant his sword for a thrust would find the resistance lacking, his weight transfer sluggish and imprecise.</p>
<p>Thump... Thump... Thump...</p>
<p>Each of Rex’s footsteps felt like a heartbeat echoing through the earth. Inside that field, the Aethelgard formation was fighting a war against their own momentum.</p>
<p>To them, it felt as though the air had thickened into syrup, as though every movement required an exhausting expenditure of effort. From Rex’s perspective, they moved like drowning men desperate, flailing, and fatally slow.</p>
<p>’Left flank is staggering,’ Rex noted, his mind processing the battlefield as a series of failing equations. ’Their reaction times have dropped by approximately twelve percent... Perfect.’</p>
<p>Then, the pressure changed. The ambient noise of screams and clattering armor fell away as a new, sharper magical signature pierced through the chaos.</p>
<p>An apostle had arrived.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t know his name. He wouldn’t learn it, nor would he care.</p>
<p>The man was a mid-tier asset in the divine network, drawing power from one of the lesser pantheon deities. By mortal standards, he was a walking catastrophe.</p>
<p>His magic wasn’t a crude fireball or a physical blade; he wielded a binding structure that operated on the soul’s adjacent register, a metaphysical tether designed to lock a target’s essence in place before they could even think to defend themselves.</p>
<p>The Apostle charged through the panicked ranks, his eyes blazing with holy fervor. "By the Light of the Third Throne, I bind you, demon—!"</p>
<p>Rex’s Divine Perception flickered to life. In the microsecond before the Apostle’s hands even formed the spell, Rex saw it.</p>
<p>He didn’t just see the magic; he saw its blueprint. He saw the way the energy braided together, the way it sought to anchor itself to the soul’s frequency, and the exact mathematical structure of the binding spell.</p>
<p>It was like reading an open book written in glowing ink.</p>
<p>’Interesting,’ Rex thought. ’A soul anchor.’</p>
<p>’High complexity, low adaptability. Let’s see how it handles a perfect counter.’</p>
<p>Full Potential Adaptation engaged instantly.</p>
<p>The Apostle unleashed the spell. A burst of prismatic light erupted from his palms, rushing toward Rex like a coiled serpent seeking to constrict.</p>
<p>The spell struck. For half a heartbeat, Rex felt a strange, phantom tightening across his very essence, a ghostly pressure that suggested his soul was being grasped by invisible hands.</p>
<p>But then, the analysis was completed.</p>
<p>The adaptation protocols wrapped around Rex’s spiritual signature like a flawless armor. The binding spell reached him, found no purchase, and simply... unraveled.</p>
<p>The intricate magical weave didn’t shatter; it dissolved. The energy bled away into nothingness, torn apart at the molecular level as if a loose thread had been pulled from a piece of silk.</p>
<p>The Apostle froze. The prismatic light died in his hands.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was more deafening than the battle raging around them.</p>
<p>Rex watched the man’s face. It was a masterpiece of psychological collapse.</p>
<p>In two seconds, a decade of religious certainty, years of training, and the entire identity of a divine warrior evaporated.</p>
<p>The Apostle’s eyes went wide, pupils contracting as he stared at his own hands, then at Rex, then at the empty air where his greatest weapon should have been. The realization that he wasn’t just outmatched and that he was fundamentally irrelevant was worse than any wound.</p>
<p>"How..." the Apostle whispered, his voice cracking, stripped of all divine authority. "How can you... how is this possible?"</p>
