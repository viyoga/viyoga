<p>Zane had drifted back to the plaza’s edge the moment the sky began to scream. As Ignivara and Rex ascended into the stratosphere, he hadn’t just retreated; he had recalculated.</p>
<p>He repositioned with the cold, surgical intelligence of a grandmaster shifting a piece on a board, his eyes tracking the changing geometry of the battlefield. His Void working hummed at a low, predatory frequency, a passive spatial displacement that rippled the air around him like heat haze, constantly scanning for the exact insertion points he would need to strike if the heavens decided to fall.</p>
<p>His breathing was a rhythmic, terrifyingly controlled tempo. He had shed the heavy, forward-weighted stance he had used against Tremor, shifting into a loose, lateral posture, the stance of a man who was no longer bracing for a hammer but preparing to dance with a blade.</p>
<p>He was reading Apollo. He was dissecting him.</p>
<p>"You are faster than I expected," Zane said, his voice carrying across the distance with a chilling clarity.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a compliment; it was a cold data point being filed into a mental ledger.</p>
<p>"You repositioned while the energy was still settling in my veins," Apollo countered, his voice steady despite the lingering tremor in his limbs. "You didn’t just wait... but you reassessed the entire board."</p>
<p>"I reassessed while watching you accept a half measure," Zane said, his eyes narrowing. "Most Apostles would have demanded a full restoration, clinging to the safety of peak capacity before stepping back into the fray."</p>
<p>"You accepted the partial, and I do am surprised that you judged it sufficient."</p>
<p>"Because it is sufficient," Apollo snapped, the heat of his conviction finally breaking through his exhaustion.</p>
<p>"That is the judgment I am accounting for," Zane replied smoothly. "And that is exactly why you are dangerous, Apollo."</p>
<p>"You act on instinct and ’honor,’ while the world acts on reality."</p>
<p>Apollo felt a shiver go down his spine, the distinct, unsettling sensation of being understood by someone who intended to use that understanding as a weapon. It wasn’t the warmth of being known; it was the coldness of being mapped.</p>
<p>"You’re doing it again," Apollo said, his voice dropping an octave, turning heavy with a simmering resentment. "You’re looking at Rex and seeing a monster because it’s easier than looking at yourselves and seeing the architects of this chaos."</p>
<p>Zane didn’t flinch. "If you are referring to his brutality in the sky, then yes."</p>
<p>"He is a force of unbridled destruction..."</p>
<p>"He didn’t just defeat Ignivara; he attempted to erase her."</p>
<p>"That kind of power, untethered from the traditional hierarchies of the Academy, is a threat to the very fabric of our order."</p>
<p>"The ’order’?" Apollo let out a sharp, bitter laugh that sounded like breaking glass. "Is that what you call it? The Legion of Anti-Reincarnators calls it ’order,’ too?!"</p>
<p>"You and Ignivara... you represent a system built on the systematic execution of anyone who doesn’t fit your narrow, stagnant definition of ’natural.’"</p>
<p>Zane’s expression hardened, the air around him growing heavy as his Void energy reacted to his rising tension. "We represent stability."</p>
<p>"We protect the sanctity of the world from the anomalies that threaten to unbalance it."</p>
<p>"Reincarnators are variables we cannot control, Apollo."</p>
<p>"They are cracks in the foundation."</p>
<p>"They are people!" Apollo roared, the sound echoing off the ruined walls of the plaza. "Veran, Ren, Brant... they weren’t ’variables.’ They were souls!"</p>
<p>"They were students, friends, and comrades who believed in the Academy, only to be hunted down like animals by the very people sworn to protect the balance!"</p>
<p>"You call Rex a monster because he has the power to fight back? Because he refuses to be a ’variable’ that you can just delete?"</p>
<p>"He is a predator masquerading as a student," Zane countered, stepping forward, his presence expanding like a shadow swallowing the light. "He possesses the same terrifying, unpredictable essence as Tremor!"</p>
<p>"He doesn’t obey the laws of energy; he breaks them. If he can dismantle a dragon with that much casual cruelty, what makes you think he won’t dismantle us once he’s finished with his enemies?"</p>
<p>"Because Rex fights to survive!" Apollo stepped toward him, his eyes blazing with a fierce, righteous fury. "He fights because the world gave him no other choice!"</p>
<p>"You and the Legion... you’re the ones who create the monsters!"</p>
<p>"You hunt the Reincarnators, you push them to the brink, you strip them of their homes and their names, and then you have the audacity to act surprised when they turn into gods of destruction!"</p>
<p>Apollo gestured wildly toward the distant, smoking crater where the dragon had fallen. "You see a man who is untrustworthy!"</p>
<p>"I see a man who is finally, for the first time, truly free of your lies!"</p>
<p>"You call him a threat to the ’order,’ but the ’order’ is a lie meant to keep the strong in check and the different in graves!"</p>
<p>"If being ’honorable’ means standing with the man who just tore the sky apart, then call me a fool!" Apollo gritted his teeth. "But at least my hands aren’t stained with the blood of those you called ’anomalies’ just to keep your precious world quiet!"</p>
<p>The tension between them was a physical weight, a coiled spring ready to snap. Zane stared at Apollo, his eyes cold and unyielding, while Apollo stood his ground, a lone bastion of defiance against the very system that had tried to erase him.</p>
<p>"Then you have chosen your side, Apollo," Zane said, his voice a deadly whisper. "And God help you when the ’anomalies’ decide they are finished playing by your rules."</p>
<p>"I have the goddess of life by my side...!"</p>
<p>A flicker of something passed over Zane’s face. It wasn’t quite guilt. Guilt was a heavy, messy emotion for a man of his discipline, but it was the shadow of it.</p>
<p>It was that specific, hollow tension people carried when they had made a monumental decision and were still forced to live with the weight of it every single day.</p>
<p>"I did not plan the canyon," Zane said, his voice cutting through the settling dust like a blade. "I did not orchestrate the carnage."</p>
<p>"I was the observer... I was gathering intelligence and transmitting it..."</p>
<p>"What Celestina chose to do with that data... that was a variable beyond my control."</p>
<p>"You knew exactly who you were transmitting to," Apollo countered, his voice trembling not with weakness, but with a suppressed, volcanic rage.</p>
<p>He took a step forward, the cracked stone crunching beneath his boots. "You didn’t just send reports, Zane."</p>
<p>"You fed the beast. You knew the Legion’s operational purpose!"</p>
<p>"You knew that every ’data point’ you sent was a death warrant for someone like me."</p>
<p>Zane didn’t flinch, but his Void energy rippled more violently, a dark, swirling aura that seemed to swallow the light around his feet. "I knew they were eliminating Reincarnators."</p>
<p>"And yes, I believed the argument Celestina presented to me: that the Reincarnators being purged were the ones whose presence in Erosyne created the highest risk of systemic collapse!"</p>
<p>"It was a logical argument. It was an unreasonable necessity."</p>
<p>"And now?" Apollo challenged, his eyes boring into Zane’s. "Now that the sky is bleeding? Now that the ’necessity’ has turned the world into a slaughterhouse?"</p>
<p>Zane’s gaze drifted. He looked at the devastation of the plaza, at the scorched earth where the battle had raged.</p>
<p>He looked at Morwenna, who was struggling to find her footing amidst the ruins, and then toward the gate where Valentina stood like a silent sentinel. His eyes finally settled on the distant, smoldering horizon where Rex had just dismantled a god.</p>
<p>"Now," Zane said, his voice dropping to a low, haunting register, "I am standing in a plaza that looks like a graveyard, staring at the fact that the very people the Legion was supposed to be ’limiting’ the Underlayer’s Reincarnator network are the ones who actually caused this carnage."</p>
<p>"The cost... the cost of our ’stability’ is starting to outweigh the value of the peace we’ve bought."</p>
<p>Apollo felt a grim sense of vindication, but it tasted like ash. "That is a very careful, very surgical way to phrase a change of position, Zane."</p>
<p>"You’re still talking like a strategist, even when you’re admitting you might have been wrong."</p>
<p>"Because my positions are never uncomplicated," Zane snapped, his composure finally fraying at the edges.</p>
<p>A small, sharp crack echoed as a piece of debris near his foot suddenly imploded under the pressure of his mana. "I never promised simplicity!"</p>
<p>"That is why I spent fourteen months transmitting rather than acting!"</p>
<p>"I was waiting for certainty!" Zane clenched his fists. "I was waiting for enough proof to ensure that when I finally committed to an irreversible action, it wouldn’t be a mistake."</p>
<p>"Fourteen months," Apollo repeated, the number feeling like a physical weight between them. "Fourteen months of watching people die while you waited for a ’certainty’ that never came."</p>
<p>"Is a long time to be uncertain," Zane admitted, his gaze dropping to the blood stained ground. "I am painfully aware of that."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy, suffocating. The air was still charged with the residual energy of the fight above the smell of ozone, the metallic tang of blood, and the lingering, terrifying echo of Rex’s power.</p>
<p>Apollo looked at Zane, and for a moment, he didn’t see an enemy or a strategist. He saw a man caught in the gears of a machine he helped build.</p>
<p>Apollo exhaled, a slow, controlled breath that emptied his lungs of the last of his frustration. He wasn’t finished with Zane.</p>
<p>He wasn’t finished with the Legion, or the Academy, or the lies that had fueled the last year of his life. But the time for debating the ghosts of the canyon had passed.</p>
<p>"Fine then... have it your way."</p>
