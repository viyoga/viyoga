<p>Rex looked at him with the flat patience of someone who had finished a task and was giving the result time to settle before moving to the next one.</p>
<p>Zane was on the platform floor with both knees down and his right wrist held against his chest, which was not a defensive posture but the posture of someone managing several simultaneous points of damage and having to prioritize which ones got attention first. His breathing was the controlled, deliberate kind that people produced when normal breathing was producing pain and they had made the adjustment without choosing to.</p>
<p>Three ribs. Rex had felt them give in sequence during the third exchange, each one a distinct structural failure as the Peak Physique’s output found the cumulative limit of what Zane’s body could absorb and absorbed it anyway.</p>
<p>The knee strike to the base of his spine had not broken anything but would be producing the specific radiating pain that took hours to fully surface and made standing difficult once it did.</p>
<p>The reaping blade was on the platform floor approximately two meters from Zane’s right hand. He had not reached for it.</p>
<p>Rex had not moved it. The distance between Zane and the blade was a decision Zane had made in the half-second after the controlling grip released, and the decision communicated something that the words had not yet had time to.</p>
<p>Rex waited, and then he used some of his Supreme Healing so that Zane at least could talk, but of course, he gave some warning. "One wrong move and you’re going straight to the afterlife."</p>
<p>And Zane didn’t move at all other than lowering his head even more to accept defeat and the mercy.</p>
<p>"Now..."</p>
<p>"The void working," Rex said. "How many more?"</p>
<p>Zane looked up at him. The expression on his face was not the composed assessment he had carried through the approach and the initial exchange.</p>
<p>That version of Zane’s expression had disappeared somewhere in the third or fourth minute of the fight, replaced by the look of someone who has encountered a ceiling they did not know existed until they hit it.</p>
<p>"Three," Zane said.</p>
<p>His voice was steady, which was an effort. "I used three."</p>
<p>"So two discharges left," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Two," Zane said.</p>
<p>He exhaled slowly, the controlled exhale of someone managing rib pain. "At full output, I can manage maybe three, but only at partial capacity."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him.</p>
<p>"Why are you telling me that?" Rex said.</p>
<p>Zane held his gaze for a moment.</p>
<p>"Because you asked," he said. "And because I’ve been on the ground for forty seconds and you haven’t done anything with the time except wait, which means you’re giving me time to talk, and if you’re giving me time to talk, it’s because you want to hear what I say, and lying to someone who just demonstrated they can end this conversation whenever they choose seems like the wrong use of the opportunity."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing, but then he laughed for a bit.</p>
<p>"Good."</p>
<p>"You’re going to do it anyway," Zane said. "Whatever you’ve decided. The talking doesn’t change it."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Then why are we talking?"</p>
<p>"Because you’re the only person who has engaged me honestly tonight," Rex said. "I’m giving you a moment to acknowledge the quality of your own assessment."</p>
<p>Zane looked at him, and his expression reflected a change that had occurred since the fight, specifically the look of someone who has had their understanding of the world altered in an unexpected way.</p>
<p>"You were operating below your ceiling," he said.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I thought I understood what that meant when I watched you from the lower district," Zane said. "The engagements you were running looked efficient. Precise. Fast. I thought sixty percent was a high estimate."</p>
<p>He paused, and the pause had the quality of someone recalculating.</p>
<p>"It wasn’t a high estimate," he said.</p>
<p>"No," Rex said.</p>
<p>"You were running at sixty percent, and I couldn’t land a clean hit," Zane said. "I found two openings in the whole exchange..."</p>
<p>"Two... Against someone at sixty percent who was letting me generate offense." He looked at the platform floor for a moment. "What does a hundred feel like?"</p>
<p>It was not quite a question. It was a specific statement from someone who had reached a number and was reflecting on its implications.</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>"Right," Zane said.</p>
<p>He shifted his position slightly, moving from both knees to one, which was the adjustment of someone trying to assess whether standing was an available option and deciding it was not quite yet.</p>
<p>"I was watching you in the city," Zane said. "Before the spire..."</p>
<p>’I saw what you did in the commercial district..."</p>
<p>"The earth-manipulation contact, the way you closed the substrate access before the speech so he had nowhere to go when he ran the read."</p>
<p>"That was setup, not reaction."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"You closed access points before you made the announcement," Zane said. "Before anyone was running."</p>
<p>"Which means you had already mapped every viable exit in this city and addressed them before you gave anyone a reason to use them."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>Zane looked at him with the expression that was no longer the assessment of a combatant but the assessment of someone who was understanding the full shape of what they had walked into.</p>
<p>"This wasn’t a purge," Zane said. "A purge is reactive."</p>
<p>"You deal with the problem after it surfaces."</p>
<p>He paused.</p>
<p>"This was a demolition," he said. "You designed its structure before anyone even knew it existed."</p>
<p>’The speech, the marking, the sealed exits, the militia positioning, everything..."</p>
<p>"It was already built when you gave the speech."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him with the patient attention of someone who was waiting to see where the assessment went.</p>
<p>"You’ve been building this for months," Zane said. "From outside the city... From the surface..."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>Zane absorbed this.</p>
<p>"Why?" he said.</p>
<p>The word felt smaller than the rest of the conversation, reflecting the specific question of someone who had understood the how and was now confronting the what of the why.</p>
<p>"Because the Underlayer needs to become something it is not currently," Rex said. "And the gap between what it is and what it needs to be is not a gap that patient governance closes."</p>
<p>"It requires a different mechanism."</p>
<p>"You’re the mechanism," Zane said.</p>
<p>"For this part," Rex said.</p>
<p>Zane looked at him for a long moment, his expression shifting to something that was not quite fear in the conventional sense but included several of its components, specifically the recognition of someone who has faced a level of intent they were not equipped to counter.</p>
<p>"What happens to me now...?" he said. "After I give you everything you wanted..."</p>
<p>"Does the mercy... still count?"</p>
<p>"You’re not marked," Rex said. "Your system profile is utility-class, and your combat capability is the specific kind that the post-reconstruction forces will need."</p>
<p>He released the controlling grip he had maintained and stood, giving Zane the space to make his own assessment of the offer.</p>
<p>Zane stayed on the platform floor for a moment. He looked at the reaping blade two meters away and made the decision not to reach for it, which was a decision Rex had been waiting to see him make.</p>
<p>He pushed himself upright slowly, managing the rib damage with the careful movement of someone who had mapped the pain points and was routing around them.</p>
<p>Standing cost him something visible, but he stood anyway. Thanks to Rex’s healing.</p>
<p>"You can keep fighting the reconstruction," Rex said. "Or you can fight for it."</p>
<p>Zane was quiet. He reached down and picked up the reaping blade with his left hand, not his dominant hand, and turned it once to check the edge.</p>
<p>Then he sheathed it.</p>
<p>"The void working," he said. "At range, above forty meters, it suppresses dimensional stability in a localized area."</p>
<p>"Teleportation and spatial displacement become unreliable within the affected zone."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him.</p>
<p>"Eleven minutes at forty meters," Zane continued. "Shorter at greater range... and longer at closer."</p>
<p>He held Rex’s gaze with the controlled steadiness of someone who had made a decision and was committing to it. "Standard elemental readers can’t detect it before deployment."</p>
<p>"Anything above that threshold can."</p>
<p>"Why are you telling me this?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"Because you previously inquired about how the void works," Zane said. "And because if I’m going to be useful to you, you should know what the actual specifications are rather than the estimate you could make from watching me use it."</p>
<p>Rex held his gaze for a moment.</p>
<p>"I’ll find you when the restructuring review begins," Rex said.</p>
<p>Zane nodded once, the specific nod of someone who had received a clear instruction and accepted it, and walked toward the shaft.</p>
<p>He stopped at the edge without looking back.</p>
<p>"The forty percent," he said.</p>
<p>His voice was quieter than it had been, the register of someone asking a question they were not certain they wanted the answer to. "What is it actually for?"</p>
<p>Rex looked at him.</p>
<p>"You’ll find out," Rex said, "when the time comes to use it."</p>
<p>"Thank you... for giving me mercy..." Zane bowed down. "I’ll pledge my alliance to you."</p>
<p>Zane stepped into the shaft and descended, and Rex watched him go and noted his movement pattern, the specific quality of someone who had been reduced to something and was rebuilding from it in real time.</p>
<p>Behind him, Mordecai said, quietly, "Who... was that...?"</p>
<p>"Someone useful," Rex said.</p>
<p>"He nearly killed me," Mordecai said.</p>
<p>"He was aiming for you," Rex said. "There’s a distinction."</p>
