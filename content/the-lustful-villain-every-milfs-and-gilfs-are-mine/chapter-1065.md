<p>Rex moved through the final stages of the engagement with the cold, methodical efficiency of a surgeon performing a necessary amputation.</p>
<p>There was no flourish. There was no sadistic glee. There was no theatrical grandstanding.</p>
<p>To Rex, this was not a battle of good versus evil, nor was it a moment of heroic triumph.</p>
<p>It was work.</p>
<p>The God of Time screamed a sound that tore through the fabric of the domain like serrated steel through silk as Rex systematically dismantled his remaining defenses.</p>
<p>Each divine strike Kaeleth threw was met not with a counterattack, but with a flawless, silent neutralization.</p>
<p>Rex walked through the storm of temporal anomalies as if they were nothing more than static on a screen, his body adjusting, his cells recalibrating, his mind absorbing the divine mathematics of failure.</p>
<p>When the final moment arrived, the domain itself seemed to hold its breath. The amber light dimmed, turning a bruised, sickly violet as the structural integrity of the space began to fail.</p>
<p>Rex stood before Kaeleth’s form, which was no longer a majestic god but a compressed, fractured husk of light and agony. The God’s essence was hemorrhaging, leaking raw chronological energy that crystallized in midair before shattering like ice.</p>
<p>Rex raised his hand. His eyes glowed with the cold fire of soul manipulation.</p>
<p>He didn’t seek to kill the entity; that would be messy and inefficient and would leave a vacuum that the divine framework would immediately fill with something worse. Instead, he targeted the precise metaphysical tendons that anchored Kaeleth to this reality.</p>
<p>Rex carried out the task: he carefully cut the entity’s links to the specific points that kept it active in Aethelgard’s dimension.</p>
<p>The impact was silent but absolute.</p>
<p>A psychic shockwave rippled outward, and suddenly, the weight of the domain vanished. The temporal domain’s influence on Aethelgard collapsed.</p>
<p>This wasn’t just the collapse of Rex’s attack that had already been neutralized by Lustia’s counter frequency. This was something deeper.</p>
<p>This was the removal of the ambient temporal authority that Kaeleth had been quietly weaving into the very foundation of Aethelgard for two years.</p>
<p>The low-level divine presence, the subtle pressure that had been causing the minute time discrepancies, and the invisible hand that had been tilting the world’s clock were gone.</p>
<p>The foundational layer’s temporal substrate shuddered and then settled, returning to its natural, unmanaged state. The ’clock’ of the world stopped being pushed by a divine finger and began to tick on its own.</p>
<p>Rex stood alone in the center of the dying domain.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s form remained, but it was diminished. He was a ghost of his former self, a fading echo clinging to the wreckage of his own authority.</p>
<p>The God looked less like a creator and more like a broken instrument, scarred at its structural foundation, struggling to maintain consciousness amidst the aftermath of his own annihilation.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at him, his shadow stretching long across the fractured amber floor. His breathing was steady, his pulse calm.</p>
<p>"Celestina’s contract is void," Rex said, each word landing with the weight of a closing tomb. "The legal fiction that allowed you to operate here has been erased."</p>
<p>"Your operational presence in Aethelgard’s layer is severed."</p>
<p>He stepped closer, the sound of his boots echoing in the hollow space where divinity used to reside.</p>
<p>"The time disruptions you have been managing through Celestina are going to continue," Rex continued, his voice devoid of threat because the outcome was already a certainty. "Reincarnators are not going to stop arriving."</p>
<p>"The anomalies they bring, the fractures they create in the timeline—those are going to continue producing exactly what they have always produced."</p>
<p>Rex leaned down, meeting Kaeleth’s fading, perceptual eyes. For the first time, the God saw not a mortal, but a new inevitability.</p>
<p>"The difference," Rex said, his voice dropping to a quiet, terrifying whisper, "is that going forward, they are going to produce what they produce in a world where the management approach is mine, not yours."</p>
<p>Rex reached into the empty space where his intent resided and produced the object he had been carrying since the streets—the artifact of their first collision.</p>
<p>In the soul register’s spatial representation, the temporal designation’s navigational component had externalized into a specific, jagged shape—the very thing Rex had hurled at Kaeleth as he breached the domain’s defenses.</p>
<p>It looked like a shard of broken time itself, pulsing with a dying, sickly light. Rex held it in his palm, feeling the residual heat of the divine energy that still clung to its edges like radioactive ash.</p>
<p>He looked at the object, then slowly raised his gaze to meet Kaeleth’s fractured, fading essence.</p>
<p>"Your Apostle," Rex said, his voice carrying the flat, hollow disappointment of a man who had witnessed a profound failure of judgment, "was a very sad choice."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavier than the gravity Rex had just unleashed. Kaeleth couldn’t even respond; the God could only watch with fading eyes as Rex opened his fingers.</p>
<p>The object fell. It didn’t just drop; it seemed to plummet through layers of reality, shattering into meaningless static before it even hit the divine floor.</p>
<p>"Go find a better one," Rex said.</p>
<p>The words weren’t an insult; they were a dismissal. A final verdict.</p>
<p>Rex turned his back on the God. He didn’t look back at the wreckage, the bleeding light, or the collapsing throne of eternity.</p>
<p>He simply walked.</p>
<p>He approached the boundary he had torn open to enter, and as he stepped through, he activated Dimensional Manipulation: Cross Layer Traversal.</p>
<p>The sensation was a violent, nauseating wrenching of his atoms as he was pulled through the medium change, feeling the pressure of two conflicting realities grinding against his skin.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, he was nowhere, suspended in the crushing void between layers, before the familiar, solid reality of the physical plane rushed back to claim him.</p>
<p>The transition was seamless. The sudden return of standard atmospheric pressure felt like a physical blow, but it was a welcome one.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the Nightwing household’s rear courtyard.</p>
<p>The world had resumed its heartbeat. The morning light was still the morning light, warm and indifferent to the cosmic execution that had just occurred.</p>
<p>From the distance, the rhythmic, mechanical sounds of reconstruction from the northern district drifted on the wind, the sound of a world rebuilding itself from the scars of yesterday.</p>
<p>A pigeon that had been frozen mid-flap in the temporal stasis now completed its circle, its wings beating steadily against the blue sky as if nothing had happened.</p>
<p>Rex stood motionless in the courtyard, his chest rising and falling in a slow, controlled rhythm. He breathed in the scent of damp earth and morning dew, grounding himself in the mundane.</p>
<p>’It wasn’t real,’ his mind whispered, a reflex from the adrenaline still coursing through his veins. ’It was an externalization.’</p>
<p>He reached into his jacket pocket, his fingers brushing against the fabric, and confirmed what his instincts already knew: the object he had held in Kaeleth’s domain had been nothing more than the Soul Manipulation’s externalization function.</p>
<p>It had never been a physical object carried through the portal; it had been a manifestation of his will, conjured from the raw data of the soul register. He had never truly brought anything into that domain but himself.</p>
<p>He looked down at his hands. They were steady.</p>
<p>No tremors. No divine residue.</p>
<p>On his wrist, the Mark of Lust glowed faintly before settling into its baseline pulse, a rhythmic reminder of the power he now wielded.</p>
<p>A series of cold, synthetic chimes echoed directly in his consciousness.</p>
