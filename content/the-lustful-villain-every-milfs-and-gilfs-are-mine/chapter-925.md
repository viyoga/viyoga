<p>Diana wandered through the sitting room’s outer doorway several minutes later, her breathing still slightly elevated from her own solitary training session. A light sheen of perspiration glistened on her skin, and her hair was pulled back in a practical, messy knot.</p>
<p>She stopped dead at the threshold when she saw the state of the room. The once elegant space had been transformed into something resembling a battlefield of intellect: scattered parchments, ink-stained fingers, and a heaviness in the air that spoke of desperate urgency.</p>
<p>"What in the hell did I walk into?" she asked, her voice carrying a note of genuine confusion as she surveyed the organized chaos.</p>
<p>"Divine artifact reconstruction," Lily answered immediately, not even lifting her gaze from the ancient text beneath her fingers. Her voice was flat with concentration. "Come help."</p>
<p>"I need someone to hold this volume open while I copy the relevant passage, and my hands are already full of references."</p>
<p>Diana didn’t argue. She crossed the room with the fluid grace of a soldier, stepping into the circle of lamplight.</p>
<p>But she didn’t remain a mere bystander for long. As Elizabeth droned on about harmonic frequencies and dimensional layers, Diana’s mind trained in the brutal mathematics of ballistics and combat began to process the data differently.</p>
<p>"Wait," Diana interrupted, leaning over one of the fragments where a series of jagged lines illustrated a resonance pattern.</p>
<p>She traced the curve with a callused finger. "This isn’t just some abstract magical wave..."</p>
<p>"Look at the way the energy spikes here, then tapers off sharply before the secondary oscillation."</p>
<p>"That pattern... it resembles the specific geometry of an archer’s draw weight curve at the point of release."</p>
<p>The room went deathly silent. Elizabeth froze mid-sentence, her quill hovering inches above a fresh sheet of parchment.</p>
<p>She stared at Diana’s finger, then back at the fragment, her analytical mind racing to reconcile the academic theory with the raw physical reality Diana had proposed.</p>
<p>"An archer’s draw weight curve," Elizabeth repeated slowly, her voice hushed as if speaking aloud might shatter the revelation.</p>
<p>"You’re suggesting the resonance isn’t a passive vibration but a mechanical tension, something that builds up force like a drawn bow before releasing into the dimensional layer. "A pause. "That is... an angle I had not considered."</p>
<p>"It’s almost disgustingly practical."</p>
<p>The door creaked again, and Theo appeared in the doorway. He lingered there for a moment, caught between the instinct to retreat and the magnetic pull of the gathering.</p>
<p>For days, he had been a ghost in his own home, drifting through corridors like someone who had been deliberately, painfully excluded from the world of the living.</p>
<p>He hesitated at the threshold, his shoulders tense.</p>
<p>"Do you need another set of hands?" he asked.</p>
<p>His voice was quiet, directed at the room in general, but his eyes immediately flickered to the far end of the table where his mother’s letters lay, a small, deliberate courtesy to Diana that didn’t go unnoticed. She saw the effort it took for him to acknowledge her presence without lingering on the pain in his expression.</p>
<p>"We need someone who can read the old military cataloguing shorthand," Elaris said, beckoning him forward with a wave of her hand. "The Nightwing archives use half-deciphered tactical notations from the old wars."</p>
<p>"You were the only one in this household who ever bothered to learn it properly, since your father gave up halfway through and I never had the patience for his sloppy scribbles."</p>
<p>Theo sank into an empty chair, and slowly, the room fell into a rhythm. For the next hour, the sitting room hummed with the specific, focused energy of people working toward a shared survival.</p>
<p>Papers passed from hand to hand; there were sharp gasps of discovery when a relevant detail surfaced, the scratching of Lily’s quill as she copied passages, Elizabeth’s precise, clipped questions that demanded absolute accuracy, and Elaris’s running commentary, part genuine historical insight, part the simple warmth of someone enjoying the rare comfort of company.</p>
<p>For the first time in days, the hollow exhaustion seemed to drain from Theo’s face. He looked less like a man haunted by sleeplessness and more like someone who had finally found a task large enough to occupy the fractured parts of his mind, the parts that otherwise kept circling back to the bloodstains in the training hall and the terrifying images of what he had witnessed there.</p>
<p>The fragile peace of their shared purpose held until the heavy tread of boots echoed in the hallway. The atmosphere in the room shifted instantly, the scholarly tension snapping into something sharper, more primal.</p>
<p>Rex walked in.</p>
<p>Rex entered through the main doorway like a force of nature that had momentarily settled into stillness. He carried the residual heat of the afternoon’s second training session, his skin still flushed and slick with the faintest sheen of sweat that made his muscles catch the lamplight with predatory definition.</p>
<p>His sleeves were pushed past his elbows, revealing forearms corded with exertion, and he moved with that loose, dangerous satisfaction of a man who had spent hours dismantling opponents and found the process utterly exhilarating.</p>
<p>"There you all are," he said, his voice carrying a gravelly undertone that cut through the scholarly quiet. "I was told there was a war council forming in here, and apparently, I wasn’t on the invitation list."</p>
<p>"It formed itself out of necessity, not invitation," Elizabeth replied without breaking her stride.</p>
<p>She didn’t even lift her gaze from the fragment she was cross-referencing, her quill scratching feverishly against the parchment. "You would have been an obstacle for the first hour—too much noise, too much... presence."</p>
<p>"But you are welcome to be in the way now that the actual thinking has been done."</p>
<p>Rex let out a low, amused huff.</p>
<p>"Elizabeth Von Starlight," he said, tilting his head as he approached the table. "Tell me honestly... have you ever once, in the entire span of our acquaintance, greeted me without an insult woven into the fabric of the sentence?"</p>
<p>"I am simply economical with my affection, Rex," Elizabeth said.</p>
<p>She finally looked up, and though her words were sharp, the infinitesimal, private curve at the corner of her mouth betrayed her. It was a challenge wrapped in a compliment.</p>
<p>Lily, however, had no interest in verbal sparring. The moment she saw him, she abandoned her mountain of books.</p>
<p>She crossed the room with an unrestrained warmth that she never bothered to mask around this circle; they were past the point of pretense. She wrapped her arms around his waist, tucking herself against his broad chest as if seeking shelter from the world.</p>
<p>Rex’s reaction was instinctual, almost unconscious; his large hand came up to rest against the small of her back, pulling her closer in a gesture so practiced it seemed written into his very bones.</p>
<p>"You smell like you’ve been standing in the sun all day, correcting people’s footwork and breaking their spirits," Lily murmured into the fabric of his shirt, her voice muffled but fond.</p>
<p>"It is a demanding occupation," Rex admitted, his voice softening as he rested his chin briefly atop her head. "Someone has to remind them that technique matters more than rage."</p>
<p>"Poor thing," Lily said, pulling back just enough to look up at him with an expression of exaggerated, fond exasperation. "Elaris has adopted a new research project, and we have all been conscripted into her army whether we intended to be or not."</p>
<p>Rex’s amber eyes swept the room, taking in the tableau of organized desperation. He saw the scattered documents, Elizabeth’s razor-sharp focus, and Diana, who had shifted from helping Lily to holding a volume open for Theo.</p>
<p>The two of them were working through a complex passage with the quiet, unspoken cooperation of two people who had decided that the survival of the household outweighed whatever unspoken tension simmered between them.</p>
<p>Rex moved toward the table. He offered Elaris a warm, easy smile that spoke of genuine affection, then leaned down to exchange a few hushed, rapid-fire words with Elizabeth about the resonance frequency finding.</p>
<p>He watched her expression sharpen with renewed interest at his input; he knew exactly how to stoke her intellectual fire.</p>
<p>Then, his gaze landed on Diana.</p>
<p>She had already looked up. Her eyes met his before he had even finished crossing the distance between them.</p>
<p>It was a wordless communication, a silent recognition of the shared violence they both carried, honed in the training hall and the aftermath of battle. No words were needed to bridge the space between them.</p>
<p>"Come upstairs with me," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice dropped to a register meant only for her, low and intimate and carrying an unmistakable command that made the air between them vibrate. In the large room, privacy was an illusion, but the others were all too practiced in the art of pretending not to hear.</p>
<p>Diana didn’t hesitate. She closed the ancient volume with deliberate gentleness, setting it down in front of Theo.</p>
<p>Theo took the book without looking up immediately, his movements carrying the studied casualness of a man who knew exactly what kind of storm was approaching and was simply bracing for impact.</p>
<p>"Now?" Diana asked. It wasn’t a question of permission; it was an acknowledgment of inevitability.</p>
<p>"Now," Rex confirmed.</p>
