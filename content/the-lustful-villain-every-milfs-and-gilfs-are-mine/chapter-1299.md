<p>Helena refused to accept it. Her mind, trained in the rigid discipline of magical theory, recoiled against the impossibility of his claim.</p>
<p>’Impossible,’ she screamed internally. ’The laws of mana do not bend to mere will.’</p>
<p>’They bend to structure... And I understand the structure!’</p>
<p>With a guttural scream of defiance that tore at her throat, she forced her consciousness to spark. She didn’t need her hands; she would use raw, unfiltered intent.</p>
<p>A Flame Lance flared into existence in the air beside her temple, a needle of concentrated crimson fury. It was a perfect spell: thin, aerodynamic, and devastatingly fast.</p>
<p>It hissed through the air toward Rex’s sternum like a bullet.</p>
<p>Halfway there, it simply... died.</p>
<p>There was no explosion. No dissipating smoke.</p>
<p>The red light simply vanished as if someone had pinched the wick of a candle, the magical energy neutralized before it could even register as heat. The last pathetic wisps of ember were scattered by the wind like ash.</p>
<p>"No..." she whimpered, her eyes widening. "No, no, no!"</p>
<p>Desperation took over. She summoned an Ice Shard, pouring every remaining drop of her energy into a jagged spike of absolute zero.</p>
<p>It materialized inches from her own cheek, but before it could even launch, it shattered into harmless, glittering sparkles three feet from her face.</p>
<p>The spell didn’t fail; it was unmade.</p>
<p>Her mind raced, hunting for any alternative. She reached deep, grasping for the connection between her soul and the bedrock of the world.</p>
<p>She called for the Stone Fist, a spell she had cast ten thousand times, a spell that felt as natural as breathing. She commanded the earth to rise, to crush, to defend.</p>
<p>But the earth remained silent.</p>
<p>The ground beneath her palms felt heavy, indifferent, and terrifyingly foreign. The soil didn’t vibrate with her call; it didn’t stir with the familiar hum of elemental resonance.</p>
<p>Every grain of sand, every shard of granite in the Starlight Garden, now belonged to a different master. The earth would obey Rex’s will more readily than a loyal hound obeys its owner.</p>
<p>She was a stranger in her own domain.</p>
<p>’I have to move...!’</p>
<p>’I have to get away from him,’ panic clawed at her chest.</p>
<p>She tried to flee. She managed to plant one foot beneath her, muscles straining to push against the oppressive atmosphere.</p>
<p>But as soon as she attempted the motion, the gravity didn’t just resist it; it punished. The pressure doubled, then tripled.</p>
<p>Her bones groaned under the sudden, impossible load.</p>
<p>With a muffled, broken sob, her legs gave out completely, and she folded back into the dirt, her face pressed against the unyielding earth.</p>
<p>It was the kind of despair that arrives in suffocating layers.</p>
<p>First came the sharp shock of failure. Then the cold, numb flicker of denial.</p>
<p>Finally, the slow, creeping horror of understanding: this was not a fight she was losing. This was a new reality she was trapped inside.</p>
<p>A woman who had spent forty-five years believing that any problem could be solved through understanding, through control, through the elegant application of theory was now kneeling in the wreckage of her own life.</p>
<p>She had no solution. There was no counterspell for a man who had already written the rules of the universe.</p>
<p>Rex watched her. He watched the way her pupils dilated in terror, the way her lips began to tremble uncontrollably, the way her entire body surrendered to the crushing weight.</p>
<p>He felt no sadistic pleasure in it. There was no cruelty in his gaze, no malice.</p>
<p>His expression remained as clinical as a surgeon’s as he observed the collapse of her spirit. He was simply witnessing the inevitable conclusion of his own adaptation.</p>
<p>He didn’t walk; he moved.</p>
<p>He crossed the ten yards between them in a single, blurred motion that defied the eye, his shadow engulfing her like the coming of night.</p>
<p>Rex’s Peak Superhuman Physique didn’t just make him fast; it made him a violation of physics. He appeared before her before the displaced air could even reach her face, a predator materializing from a blur of motion.</p>
<p>His fist drove into her solar plexus with the focused force of a battering ram.</p>
<p>THWUMP.</p>
<p>Helena folded over his knuckles with a sound like a bellows being violently squeezed flat. The air didn’t just leave her lungs; it was purged from her body in a spray of crimson.</p>
<p>Blood spattered across the scorched grass in a grotesque pattern. Her eyes went wide, glassy with shock, her mouth stretching open in a silent, desperate gape as her diaphragm convulsed.</p>
<p>Before she could even collapse, the world shifted.</p>
<p>VROOOOM.</p>
<p>The invisible hand of his telekinesis seized her. She was yanked ten feet into the air, dangling helplessly like a rag doll with severed strings, her torn traveling dress fluttering pathetically in the wind.</p>
<p>Then, with terrifying casualness, he slammed her down.</p>
<p>CRACK BOOM.</p>
<p>She hit the lawn with such devastating force that the entire garden shuddered as if struck by an earthquake. A crater spiderwebbed outward from beneath her spine.</p>
<p>A wet, bubbling cough tore from her throat as she struggled to find air that wasn’t there. But there was no respite.</p>
<p>Before her heart could even register the pain, she was hauled upward again.</p>
<p>Slam.</p>
<p>The next impact was to the left.</p>
<p>The next to the right.</p>
<p>It became a steady, almost thoughtful rhythm.</p>
<p>Up and down.</p>
<p>Up and down.</p>
<p>The choreography of her destruction was precise. Rex wasn’t mindless; he was calculating.</p>
<p>He was adjusting the angle of every impact, calibrating the downward velocity to ensure she broke but never shattered beyond repair. He was maintaining the exact threshold of agony required to keep her conscious.</p>
<p>Between the brutal throws, he moved in to finish the work.</p>
<p>His Martial Arts Mastery guided every strike, transforming his body into a weapon of surgical cruelty. He closed the distance with terrifying economy of motion.</p>
<p>A lightning-fast hook caught her jaw, CRACK,CRACK sending a shockwave through her skull that rattled her teeth against each other and split her lip open.</p>
<p>A vicious, short-range kick found her ribs, striking precisely where the bone was thinnest; the sound of splintering calcium was audible even over the wind.</p>
<p>Helena let out a thin, broken scream that died halfway through.</p>
<p>A knee drove into her sternum, leaving her wheezing blood. A palm strike landed against her shoulder with the wet pop of a dislocated joint, sending a white-hot bolt of agony screaming down her arm.</p>
<p>He hit her where it hurt most—the nerves, the joints, and the soft tissue—and never once crossed the line into the lethal. His hands were steady as a surgeon’s, his movements economical, wasting not a single calorie of energy.</p>
<p>Her proud traveling dress ripped along one sleeve, exposing skin already mottled with purple bruises. One eye began to swell shut, a dark plum against her pale skin.</p>
<p>Her face, once the picture of aristocratic poise and magical authority, grew puffy, unrecognizable, and streaked with a mask of blood, sweat, and dirt.</p>
<p>"You know," Rex said, his voice disturbingly calm, conversational as if he were discussing the afternoon weather over tea. "I used to admire your composure."</p>
<p>Between the devastating impacts, he would step close, speaking to her while she hung suspended in his telekinetic grip.</p>
<p>"The third strongest mage in Aethelgard," he said, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial murmur.</p>
<p>He reached out, winding his fist through her matted, blood-soaked hair. With terrifying strength, he hauled her head back, forcing her to meet his cold, unblinking gaze.</p>
<p>"The woman who kept my secret for months because she thought she was the cleverest person in every room she entered."</p>
<p>He leaned in closer, his breath warm against her bruised cheek, his smile widening just enough to show teeth.</p>
<p>"Look at yourself, Helena... I mean, it’s like, really look..."</p>
<p>"Look at what all that cleverness, all those years of study, all that precious control... bought you."</p>
