<p>"Tell me what you know," Rex said, the golem's voice dropping into a low, commanding rumble that seemed to vibrate the very air around them.</p>
<p>Veran Caulstow stared down at the construct, his face a mask of calculated neutrality. For a long moment, he simply weighed the situation, his mind working at a speed that matched the lethal efficiency of his design.</p>
<p>He knew the stakes. He knew that in this moment, giving too little information was just as dangerous as giving too much.</p>
<p>"I was tracking the purge's acoustic signature through the substrate," Veran said, his voice flat and precise. "My system has a latent geological sensitivity component that the suppression layer doesn't completely mask."</p>
<p>"Even at maximum depth, significant seismic events register... I can't ignore them."</p>
<p>"You felt the reconstruction from the surface," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I felt something massive and sustained for approximately three hours," Veran replied, his eyes never leaving the golem's. "The final engagement was the largest."</p>
<p>"Whatever activated in the third hour produced a substrate vibration so intense it shook the foundation of this building... It wasn't a local event."</p>
<p>"And your conclusion?" Rex demanded.</p>
<p>"That the Underlayer's power structure had been violently upended," Veran said. "And that whatever caused that shift was either already on the surface or was coming here shortly."</p>
<p>"It was only a matter of time... I just didn't expect the time to be this morning."</p>
<p>He paused, his jaw tightening. "And then eleven geological formations moved through the substrate beneath my building at approximately one hour after dawn."</p>
<p>"That confirmed the second half of my theory."</p>
<p>Rex shifted his focus, narrowing the golem's awareness into a pinpoint of intense scrutiny. He pushed his passive energy perception to its limit, peeling back the layers of Veran's suppressed signature.</p>
<p>The readings were stark.</p>
<p>This wasn't a hero designation. It wasn't the polished, front-line combat architecture used by the Academy's elite.</p>
<p>This was something darker, something more specialized. It was a hunter designation.</p>
<p>A system designed not for the glory of the battlefield, but for the cold precision of the hunt. Pursuit, neutralization, extraction. This was the architecture of an assassin: a man designed to find those who did not want to be found and remove them from the equation.</p>
<p>"Your system is a hunter designation," Rex said, the realization hitting the air with a cold weight.</p>
<p>Veran's expression didn't change, but there was a tightening in his shoulders, a subtle shift in his center of gravity. He was ready for a fight.</p>
<p>"What were you hunting on this island?" Rex asked.</p>
<p>A long, tense silence followed. Veran seemed to be debating exactly how much of his world he was willing to expose.</p>
<p>Finally, he spoke. "Legion contacts."</p>
<p>Rex felt a surge of focus. This was the third significant data point, and it changed the entire complexion of the morning.</p>
<p>"Two years," Rex said, the golem's voice now laced with a dangerous edge. "You've been operating as a sleeper agent for two years, hunting the Legion's surface network."</p>
<p>"My organization became aware of the Legion's surface activities three years ago," Veran said, his tone clipped. "I was positioned here when the network density in Aethelgard became critical."</p>
<p>"I was tasked with mapping their structure, identifying key nodes, and monitoring their interactions with the local population."</p>
<p>"Your organization," Rex repeated. "Who are they?"</p>
<p>"I'm not authorized to disclose the full name," Veran said, his voice taut with the effort of maintaining his composure. "But I will tell you this: we are not the Legion, we are not the Apostle network, and we have no affiliation with the parties involved in last night's conflict."</p>
<p>"We are an independent entity with a very specific interest in the Legion's failure."</p>
<p>"A third party," Rex said. "Monitoring the Legion from the shadows."</p>
<p>"Yes," Veran said.</p>
<p>"For three years," Rex said, his thoughts racing through the implications.</p>
<p>"For longer than that," Veran corrected, his voice turning cold.</p>
<p>Rex processed the information. A hunter class reincarnator, operating under deep suppression, embedded in the civilian population of Aethelgard for two years, serving an organization that had been tracking the Legion for at least three.</p>
<p>This wasn't just a rogue agent; this was a sophisticated intelligence operation.</p>
<p>"How many Legion contacts did you identify?" Rex asked.</p>
<p>Veran looked at the golem, his eyes hard. "Eleven."</p>
<p>"The same eleven that the reconstruction's purge eliminated last night," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Exactly," Veran said. "The purge was a surgical strike."</p>
<p>"You didn't just destroy the network; you erased a very specific list of names. My list."</p>
<p>"Where is your documentation?" Rex demanded.</p>
<p>Veran's hand moved instinctively toward his inner pocket, his fingers tensing. "Secured. Encrypted and hidden from any standard scan."</p>
<p>"I am going to need it," Rex said, the golem's voice now commanding and absolute. "Now."</p>
<p>Veran Caulstow stared at the golem, his body poised for a desperate gamble. He had played this hand for two years, and now the cards were being snatched from the table.</p>
<p>"The central market square," the golem rumbled, its voice echoing off the shuttered windows of the courtyard. "Thirty minutes."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex countered, his mental presence pressing against Veran's resolve like a physical weight. "But you will not come empty-handed."</p>
<p>"Bring the documentation."</p>
<p>Veran's eyes narrowed, a flicker of genuine tension finally breaking his stony mask.</p>
<p>"It is not portable," he said, his voice dropping to a defensive growl. "The data is stored in the building's lower level, housed in a secured case that requires my specific energy signature to unlock."</p>
<p>"It cannot be moved without a full extraction process."</p>
<p>"Then the square is merely the meeting point," Rex decided, his tone leaving no room for negotiation. "After the square, I will come to you."</p>
<p>"But hear me, Veran Caulstow: I need that documentation before the morning is spent."</p>
<p>"Do not make me come looking for it."</p>
<p>Veran stared at the monolithic construct for a long, heavy moment. He was a man who lived by calculated risks, and he was currently weighing the danger of an unknown geological god against the danger of withholding his life's work.</p>
<p>Finally, he gave a short, sharp nod, the grim acceptance of a professional who had just realized the rules of the game had changed entirely.</p>
<p>"The market square," Veran repeated, a final confirmation.</p>
<p>"Twenty-two minutes," the golem stated.</p>
<p>With a decisive, heavy thud, the second-story window slid shut, leaving the courtyard in a sudden, ringing silence.</p>
<p>Rex released the fourth golem's primary focus, letting the relay settle back into its wide net monitoring mode, though he kept Veran's profile burned into the foreground of his consciousness. He was a hunter designation reincarnator who had been operating in the shadows for two years, tracking the exact same eleven Legion nodes that the reconstruction had purged last night.</p>
<p>It wasn't just a coincidence; it was a collision of two massive, invisible currents. Veran's organization had been building a dam for years, only to wake up this morning and find that Rex had already broken the river.</p>
<p>He needed to know who these people were. He needed to determine whether they were allies or a new variable to neutralize.</p>
<p>Before the morning could slip away, Rex shifted his vast attention to the fifth golem.</p>
<p>The fifth golem was waiting in the Academy's outer practice grounds, positioned with surgical precision. Rex had timed its emergence to the exact second Ren Askar would finish his pre-dawn drilling routine, a routine the substrate had been meticulously logging since the moment the network went live.</p>
<p>The stone flagstones groaned and shattered as the golem surged upward, erupting into the center of the training field just as Ren Askar transitioned from his warm-up circuit into the first, lethal movement of his morning combat forms.</p>
<p>Ren Askar was twenty-six, a hero designation. He carried the unmistakable physical architecture of a high-tier combat system, his body a finely tuned instrument of the academy's conditioning.</p>
<p>Rex had analyzed the combat profiles of the academy's student body with clinical detachment, and even by their elite standards, Ren Askar was a standout specimen of peak efficiency and raw, disciplined power.</p>
<p>But more importantly, Ren Askar was flagged.</p>
<p>He wasn't a Legion contact, but he fell into a much more predictable, and therefore more dangerous, category. He was the archetype of the Apostle network's ideal asset: a hero designation, fully integrated into the Academy's social and political infrastructure, with zero intelligence ties to any outside influence.</p>
<p>He was a man of pure, unadulterated loyalty to the established order. When the Apostle network issued the call for the canyon expedition, Ren Askar wouldn't ask why; he would simply move because his very soul was designed to respond to the command.</p>
<p>And as Rex watched the young man freeze in mid-strike, staring at the sudden intruder in his sacred training space, Rex reached a cold, singular conclusion.</p>
<p>On this island, a man like Ren Askar was not an asset. He was a liability.</p>
<p>Ren Askar looked at the golem that had surfaced through his practice ground flagstone and came to a combat stance in approximately one second, which was the correct response time for someone whose combat-class system had been running its passive awareness component through the morning drill.</p>
<p>"What is that...?" he said, not to the golem but to himself, the specific statement of someone whose tactical assessment was running faster than his verbal processing.</p>
<p>"Ren Askar," the golem said.</p>
