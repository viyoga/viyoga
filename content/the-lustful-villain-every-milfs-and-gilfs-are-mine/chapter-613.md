<p>The air in the chamber did not merely vibrate; it groaned under the weight of two colliding realities. The speed of Cassandra’s lunge was a terrifying anomaly.</p>
<p>Unlike her previous movements, which followed the fluid grace of the void, the Blood Oath’s enhancement was a violent, explosive burst concentrated at the very instant of her movement’s initiation. The first meter of her approach was a blur of impossible physics, a sudden lunge that bypassed the concept of acceleration, moving from stillness to a lethal velocity in a heartbeat.</p>
<p>Rex did not retreat. To retreat was to surrender the initiative to a predator.</p>
<p>Instead, he stepped into the storm.</p>
<p>As Cassandra’s blade descended, Rex brought his gauntlets upward in a crushing parry. The moment the Blood Oath blade met the geological divine authority of his armor, the world seemed to hold its breath.</p>
<p>The feedback was instantaneous and unnatural. The blood affinity coating on her sword, driven by the divine mandate of her sacrifice, did not simply strike the stone; it attempted to infect it.</p>
<p>The crimson essence surged forward, seeking to propagate through the mineral veins of his compressed gauntlets, treating the divine stone as if it were living tissue.</p>
<p>Rex felt the invasive pressure of the blood magic attempting to rewrite his armor’s composition. His mind, honed by the relentless clarity of his power, processed the threat in a fraction of a second.</p>
<p>"The blood goes through stone," Rex observed, his voice tight with the strain of the sudden, microscopic invasion.</p>
<p>"Yes," Cassandra replied, her eyes burning with a crimson ferocity as she wrenched the blade back, the friction of the parry throwing off sparks of red and gold.</p>
<p>"If the stone is not reinforced at the molecular level," Rex countered, his eyes narrowing as he felt the blood magic gnawing at the edges of his defense.</p>
<p>"If the stone is not," she agreed, her voice a low, dangerous hum.</p>
<p>She lunged again. This time, the angle was surgical.</p>
<p>She didn’t aim for the center of his guard; she aimed for the seam, the microscopic junction where the different mineral zones of his gauntlets met, the precise point where his molecular reinforcement was at its thinnest.</p>
<p>Rex’s reaction was a masterpiece of instinctive authority. His earthen power read the blade’s approach vector before it even arrived.</p>
<p>In the millisecond before contact, he commanded the density of the target zone to shift. He flooded the seam with hyper-compressed minerals, a sudden surge of geological mass that raised the density beyond the threshold of the blood’s penetration.</p>
<p>The collision was not the ringing of metal or the thud of rock. It was a thunderous, metaphysical roar, the sound of two divine authority expressions colliding and negotiating the right to exist in the same space.</p>
<p>For three agonizing seconds, the two powers fought a silent, invisible war at the point of contact. The void blood screamed to consume, and the earth stone roared to endure.</p>
<p>Rex won the negotiation, but the victory was scarred. The blade did not pierce him, but it left a jagged, crimson-stained crack running through the reinforced section of his gauntlet.</p>
<p>"We’re breaking each other," Cassandra said, her breathing heavy, the red glow of her eyes pulsing in sync with the cracks in her sword.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, a grim, exhilarated smile tugging at his lips. "That’s what equal tiers do."</p>
<p>"We’re not equal tiers," she snapped, the frustration in her voice cutting through the tension like a blade.</p>
<p>It was the sound of a goddess realizing that the math of the universe was shifting beneath her feet.</p>
<p>"The gauntlets are a separate authority from my primary system," Rex explained, his voice regaining its cool, analytical edge despite the carnage. "In this specific register, the difference between our tiers is smaller than it is in the others."</p>
<p>"That’s why you’re testing it," she said, her stance widening, her aura flaring as she prepared for a third, even more violent exchange.</p>
<p>"I need to know where the gaps are," Rex said, his eyes glowing with a predatory light. "Before I use it somewhere, it matters more."</p>
<p>Cassandra leaned forward, the void and the blood swirling around her in a lethal, beautiful dance of destruction.</p>
<p>"Where does it matter more?" she challenged, her voice a deadly whisper that promised the end of all things.</p>
<p>Rex remained silent, his posture relaxed, almost casual, as he stood amidst the ruin of the chamber. He wasn’t just fighting; he was dissecting.</p>
<p>To him, the fight wasn’t a struggle for survival but a high-stakes laboratory where every strike from Cassandra was a data point, and every parry was a way to calibrate his own godhood.</p>
<p>Cassandra didn’t give him the luxury of a second thought. She lunged again, but the rhythm of the battle shattered.</p>
<p>Gone were the fluid, sweeping strikes of her previous forms, replaced by a terrifying, calculated shift in combat philosophy. She had abandoned the predictable patterns she had spent months perfecting to counter Rex’s known abilities.</p>
<p>She was no longer fighting the man; she was weaponizing the Blood Oath itself.</p>
<p>With a violent, sweeping motion of her sword, the crimson essence began to peel away from her skin. It didn’t spray; it flowed, controlled and surgical.</p>
<p>The blood did not fly toward Rex as projectiles; instead, it wove itself through the air, constructing a nightmare of three-dimensional architecture. Ribbons of thick, pulsating gore spiraled and hardened into geometric lattices, forming a cage of visceral matter that began to tighten around Rex’s position.</p>
<p>Rex watched the crimson structures manifest with a look of genuine, scholarly interest. He didn’t even raise his guard; he simply observed the geometry of her trap.</p>
<p>"You’ve been saving this," Rex remarked, his voice smooth and infuriatingly calm, as if they were discussing the weather rather than a death trap.</p>
<p>"Since our first conversation," Cassandra replied, her voice strained by the massive energy expenditure required to maintain the structures.</p>
<p>"You realized your primary disadvantage wasn’t your strength but your inability to constrain my movement geometry," Rex analyzed, his eyes tracing the intricate patterns of the blood lattice. "And you built a solution using the one affinity that operates independently of my telekinetic deflection."</p>
<p>"The blood constructions are not an energy type," she countered, her eyes glowing with a fierce, desperate intelligence. "They are physical."</p>
<p>"Physical and affinity-infused," Rex corrected, his geological perception reading the very frequency of the blood. "Which means they have the primordial frequency calibration layered into their material structure."</p>
<p>A predatory grin played on his lips. He understood the trap perfectly. "My telekinesis can move energy, Cassandra."</p>
<p>"It can push or pull a blast of light. But it can’t disrupt the fundamental existence of these structures."</p>
<p>"To move them, the system has to treat them as mass, not as power."</p>
<p>"Yes," she whispered, her hands trembling slightly as she commanded the cage to contract.</p>
<p>"And the constructions are indexed to my movement signature," Rex continued, stepping slightly to the left.</p>
<p>As he moved, the blood ribbons hissed through the air, shifting instantly to close the gap he had just created. "They’ll close every vector the system predicts I will take."</p>
<p>"Every vector the system can produce," Cassandra corrected, her gaze locking onto him with predatory focus. "The Blood Oath’s constructions are calibrated to all system-registered movement."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her, his expression shifting from amusement to a terrifying, absolute confidence. The air around him seemed to grow heavy, not with magic, but with the sheer, concentrated presence of a man who had surpassed the need for it.</p>
<p>"What about movement that the system doesn’t register?" he asked.</p>
<p>Cassandra’s eyes widened. The blood lattice began to pulse, sensing his intent, preparing to snap shut on his very soul.</p>
<p>"Peak Physique," Rex said, his voice dropping into a register of absolute authority, "operates at a level that exists independently of system taxonomy."</p>
<p>"It is the pure, unadulterated power of the flesh, unbound by the rules of the world."</p>
<p>In a sudden, violent burst of motion, Rex moved.</p>
<p>It was not the explosive, magical speed of an SSS class skill. It was something far more unsettling.</p>
<p>It was the raw, terrifying velocity of a biological machine pushed to its theoretical absolute maximum. There was no magical flair, no system notification, and no shimmering aura of power.</p>
<p>There was only the sudden, silent displacement of air as his muscles, tendons, and bones acted with a perfection that transcended the divine.</p>
<p>The blood constructions reacted instantly to his intent, but they were hunting a ghost. They tracked the system’s predicted path, the magical "afterimage" of where a god should be.</p>
<p>They lunged toward the space Rex had occupied a microsecond before, snapping shut on empty air.</p>
<p>Rex was already through the cage.</p>
<p>He bypassed the lethal geometry of the Blood Oath as if it were nothing more than mist, his body slipping through the gaps in the blood lattice before the structures could even register his physical presence. He halted a mere twelve centimeters from Cassandra, his breathing steady, his eyes bright with the thrill of the discovery.</p>
<p>Cassandra froze. The blood constructions around them began to lose their shape, dissolving into a fine crimson rain as her concentration wavered.</p>
<p>She stared at him, her expression a mask of profound realization. She had found the gap, the one flaw in her perfect logic, and he had exploited it with the ease of a man walking through a doorway.</p>
<p>"There it is," Rex said, his voice a low, triumphant purr.</p>
<p>"There it... is..." Cassandra breathed, the weight of his superiority finally sinking in.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t give her time to mourn her failed trap. He leaned forward slightly, his aura beginning to flare with a new, even more intense heat.</p>
<p>"Come at me again," Rex commanded, his eyes burning with the hunger of a man who had finally found a toy worth breaking.</p>
