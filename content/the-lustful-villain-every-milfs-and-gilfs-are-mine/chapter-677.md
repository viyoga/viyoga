<p>Gorvasha leaned back slightly, her eyes narrowing as she connected the dots. "You’re not just waiting for her response."</p>
<p>"You’re using Zane as a catalyst. You’re forcing her hand, pulling her timeline toward you."</p>
<p>"The reconstruction is going to take months," Rex said, his tone turning cold and clinical. "Celestina is a strategist; she knows that a city undergoing a total purge and structural overhaul is at its most vulnerable."</p>
<p>"She’ll see the chaos, the smoke, and the shifting foundations, and she’ll identify an optimal window to strike... Likely within the next six to eight weeks."</p>
<p>"And you want her to strike within that window," Cassandra realized, her voice filled with a new level of respect for his ruthlessness.</p>
<p>"I want her to move before the reconstruction is complete," Rex countered, his eyes burning. "Not because we’re weak, but because a response that arrives during active reconstruction hits a city that is already in a state of total mobilization."</p>
<p>"The population is awake, the command structure is razor sharp, and every scrap of labor and material is centralized for a single purpose."</p>
<p>"We won’t be a disorganized mess; we will be a coiled spring."</p>
<p>Cassandra’s eyes widened slightly. "You’re using the reconstruction itself as a military posture."</p>
<p>"You’re turning a rebuilding effort into a trap."</p>
<p>"The reconstruction creates the perfect illusion of vulnerability," Rex said, his grin widening into something truly villainous. "Visible damage, active resource allocation, and a newly established leadership that looks untested to the untrained eye."</p>
<p>"To Celestina, it looks like an invitation... It looks like a feast."</p>
<p>"And she will walk right into the mouth of it," Cassandra whispered.</p>
<p>"The canyon site Gorvasha identified," Rex said, his voice hardening. "The geological terrain, the proximity to the second stratum boundary, the defensive advantages of the substrate... that wasn’t just a location for rebuilding."</p>
<p>"It was a kill zone."</p>
<p>"You’re provoking her," Cassandra said, a small, dangerous smile touching her lips. "You’re throwing the gauntlet down and daring her to pick it up."</p>
<p>"I’m giving her the truth," Rex corrected, his eyes flashing with a terrifying, pure evil. "Whether she acts on it is her choice. But she will act."</p>
<p>"She cannot tolerate the underlayer of this governance."</p>
<p>"She cannot tolerate a reincarnator system holder claiming absolute authority over a territory that borders the very foundations of Aethelgard."</p>
<p>"She will come, Cassandra. And when she does, she will find that the ’vulnerability’ she sought is actually the jaws of a predator waiting to snap shut."</p>
<p>"The intelligence advantage is a two-way street," Rex said, his voice carrying a dark, melodic confidence.</p>
<p>He leaned back, a predator savoring the scent of a trap being laid. "She learns exactly what I permit her to know."</p>
<p>"And in return, I learn precisely where her response originates..."</p>
<p>"We aren’t just fighting a war; we’re conducting a grand experiment."</p>
<p>Pavellia didn’t smile. Her eyes remained flat, clinical, dissecting the layers of his madness.</p>
<p>"There is a variable you are playing with, My Lord," she said, her voice cutting through his amusement. "Zane Mortavius wasn’t just a witness; he was a participant."</p>
<p>"He saw the whole thing, especially the Blood Oath form, in its full, terrifying glory... also, how you handle everything," Pavillia said, holding her chin. "His report will be meticulous."</p>
<p>"He will document the activation, the sheer scale of its effects, and most importantly, its perceived limitations."</p>
<p>She shifted her gaze to Cassandra for a heartbeat before locking back onto Rex. "Celestina will see the ceiling."</p>
<p>"She will believe she knows the absolute limit of the Blood Oath’s power."</p>
<p>A heavy, suffocating silence descended upon the hall. Cassandra’s stillness intensified; she was a soldier recognizing the danger of a false sense of security.</p>
<p>A strategist views a known limit as a weakness to exploit.</p>
<p>"She will," Rex agreed, his eyes gleaming with a cruel, knowing light.</p>
<p>"That fundamentally alters her operational calculus," Pavellia continued, her mind already remapping the battlefield. "If she believes the Blood Oath was the peak of one of Underlayer’s greatest powers and it still fell short, she won’t waste resources trying to break it."</p>
<p>"She will build her entire response around the force that defeated it."</p>
<p>"Which is exactly what I want her to do," Rex said, his voice dropping to a low, triumphant purr.</p>
<p>Pavellia stared at him, absorbing the sheer audacity of the gambit.</p>
<p>"She will build a fortress of countermeasures based on a profile Zane provides," Rex explained, his hands tracing an invisible map in the air. "And that profile will be perfect."</p>
<p>"It will be accurate as well..."</p>
<p>"It will be a masterpiece of documented data. But she will be building a fortress to fight a ghost." Rex raised his finger. "Because Zane was not there when the real game changed."</p>
<p>"He has no data on the gravity manipulation I’ve just learned. Well, yes, he did see the storm, but he missed the lightning."</p>
<p>"The new acquisition is absent from his report," Cassandra noted, her voice sharp with realization.</p>
<p>"Correct," Rex said, a mocking smirk dancing on his lips. "His vision of my capability closes roughly three hours before the power was integrated into my soul."</p>
<p>"He is giving her a perfect map of a territory that no longer exists," Rex smirked. "He is giving her a masterpiece of obsolete information."</p>
<p>"The gap you will exploit," Cassandra whispered, her eyes narrowing.</p>
<p>"It’s more like the gap I will crush her with," Rex corrected, his tone shifting from teasing to lethally serious.</p>
<p>Gorvasha, who had been watching the exchange with the silent, calculating intensity of a tectonic plate shifting, finally spoke.</p>
<p>"The canyon site," she said, her voice grounding the room. "The geological composition you described for the kill zone."</p>
<p>"If we pair that with the Earthen Authority’s application..."</p>
<p>"Go on," Rex commanded, his interest piqued.</p>
<p>"There is a specific formation in the northern reach of the canyon system," Gorvasha said, her eyes alight with the thrill of a new weapon. "I have watched it for fourteen months, waiting for a reason to strike it."</p>
<p>"It sits at the precise intersection of three primary fault lines..."</p>
<p>"It is the most structurally complex point in the entire canyon, but more importantly, it is the point of highest geological energy density."</p>
<p>"A leverage point," Rex breathed, the strategist in him recognizing the sheer potential for carnage.</p>
<p>"More than that," Gorvasha countered. "If the Earthen Authority operates at that intersection, it wouldn’t just be applying force to a single structure."</p>
<p>"It would be applying force to the junction of three. The force multiplication wouldn’t just be significant; it would be cataclysmic."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her, his gaze intense, acknowledging a piece of the puzzle that had just turned the entire board upside down. "How long until you can map the full extent of that energy?"</p>
<p>"The preliminary reads are done," Gorvasha replied instantly. "But for a detailed survey, the Earthen Authority needs direct geological contact..."</p>
<p>"We need the passive awareness to flow through the formation from a contact point on site. Two hours, assuming we have access."</p>
<p>"Access is a triviality," Rex said dismissively.</p>
<p>"Then the survey can happen today," Gorvasha said. "We can have the data before the reconstruction review is even concluded."</p>
<p>"No," Rex commanded, the tyrant returning to the fore. "After the review..."</p>
<p>"The review establishes the new operational structure. Once the bones of our new empire are set, the military planning can run in parallel with the repair work."</p>
<p>"We will build the trap and the anvil at the same time."</p>
<p>Gorvasha gave a sharp, decisive nod, the silent respect of one master of the earth to another.</p>
<p>Mordecai, who had remained a silent, brooding presence throughout the strategic maneuvering, finally shifted in his seat. He had been listening not just to the words but also to the underlying rhythm of the plan, rebuilding his mental models of the coming war as the pieces fell into place.</p>
<p>When he finally spoke, his voice carried the weight of a man who had seen the end of the world and was preparing for its rebirth.</p>
