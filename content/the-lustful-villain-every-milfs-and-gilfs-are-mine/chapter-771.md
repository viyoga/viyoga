<p>The dragon began its descent, a massive, dying star of scales and scorched flesh, plummeting toward the city with a terminal, whistling roar: WHHHHHHH OOOOOOOM! It was a catastrophe in motion, a mountain of meat and bone destined to pulverize anything in its path.</p>
<p>But Rex was faster.</p>
<p>With a violent, invisible wrench of his telekinesis, he caught the beast in mid-air. The sheer weight of the dragon strained against his mental grip, the air itself groaning under the sudden deceleration. SHHHHH THUD!</p>
<p>He didn’t just stop the fall; he steered it. He redirected the massive, tumbling carcass toward the northern section of the plaza, a wasteland of jagged stone and pulverized masonry where the golem impacts had already turned the architecture into a graveyard of rubble.</p>
<p>The dragon slammed into the broken earth with a bone-shaking, final thud: KRA BOOOOOM! Dust and pulverized stone erupted in a massive cloud, settling slowly over the limp, broken form of the beast.</p>
<p>It landed with the heavy, absolute finality of something that had ceased to be a threat and had become merely a corpse.</p>
<p>Aurelia had not moved. She remained a statue of seasoned discipline, her eyes wide and unblinking.</p>
<p>She had watched the entire sequence—the surgical stone strikes, the apocalyptic elemental discharge, and the impossible telekinetic catch—with the haunted, analytical gaze of a combat authority who had spent thirty years mastering the art of war, only to realize she had just witnessed a god rewriting the rules of the game.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy, thick with the smell of ozone, burnt scales, and the settling dust.</p>
<p>"You caught it," she said finally.</p>
<p>Her voice was small, stripped of its usual command, sounding almost breathless.</p>
<p>"Dropping it on intact structures was not efficient," Rex replied.</p>
<p>He didn’t look triumphant; he looked like a mathematician who had just finished a long, tedious equation. His breathing was steady, though the air around him still shimmered with residual heat.</p>
<p>"The northern rubble zone was already structurally compromised."</p>
<p>"The additional mass impact there produces less net damage to the city than the same mass impact anywhere else."</p>
<p>Aurelia stared at him, a dry, incredulous laugh catching in her throat. "You calculated where to drop an unconscious dragon... in order to minimize property damage?"</p>
<p>"The people in this city are going to need the structures that are still standing," Rex said simply, his voice carrying the weight of a man who felt the burden of every brick and every life. "It seemed worth the additional working cost."</p>
<p>Aurelia looked at him then, but the gaze had shifted. The complicated, political mask of the Nightwing household head had shattered, revealing a woman who viewed him as a mere variable in her son’s life.</p>
<p>The way she looked at him now was simpler, harder, and infinitely more dangerous. It was the look of a predator who had just realized the prey was actually the master of the jungle.</p>
<p>She was recalculating everything. The reality of what he had just done incinerated every assumption, every bias, and every prior thought.</p>
<p>Rex turned his head to meet her gaze.</p>
<p>She was projecting a thousand emotions at once: the shock of being saved by the very person she had most complicatedly judged and the dawning, terrifying realization that the direction of their relationship was shifting into a territory she hadn’t prepared for.</p>
<p>Behind her, Iris stood. Her expression was different, simpler, yet infinitely more profound.</p>
<p>The sharp, jagged grief of Veylor’s fall had settled into her features, no longer a screaming wound but a deep, permanent scar. It was the flat, heavy quality of a soul that had moved past the initial shock of tragedy and into the long, silent endurance of it.</p>
<p>Rex looked at Iris.</p>
<p>Iris looked back at him.</p>
<p>In that moment, the unspoken debris of the morning filled the distance between them. There was a gravity in their shared gaze, a silent communion born from the blood and the fire and the impossible things they had just survived.</p>
<p>It was a look that required no words; to speak would be to diminish it, to break the fragile, sacred tension of the moment. Rex held her eyes for a single, heart-stopping second.</p>
<p>Iris held him back, her gaze a silent anchor.</p>
<p>Then, Rex was the one to break it. He looked away, aware that continuing to gaze at her would feel like stealing, as it would carry more emotional weight than the moment could bear.</p>
<p>He turned his focus back to Aurelia.</p>
<p>"The household owed me an apology," Rex said.</p>
<p>The words were cold, stripped of any pretense of politeness. They hung in the air like the settling ash of the battlefield.</p>
<p>"This is not the way I would have chosen to collect it."</p>
<p>Aurelia fell silent. The wind whistled through the jagged ruins of the plaza, the only sound in a moment of profound, heavy stillness.</p>
<p>When she finally spoke, the polished, authoritative timbre of the Nightwing matriarch had vanished. The mask of the powerful household head had been stripped away, leaving behind the raw voice of a woman who had spent thirty years mastering every variable in her life, only to find herself standing in the wreckage of a reality she no longer controlled.</p>
<p>It was the voice of someone meeting a terrifying truth with brutal, unvarnished honesty.</p>
<p>"I told myself," she began, her gaze drifting to the northern rubble where the dragon’s broken, steaming carcass lay, "that what happened with Theo was a household matter."</p>
<p>"That it was being handled internally..."</p>
<p>"That the man... Rex Rexilion was merely resilient, a young man with an expedition ahead of him who would eventually be fine." She looked at the smoke still curling from the site of the elemental explosion, at the scarred earth where the dragon had been pulverized. "I told myself all of that."</p>
<p>"I believed the lies because they were convenient. And the part I did not believe... the part that sat like a stone in my gut... I simply chose not to look at it directly."</p>
<p>She turned her eyes to him, her gaze piercing. "That was not a failure of information, Rex."</p>
<p>"I had the data... It was a failure of character... I saw the truth, and I looked away."</p>
<p>"You did," Rex said.</p>
<p>He didn’t offer a warm smile, nor did he withhold a grudging forgiveness. His voice was a flat, undeniable acknowledgment of a fact.</p>
<p>He was a man who dealt in truths, not emotions.</p>
<p>"The expedition happened," Aurelia continued, her voice gaining a desperate, rhythmic momentum. "The canyon happened."</p>
<p>"I watched Iris crawl back from that hell, and I watched the hollowed-out thing she had become, and even then, I told myself it was managed..."</p>
<p>"That the household’s relationship with the Academy was stable..."</p>
<p>"That you were just a second-year student with a designation I didn’t quite grasp, and that Iris understood something about you that didn’t require my intervention." She paused, her breath hitching. "And then this morning happened."</p>
<p>"This morning happened," Rex agreed.</p>
<p>"Rex Rexilion," she said, and her voice finally landed in a place of absolute, terrifying clarity, the same clarity he had displayed when he refused to break under the dragon’s weight. "The honor of this household is yours..."</p>
<p>"Whatever my son did... whatever we allowed to be done in this house’s name... you have answered it in full."</p>
<p>Then, the most powerful woman in the district did something that felt more seismic than the dragon’s impact.</p>
<p>She bowed.</p>
<p>It was a deep, formal, and absolute gesture of submission and respect.</p>
<p>Rex stared at her. He looked at the bowed head of the woman who had once looked down on him as a mere footnote in her family’s history.</p>
<p>His mind drifted, a brief, violent flicker of memory. He thought of Theo, locked away in his room after the dinner, the smug arrogance on the boy’s face shattering the moment he realized the game had changed.</p>
<p>He thought of Elaris, her hands warm against his before the expedition, her eyes pleading with him to bring Iris back safely, a request born of the terrifying knowledge of what the world could do to a person.</p>
<p>And now, here he was. Standing in the ruins of a city he had technically helped dismantle, receiving the fealty of a dynasty.</p>
<p>He hadn’t broken Aethelgard because the Tremor had done that, but the distinction was vital. He was the architect of the resolution, not just a witness to the chaos.</p>
<p>He accepted the bow.</p>
<p>Behind Aurelia, Iris was watching him. Her expression was a complex, silent tapestry.</p>
<p>She looked like she wanted to scream, or cry, or perhaps pull him into the wreckage with her, but she remained still. She was holding back the words, knowing that to speak them would be to force them both to confront the sheer, bloody magnitude of what they had just survived.</p>
<p>The morning was not yet over, and the world was still bleeding.</p>
<p>Rex met her eyes for a single, searing second. A silent pact was made in the space between them: Not now. Not here.</p>
<p>Then, he broke the connection.</p>
<p>He turned his gaze to the horizon. His mind, fueled by the foresight, began to rapidly process the incoming data streams.</p>
<p>He saw the damage to the city, the smoke rising from the residential districts, the terrified citizens beginning to emerge from their shelters like ghosts through the haze. He tracked the engagement states of the remaining threats, the dying embers of the dragon’s energy, and the frantic, shifting positions of Zane and the others.</p>
<p>The Foresight showed him the end of the fight above the city, but it also showed him the beginning of the aftermath.</p>
<p>There was still work to do. The blood was still wet, the stone was still broken, and the world was still spinning toward the next catastrophe.</p>
<p>There was always... always... still work to do.</p>
