<p>The air in the plaza didn’t just feel heavy; it felt wrong. It was thick with the copper tang of fresh slaughter and the suffocating, dusty scent of pulverized granite.</p>
<p>The telepathic field, once a harmonious hum of the Apostle network, was now a jagged, screaming cacophony of tactical desperation.</p>
<p>Valentina’s presence in the mental ether had shifted. There was no more debate, no more philosophical tug of war with Rex’s encroaching darkness.</p>
<p>She had abandoned the luxury of argument for the cold, unyielding necessity of survival. Her transmission register had sharpened into a razor-edged frequency of tactical coordination.</p>
<p>Through her spatial awareness, she was mapping the golem network not as mere constructs but as a spreading, geological cancer. She was screaming out vectors, directing the remaining Apostle assets to intercept the shifting masses of stone before those golems could complete their next massacre.</p>
<p>But the golems were no longer just machines of war; they were engines of genocide.</p>
<p>Rex felt the shift in the frequency, a subtle vibration in the very substrate of the island. He felt the Apostles scrambling, the desperate, frantic energy of a species realizing the predator isn’t just at the door; the predator is the floor beneath their feet.</p>
<p>Through the Foresight, Rex watched the carnage unfold in slow, beautiful detail. He saw the way the golems didn’t just kill; they erased.</p>
<p>He saw a group of defenders caught in a pincer movement of basalt claws, their screams cut short by the sickening "CRACK SPLAT" of ribcages collapsing and skulls being flattened into the dirt. He felt the sheer, unadulterated joy of the genocide as the earth drank the blood of the fallen, turning the soil into a red, viscous mire.</p>
<p>And then, he felt her.</p>
<p>The geological passive awareness of his connection to the very bones of the world traced a rhythmic, powerful vibration approaching the plaza’s edge. The Foresight sharpened, feeding him the data: four seconds.</p>
<p>Four seconds before her physical form breached the visual horizon, the Foresight had already calculated the specific, lethal intent behind her approach.</p>
<p>Morwenna.</p>
<p>She was the shadow that the Nightwing household whispered about in hushed, reverent tones. She wasn’t just a combatant; she was a catastrophe in human form.</p>
<p>Rex had spent months observing her, dissecting her patterns, and the conclusion was always the same: she was a predator of a different order. She didn’t possess a loud, booming system that announced her presence like a herald; her power was the terrifying expertise of the silent blade, the kind of strength that didn’t need to scream because it never missed.</p>
<p>As she stepped into the plaza, the ground seemed to steady itself in her presence, a momentary truce between the earth and its master.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t move. He stood amidst the wreckage of the recent slaughter, a smear of Apollo’s blood drying on his cheek, a dark, triumphant grin playing on his lips.</p>
<p>He looked at her, his eyes gleaming with the madness of a man who had just tasted divinity through destruction.</p>
<p>"Lady Morwenna," Rex said, his voice cutting through the settling dust with a terrifying, calm clarity. "Welcome home."</p>
<p>"I’d say you picked a bad time to come back, but honestly..." He let out a short, dark chuckle that sounded like stones grinding together. "...your timing’s perfect for me."</p>
<p>"The feast is already laid out."</p>
<p>Morwenna didn’t flinch at the sight of the gore, nor at the monstrous, shifting golems looming in the periphery. Her eyes were cold, ancient, and utterly unimpressed by the carnage.</p>
<p>"Save it," Morwenna said.</p>
<p>Her voice was a flat, controlled monotone, the sound of a storm held behind a dam. She had already assessed the landscape, the broken bodies, the shattered golems, and the sheer scale of the massacre Rex had orchestrated.</p>
<p>She had arrived past the point of pleasantries, already locked into a state of lethal readiness. "I’ve heard about you... it seems like I heard enough about the underlayer that has changed because of you... or maybe you are the one that’s in disguise right now."</p>
<p>Rex’s grin widened, revealing teeth that seemed too sharp for a man. "That’s incredibly flattering, Lady Morwenna. Truly."</p>
<p>"It wasn’t meant to be flattering," Morwenna countered, her gaze narrowing as she stepped over the mangled remains of a fallen defender, her boots treading through a pool of thickening blood without a moment’s hesitation. "It was meant as a warning."</p>
<p>"And looking at this... looking at you... I realize now that I should have listened to it much sooner."</p>
<p>The air between them didn’t just crackle; it screamed.</p>
<p>The initial strike from Morwenna was a violent, reality-bending insult to Rex’s senses. His foresight had predicted a linear trajectory, a lunge, a strike, and a specific sequence of kinetic energy.</p>
<p>But the moment she moved, the predicted event chain shattered like cheap glass. She wasn’t just moving; she was warping the very concept of distance.</p>
<p>A wave of spatial distortion slammed into the plaza, a medium-range ripple of distorted reality that made the twenty meters of blood-soaked cobblestone between them fluctuate wildly. One second, she felt miles away; the next, the air buckled, and the distance collapsed as if the world were being folded by a giant, invisible hand.</p>
<p>Rex felt the sickening lurch in his gut as the spatial axis shifted. He threw himself sideways, a desperate, instinctive lunge that barely cleared the zone of distortion.</p>
<p>The ripple hissed past him, shearing the top off a nearby stone pillar as if it were made of soft butter.</p>
<p>"Fucking hell..." Rex spat, his boots skidding through a mixture of grit and gore as he regained his footing.</p>
<p>He wiped a spray of someone else’s blood from his jaw, his eyes burning with a newfound, predatory respect. "It seems like you used a counter for my foresight."</p>
<p>"You’re not just predicting the future; you’re fucking breaking it."</p>
<p>"You’ve been holding that one back, haven’t you?" Rex shrugged. "Waiting for the right moment to pull the rug out from under me."</p>
<p>Morwenna stood perfectly still amidst the chaos, her breathing shallow and rhythmic, her presence an island of terrifying calm in a sea of carnage.</p>
<p>"Thirty years I’ve mastered all sorts of counter magic and technique," she said, her voice cutting through the heavy air like a cold blade. "I don’t waste tricks on people who don’t deserve them."</p>
<p>"You’ve earned this, Tremor."</p>
<p>"I’m honored. Truly," Rex grinned, though his heart was hammering a frantic rhythm against his ribs. "But... let’s see if that counter can actually keep up!"</p>
<p>She didn’t give him time to savor the moment. She was already moving, and the difference in their combat styles became immediately, brutally apparent.</p>
<p>Fighting Apollo and Kaelira had been like fighting a thunderstorm: loud, overwhelming, and chaotic. But Morwenna was a surgical strike.</p>
<p>Her combat was the product of decades of refinement, a terrifying accumulation of muscle memory and optimized magical flow. Every movement was stripped of redundancy; there was no wasted breath, no unnecessary flair, no "cool" poses.</p>
<p>It was the pure, distilled essence of lethality. She had spent thirty years making mistakes against monsters and surviving them, and that survival had forged a terrifyingly adaptive style.</p>
<p>Rex had the Foresight, the Earthen Authority, and the godlike anchoring of the geological substrate. He had the sheer, unadulterated power of a high-tier villain system.</p>
<p>But Morwenna had thirty years of experience.</p>
<p>A massive golem, a jagged hunk of fused obsidian, surged toward her with a deafening roar. Rex commanded it to crush her, to turn her into a red smear on the plaza.</p>
<p>Morwenna didn’t dodge. She didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>With a subtle, almost imperceptible shift in her stance, she caught the golem’s momentum. She didn’t absorb the impact; she redirected it.</p>
<p>CRACK! SHHH!</p>
<p>The golem’s own massive weight was turned against itself, its momentum diverted into a sideways spin that sent the multi-ton construct crashing into a row of nearby buildings, pulverizing them into dust.</p>
<p>"You fight like someone who’s never had to fight for real," Morwenna said, her eyes never leaving Rex’s.</p>
<p>Her voice was devoid of emotion, yet it carried a stinging weight. "Everything you do has a backup plan built into it."</p>
<p>"You’ve never had a moment where the fucking backup plan wasn’t there to catch you."</p>
<p>"Is that an insult, or just a goddamn observation?" Rex barked, his teeth bared in a snarl.</p>
<p>He sent a second construct, a spiked, heavy set mass of granite, hurling itself at her with enough force to shatter a mountain.</p>
<p>"Both," Morwenna replied.</p>
<p>She stepped into the strike, her hand moving in a blur of motion. As the stone spiked toward her, she struck the exact structural weak point of the magical construct.</p>
<p>SNAP!</p>
<p>The sound of the golem’s core fracturing was like a bone breaking in a silent room. The construct didn’t just break; it imploded, the stone shards flying outward in a lethal spray.</p>
<p>"It also means I know exactly what happens when I take those backup plans away, one by one."</p>
<p>"I’ve done it before, Tremor. To people much stronger than you think you are."</p>
<p>"Then come and try it, you old witch!" Rex roared, his eyes flashing with a manic, violent light.</p>
<p>He surged forward, the earth beneath him erupting in a series of jagged stone spikes to force her into a defensive position. "But a word of warning: my backup plans have their own fucking backup plans!"</p>
<p>"Of course they do," Morwenna said.</p>
