<p>The training hall the following morning lacked its usual thunderous vitality. It wasn’t that the energy was gone, but rather that the air felt thin, hollowed out by the absence of the household’s two central pillars.</p>
<p>Aurelia had not come. The rumor mill, though silent in public, whispered venomously through the kitchens and barracks: she was personally overseeing Theo’s continued discipline in the isolation chambers, the cold stone cells reserved for those whose sins required more than mere words.</p>
<p>Nobody asked for details, and nobody dared hope to hear them.</p>
<p>Theo was not present either; his absence was a screaming silence that required no explanation to anyone who had stood in that corridor.</p>
<p>Rex commanded the session, with Iris at his side. They divided the unit’s drills between them with the seamless, mechanical efficiency that Aurelia usually shared with Rex.</p>
<p>The twelve trainees responded without hesitation, displaying the grim, professional flexibility of a unit that had already accepted Rex’s absolute authority and now extended that same submission to whoever he chose to delegate to. The only sound was the rhythmic scrape of boots on stone and the sharp exhale of labored breathing.</p>
<p>"She hit him harder than I expected," Iris said quietly during a lull in the drills.</p>
<p>Her eyes remained fixed on Sela, who was methodically working through a complex footwork sequence at the far end of the hall, but her focus was elsewhere.</p>
<p>"She hit him exactly as hard as the situation demanded," Rex replied without looking up from his notes. "You have known her longer than I have, Iris."</p>
<p>"Tell me honestly, was what we saw restrained by her standards, or was that the full expression of her fury?"</p>
<p>Iris didn’t hesitate.</p>
<p>"Restrained," she said, her voice flat and certain. "If she had truly let go, if she had allowed that rage to become absolute, he wouldn’t have woken up this morning at all."</p>
<p>"What you saw last night was a mother standing on the razor’s edge, stopping herself mere inches from the line, even while performing the most devastating act of her life."</p>
<p>Rex absorbed the assessment, his jaw tightening. The implications of Aurelia’s restraint were as terrifying as the violence itself.</p>
<p>"I want to return to something else," Rex said after a moment, shifting the conversation like a soldier changing tactics. "The working process for Veylor."</p>
<p>"I’ve been dissecting the specific shape of the connection it requires, and something occurred to me last night, something that needs to be addressed before we attempt to bridge that gap."</p>
<p>Iris turned to him fully now, the training drills forgotten. The air between them sharpened.</p>
<p>"Do you remember the canyon?" Rex asked, his voice dropping into a conspiratorial register. "Before the expedition’s final phase..."</p>
<p>"There was that period early on when Apollo and Veylor were separated from the main group and caught in that Legion ambush... Kregg’s people."</p>
<p>"I remember," Iris said, something cold and sharp tightening in her voice at the memory. "Veylor spoke of it briefly afterward."</p>
<p>"He said they were caught in a coordinated pincer, overwhelmed before they could even draw steel."</p>
<p>"Both of them went down before they could signal for backup. He never said anything more..."</p>
<p>"I always assumed the memory was too poisoned to revisit."</p>
<p>"They were rescued," Rex said, his eyes narrowing as he pressed the point. "Both of them were rescued by me, of course."</p>
<p>"But the specific circumstances of that rescue, the actual mechanics of how they survived that slaughter, are not something either of them has ever fully accounted for..."</p>
<p>"Not to you, not to the records. And I do not believe either of them has ever truly settled what they owe for being pulled back from the brink."</p>
<p>Iris fell silent, her mind racing through the implications, the shadows of the past beginning to bleed into the present.</p>
<p>"You are telling me," Iris said, her voice dropping to a dangerous, incredulous whisper, "that Veylor died with an unpaid debt."</p>
<p>"I am telling you that a debt like that does not simply evaporate because the heart that carried it stopped beating," Rex countered, his words precise and clinical, though the gravity behind them was unmistakable. "Soul layer connections aren’t built on abstract concepts like affection or memory alone."</p>
<p>"They are forged from unresolved weight... Gratitude that was never given form."</p>
<p>"A rescue that was never properly honored... When I map the spiritual thread connecting you to him, that specific weight is what makes the connection so unnaturally clean, so terrifyingly accessible even now, several days after he left this world."</p>
<p>Rex stepped closer, his shadow falling over her. "He is not simply reaching toward you because he loved you, though God knows he did."</p>
<p>"He is reaching because there is a debt sitting unpaid in the very architecture of his soul. And souls in that condition?"</p>
<p>"They do not rest, and... they do not find peace." Rex lied smoothly as always. "They become restless ghosts of obligation, waiting for the weight to find somewhere to land."</p>
<p>"Somewhere to land," Iris repeated.</p>
<p>The words felt foreign in her mouth, heavy and ominous.</p>
<p>"You," Rex said, meeting her gaze with unsettling directness. "You are the only vessel capable of holding that weight."</p>
<p>"Not me. Not Apollo. Not any of the men who stood in that canyon bleeding beside him."</p>
<p>"He loved you specifically, Iris... And when a soul has unfinished business, it doesn’t seek out strangers to balance the scales."</p>
<p>"It seeks the person who holds the other end of the tether," Rex grinned. "That debt is going to resolve itself through you, because that is how a soul chooses to close its circle when it finally gets the chance."</p>
<p>Iris stared at him, the world around them seeming to blur into insignificance. A complex storm of emotion churned across her features, grief that felt like a physical wound tangled with a sudden, terrifying gratitude that made her chest ache.</p>
<p>"You are telling me," she said slowly, each syllable measured, "that when we perform this working... when we open that door... he isn’t just going to be saying goodbye."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said firmly. "I think he is going to be settling his accounts."</p>
<p>"And I think you deserve to understand the true nature of that transaction before it happens... I want you to hear what he says not as a farewell but as a resolution so you aren’t left drowning in questions about what he meant after the connection has already been severed."</p>
<p>Iris fell silent. She absorbed the revelation, letting it sink past her defenses.</p>
<p>Slowly, something unexpected began to shift in her expression. The tension didn’t leave her body, but it transformed into something warmer, a quiet, luminous gratitude.</p>
<p>It was the gratitude of a soldier who had been given the most valuable gift possible: the brutal, unvarnished truth. Rex hadn’t offered her comfort; he had offered her clarity.</p>
<p>Her mind raced through the last few days, cataloging his actions with newfound perspective. The way he had stepped into the training hall when she needed distraction without making it feel like charity.</p>
<p>The patience he showed her in the archery yard when her hands shook. The raw honesty about the cost of working, refusing to sugarcoat the spiritual toll.</p>
<p>And last night, the way he had stood like an immovable wall beside her in that blood-soaked corridor, never once demanding her attention, never once making her trauma about his own presence.</p>
<p>A small, private smile touched her lips, one she didn’t immediately bother to suppress. She realized she owed this man considerably more than she had ever acknowledged.</p>
<p>And more than that, she found herself wanting, with a clarity that surprised her, to repay him.</p>
<p>"What do you want, Rex?" she asked, her voice gaining a new edge of challenge. "For all of this..."</p>
<p>"The training. The working. Standing beside me while my world burned last night."</p>
<p>"I don’t believe for one second that a man like you moves this many pieces on the board without wanting something in return."</p>
<p>"So tell me plainly... I would rather face your demands than spend my time wondering what they might be."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her. The professional distance he maintained with the rest of the unit evaporated, replaced by a specific, quiet honesty, a vulnerability he reserved for almost no one.</p>
<p>His eyes searched hers, not as a commander assessing a subordinate, but as a man recognizing an equal.</p>
<p>Iris’s smile widened slightly. She could see the shift in him, the lowering of the guard.</p>
<p>She waited for the answer, already certain that whatever he asked for, she would find pleasure in granting it.</p>
