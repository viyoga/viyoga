<p>Theo stood like a specter at the periphery of the courtyard, positioned precisely where the lantern light failed and the encroaching darkness began.</p>
<p>He was close enough to be acknowledged as present, a loyal servant, and a functional piece of the Nightwing machinery, but far enough from the warm, pulsing center of the celebration that his isolation felt deliberate rather than accidental.</p>
<p>Since that disastrous night in the guest room, his existence had been governed by two razor-sharp conditions.</p>
<p>Aurelia had delivered them not with anger, but with a devastating, glacial finality that left no room for negotiation. Her voice had been quiet, each word a tombstone.</p>
<p>’Attend to your duties without complaint.’</p>
<p>’And never under any circumstance approach Rex, Diana, or Iris with anything beyond the flat, hollow courtesy owed to any other member of this household.’</p>
<p>Theo had obeyed. He had become a master of restraint, a connoisseur of silence.</p>
<p>But obedience came at a price that no one in the household seemed to measure. As he watched the festivities, every heartbeat felt like a fresh wound.</p>
<p>His eyes involuntarily traced the patterns of the evening: he watched Diana laugh, a sound of genuine, unguarded mirth at something Sela said; he watched Rex move through the crowd with an easy, almost effortless authority, commanding the room not through rank, but through the strange, magnetic gravity he had earned.</p>
<p>Rex had become something more than a temporary instructor; he had become a pillar around which their entire world had reorganized.</p>
<p>’A replacement,’ Theo thought bitterly, the word tasting like copper in his mouth. ’Not just a guest, but a new foundation.’</p>
<p>He drank slowly, letting the bitter wine numb the edges of his awareness. He was a man serving a sentence he understood he had earned, yet a small, treacherous part of his soul screamed at the injustice of it.</p>
<p>He was watching the people he loved rebuild their lives, constructing new rooms of affection where he no longer had a key.</p>
<p>Then, the patterns shifted.</p>
<p>His instincts honed by years of vigilance and sharpened by a month of obsessive observation snapped to attention.</p>
<p>First, Elaris slipped away.</p>
<p>She had moved with that particular, deliberate grace she used when she intended to be alone, disappearing past the second round of toasts.</p>
<p>Then, almost immediately after, Rex excused himself from a conversation with Rilena and Sela. It wasn’t a hurried retreat; it was a smooth, practiced withdrawal, the kind practiced by someone who knew exactly where they were going and exactly when they would be missed.</p>
<p>Theo’s pulse quickened. A dangerous, familiar tightness coiled in his chest.</p>
<p>’Don’t,’ his rational mind commanded. ’Stay in the shadows.’</p>
<p>’Stay in your place. Do not become the fool again.’</p>
<p>The memory of the guest room burned behind his eyes—the humiliation, the shattered pride, and the raw, bleeding exposure of his own failure.</p>
<p>He understood now, with a clarity that felt like swallowing broken glass, that his actions then had been born of desperation and delusion. He had learned the lesson, but the wound refused to scab over.</p>
<p>Yet, as he watched the two figures disappear into the darkness beyond the courtyard’s edge, a pathetic, undeniable hunger rose within him. He told himself he was merely observing.</p>
<p>He told himself he was simply cataloguing the evening’s movements, maintaining his situational awareness. But deep down, beneath the layers of self-deception, he knew the truth.</p>
<p>He needed to see. He needed to witness the reality of his grandmother’s late-night arrangement with Rex.</p>
<p>He needed to know if the rumors of their private understanding were true or if his own twisted imagination was to blame.</p>
<p>He needed to know, with the cold certainty of a man who had already lost everything, exactly what kind of game was being played in the dark.</p>
<p>Theo moved like a ghost through the corridors, his boots finding the silent patches of stone, his breathing shallow and controlled.</p>
<p>He kept to the deepest shadows, hugging the cold masonry of the household’s inner passages. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic, trapped bird, but his hands remained steady.</p>
<p>He was a predator of information now, not of violence.</p>
<p>From the darkness of a corner, he watched Elaris slip into the guest room’s hallway, her movement fluid and purposeful. Minutes later, Rex followed.</p>
<p>There was no hesitation in Rex’s stride, none of the nervous energy that had plagued Theo during his own worst moments.</p>
<p>Rex walked with that same infuriating, unhurried confidence he brought to every room he entered, the confidence of a man who already owned the space he occupied.</p>
<p>Theo crept closer, his muscles coiled, drawn toward the guest room’s door like a moth to a flame.</p>
<p>’This isn’t what I did before,’ he whispered to himself, a desperate mantra to ward off the guilt. ’The blade was madness.’</p>
<p>’The blade was rage. This... this is simply seeking the truth.’</p>
<p>’I am not acting on a lie this time.’</p>
<p>’I am gathering evidence.’</p>
<p>’I am seeing what is real.’</p>
<p>But even as he rationalized his betrayal, his inner mind screamed at him. ’You are a coward, Theo.’</p>
<p>’You are a voyeur in your own home, scavenging for scraps of truth while you starve in the dark.’</p>
<p>He reached the door, pressing his body against the wall. He positioned himself close enough that a thin, tantalizing sliver of lamplight from within spilled across the threshold, licking at the edge of his vision.</p>
<p>Then came the sound: the low, warm murmur of voices. Not the sounds of confrontation or argument, but something intimate, something quiet and conspiratorial.</p>
<p>His pulse throbbed in his ears, drowning out the distant sounds of the celebration. He leaned in, tilting his head, desperate to catch a single syllable, a single syllable that would confirm his deepest suspicions.</p>
<p>He never heard them approach.</p>
<p>The darkness behind him didn’t shift; it simply materialized into violence. Before he could even draw a breath to gasp, Diana’s hand slammed across his mouth like a vise, cutting off his air and his voice in one brutal motion.</p>
<p>Simultaneously, Iris’s forearm locked across his throat with the precision of a guillotine.</p>
<p>The impact sent him stumbling against the doorframe, the wood groaning under his weight. The air in his lungs turned to fire as Iris applied pressure, her forearm grinding against his windpipe, threatening to collapse it.</p>
<p>He clawed at her arm, but her grip was absolute steel wrapped in silk.</p>
<p>Diana leaned close to his ear from behind, her breath cold against his skin, her other hand pinning his shoulder against the wall to prevent any desperate thrashing.</p>
<p>They moved with a terrifying, synchronized efficiency, the kind of coordination that only comes from two people who had rehearsed this exact scenario. They hadn’t just anticipated his betrayal; they had been waiting for it.</p>
<p>"You did not learn anything," Iris murmured.</p>
<p>Her voice wasn’t hot with anger. There was no venom, no shouting, no righteous fury.</p>
<p>Instead, there was something far more devastating: a flat, hollow, weary disappointment. It was the voice of someone who had expected exactly this from a broken man and was simply bored by his failure to change.</p>
<p>"We told your mother we would handle this quietly if it ever happened again," Iris continued, her forearm tightening just enough to make the world begin to gray at the edges of his vision. "She agreed it would be kinder to deal with you here, in the dark, than to wake the entire household with your pathetic attempts at spying a second time."</p>
<p>Theo fought with the desperate, scrabbling energy of a cornered animal, his fingernails raking uselessly against Iris’s forearm while his muffled screams died against Diana’s palm.</p>
<p>His brain screamed for oxygen, his carotid artery throbbed against the crushing pressure of Iris’s limb, and for a terrifying second, he thought he might actually break free through sheer panic.</p>
<p>But he was fighting trained predators, not mere servants.</p>
<p>Diana shifted her grip, her fingers finding the exact nerve cluster at the base of his skull, a precise, lethal pressure point she had mastered during her own grueling weeks of correction under Rex’s exacting tutelage.</p>
<p>The moment her thumb pressed home, a white-hot lightning bolt of agony shot through Theo’s spine, followed immediately by a terrifying numbness.</p>
<p>His legs turned to water. The corridor didn’t just tilt; it dissolved.</p>
<p>The stone walls became liquid, the shadows stretched into grasping claws, and his consciousness began to fray at the edges like burning parchment. His last conscious thought was not one of terror or anger, but a devastating, hollow irony: the soft, melodic sound of Elaris’s laughter was drifting faintly from behind the closed guest room door.</p>
<p>She was inside, warm and safe, laughing at something Rex had said, utterly oblivious to the fact that his own flesh and blood had been neutralized like a common intruder mere paces away.</p>
<p>Then, the blackness rushed in to claim him.</p>
<p>Theo went down without another sound, his body collapsing into a heap of useless limbs.</p>
<p>Diana and Iris did not simply drop him; they lowered him to the cold stone floor with a practiced, clinical gentleness.</p>
<p>There was no cruelty in their movements, only the dispassionate efficiency of two women who knew exactly how much force was required to teach a lesson without leaving permanent scars. They were not executioners; they were custodians of order.</p>
<p>Diana lingered for a heartbeat, straightening his disheveled collar with an absent, almost fond gesture the way one might fix the clothes of a child who had wandered too far from the path.</p>
<p>"He is going to wake up with a splitting headache and absolutely no memory of the last few minutes," Iris said, releasing her grip on his throat and stepping back.</p>
<p>Her voice was flat, carrying the exhaustion of someone who had performed a tedious but necessary chore. "Which, considering his track record, is honestly the kindest outcome available to him tonight."</p>
<p>"Better than what happened the first time," Diana agreed quietly, her eyes studying Theo’s unconscious form with something resembling pity. "Last time we would have left him bleeding on the floor."</p>
<p>"Tonight, we simply leave him with his dignity intact, even if he doesn’t know he nearly lost it."</p>
