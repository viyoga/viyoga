<p>Elaris looked at him with an expression of pure, unadulterated satisfaction, the kind she reserved for moments of genuine truth when she chose to forgo her usual theatrical flair in favor of something more profound.</p>
<p>"That is the first time anyone has described my particular gift as a talent rather than an inconvenience," she said, a playful glint dancing in her eyes. "Aurelia usually prefers the term ’premature certainty.’"</p>
<p>"A ’premature certainty’ that has been consistently, undeniably correct," Aurelia interjected.</p>
<p>Her tone carried the weary, affectionate weight of an older sister or the older daughter who had been arguing the same point for years, a debate that had long since transitioned from a conflict into a ritual of their bond.</p>
<p>She turned her gaze back to Rex, her posture becoming ironclad. "Which is a fact that I am acknowledging now... Formally... In the presence of witnesses..."</p>
<p>"Noted," Rex replied. His voice was a calm anchor in the shifting emotional tides of the corridor.</p>
<p>Aurelia reached behind her, her hand clamping firmly onto Theo’s arm. With a decisive, unyielding motion, she hauled him forward into the light of the conversation.</p>
<p>Theo’s face was a mask of suppressed agony. He looked like a man caught in a vise between two opposing forces: the ancestral loyalty that demanded he stand there and the blistering, jagged pride that made his very skin crawl at the humiliation of it.</p>
<p>He was a man being forced to kneel before the very person he had tried to break.</p>
<p>Theo looked at Rex.</p>
<p>Rex looked at him.</p>
<p>In that moment, Rex’s internal state was one of terrifying, crystalline clarity. It was the pure, predatory satisfaction of a grand architect watching the final stone of a cathedral click into place.</p>
<p>For months, he had been weaving a web of leverage, using the Nightwings’ own arrogance as the silk. The Morr Nightwing, the man who had struck him in the street, the man whose impulsive, arrogant fist had provided Rex with the perfect catalyst to dismantle the household’s standing, was now standing there, trapped.</p>
<p>Rex felt a slow, dark heat rising in his chest. He didn’t just want an apology; he wanted a total surrender.</p>
<p>He wanted to strip Theo of every ounce of the unearned dignity he clung to. As he watched Theo squirm, a tiny, almost imperceptible grin began to tug at the corners of Rex’s mouth—not a friendly smile, but the slow, creeping grin of a villain who had finally cornered his prey.</p>
<p>He had secured the respect of the family; now, he was going to systematically ruin the man who had dared to stand in his way.</p>
<p>Theo stared at Rex, his eyes performing a frantic, silent arithmetic of pride, trying to calculate the cost of this moment and realizing, with a sinking dread, that the debt was far higher than he could ever pay.</p>
<p>He turned to Aurelia. He wasn’t looking for permission; she had already stripped him of his agency, but he was looking for a lifeline, the kind of steadying strength people seek from authority when their own character is failing them.</p>
<p>But Aurelia gave him no such mercy. Her expression was a wall of cold, flat expectation.</p>
<p>She wasn’t there to hold him up; she was there to ensure he didn’t flinch.</p>
<p>He looked back at Rex, his eyes burning with a mix of resentment and shame.</p>
<p>Aurelia reached out and tapped the back of his head. It wasn’t a gentle, sisterly gesture.</p>
<p>It was a sharp, commanding strike, a physical reminder of his place and his duty.</p>
<p>Theo’s chin snapped down, his gaze forced to the floor.</p>
<p>"I apologize," Theo said. His voice was hollow, stripped of its usual bravado, possessing the flat, controlled quality of a man reciting a death sentence.</p>
<p>He was forcing the words through a throat constricted by shame. "For striking you in the public street... For the behavior that preceded it..."</p>
<p>"And for the household’s treatment of you throughout your time in Aethelgard."</p>
<p>Theo slowly raised his head.</p>
<p>His eyes were performing that frantic, vulnerable dance people do when they have just surrendered a piece of their soul and are waiting, breathless, to see if the transaction was worth the cost. He was looking for a crack in the armor, a hint of a trap, or the satisfaction of a victor.</p>
<p>Rex, however, met his gaze with a mask of warmth. He offered the slightly surprised, gentle expression of a man who had truly not expected an apology of such unvarnished sincerity.</p>
<p>He allowed the moment to breathe, processing the weight of Theo’s words with a grace that felt almost divine.</p>
<p>"Thank you," Rex said.</p>
<p>Then, he shifted his tone, employing that specific, polished register of graciousness, the kind of magnanimity that people remember and speak of in legends.</p>
<p>"I hold nothing against you, Theo... What matters to me is the stability of the people on this island, and you are a vital part of that."</p>
<p>The effect was immediate and devastating. Theo’s expression underwent a violent, internal shift.</p>
<p>The rigid flatness he had used to shield his pride, the controlled mask that had been keeping his ego from shattering, collapsed. It wasn’t gratitude that filled the void, but a profound, disorienting sense of vertigo.</p>
<p>He had arrived prepared for a siege, braced for Rex to demand a pound of flesh, but instead, he had found open ground. He had expected a wall to crash against, and instead, he found himself standing in a vast, empty field, unsure of how to balance himself.</p>
<p>"You should," Theo blurted out.</p>
<p>The words escaped before his intellect could intercept them, a raw, involuntary eruption from the part of his soul that operated far ahead of his pride.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes sharpened, the warmth remaining but a new, predatory curiosity flickering beneath the surface. "You should?"</p>
<p>"You should hold it against me!" Theo snapped, his voice losing its controlled cadence.</p>
<p>The polished veneer was gone, replaced by something jagged, honest, and deeply uncomfortable. "What I did... it wasn’t something that should just be absorbed into the ledger of ’moving on. ’I didn’t just disagree with you; I struck you in the public street!"</p>
<p>"I treated you like an inconvenience, a problem the household had to ’manage’ rather than a man who deserved basic respect."</p>
<p>"And the reason for that?" He choked on the words, his jaw tightening. "The reason had nothing to do with who you actually are..."</p>
<p>"It had everything to do with the lie I told myself about what you represented."</p>
<p>He stopped, his chest heaving slightly, the silence that rushed in to fill the gap feeling heavy and suffocating.</p>
<p>It was the silence of a moment that had broken the script. The rehearsed tension of a formal apology had been incinerated, leaving behind a raw, bleeding truth that none of them had anticipated.</p>
<p>Aurelia watched her son, her expression far from surprised. It was the look of a woman who had known a man her entire life and had just witnessed a ghost finally stepping into the light, a version of him that had always existed in the shadows, only now finding the courage to breathe.</p>
<p>Elaris was unnervingly still. She possessed the quiet, breathless stillness of someone witnessing a sacred ritual, terrified that even a single heartbeat might shatter the fragile, beautiful honesty of the moment.</p>
<p>Rex leaned in slightly, his gaze locking onto Theo’s with an intensity that felt like a physical weight. The dark, villainous part of him was delighted; the mask had fallen, and the real Theo was finally exposed.</p>
<p>"What did you think I represented?" Rex asked, his voice a low, melodic probe, pulling the truth from the wreckage of Theo’s pride.</p>
<p>Theo met his gaze steadily. The decision to strip himself bare had already been made; the heavy cost of the initial apology had been paid, and the momentum of truth was now carrying him forward, making the next part of the confession feel less like a choice and more like an inevitability.</p>
<p>"Something that was going to change the things I didn’t want changed," Theo said, his voice gaining a hard, crystalline edge. "Iris’s position in the household."</p>
<p>"The internal structure of the Nightwing legacy..."</p>
<p>"The specific, comfortable arrangement of the world that I had grown up in was an arrangement that your very existence was making unstable." He looked at Rex with the brutal, unvarnished honesty of a man who had stopped trying to manage his image and had simply decided to exist in the truth. "I didn’t look at the man standing in front of me..."</p>
<p>"I looked at the seismic shift your presence was forcing upon the landscape of my life."</p>
<p>"And now?" Rex prompted, his voice a velvet trap, inviting the final blow.</p>
<p>Theo’s expression fractured into something complex and deeply unsettled.</p>
<p>"Now, I watched what you did yesterday," Theo said, the words tumbling out with a newfound, heavy gravity. "And the day before..."</p>
<p>"I have watched the way Iris has transformed since the canyon, and I heard the weight in my mother’s voice this morning when she told me we were coming here." He paused, his eyes searching Rex’s for a sign of mockery, finding only a terrifyingly calm reception. "The shape of the world is different than it was..."</p>
<p>"It is jagged, and it is unfamiliar... I am still... trying to find my footing in what it has become."</p>
