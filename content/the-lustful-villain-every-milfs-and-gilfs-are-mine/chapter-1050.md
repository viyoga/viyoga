<p>"You need to help them," Rex said.</p>
<p>The command was simple, stripped of any pleading. It wasn’t a request born of empathy; it was a statement of tactical necessity delivered with the cold certainty of a chess player pointing out an obvious move.</p>
<p>Apollo didn’t move immediately. His mind was already racing, dissecting the problem with the ruthless efficiency of a strategist.</p>
<p>"My designation," Apollo said, his voice tight as he mentally ran through the complex physics of his own existence, "reverses entropy."</p>
<p>"Temporal rollback is a specific type of entropy; it operates on the cellular development level rather than the standard decay level."</p>
<p>"I am designed to mend what breaks and restore what withers, not to re-stitch the fabric of time itself."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said. His patience was absolute.</p>
<p>"My designation’s reversal at full activation covers entropy of the standard kind, the degradation of matter, the loss of energy, and the inevitable march toward disorder," Apollo continued, his brow furrowing as he stared at the flickering city. "The temporal variety is a different mechanism."</p>
<p>"It’s not just decay; it’s a displacement of sequence."</p>
<p>"But the mechanism’s effect on the biological system," Rex interjected, cutting through Apollo’s hesitation like a scalpel, "is the same category of biological deviation from the system’s natural state that your designation is optimized to address."</p>
<p>"Whether the wound is caused by time slipping backward or by cellular death, the result is a system that has diverged from its proper trajectory."</p>
<p>"You fix trajectories, Apollo."</p>
<p>Apollo’s gaze swept across the carnage. He looked at the unconscious civilians convulsing in the streets below, their bodies trapped in a nauseating stutter between what they were and what they should be.</p>
<p>He looked at Theo, a man whose power had become a weapon of mass disorientation, a broken god weeping over a broken world. Then he looked back at Rex.</p>
<p>In Rex’s eyes, Apollo saw no comfort. He saw only the cold calculation of a man who believed that if a tool existed to fix a problem, it was a moral failure not to use it.</p>
<p>"You want me to try," Apollo said. It wasn’t a question; it was a challenge.</p>
<p>The weight of the responsibility pressed down on him: the possibility of failing, of miscalculating the reversal and making the cellular damage worse, of accidentally accelerating the very decay he meant to stop.</p>
<p>"I want you to stop them from losing that development time permanently," Rex said, his voice unwavering. "Whether the specific mechanism falls within your designation’s optimal range is something you find out by trying."</p>
<p>"Hesitation here isn’t noble, Apollo. It’s just inefficient."</p>
<p>Apollo stared at him for one more second, a long, charged moment where the two most powerful forces in the area measured each other’s resolve. Then, without another word, Apollo moved.</p>
<p>He didn’t fly; he launched. He moved toward the field’s outer radius with a terrifying, singular purpose.</p>
<p>Every muscle in his body was coiled for execution, his movements precise and economical. He was a man who had made a decision and had closed off all avenues for doubt.</p>
<p>He was moving to become the suture for a wound in reality.</p>
<p>Rex watched him go, his eyes tracking the golden streak of Apollo’s descent into the chaos. Only when Apollo had crossed the threshold of the distortion did Rex turn his attention back upward.</p>
<p>Theo was still suspended in that amber gold nightmare, his eyes fixed on the collapsing bodies below, his breathing shallow and ragged. He looked like he was drowning in his own guilt.</p>
<p>"They will be addressed," Rex said. The words were quiet, but they cut through the ambient hum of the temporal field.</p>
<p>Theo’s head snapped up. His eyes were bloodshot, wild with a desperate, searching pain.</p>
<p>"What?"</p>
<p>"Apollo is going," Rex said, his voice steady as a heartbeat. "His designation covers the biological deviation."</p>
<p>"The people in the field are going to be all right."</p>
<p>Theo’s throat bobbed as he swallowed hard. A flicker of something, hope perhaps, or perhaps just relief, touched his ravaged features.</p>
<p>"Because of him," Theo whispered. "Because he’s... good."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him, and for a moment, the mask of the philosopher slipped, revealing the terrifying emptiness beneath. "Because of the situation’s available resources, Theo."</p>
<p>"Nothing more."</p>
<p>Theo watched with ravenous, desperate attention as Apollo descended into the outer radius of the devastation. Through the haze of his own exhaustion and the ringing in his ears, Theo could see Apollo kneeling beside the first of the collapsed civilians, a small child whose body had been shuddering in a nauseating temporal loop.</p>
<p>Apollo’s activation was not the overwhelming solar flare of a battlefield resurrection. There was no thunderous chorus of divine light.</p>
<p>Instead, it was something far more intimate and terrifyingly precise. A soft, controlled hum of energy emanated from him, a focused thread of golden radiance that didn’t explode outward but rather threaded itself into the child’s broken cellular structure.</p>
<p>It was the work of a surgeon, not a savior; the designation’s power was honed by Apollo’s own iron will to reverse the specific entropy the temporal field had forced upon the child’s body.</p>
<p>The child’s breathing stuttered, then smoothed. With a wet, gasping sound, the boy sat up, blinking rapidly as his biological clock snapped back into its proper alignment.</p>
<p>Rex watched the process with detached fascination. His mind, operating at a processing speed no human could comprehend, immediately catalogued the data.</p>
<p>’The life affinity designation can address temporal distortion’s biological effects when focused with sufficient precision. ’ He didn’t feel relief; he felt the cold satisfaction of a scientist confirming a hypothesis.</p>
<p>This was a new variable, a new tool to be understood and, eventually, exploited.</p>
<p>He filed the information away in the vast, silent archives of his consciousness. Then, he turned his gaze upward to Theo.</p>
<p>"The children are being addressed," Rex said, his voice cutting through the static of the failing temporal field. "The adults will recover on their own as the field loses its remaining intensity."</p>
<p>"No one will die from the field’s secondary effects."</p>
<p>Theo didn’t respond immediately. He remained suspended in the air, his amber aura flickering like a dying candle, his shoulders slumped under the impossible weight of what he had done.</p>
<p>His eyes remained fixed on the distant golden speck that was Apollo, working in the dirt.</p>
<p>"I did not mean for that to happen," Theo whispered.</p>
<p>The words were hollow, stripped of conviction, the plea of a man who wanted to believe his intentions mattered more than his consequences.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said.</p>
<p>"That does not make it so," Theo started, his voice rising with a desperate need to justify the unjustifiable.</p>
<p>"Not okay," Rex interrupted, his tone flat and final. "No... It is not okay."</p>
<p>"The field at scale produced effects you did not intend, and those effects reached people who were not participants in this engagement."</p>
<p>"That is the specific, brutal reality of combat at this scale when the scale is operated beyond the operator’s experience level."</p>
<p>"Intent is irrelevant when the output exceeds control."</p>
<p>Theo’s head snapped toward Rex, his eyes blazing with a flash of wounded anger. "You sound like you’re giving a lecture."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said simply. "Because you need to learn the lesson before you kill something you can’t bring back."</p>
<p>They remained thirty meters apart, suspended in the charged air above the ruins of the Nightwing household. The morning light was beginning to bleed through the clouds, cruel and honest.</p>
<p>It didn’t hide anything; it illuminated every jagged shard of broken concrete, every pool of blood soaking into the cracked pavement, and every twisted piece of metal that had been warped by the temporal storm. The light made the carnage undeniable.</p>
<p>Rex looked at the scene, but his mind was already three steps ahead, moving through the complex calculations he had been running since the moment Theo’s power had first breached containment.</p>
<p>His capability allowed for a terrifying level of parallel processing he wasn’t just observing; he was orchestrating.</p>
<p>Throughout the second phase of the engagement, the moment Theo had unleashed his area expression Rex’s mind had been divided into four simultaneous streams of analysis:</p>
<p>First: The full capability profile of Theo’s designation and the terrifying implications it held for their inevitable approach to Kaeleth.</p>
<p>Second, the specific mechanism he had just witnessed was how Apollo’s life affinity could be manipulated to counter temporal entropy.</p>
<p>Third: The precise location of Aurelia within the wreckage of the Nightwing interior, tracking her heat signature and biological rhythm through layers of debris.</p>
<p>Fourth: The calculation of the exact moment, the precise nanosecond, that this engagement’s current trajectory would create the opening he required.</p>
<p>Rex’s gaze remained on Theo, but his true focus was elsewhere. He was not waiting for the fight to end.</p>
<p>He was waiting for the moment of maximum vulnerability.</p>
<p>He was waiting for Aurelia.</p>
