<p>[Present]</p>
<p>The throne room was not a place of ceremony. Celestina had no subjects to impress, no nobles to intimidate, and no court to maintain.</p>
<p>She held no kingdom; she managed an apparatus, a complex, multi-tiered structure of operatives designed to keep her at its invisible center. The room existed for one purpose: thought.</p>
<p>It was a cathedral of silence, constructed from the same cold, unyielding stone that formed the Seam House itself. The throne was a slab of ancient granite positioned at the far end of the chamber.</p>
<p>Afternoon light bled through narrow, slit-like windows, cutting across the center of the room in sharp amber blades, but the throne itself remained swallowed in deliberate shadow. Celestina sat within that darkness, her posture perfect, the practiced stillness of a woman who had spent decades learning how to occupy space without disturbing it.</p>
<p>Her eyes drifted to the time crystal resting on the armrest’s built-in shelf. It was dark.</p>
<p>It had remained dark since Rex had returned from Ignivara, his face splattered with the dried crimson of whatever atrocity he had performed to secure the mission. He had crushed the communication crystal he’d retrieved, severing the direct channel to the field in a moment of violent finality.</p>
<p>But with this crystal, this was different. This was not the blue-white stutter of the operational relay network.</p>
<p>This was Kaeleth’s direct line. A divine frequency.</p>
<p>It had been dark because Kaeleth had not called. Until now.</p>
<p>The crystal suddenly ignited, pulsing with a deep, ominous amber gold that seemed to bleed light into the surrounding shadows. Celestina reached for it with the flat, mechanical efficiency of someone who had been cultivating a specific kind of patience for exactly this moment.</p>
<p>"Tell me," she said. Her voice was a scalpel: clean, precise, and devoid of emotion.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s presence flooded through the crystal. It wasn’t sound; it was pressure.</p>
<p>It was that same terrifying weight without mass she had felt in the tower, the feeling of a predator’s gaze pressing against her skin.</p>
<p>"The worker’s anchor has been severed," he said. The words weren’t spoken so much as they were imposed upon reality.</p>
<p>Celestina’s fingers did not twitch. Her pulse did not quicken.</p>
<p>Her mind, however, immediately began cataloging the implications, spinning through contingency plans like a clockwork engine. She was a creature whose reactions were entirely internal, converting shock into immediate, cold assessment.</p>
<p>"Completely?" she asked, her voice dropping half an octave.</p>
<p>"Completely," Kaeleth confirmed. "The reincarnator’s specific anchor point was violently disconnected from the working’s framework approximately six hours ago."</p>
<p>"The severance was not a gradual decay; it was a catastrophic system event tied directly to his divine patron."</p>
<p>A pause followed, one heavy with the implication of something unprecedented.</p>
<p>"I cannot identify the patron from the event’s signature," Kaeleth continued, his divine tone carrying something akin to genuine irritation. "The signature was masked using a technique I have not encountered in all my eons of observation."</p>
<p>"Don’t tell me... it’s the presence manipulation," Celestina said immediately. The words felt like ash in her mouth.</p>
<p>"You know this technique," Kaeleth stated. It wasn’t a question; it was a demand for confirmation.</p>
<p>"I know of it," she replied, her mind racing through archived intelligence reports. "It was documented in the Legion’s assessment of high-tier reincarnator abilities."</p>
<p>"We identified it three years ago as a theoretical threat vector, a way for a patron to cloak their influence behind a veil of static..."</p>
<p>"I never expected to see it deployed in this specific context... Never."</p>
<p>"The masking was not reactive, Celestina," Kaeleth said, his voice growing colder, more dangerous. "The masking was applied retroactively."</p>
<p>"The patron’s signature was already hidden when the system event occurred."</p>
<p>"The disconnection didn’t trigger the mask; the mask allowed the disconnection to happen unseen."</p>
<p>"The sequence indicates something far worse than an accident."</p>
<p>"It indicates planning," Celestina finished for him.</p>
<p>She sat in the growing darkness of her throne room, the amber light of the crystal casting jagged, skeletal shadows across her face. The fog she had spent years building, the perfect, seamless lie she had engineered with a god, hadn’t just been pierced; it had been surgically excised.</p>
<p>"He knew about the working," Celestina said.</p>
<p>The words weren’t a question; they were a diagnosis. "He wasn’t just caught in the fog, but he was waiting for it to lift."</p>
<p>"He knew about the working," Kaeleth confirmed, his voice carrying a rare edge of something almost resembling fury, not at Celestina, but at the violation of his own divine mechanics. "Or he knew about a working of this nature."</p>
<p>"The specific response, the exact frequency used to destabilize the framework, was too precise."</p>
<p>"It wasn’t a blunt instrument breaking a door down; it was a master key turning in a lock I didn’t even know existed."</p>
<p>"His patron told him," Celestina stated, her mind already dissecting the tactical implications.</p>
<p>"His patron has been observing the working’s effects on his apostle and identified the source," Kaeleth explained.</p>
<p>The amber light of the crystal flared brighter, as if responding to his agitation. "The counter frequency that shattered the working’s full framework was delivered through the patron’s domain connection, a direct, surgical strike that bypassed every layer of temporal protection I had established."</p>
<p>Celestina felt a coldness settle in the marrow of her bones. "The patron has been watching you."</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>"For how long?" Celestina demanded.</p>
<p>Her voice remained controlled, but there was an undercurrent of steel that demanded answers. "I am not asking to provoke you, Kaeleth, but I am asking because the answer dictates whether this was a reactive strike or a premeditated ambush."</p>
<p>"I need to know if the design of this work was compromised from its first hour, or if your god was simply waiting for the right moment to strike."</p>
<p>A silence stretched between them, a silence that felt heavier than the stone of the throne room. When Kaeleth finally spoke, the admission carried a weight that made the very air in the chamber feel thin.</p>
<p>"I do not know," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>For an entity whose entire existence was defined by the measurement of eternity, the admission was catastrophic. It was the sound of a compass spinning wildly in a void.</p>
<p>"That itself concerns me more than the counter frequency did," Kaeleth continued, his voice dropping to a low rumble. "I should be able to trace the patron’s observation window with reasonable precision."</p>
<p>"I have traced far subtler intrusions into far older workings than this one—ghosts in the machine that lasted millennia before I detected them... But I cannot get a clean read on this specific presence."</p>
<p>"It is either so profoundly shielded that it defies my perception or, more troubling, it was never fully inside my domain’s observation range to begin with."</p>
<p>"Which would mean it was watching from somewhere outside my ability to watch it back."</p>
<p>Celestina’s grip on the armrest of her throne tightened. Her knuckles were white against the dark granite.</p>
<p>"That is not a comfortable answer."</p>
<p>"No," Kaeleth said, and the finality of it was chilling. "It is not."</p>
<p>Celestina stared into the amber-gold depths of the crystal, watching the light pulse like a dying heartbeat. The years of planning, the decades of preparation, the blood spilled to create the perfect, unassailable lie—all of it had been dismantled in a single, silent moment of divine intervention.</p>
<p>"What is the work’s current state?" she asked.</p>
<p>"Collapsed," Kaeleth said. "The anchor point severance produced a cascading structural instability that the counter frequency exploited like a wedge in a crack."</p>
<p>"The working is no longer active in Aethelgard..."</p>
<p>"The loop condition has evaporated, and the reset condition is gone."</p>
<p>"The mechanism has been destroyed."</p>
<p>"And the people in the city?" Celestina asked, her mind immediately jumping to the fallout. "The operatives? The civilians? The exposure to such a sudden collapse</p>
<p>"They have retained everything," Kaeleth interrupted. "All memories, all developments, all physical changes that occurred during the working’s duration."</p>
<p>"The counter frequency was designed to preserve the timeline’s integrity while surgically removing the working architecture."</p>
<p>"They did not reset, and they did not lose time... They simply... woke up."</p>
<p>"They are no longer within a managed temporal framework..."</p>
<p>"They are back in the raw, unfiltered stream of causality."</p>
<p>Celestina closed her eyes for a fraction of a second. "He is free."</p>
<p>"He has been free since the anchor severance," Kaeleth said. "And in the hours since that occurred, he has not been hiding."</p>
<p>"He has been making specific, coordinated strategic movements that suggest he knows far more than just the existence of the working..."</p>
<p>"He knows about us."</p>
<p>The implications crashed over her like ice water. The prey had not only escaped the trap, but the prey had also been waiting for the trapper to set it.</p>
<p>"Tell me," Celestina said, her voice deadly quiet. "Tell me everything."</p>
