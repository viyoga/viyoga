<p>The silence that followed was different. It was no longer the oppressive weight of a predator assessing prey, nor the sharp edge of a debate.</p>
<p>It was something far more profound: the sound of a cosmic mind recalibrating. The very fabric of reality seemed to shudder as Kaeleth shifted his assessment of the mortal standing before him.</p>
<p>The divine presence didn’t just observe her now; it began to weigh her as an equal variable in a grand equation.</p>
<p>"That is the first genuinely useful thing you have offered me tonight," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>His voice had lost its mocking edge, replaced by something colder, something almost resembling respect. "Not the working itself... But the cover for it."</p>
<p>"You understood that I would eventually require plausible deniability, so you built the legal architecture into the proposal before I could even ask for it."</p>
<p>Celestina felt a tremor of triumph in her chest, though she kept her features as frozen as ice. "I told you, Kaeleth..."</p>
<p>"I did not come here for a single conversation, but I came here to conclude a transaction."</p>
<p>"The working will be inextricably tied to your operational objectives," Kaeleth warned, his aura flaring like a dying sun, casting jagged shadows across the chamber. "Understand this: if your objectives shift, if your purpose wavers or changes direction, the working’s anchor will become unstable."</p>
<p>"The temporal loop will fracture, and the resulting backlash could tear Aethelgard apart before the reset even begins."</p>
<p>"My objectives will not change," Celestina said, her voice ringing with the conviction of someone who had already burned every bridge behind her. "I have sacrificed everything my morality, my peace, my humanity to this singular purpose..."</p>
<p>"I am not a woman who wavers."</p>
<p>"They have not changed in twelve years," Kaeleth observed. "I have watched your organization’s trajectory from the periphery."</p>
<p>"You are consistent. That is a rare quality in your kind."</p>
<p>Celestina’s eyes narrowed.</p>
<p>"You were watching me long before tonight." It wasn’t quite a question; it was a realization that chilled her more than any divine threat.</p>
<p>"I watch every sustained disruption to the timeline," Kaeleth said. "An organization that eliminates reincarnators with such relentless consistency over twelve years produces a very specific, very legible pattern in the fabric of causality."</p>
<p>"Each elimination you make is a small stitch, a minor correction that my observation function registers automatically, whether I am paying direct attention or not. I noticed your pattern approximately four years ago."</p>
<p>A muscle jumped in Celestina’s jaw.</p>
<p>’Four years...’ She had thought she was a ghost, a shadow moving through the world unnoticed.</p>
<p>"I did not know it was you, specifically, standing at the center of that web," Kaeleth continued, his presence expanding until it felt as though the walls of the tower were dissolving into the void. "Until you climbed this tower tonight and made yourself visible on purpose."</p>
<p>"You stopped being a pattern and became a person."</p>
<p>"Four years," Celestina repeated.</p>
<p>A strange, dark satisfaction curled in her throat. "I did not expect to have been noticed that early."</p>
<p>"You were thorough," Kaeleth said, and there was a terrifying sincerity in his words. "Thoroughness is legible to anyone whose entire function is reading patterns, even when the thorough person believes herself to be invisible."</p>
<p>"You left too many clean graves, Celestina."</p>
<p>"Yes," she whispered. "Perhaps I left too many."</p>
<p>"I will do this," Kaeleth declared.</p>
<p>The shift in the air was instantaneous. The tension didn’t vanish; it solidified.</p>
<p>The divine intent settled into the environment like molten lead, heavy and irreversible. "The working begins when you are ready."</p>
<p>"The reset condition will be exactly as you described and the duration will remain indefinite, lasting until the condition is met or until you withdraw the anchor yourself."</p>
<p>Celestina took a breath, but she didn’t relax. The deal was struck, but the weapon needed to be perfect.</p>
<p>"There is one more thing I want confirmed before I agree to the terms."</p>
<p>The divine presence stilled. "Speak."</p>
<p>"The people inside the working during its duration, however long that may last I do not want them merely confused about elapsed time in the abstract," she said, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper that carried more power than a scream. "I want the confusion to be load bearing."</p>
<p>"I want a reincarnator standing in that city, at any point during the working’s life, to be fundamentally, biologically unable to say with confidence how long it has been since they arrived in this world..."</p>
<p>"Not approximately unable. Not ’I lost track of time.’ I want them to be actually unable, in a way that no conversation, no calendar, and no comparison of notes can resolve."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s aura pulsed with curiosity. "Why that specific cruelty?"</p>
<p>"Because a reincarnator who knows exactly how long he has been building something is a reincarnator who can defend the timeline of his creation," Celestina said, her eyes blazing with cold calculation. "A reincarnator who can point to a date and say, ’I have held this position for six months,’ possesses a weapon of certainty."</p>
<p>"But a reincarnator who cannot say for certain whether it has been four months, six months, or eight months is a man whose entire operational history becomes soft... unverifiable... and fragile..."</p>
<p>"I want that softness available to me as a weapon just in case the reset condition is never met and I need another way to unmake everything they have built."</p>
<p>"You are already planning for the working’s failure," Kaeleth said, his voice dropping to a register that made the stone floor beneath Celestina’s boots vibrate. "You are asking me to initiate a cosmic operation while simultaneously preparing the tools to dismantle it."</p>
<p>"You are asking for a weapon and a shield in the same breath."</p>
<p>"I plan for failure in every arrangement I make," Celestina shot back, her voice unyielding despite the way her heart hammered against her ribs. "Failure is not an inconvenience; it is a statistical certainty."</p>
<p>"It is the specific reason my organization has survived twelve years in a world that would prefer it did not exist."</p>
<p>"We survive because we assume every knife at our throat is already halfway through our skin."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s presence surged, a cold wave of divine inevitability. "The confusion you are describing will not be limited to your targets, Celestina..."</p>
<p>"You cannot surgically remove the concept of time from only certain minds without leaving scars on the fabric itself."</p>
<p>"If I remove the population’s ability to measure elapsed time with precision, the effect touches the foundational layer of causality, the very substrate that every soul in that city is connected to."</p>
<p>"Your own operatives will be drowned in it."</p>
<p>The god’s eyes, twin voids of ancient knowledge, bored into hers. "Valentina Von Starlight, who has led that academy for decades with ruthless precision, will find herself equally adrift."</p>
<p>"She will become unable to say with total confidence how long it has been since specific events occurred, even events she believes she remembers with absolute clarity."</p>
<p>"Her certainty will become as soft as the peasant’s."</p>
<p>Celestina steeled herself. A flash of doubt flickered in her mind—the image of Valentina’s face, sharp and commanding—but she crushed it.</p>
<p>Doubt was a luxury she had burned away years ago.</p>
<p>"That is acceptable," Celestina said. "Valentina has kept meticulous records for decades precisely because she does not trust her own memory over long periods."</p>
<p>"She has built her authority on documentation... When the fog rolls in, she will not reach for her recollection; she will reach for her ledgers."</p>
<p>"And those ledgers, rewritten by the very mechanics of the working, will agree with the working’s version of reality rather than the true one."</p>
<p>"I am not concerned about my own people being caught in the fog, but I am concerned about the fog holding."</p>
<p>She leaned forward, her shadow stretching long and thin across the divine chamber. "A fog that only surrounds my targets while leaving everyone else perfectly clear-eyed would not be a natural phenomenon."</p>
<p>"It would be an obvious anomaly, a beacon of suspicion, and it would be the one unnatural feature in an otherwise seamless confusion..."</p>
<p>"I want the lie to be absolute."</p>
<p>"You want the fog to be completely dense so that it never appears artificially created," Kaeleth mused, the realization rippling through the air like a shockwave.</p>
<p>"Yes," Celestina said, her voice carrying a grim satisfaction. "The moment anyone in that city can point to a single person whose sense of time is suspiciously precise while everyone else’s has eroded, they will start asking questions."</p>
<p>"And the questions will lead directly back to us... Total confusion is safer than selective confusion."</p>
<p>"It is also, I will admit, the more honest version of what I am asking for... so I would rather build one true, all-consuming fog than a hundred small, manufactured certainties that I would have to defend individually."</p>
<p>"That is the most honest thing you have said to me tonight," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>The words felt like a benediction and a curse all at once. "This statement stands out in a conversation that has otherwise been constructed entirely out of careful phrasing and tactical obfuscation."</p>
<p>"I try to save the honesty for the parts that matter," Celestina replied, her mask slipping just enough to reveal the cold steel beneath. "It is more effective that way."</p>
<p>She had been forty-three years old when she made that bargain.</p>
<p>The conversation had lasted approximately twenty minutes, but the consequences would last for centuries.</p>
<p>The work had begun six months ago, or perhaps it had been eight. Or perhaps it had only been three weeks.</p>
<p>To the people inside the radius, time had become something viscous, something that could be stretched or compressed without warning. They would reach for dates, for seasons, for the passage of years, only to find their fingers slipping through something that felt like smoke.</p>
<p>Every dated record in Aethelgard would now agree with the working’s version of reality, a consensus of lies written in ink and stone.</p>
<p>No one’s actual memory would be permitted to keep an honest count because no one’s memory had been allowed to hold a steady rhythm since the night Celestina Von Starlight stood atop a lonely tower and made a foreign god notice her on purpose.</p>
