<p>She leaned forward, her forehead nearly touching his ghostly image, though she knew she would only feel the biting chill of his displaced existence. "But you are gone..."</p>
<p>"You have been gone for twenty-six days, and I have spent every single one of those days pretending... In some quiet, rotting corner of my soul, I have been waiting."</p>
<p>"I have been pretending that some part of you was still standing behind me, watching over me, waiting for me to catch up to you... And that pretense is killing me."</p>
<p>Her fingernails dug into Rex’s palms so hard she felt the wet heat of blood beginning to seep between their joined hands; the price of the ritual was becoming physical, brutal.</p>
<p>"I need to move on, Veylor," she whispered, her tears dripping onto the floor like molten silver in the candlelight. "I need to actually allow myself to live again..."</p>
<p>"And I cannot do that while I am still holding onto a ghost..." Iris shook her head. "I cannot build a future while I am still shackled to a corpse."</p>
<p>"I need to let you go... for both of us... and the greater good."</p>
<p>The silence that followed Iris’s confession was absolute, a suffocating weight that seemed to press the very oxygen from the room.</p>
<p>It stretched long enough that panic began to claw at her throat. She wondered if the working had catastrophically failed, if the connection had severed mid-sentence, leaving Veylor to dissolve back into the formless fog of the abyss without the closure she so desperately craved.</p>
<p>Then, from where he knelt like a living statue, his eyes still closed and his grip on her hands unyielding despite the visible strain in his tendons, Rex began to laugh.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a cruel laugh, nor was it mocking. It was the low, rumbling amusement of a man who had witnessed the universe conspire to make a moment of profound tragedy swing violently into the realm of the absurd.</p>
<p>The sound was jarring against the backdrop of Iris’s raw grief. She turned to glare at him, her eyes red rimmed and furious even through the tears, but the expression died halfway when she saw Veylor’s translucent form actually blink.</p>
<p>The ghost looked genuinely startled, the specific expression of a dead man who had expected a celestial reunion but found himself caught in the middle of a domestic argument anchored by a laughing necromancer.</p>
<p>"You couldn’t have led with something gentler, could you?" Rex said, his voice rough but warm.</p>
<p>He still didn’t open his eyes; he couldn’t. The magical feedback would shatter his concentration, but his shoulders shook with the effort of containing the mirth. "You waited twenty-six days of agonizing torment for one single conversation, and the very first thing out of your mouth is breaking up with a dead man."</p>
<p>"I did not plan it to sound like that!" Iris snapped, her mortification rising like bile.</p>
<p>She wiped at her face with the back of her wrist, smearing tears and salt across her cheeks. "I meant it kindly, Rex! I meant it as an act of mercy!"</p>
<p>"I understood the kindness underneath the brutality, Iris," Veylor said. Despite the ethereal glow of his skin, there was a new softness in his expression.</p>
<p>A small, unsteady laugh of his own escaped him, a broken, haunting sound that seemed to vibrate in the space between them. "You have never once in your life said anything gently..."</p>
<p>"You approach everything like a siege, and that’s why I loved that about you."</p>
<p>"I’m not going to pretend I’m surprised that even a supernatural visitation came out sounding like an assassin’s report rather than a farewell."</p>
<p>"I am trying, Veylor," Iris whispered.</p>
<p>She was caught in that terrible, honest limbo where laughter and sobbing become indistinguishable, two opposing forces tangled together in her chest until she couldn’t tell which one was winning. "I am trying so hard to do this right."</p>
<p>"I know you are," Veylor said.</p>
<p>The warmth in his voice was undeniable, undiminished by the crushing grief that still sat like lead in his ghostly chest. His form flickered brighter for a moment, a surge of recognition passing between them.</p>
<p>"And I am not angry... I want you to hear that as clearly as you wanted me to hear the rest of it."</p>
<p>"I am not hurt by your honesty."</p>
<p>He leaned forward as far as his spectral nature would allow, his luminous eyes searching hers. "You deserve to move on, Iris."</p>
<p>"You deserve to let someone hold you, someone who has bones you can touch, someone who can actually answer your touch with heat instead of this cold light..."</p>
<p>"I have thought about very little else in that grey place except how much I wanted exactly that for you... Even while some smaller, uglier part of me, the part that still belongs to you, hated the very idea of it."</p>
<p>Veylor’s form flickered violently, his edges fraying like burnt parchment. The instability was terrifying to witness the raw, metaphysical strain of a soul being forced into a shape it was never meant to hold for this long.</p>
<p>The luminous silver of his skin began to pulse erratically, each pulse accompanied by a low, harmonic hum that made Iris’s teeth ache. His expression shifted, the resigned acceptance dissolving into something far more primal: desperation.</p>
<p>It was a raw, grasping quality, the look of a drowning man reaching for a lifeline that was already slipping through his fingers.</p>
<p>"Is there truly no other way?" he asked.</p>
<p>His voice had lost its melodic echo; it was now thin, strained, as if he were speaking through a throat filled with ash. He wasn’t looking at Iris anymore; he was looking past her, his luminous gaze fixed on Rex with a pleading intensity.</p>
<p>"There must be some loophole... Some crack in the foundation."</p>
<p>"Tell me there is a way to keep some thread between us, Rex...! A dream, once a year?! A sign in the wind she would recognize as mine?!"</p>
<p>"A bird that flies wrong, a shadow that lingers too long?"</p>
<p>Veylor’s form convulsed, a spark of static energy jumping from his shoulder to the floor. "I do not need to be here like this."</p>
<p>"I do not need flesh and bone and this terrible, borrowed solidity... I only need to know there is some door left open... Even if it’s a crack or even... a door that opens only once in a lifetime."</p>
<p>"Just... don’t tell me I’m truly erased."</p>
<p>The tension in the room reached a breaking point. Iris could feel the magical pressure building, a physical weight pressing against her chest like a stone.</p>
<p>She could hear Rex’s breathing, heavy and ragged, each exhale sounding like grinding metal as he fought to maintain the ritual’s integrity against the encroaching void.</p>
<p>Then, Rex opened his eyes.</p>
<p>They were bloodshot, the whites mapped with crimson veins from the sheer strain of the necromantic channeling. The pale, unnatural light of the workshop reflected in his pupils like twin dying stars.</p>
<p>When he spoke, the sound wasn’t comforting. It carried the specific, unshakeable finality of a judge passing sentence, the voice of a man who had already mapped every possible outcome, weighed every cosmic variable, and found no salvation.</p>
<p>"There is no door, Veylor," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice was gentle, but it was a gentleness that offered no shelter from the truth. "I am not here to give you false hope to soften the blow."</p>
<p>"That would be a cruelty worse than the truth itself."</p>
<p>Rex’s knuckles were white where they gripped Iris’s hands, and Iris could feel the heat of his blood where the magical feedback was beginning to tear at his own capillaries.</p>
<p>"The working I used to pull you across the veil draws on a connection that only exists because the thread between you and Iris has not yet completed its transition," Rex explained, each word measured and precise, though his jaw was clenched so tight it looked ready to shatter. "You are still tethered to her by the unfinished business of your grief and her refusal to let go."</p>
<p>"This ritual isn’t creating something new... it is exploiting the wound you left behind."</p>
<p>"But once this conversation ends, once she says what she needs to say and truly releases you, that wound will begin to scar..."</p>
<p>"The thread will finish what it was always going to finish: it will sever."</p>
<p>Rex’s gaze locked onto Veylor’s fading form, unflinching. "There will be no dreams, Veylor."</p>
<p>"No signs in the wind... No whispers in the night... and what is happening right now is not a glimpse through a window."</p>
<p>"This moment is the door. And it is closing the very instant I release the work."</p>
