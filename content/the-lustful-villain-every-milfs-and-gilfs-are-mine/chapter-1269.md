<p>The silence of the manor was suddenly shattered by the heavy thud of the front door swinging open, the sound echoing through the vaulted corridors like a gunshot.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t startle; he simply shifted, his body responding to the sound with the fluid grace of a predator sensing movement in the brush.</p>
<p>He stepped out of the dining room and into the dimly lit hallway, his bare feet silent on the marble as he waited.</p>
<p>Lily came first.</p>
<p>She appeared in the doorway as if stepping out of a dream, backlit by the pale moonlight spilling in from the entrance. She had dressed with a visible, nervous intention.</p>
<p>She wore a simple dress of pale green silk, the color of spring leaves after rain, with a modest neckline that hinted at the soft curves beneath without revealing too much. The skirt swished against her legs as she moved, a soft whisper of fabric that seemed to betray her agitation.</p>
<p>Rex could tell she had struggled with her appearance; the ribbon tying back her hair was slightly askew, a small imperfection that spoke of unsteady hands, and the dress itself bore the faint wrinkles of someone who had changed it at least twice before finally settling on this.</p>
<p>Her cheeks bore a delicate, feverish flush, and her eyes—those bright, expressive eyes—were luminous, shining with an intensity Rex hadn’t seen in weeks.</p>
<p>The moment her gaze locked onto his, the careful composure she was trying to maintain shattered. She crossed the hall in a desperate rush, her footsteps quick and uneven, until she reached him.</p>
<p>Her small hands flew to his arm, gripping his bicep with surprising strength, as if she needed the physical contact to anchor herself to reality.</p>
<p>’Is this really the night?’ Her voice didn’t come through the air, but through the psychic link they shared, arriving in his mind like a fragile, breathless whisper. It was small, shy, and laced with a raw vulnerability that made something stir in his chest. ’The one you promised?’</p>
<p>’I thought... I thought you might have forgotten, Rex.’</p>
<p>’With everything that has happened, with the blood and the chaos... I did not want to ask.’</p>
<p>’I was afraid you would say it was over.’</p>
<p>Rex looked down at her, his expression softening just enough to disarm her. He didn’t just answer with words; he reached through the connection, letting a deliberate wave of warmth deep, steady, and possessive bleed into his thought so she would feel it like a physical caress against her heart.</p>
<p>’I never forget a promise made to you, Lily,’ he answered, his mental tone carrying the weight of an oath. ’Not the small ones, and certainly not this one.’</p>
<p>’Come inside. Sit down. You look beautiful—more beautiful than you have any right to be.’</p>
<p>The effect was immediate. A crimson flush crept up from her neckline, spreading across her cheeks until her ears burned pink.</p>
<p>For one unguarded moment, she leaned forward, burying her face against the hard muscle of his shoulder, breathing him in. It was a moment of pure, unguarded need before she remembered where she was and who she was supposed to be.</p>
<p>When she pulled away, she drew herself up with a shaky exhale. She lifted her chin, forcing her shoulders back in an attempt to reclaim some semblance of aristocratic dignity.</p>
<p>She walked past him toward the dining room with measured steps, pretending to be the composed daughter of House Starlight, pretending that this surrender, this intimacy, was something she performed every day.</p>
<p>But Rex could still feel the frantic, quick flutter of her heartbeat through the link, and he knew better.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Diana arrived a few minutes later, and she was a far more complex puzzle for Rex to solve. Unlike Lily’s obvious vulnerability, Diana carried herself with the disciplined, lethal grace of a weapon.</p>
<p>She wore a dress of deep midnight blue, simpler than Lily’s, cut closer to her athletic frame, emphasizing the lean strength of her shoulders and the dangerous elegance of her movements.</p>
<p>She walked into the hall with the straight-backed calm of an archer who had spent a lifetime mastering the art of stillness, but Rex could read between the lines of her composure.</p>
<p>There was a faint, telltale flush along her cheekbones that no amount of discipline could fully suppress, and when her gaze finally met his, her eyes dropped for a fraction of a second, a momentary lapse in her armor, before snapping back up with defiant intensity.</p>
<p>She was fighting herself, trying desperately to maintain the facade of the stoic soldier, but Rex found the effort endearing. It was the strain of a predator trying to pretend it wasn’t hungry.</p>
<p>"You cooked," she said, her voice steady, though her eyes flickered past him to the amber lit table, taking in the elaborate spread.</p>
<p>"I assembled," Rex corrected smoothly, stepping closer to her, close enough to feel the restrained energy rolling off her. "The cooking was done by a roast that did most of the work itself while I played host."</p>
<p>A ghost of a smile touched her lips, almost imperceptible, but there. "Lily’s been talking about tonight since dawn... She changed her dress three times."</p>
<p>"I-I did not!" Lily’s voice drifted from the dining room, indignant and bright. Then, softer, almost sheepishly: "...only twice."</p>
<p>Rex watched them both, his analytical mind cataloging the subtle fractures in their spirits. Despite the silk and the candlelight, they were both still carrying the war.</p>
<p>He could see it in the way Diana’s gaze occasionally drifted toward the far wall, her pupils contracting as if she were listening to the distant echo of screams on a battlefield. He could see it in the way Lily’s laughter would cut off half a second too early, leaving a hollow silence behind it.</p>
<p>They had both known Apollo. He had been their comrade, their friend, and in the private spaces of their hearts, something more.</p>
<p>They had watched him die not in some glorious final stand, but in the grim, ugly reality of a corridor broken by the wrong end of a magical discharge. Rex knew the memory of that moment was a wound that wouldn’t close; he had been careful not to push, knowing that some ghosts are better left in the dark.</p>
<p>He waited until they were both seated, the candlelight dancing between them, before he spoke. He kept his voice low, rough with a carefully manufactured sincerity, ensuring it sounded like raw honesty rather than the calculated move of a strategist.</p>
<p>"I know the last week has been hell," he said, his dark eyes moving between them. "I know both of you have been carrying more than you’ve said aloud."</p>
<p>"I’m not going to stand here and lie to you... and I’m not going to tell you it was fine, because it was devastating... It was brutal."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the weight of his words settle. "But I’ve been thinking about it."</p>
<p>"And I think that when the world feels like it’s ending, that is exactly when people deserve a night where nothing is asked of them."</p>
<p>"No duties, no grief, no expectations." He leaned forward slightly, his presence filling the space between them. "The city can wait until morning... The war is over."</p>
<p>"You’ve given everything anyone could ask. Tonight... tonight belongs only to you."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was charged with tension. Lily’s eyes began to shine with unshed tears, her breathing quickening as the emotional dam began to crack.</p>
<p>Diana didn’t move at first; she simply stared at him, searching his face for any sign of deception, any hint of the commander instead of the man.</p>
<p>When she finally found none, she let out a long, shuddering exhale. The rigid stiffness in her shoulders and the stance of a soldier on guard finally collapsed, leaving behind only the woman.</p>
