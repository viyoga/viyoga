<p>Rex initiated the sequence with the clinical detachment of a surgeon cutting into living tissue. Temporal Stop.</p>
<p>The world didn’t just freeze; it died. The cascade of sound, movement, and life evaporated in an instant.</p>
<p>The mechanism was flawless: the navigation component stolen from Theo’s designation acted as the perfect mirror.</p>
<p>Through his Full Potential Adaptation, Rex had already experienced the initial shock of the temporal freeze that first contact shudder, where reality stutters before the mind catches up.</p>
<p>But now, the adaptation had fully extended its coverage, stitching his consciousness into the frozen moment.</p>
<p>Two seconds of absolute, terrifying stillness. Then, the adaptation locked in.</p>
<p>Rex began to move. He walked through the silent city like a ghost haunting a museum of corpses.</p>
<p>Pigeons were suspended mid-flight, their wings locked in impossible angles, feathers frozen in the air like shards of glass.</p>
<p>Reconstruction crews were statues of flesh and steel, caught mid-swing, their faces twisted in expressions of mundane labor that now looked grotesque in their immobility.</p>
<p>To Rex, the world had become a silent photograph, and he was the only living thing capable of breaking its frame.</p>
<p>’This is the silence before the scream,’ Rex thought, his boots making no sound on the cobblestones. ’The silence of a god who doesn’t know he’s already been betrayed.’</p>
<p>He reached into the fabric of reality, applying dimensional manipulation directly to the precise dimensional address he had extracted from Theo. He didn’t need a portal; he didn’t need a gateway.</p>
<p>He was constructing something far more dangerous. At the interface where his teleportation skill’s spatial navigation met the dimensional manipulation’s cross-layer traversal, a new capability crystallized: directional movement through dimensional layers without a standard anchor point.</p>
<p>He was slipping between the stitches of existence itself.</p>
<p>Then, he felt it: the layer boundary.</p>
<p>For his entire existence in Erosyne, the physical plane had been his only truth: the texture of air, the weight of gravity, and the solidity of stone.</p>
<p>But the boundary to the adjacent layer wasn’t a wall or a barrier. It was a transition of medium. It felt like stepping from the thinness of air into the crushing, viscous embrace of deep water.</p>
<p>The reality around him rippled, the colors bleeding at the edges as the fundamental laws of physics began to warp.</p>
<p>Rex pushed through.</p>
<p>The world exploded into amber gold.</p>
<p>He emerged not into a place, but into a concept. Kaeleth’s domain was not composed of mountains, sky, or seas.</p>
<p>It was composed of the very temporal symbols. Rex had witnessed during the God’s brief communications with the mortal world the complex, looping notations that mapped time’s properties.</p>
<p>But here, there was no parchment, no sky to hold them. The symbols were the environment.</p>
<p>The ambient medium of this realm was a living, breathing notational framework. Great spirals of golden glyphs writhed through the amber void like celestial serpents; cascading waterfalls of mathematical symbols flowed through the air, each one a heartbeat of time itself.</p>
<p>This was the substrate of eternity manifested as reality. To stand here was to stand inside the clockwork of the universe, where every symbol was a law and every motion was a second passing.</p>
<p>’Magnificent,’ Rex thought, his eyes reflecting the golden chaos. ’A kingdom built of math and memory.’</p>
<p>’How fragile it must be when the equation changes.’</p>
<p>He immediately checked his Presence Manipulationpresence manipulation. The divine class suppression was holding steady at its baseline.</p>
<p>He could feel the domain’s defensive architecture, the ancient, automatic sentinels of Kaeleth’s will brushing against him like invisible fingers. He watched the invisible gauges of his own power as they interacted with the realm’s defenses.</p>
<p>The domain’s consciousness registered him. But it did not register a predator.</p>
<p>It did not register a usurper.</p>
<p>Through the active feedback of his compression, Rex confirmed the deception: Kaeleth’s defenses read him as nothing more than a moderately capable mortal reincarnator, a stray soul who had stumbled through an unusual navigation method.</p>
<p>To the god’s domain, Rex was not a threat to be eliminated; he was a curiosity, a bug caught in the gears that was too small to break the machine.</p>
<p>’A curiosity,’ Rex mused, a cold, predatory smile spreading across his face. ’Let them think that.’</p>
<p>’Let them marvel at the anomaly before I tear their heart out.’</p>
<p>With the confidence of an assassin who has already won, Rex turned away from the border and walked deeper into the golden heart of the domain.</p>
<p>At the heart of the amber gold vortex, Rex found him.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was not the distant, ethereal silhouette that appeared in mortal prayers or divine communications.</p>
<p>Here, in the sanctum of eternity, the God of Time was not a being inhabiting a space; he was the space. He was the convergence of every looping symbol, every cascading glyph, every heartbeat of chronological flow.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was the environment expressing itself with terrible, singular intent, a consciousness woven into the very fabric of the temporal framework.</p>
<p>But even a god was not immune to distraction.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was focused on a visualization bleeding through the domain’s ambient medium. Rex watched as the god observed Aethelgard.</p>
<p>The city’s entire timeline was laid bare in a grotesque, beautiful display of temporal notation. It wasn’t merely an archive; it was a live diagnostic.</p>
<p>Rex could see the jagged scars in the timeline where his own temporal distortion field had torn through the fabric of reality, the anomalous patterns showing the ripple effects of his violence.</p>
<p>He could see the collapse of those fields, the way time had buckled and groaned under his weight, and the countless lives that had been frayed and broken in the process.</p>
<p>And then, Rex saw what held the gods’ attention.</p>
<p>There was a void. A specific, screaming absence in the timeline where Theo’s designation should have been.</p>
<p>A hole in existence where a divine signature had once burned bright, now replaced by a hollow nothingness. Theo was gone, erased, dismantled, and rewritten out of the cosmic ledger.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was staring at that absence. He was studying the wound Rex had left in reality.</p>
<p>Rex did not hesitate. He stepped forward, closing the distance until he stood approximately thirty meters from the God’s central manifestation.</p>
<p>He stopped, his boots planted firmly on a floor of swirling equations.</p>
<p>’Now,’ Rex thought. ’Let’s stop pretending.’</p>
<p>With a deliberate flick of his will, Rex shattered the illusion. He dropped the Presence Manipulation suppression.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a gradual release; it was an explosion. His full divine class signature erupted from his core, flooding the amber medium with the violent force of a supernova breaking through a shell.</p>
<p>The compressed power he had been holding back surged outward, expanding with a hungry, predatory quality that demanded recognition.</p>
<p>The golden symbols around Rex recoiled as if struck by a physical blow, the mathematical notations fracturing and spinning wildly in the wake of his true nature.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s attention snapped away from the visualization. The God’s consciousness pivoted, tearing itself away from the ghost of Theo to face the reality standing before him.</p>
<p>In that instant, the divine register shifted. For the first time since Rex’s signature had first flickered across Kaeleth’s observation functions, the God’s perception produced a reaction that Rex had been orchestrating since the moment he stepped into Lustia’s domain.</p>
<p>It was a reaction that transcended language, biology, or mortal emotion. It was recognizable across every possible divine expression mechanism.</p>
<p>Fear.</p>
<p>This was not the trembling of a mortal throat or the racing of a human heart. This was something far more profound.</p>
<p>This was the existential terror of an ancient entity, a being that had maintained its absolute dominion for two thousand years, an entity that had developed a perfect, unerring instinct for what constituted a threat to its continued existence.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s entire being was screaming a single warning: ’This is not a trespasser. This is an end.’</p>
<p>The amber gold symbols that formed Kaeleth’s body began to writhe in panic. They reorganized with terrifying speed, shifting from the fluid motion of ambient presence into the rigid, sharpened configurations of active engagement.</p>
<p>The environment itself began to harden, forming weapons of pure chronological force as the god prepared to defend his throne.</p>
<p>But Rex was already moving.</p>
<p>He didn’t run; he simply ceased to be where he was and existed where he needed to be. He crossed those thirty meters in the infinitesimal span between Rex’s signature release and Kaeleth’s instinctive shift into combat form.</p>
<p>He moved through the space between seconds, faster than thought, faster than the God could react to the threat he now understood.</p>
<p>What Rex produced in that crossing was not a spell. It was not a technique.</p>
<p>It was not a calculated attack.</p>
<p>It was a thought made manifest through absolute violence.</p>
