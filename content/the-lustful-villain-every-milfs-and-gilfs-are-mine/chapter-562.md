<p>Apollo’s expression changed in a way that indicated he had received an accurate assessment and was integrating it instead of defending against it.</p>
<p>He looked at his cup. He looked at the hallway.</p>
<p>"I’ve been so focused on what the ring did to the designation," he said quietly. "On getting it back to full function."</p>
<p>"On the expedition, on Kaelira’s recovery, on the Legion investigation..."</p>
<p>"I’ve been prioritizing important matters over personal issues, thinking that the latter could wait."</p>
<p>"You know the problem with that," Rex said.</p>
<p>"That the personal things are important," Apollo said.</p>
<p>"That the personal things are the only things that actually require your presence," Rex said. "Everything else has a system."</p>
<p>"The people who love you don’t run on a system."</p>
<p>’And here is the part where I give you genuinely good advice,’ Rex thought, ’because genuinely good advice delivered at the right moment produces a specific kind of trust that nothing else can manufacture.’</p>
<p>’You’re going to hear this, and it’s going to land the way it lands because it’s true, and you’re going to think: Rex told me this in the middle of the night in my parents’ hallway, and it cost him nothing to say it, and he said it anyway.’</p>
<p>’You’re going to think: this is what a real friend looks like.’</p>
<p>’And you won’t be entirely wrong. Which is the most useful thing about this moment.’</p>
<p>This time, Apollo remained quiet for a longer period. Rex, let the silence sit.</p>
<p>It was the kind of quiet that needed to resolve on its own.</p>
<p>"When did you get good at this?" Apollo said finally, and it was not quite a question.</p>
<p>"At what?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"At seeing what people actually need," Apollo said. "Not what they’re asking for."</p>
<p>"And not what they’re performing, but the actual thing underneath."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him. "What are you talking about?"</p>
<p>"I’ve been doing it for a long time," he said. "It’s not a skill that announces itself."</p>
<p>Apollo looked at Rex with the specific quality of attention that was his when he was genuinely trying to understand something rather than simply process it.</p>
<p>"Sometimes I think there’s a version of you that I haven’t seen yet," he said. "I’m not going to describe it as a bad version, but rather as one that probably has more positive qualities."</p>
<p>’More positive qualities...?’ Rex thought. ’Yes... that’s actually a good fucking joke coming from him.’</p>
<p>’There is considerably more, but not positive.’</p>
<p>’You have seen the surface, and the surface is accurate as far as it goes, which is what makes it effective.’</p>
<p>’The part underneath the surface would not be read to you as a version of me.’</p>
<p>’It would read as a separate entity entirely.’</p>
<p>’You will meet that entity eventually.’</p>
<p>’I am looking forward to the introduction.’</p>
<p>"There’s a version of everyone who doesn’t surface immediately," Rex said. "Most people don’t need to see it."</p>
<p>"But some people do," Apollo said.</p>
<p>"I agree, some people do," Rex said.</p>
<p>Apollo held his gaze for a moment, the specific look of someone who has almost followed a thread to its end and has stopped one step short.</p>
<p>’One step,’ Rex thought. ’You are one step from it.’</p>
<p>’Right now, in this hallway, you are closer to the truth than you have ever been.’</p>
<p>’If you took one more step, if you asked one more question, if you pushed past the answer I just gave you and into the silence underneath it, something in you would know.’</p>
<p>’But you won’t.’</p>
<p>’Because you trust me...’</p>
<p>’And trust, in people like you, is not a conclusion you revisit without compelling evidence.’</p>
<p>’You decide to trust someone, and you hold that decision with both hands because releasing it would require you to revise something fundamental about how you see the world.’</p>
<p>’You’re going to hold it.’</p>
<p>’Right up until the moment you can’t.’</p>
<p>Rex walked past him toward the front door. He stopped at the threshold, his hand on the frame, and looked back.</p>
<p>"Apollo," he said.</p>
<p>Apollo looked at him.</p>
<p>"When everything around you feels like it’s moving faster than you can track," Rex said, "and you can’t tell which things to hold onto and which things to let go of, remember that the people who stayed did it with full information about who you are."</p>
<p>"They’re not staying because they don’t know what you cost them."</p>
<p>"They’re staying because they’ve decided you’re worth it."</p>
<p>He let this sit for one beat.</p>
<p>"Don’t make them regret the decision," Rex said.</p>
<p>Apollo was quiet, and the quality of his quiet was the one he had when something had reached him somewhere past the professional register, somewhere that did not have a composed response prepared.</p>
<p>"You talk like someone who’s already seen where this goes," Apollo said.</p>
<p>’I have,’ Rex thought. ’In considerable detail.’</p>
<p>"I talk like someone who pays attention," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Rex." Apollo’s voice had dropped to the register he used when he was saying something he meant without qualification. "Whatever happens..."</p>
<p>"Whatever comes next with the Legion, with the upcoming attacks from the Underlayer, and with everything that’s moving right now."</p>
<p>"I want you to know that I consider you someone I’d want on my side in the final moment of whatever this is."</p>
<p>Rex looked at him, and he tried so hard not to laugh just by hearing it.</p>
<p>’The final moment.’</p>
<p>’Apollo... you have no idea how precisely you have described the thing that is coming...’</p>
<p>’Not the shape of it, not the actors in it, not the side you will find yourself on...’</p>
<p>’But the moment itself... Yes... There will be a final moment...’</p>
<p>’I have been building toward it since before you knew I existed.’</p>
<p>’And I will be there.’</p>
<p>’That part you got exactly right.’</p>
<p>"I’ll remember that," Rex said.</p>
<p>It was not a promise. It was a statement of fact, and every word of it was true, and Apollo heard it as the thing he needed to hear, which was the most precise summary of their entire relationship that Rex could have produced in four words.</p>
<p>He held Apollo’s gaze for a moment and thought, ’This encounter is probably the last conversation we will have where you look at me like a person you trust without qualification.’</p>
<p>’The next time we are in the same space, the variables will be different, and by then I will have done things that you would not recognize as coming from the same person who stood in your mother’s front hallway and told you to stop neglecting the people who chose you.’</p>
<p>He did not say this directly.</p>
<p>He looked at Apollo one more time.</p>
<p>The earnest jaw. The open, direct eyes. The posture of a man who had decided to be fully present in a hallway at three in the evening because that was who he was and he had stopped apologizing for the cost of it.</p>
<p>’You are exactly what the story needed you to be,’ Rex thought. ’The hero...’</p>
<p>’Clean and real and genuinely worthy of what people feel for you.’</p>
<p>’I’m not going to pretend otherwise, even in my own head, because pretending otherwise would be dishonest and I don’t lie to myself...’</p>
<p>’You are worthy.’</p>
<p>’And it won’t matter soon.’</p>
<p>’Because worthy and prepared are two different things, and I have been preparing for longer than you have been paying attention, and when the moment arrives, all the worthiness in the world is not going to change what I’ve built around you.’</p>
<p>’Sleep well, Apollo.’</p>
<p>’While you still can.’</p>
<p>He nodded once, and Apollo nodded back, and Rex went out through the front door into the night.</p>
<p>The city was dark and cool and entirely indifferent to the shape of what had just been said in a hallway, and Rex walked through it with the unhurried pace of someone who had finished one thing and was moving toward the next, and he did not look back.</p>
<p>’Now...’</p>
<p>’...let’s get to that real work.’</p>
