<p>His shoulders squared by several degrees, each movement precise and mechanical. His spine lengthened, elongating his frame until he seemed to tower over the wreckage.</p>
<p>In that singular moment, the easy, slightly rumpled young man who had spent hours sitting in an armchair with a cup of tea disappeared entirely. He was gone, erased from existence.</p>
<p>In his place stood something ancient and terrible, someone who seemed taller than the ceiling had ever been, someone whose very stillness possessed a crushing physical weight.</p>
<p>His eyes, visible through the unmasked half of his face, went cold. It wasn’t the cold of anger; anger was human, and anger was fleeting.</p>
<p>This was the cold of a glacier, the cold of the void between stars. The air itself seemed to thin and starve.</p>
<p>A few of the nearest candles that had miraculously survived the collapse flickered violently before their flames simply died, snuffed out by the sheer absence of life in the room.</p>
<p>The temperature plummeted so sharply that Helena’s next breath emerged as a faint, ghostly white puff in the freezing air.</p>
<p>’This isn’t Rex,’ her mind shrieked, a panicked voice echoing in the hollow chambers of her skull. ’This isn’t him...’</p>
<p>’What have I woken up?’</p>
<p>Cassandra and Gorvasha were the closest to him, and they moved with the synchronized grace of predators recognizing their alpha. Without a single word exchanged, they shifted.</p>
<p>Cassandra went down on one knee with lethal elegance, her crimson sword laid flat across her thigh in a gesture of absolute submission. Gorvasha dropped beside her with a thunderous thud, one massive, scarred fist pressed against the broken floor, her tusks catching the dim light as she lowered her face to the dust.</p>
<p>Then, the rest of the underlayer force followed. It was not a series of individual movements but a single, rolling wave of steel and shadow.</p>
<p>Rank after rank of soldiers dropped to their knees in perfect unison, their armor clashing against the stone in one deep, ringing CRASH that echoed violently up through the hole in the ceiling and reverberated out into the streets like a funeral knell.</p>
<p>Lilith knelt last. She remained a little apart from the others, her cheeks flushed a dark, feverish crimson, her eyes lowered as if she couldn’t bear to witness the transformation.</p>
<p>Her hands were clasped so tightly in front of her that her knuckles appeared translucent.</p>
<p>"Lord Xerollion," Cassandra said.</p>
<p>Her voice was steady, but it carried across the ruined hall with the reverence of a prayer delivered to a god.</p>
<p>The name dropped into the room like a sledgehammer shattering a sheet of glass. The sound of it seemed to fracture what little hope remained in the air.</p>
<p>"You want to know who holds this city," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice had changed. It was no longer the voice of a man; it was something deeper, slower, possessing a terrifying resonance that seemed to vibrate up through the floorboards and into the bones of everyone present.</p>
<p>It was the same subsonic tone that had rolled across the plaza days ago—the same tone that had forced an entire kingdom to listen in horrified silence to the final Chapters of its own history.</p>
<p>"You are standing in front of him."</p>
<p>The declaration hung in the air, heavy and undeniable.</p>
<p>Nobody spoke.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was not merely an absence of sound; it was a physical presence, a suffocating shroud that pressed against Helena’s chest, making every heartbeat feel like a hammer blow against her ribs.</p>
<p>Helena’s mouth hung slightly open, but her throat felt as though it had been seized by invisible claws; no sound could escape. The blood drained from her face so completely that her lips took on the ghastly, bloodless grey of a corpse.</p>
<p>Her eyes were locked onto that violet veined mask, and Rex could see the frantic, desperate machinery of her mind working behind those pupils.</p>
<p>She was trying to force a shape into a hole that was fundamentally wrong, trying to fit the man she knew into the monster standing before her.</p>
<p>Every memory she cherished began to dissolve like salt in rain. The months of quiet dinners; the hours of patient, tender conversation about the delicate architecture of spells; the evening her own heart had fluttered when she had reached out to hold his hand after he took a devastating blow meant for her son.</p>
<p>All of it, every moment of vulnerability, every thread of trust, was sliding out from under her at once, leaving her standing on nothing but empty air.</p>
<p>A few feet away, Leonidas forced his head up from the debris. His eyes went wide, pupils contracting as the scattered pieces of the last few weeks finally clicked into a single, horrifying mosaic.</p>
<p>The soldiers had frozen mid-step at the mere whisper of a name. The patrols that had parted like the Red Sea for the boy in the open shirt.</p>
<p>The impossible, unnatural calm of creatures that should have been snarling at his throat. His breath left him in one slow, shuddering rush, each exhale rattling in his chest like broken glass.</p>
<p>From behind the stone barrier, a small, broken sound escaped Lily: a whimper that sounded like something precious snapping in half.</p>
<p>Through their telepathic link, Rex felt her reaction arrive like a tsunami crashing over his skull. It was a heavy, tangled maelstrom of shock, raw terror, and paralyzing confusion so potent it nearly drowned out the sensory reality of the hall.</p>
<p>There were no words in the transmission, no coherent thoughts, just her. All of her, raw and reeling, screaming in the silence of her own mind.</p>
<p>Diana took half a step back, her shoulder bumping against the rough stone wall.</p>
<p>The expression on her face wasn’t exactly fear; it was something more unsettling, a kind of blank, stunned emptiness, as if the fundamental reality she believed in had been violently pulled out from under her.</p>
<p>Her fist clenched, white knuckles against her chest.</p>
<p>Yet, amidst the chaos of breaking spirits, two figures remained unnervingly still. Valentina did not flinch. Elizabeth did not flinch.</p>
<p>Neither of them betrayed a single muscle twitch, neither of them showed a flicker of surprise. Helena, who had spent her entire life reading the micro expressions and soul-deep tells of those closest to her, saw this with a clarity so sharp it felt like a blade pressing against her ribs.</p>
<p>The realization didn’t just hurt; it severed something inside her.</p>
<p>Her head turned with agonizing slowness toward the wall of stone where the others hid.</p>
<p>"Mother," she said. Her voice was no longer the voice of a mage or a noblewoman; it was a broken rasp, a sound scraped from the bottom of a well. "Elizabeth..."</p>
<p>"You knew...?"</p>
