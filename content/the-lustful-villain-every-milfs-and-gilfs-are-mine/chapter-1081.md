<p>The chamber seemed to hold its breath. The only sound was the wet, rhythmic choking of Kaeleth as his body desperately tried to repair the impossible violation of his own soul.</p>
<p>Rex’s arm was buried to the elbow within the divine architecture of the god’s essence, his fingers twitching as they navigated the labyrinth of celestial code.</p>
<p>It was not hard to find. Among everything that Kaeleth’s soul register contained—the millennia of accumulated power, the fragmented echoes of ancient prayers, the complex weaves of divine will—the Aethelgard anchor was the brightest component.</p>
<p>It shone with a cold, absolute light, a focal point of reality that anchored the domain to the timeline itself.</p>
<p>It was the specific piece that had been most actively engaged over the period of the contract with Celestina, organized around the specific configuration of temporal authority applied to a defined location.</p>
<p>It was the heartbeat of the domain, and it was lodged directly in the center of Kaeleth’s spiritual identity.</p>
<p>Rex’s fingers closed around it. The contact felt like grasping a live wire of pure causality.</p>
<p>"This... is yours?" Rex whispered, his voice distorted by the metaphysical feedback reverberating through his arm. "This pathetic little trinket that holds everything together?"</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s eyes went completely white. His mouth opened in a silent scream, his entire frame vibrating with such intensity that the stone spikes impaling him began to crack.</p>
<p>He made a sound that was not quite pain and not quite protest; it was the sound of a fundamental law of physics being broken. It was a metaphysical groan, the sound of a concept being torn away from its definition.</p>
<p>The extraction was not surgical. Rex did not gently slide the anchor out.</p>
<p>To remove something that had been woven into the very fabric of a god’s being for two thousand years required a violent reassertion of will.</p>
<p>It required the specific, deliberate force needed to forcibly remove something that had been integrated into its context.</p>
<p>"GHRRR—AAAAAAGH!" Kaeleth’s divine form buckled.</p>
<p>The golden ichor streaming from his wounds began to spiral upward, caught in the gravitational pull of the extraction.</p>
<p>The threads of temporal authority that formed the anchor began to unravel. The threads of temporal authority came out of Kaeleth’s divine form like the way the Soul Manipulation’s documentation described how to remove a designation from a mortal body: they didn’t break; they flowed.</p>
<p>They were golden amber filaments of raw, unadulterated time, streaming out of the cavity Rex had created in the god’s chest.</p>
<p>They moved with a liquid, hypnotic grace, carrying their temporal authority property into the space between Kaeleth’s hemorrhaging form and Rex’s clenched fist.</p>
<p>SHHHHHHHH WHIRRRRR.</p>
<p>The sound was like silk tearing combined with the hum of a thousand clocks ticking at once. As the threads left the god, the domain around them began to destabilize.</p>
<p>Walls flickered between being pristine marble and ancient dust; the air itself stuttered, repeating three-second loops of Kaeleth’s agonized gasps.</p>
<p>And then, the memories arrived.</p>
<p>They hit Rex like a tidal wave of pure information. They were not Kaeleth’s personal memories; he didn’t see the god’s childhood or his private fears but something far more valuable.</p>
<p>They were the specific operational memories of the authority’s application.</p>
<p>The floodgate opened. Rex saw Aethelgard’s temporal substrate as Kaeleth had managed it, the complex, invisible scaffolding that supported the flow of time in this realm.</p>
<p>He saw the foundational layer’s structure, the intricate lattice of cause and effect that Kaeleth had maintained with obsessive care.</p>
<p>He saw the specific dimensional addresses of every implementation point, the precise coordinates where temporal authority intersected with physical reality.</p>
<p>He understood the mechanics of the layer management, the exact mathematical and magical frequencies, and the authority required to prevent reality from collapsing into chaos.</p>
<p>It was a download of absolute power. Rex felt his own consciousness expand, his mind forcibly reconfiguring itself to accommodate the sheer volume of divine data.</p>
<p>The knowledge burned through his neural pathways like liquid fire, but his adaptation seized it, cataloging, compressing, and integrating the information with terrifying efficiency.</p>
<p>Rex pulled his hand back.</p>
<p>The golden threads snapped taut, and with one final, violent yank, THWIP CRACK, the anchor was fully severed from the god.</p>
<p>Kaeleth collapsed. The divine light in his eyes died instantly, leaving behind two hollow sockets of fading amber.</p>
<p>He fell forward against the stone spikes, his body no longer fighting to regenerate but simply existing in a state of broken, hollow shock.</p>
<p>Rex stood alone in the center of the dying domain, his right hand glowing with the stolen light of two thousand years. His eyes burned with a new, terrifying understanding.</p>
<p>Rex received all of it.</p>
<p>The golden amber filaments did not merely float toward Rex; they hunted him. They coiled through the air like starving serpents, drawn to the void he had created within himself.</p>
<p>As they struck his chest, they didn’t impact skin; they phased through his sternum as if his flesh were nothing more than smoke, diving straight into the sanctum of his Soul Register.</p>
<p>Inside Rex’s consciousness, the threads began to navigate. They searched for a place to root, weaving through the existing pathways of his power.</p>
<p>What they found was not empty space but a sophisticated, hungry architecture—the precise framework that the Mark of Lust and the System had been constructing in secret since Lustia first made contact with the domain weeks prior.</p>
<p>The Mark had been preparing a throne; the threads had been looking for a kingdom.</p>
<p>"Yes..." Rex growled internally, his teeth grinding together so hard his jaw muscles bulged like granite. "Take it all... Claim it!"</p>
<p>The threads found their grooves. They spiraled into the geometric patterns of the Mark, locking into place with the satisfying click of ancient machinery finding its missing gear.</p>
<p>The temporal authority of Aethelgard’s foundational layer settled into his soul register with the terrifying inevitability of a predator claiming its territory.</p>
<p>It felt less like an acquisition and more like a homecoming, as if these two millennia of divine control had been waiting specifically for a vessel capable of containing their raw, unbridled hunger.</p>
<p>[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION]</p>
<p>[Temporal Authority Aethelgard’s Foundational Layer: ABSORBED.]</p>
<p>[Operational Control: Acquired.]</p>
<p>[Temporal Substrate Management: Active.]</p>
<p>[Soul Register Expansion: Temporal Domain (Local) Integrated.]</p>
<p>A shockwave of pure causality erupted from Rex’s body.</p>
<p>BOOM.</p>
<p>The air around him shrieked as time itself stuttered, the sound waves stretching and compressing in nauseating waves. Dust motes froze midair, then accelerated to impossible speeds, then reversed.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes burned with an amber fire that matched the stolen divinity.</p>
<p>He slowly withdrew his hand from the ruined cavity of Kaeleth’s chest. The wet sound of separation, SHLUCK, echoed off the crumbling walls.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was still.</p>
<p>The god hung from the stone spikes, a broken marionette whose strings had been severed. His divine form remained, but the light had gone flat.</p>
<p>The magnificent, towering presence that had defined the room moments ago had collapsed into something hollow and pathetic. He was not dead; death was too merciful for what Rex had done, but he was diminished.</p>
<p>The two thousand-year framework of his being remained, the general authority over time still flickered weakly in his veins, but the Aethelgard-specific implementation, the very engine of his power, was gone.</p>
<p>He was a king who had been stripped of his crown, his sword, and his throne, left only with the memory of what it felt like to rule.</p>
<p>Blood-thick, luminous divine ichor dripped steadily from Kaeleth’s shattered jaw onto the floor.</p>
<p>Drip. Drip. Drip.</p>
<p>Each drop sounded like a funeral bell in the sudden silence of the chamber.</p>
<p>Kaeleth lifted his head with agonizing slowness. His eyes, once bright with celestial certainty, were now dim, clouded with the shock of ontological theft.</p>
<p>He looked up at Rex, not with anger, but with a hollow, condescending pity that cut through the carnage.</p>
<p>"You think... you have won?" Kaeleth rasped.</p>
<p>His voice was a broken wreck of its former self, each word dragging through a throat filled with blood and divine debris. "You think you can simply... wear my skin?"</p>
<p>A wet cough wracked his body, spraying crimson across his own pale chest.</p>
<p>"You will not know how to use it," Kaeleth whispered, a cruel smile twitching at the corner of his bleeding mouth. "The temporal substrate management... it is not mere power, mortal..."</p>
<p>"It is a language. It requires the operational knowledge that I have spent two hundred years alone, perfecting for Aethelgard’s specific foundational structure."</p>
<p>"You have stolen the key, but you will never understand the lock."</p>
<p>Rex looked down at him.</p>
