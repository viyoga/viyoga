<p>The silence that followed Rex’s declaration was not merely a pause in conversation; it was a heavy, suffocating pressure, the kind of stillness that precedes a tectonic shift. The air in the room felt thick, charged with the terrifying realization that they weren’t just fighting a political faction or a rogue army; they were fighting a cosmic immune response.</p>
<p>"If her designation is genuinely divine class," the divination representative said, her voice trembling slightly as she tried to wrap her mind around the sheer scale of the revelation, "then we aren’t talking about a woman with a grand delusion."</p>
<p>"We are talking about a fundamental truth seen through a specific, terrifying lens."</p>
<p>"Her perception isn’t a lie; it is a reading of real, objective data through a divine framework."</p>
<p>"The framework might be biased, and the god’s priorities might be alien to our own, but she isn’t misreading the world."</p>
<p>"She is seeing a truth we are blind to."</p>
<p>"No," Ignivara said, her voice a grim confirmation. "She is not."</p>
<p>"Which means," the representative continued, her eyes widening as the logic crystallized into a nightmare, "that there is a sentient, divine force in this universe that views our very existence as a threat to the world’s stability."</p>
<p>"And that god has been using a human proxy to systematically prune us from existence for thirty years."</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara whispered.</p>
<p>The room absorbed this in the way a wounded animal absorbs the scent of a predator. The scale of the problem had just expanded from a local conflict to an existential war against the heavens themselves.</p>
<p>The weight of it was palpable, a crushing gravity that seemed to pull at the very souls of everyone present.</p>
<p>Apollo stared down at the polished surface of the table, his eyes tracing the grain of the wood as if searching for a way out of the trap they had just realized they were in. His mind was racing, a frantic internal monologue of tactical reassessments and existential dread.</p>
<p>’We aren’t just fighting a war,’ he thought, the realization biting like frost. ’We are fighting the architect of the house we live in.’</p>
<p>"The god’s name," Apollo said finally, his voice sounding hollow in the vast silence.</p>
<p>"I don’t know it," Ignivara admitted, shaking her head. "Celestina never spoke its name in my presence."</p>
<p>"To her, the name was irrelevant. She referred to it only as "The Balance," treating it not as a person to be worshipped but as a mathematical constant to be obeyed.</p>
<p>"It was a designation, a function, a law of nature."</p>
<p>"Can the designation be researched?" Rex asked, his gaze snapping toward Elizabeth.</p>
<p>His voice was devoid of panic, but there was a new, lethal edge to it—the sound of a man preparing to hunt a god.</p>
<p>"The Academy’s theological archives are extensive," Elizabeth replied, her fingers tapping a restless rhythm against the table. "They cover divine designations with documented human holders going back four hundred years."</p>
<p>"If the ’Balance Keeper’ has held this mantle before, there will be a paper trail."</p>
<p>"There will be a record of the previous holders, their methods, and their ends."</p>
<p>"It has appeared before," Ignivara interjected, her voice cutting through Elizabeth’s hope like a cold wind. "Celestina told us during the founding session that the designation has passed to three holders over the last two centuries."</p>
<p>"Each one was a visionary. Each one built a version of the Legion. And each one failed."</p>
<p>She paused, the grim history of the mantle hanging in the air. "None of them lasted more than thirty years."</p>
<p>"Whether it was through the death of the holder or the violent collapse of their organization, the cycle always breaks."</p>
<p>"And the god simply selects a new vessel," Apollo said, the horror of the pattern settling into his bones. "The god keeps producing new holders to finish the job."</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara said.</p>
<p>Apollo looked up, his eyes meeting Rex’s. The tactical reality was as brutal as a blade to the throat.</p>
<p>"Which means killing Celestina is a temporary fix."</p>
<p>"It’s a bandage on a gaping wound."</p>
<p>"Eliminating her doesn’t eliminate the threat... it only delays the inevitable."</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara said, her voice final, echoing the grim certainty of a death knell.</p>
<p>The silence that settled over the chamber was not the peaceful quiet of a resolved debate; it was the heavy, suffocating stillness of a room that had just been handed a burden far greater than the one it had arrived to solve. The air felt pressurized, as if the sheer scale of the cosmic threat they had just uncovered was physically compressing the space between them.</p>
<p>They had come to discuss a war; they were leaving to face a divine purge.</p>
<p>"The Seam House," Rex’s voice broke the tension, sharp and utilitarian, cutting through the existential dread like a blade through silk.</p>
<p>He didn’t have the luxury of staring into the abyss; he had a mission to execute. "Location. Current staffing. Provide us the specifics."</p>
<p>Ignivara didn’t hesitate. She laid it out with the precision of a veteran strategist, detailing the Seam House’s coordinates, its defensive perimeters, and the roster of those currently guarding its threshold.</p>
<p>The meeting stretched on for two grueling hours. Ignivara spoke with a methodical, almost haunting clarity, delivering her intelligence in a sequence designed to build a complete, terrifying picture of the Legion’s reach.</p>
<p>She was a woman who had organized her chaos before stepping into the lion’s den, and she was now systematically dismantling it for them. She answered every probing question with surgical accuracy, her voice never wavering even as the weight of her revelations grew heavier.</p>
<p>She corrected two critical misunderstandings in their existing intelligence errors that had been bleeding into their tactical planning for months, potentially costing lives in the field. Most importantly, she identified three Legion operatives currently active in the territories adjacent to the Apostle network—names and faces that had been missing from the canyon intelligence, ghosts that had been operating right under their noses.</p>
<p>When the final piece of the puzzle was laid bare, Ignivara let the silence reclaim the room for a heartbeat. She sat back, her expression unreadable but her eyes weary.</p>
<p>"I have given you everything," she said, her voice steady despite the exhaustion. "I am ready to be taken to wherever you are keeping me."</p>
<p>"I will not attempt to leave. My loyalty to the Legion is a matter of history; my loyalty to the truth is a matter of survival."</p>
<p>Valentina studied her for a long, calculating moment. She wasn’t just looking at a defector; she was looking at a high-value, high-risk asset that could either stabilize their cause or shatter it from within.</p>
<p>"You will stay in the Academy’s supervised residential section," Valentina finally declared, her tone authoritative and devoid of sentiment. "You will live under the same strict protocols as the research assistants who handle restricted materials."</p>
<p>"High security, high oversight, but with the resources you need to continue your work."</p>
<p>It was a masterful stroke of administrative judgment. It wasn’t a comfortable arrangement; it was essentially a gilded cage, but it wasn’t a punitive one either.</p>
<p>It was the precise middle ground Valentina’s mind always sought: a way to honor the value of the information provided while mitigating the danger of the provider.</p>
<p>"Thank you," Ignivara said, a small nod of respect passing between them.</p>
<p>She began to rise, her movements stiff, but then she paused. She didn’t look at any one person; instead, she addressed the room as a whole, her gaze sweeping over the leaders of the group as if imparting a final, vital warning.</p>
<p>"One more thing," she said, her voice dropping an octave, gaining a sudden, sharp intensity. "The three Legion operatives I identified in the adjacent territories... there is a specific detail you must not overlook."</p>
<p>"The one embedded in the Thornweald Collective? He is the tier two operative I was mentoring when the tides turned."</p>
<p>"His name is Daven Arless... He has been in the Legion for six years, and he is currently occupying the exact same position of influence that I held two years ago."</p>
<p>She let sink in the terrifying reality that the Legion wasn’t just a machine but a cycle, and that the next gear was already in motion.</p>
