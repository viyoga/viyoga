<p>The great hall of the Starlight household was no longer a hall; it was a crater, a hollowed-out wound in the earth where civilization had once stood.</p>
<p>Dust still drifted down from the ragged, jagged hole torn through the ceiling, slow, ghostly flakes of grey that danced in the shafts of light before settling on shattered marble pillars and the splintered, bleeding remains of the grand staircase.</p>
<p>Daylight poured in through the gaping void where the second and third floors had once been, cutting through the haze like divine spears.</p>
<p>The light fell across a floor so thoroughly pulverized that the remaining marble looked like fractured ice on a pond in early spring, glistening with blood and rainwater.</p>
<p>Somewhere in the twisted wreckage, a loose stone dislodged and rolled, clicking against debris before coming to a dead stop. Nobody in the room moved to see where it went. Nobody dared to breathe.</p>
<p>Rex stood at the absolute center of the devastation, and he let himself be looked at. He stood like a monument to impossible survival.</p>
<p>Before the eyes of every horrified witness, the last of his catastrophic wounds performed their grotesque dance of resurrection. A long, angry burn that had blackened his ribs faded from charcoal to angry crimson, then to ordinary, sun-kissed skin in a matter of seconds.</p>
<p>The torn flesh over his shoulder knit itself together with a faint, nauseatingly wet slurp squelch as muscle fibers reconnected like living thread.</p>
<p>A thin thread of frost that had been embedded deep inside his thigh melted away, running down his leg in a pathetic, steaming trickle.</p>
<p>He rolled his neck once, a casual motion like a man waking from a long, peaceful nap, and a final vertebrae settled into place with a sharp, audible click.</p>
<p>Then he was whole. Completely, terrifyingly whole.</p>
<p>He stood barefoot in the ruins, the charred remnants of his shirt hanging from his belt like a flag of surrender that nobody in the room had asked for and nobody wanted.</p>
<p>Across from him, Helena was a portrait of exhaustion and terror. Her chest heaved in ragged, desperate spasms, each breath visible in the cooling air.</p>
<p>Her hair had come loose from its tie, spilling across her shoulders in damp, matted strands that clung to her sweat-slicked face.</p>
<p>The inferno that had roared in her hands moments ago was gone, reduced to a few sullen, dying sparks that flickered weakly at her fingertips before vanishing into ash.</p>
<p>She had poured her very essence into that last spell, and the hollow, soul-deep ache of magical depletion showed in the way she trembled. She stood off balance, one foot shoved forward as if she expected the very foundation of the world to tilt and swallow her whole.</p>
<p>’I gave everything,’ her mind screamed in a silent, panicked loop. ’I gave everything and it did nothing... Nothing!’</p>
<p>Behind her, the scene was one of broken nobility. Leonidas knelt on the fractured marble, his palms pressed flat against the stone as if trying to anchor himself to reality.</p>
<p>The veins in his neck stood out like corded rope, straining with the effort of simply existing. The crushing gravitational weight Rex had placed upon him had been eased by only a fraction enough to allow him to draw air into his burning lungs, but nowhere near enough to allow him to rise.</p>
<p>He could only watch, helpless and paralyzed, as the predator prepared to claim its prize.</p>
<p>Behind the thin, improvised barrier of grey stone that Rex had conjured to shield them, four faces peered through a narrow, desperate gap.</p>
<p>The air behind the wall was thick with the smell of fear and sweat. Lily had both hands pressed so hard over her mouth that her fingers left crescent indents in her cheeks, her eyes wide and glistening with unshed tears.</p>
<p>Diana stood rigid beside her, her knuckles white as she clutched her bow against her chest as if the weapon could somehow protect them from the apocalypse unfolding mere yards away.</p>
<p>Elizabeth held herself with a preternatural stillness, her breathing shallow and controlled, while Valentina, who had already collapsed once that morning, now stood with her silver hair hanging loose and tangled around her shoulders.</p>
<p>Her eyes were fixed on the center of the hall, unblinking, as if looking away would make the nightmare dissolve.</p>
<p>None of them dared to breathe loudly. Every gasp felt like it might draw the attention of the monster.</p>
<p>Around the edges of the devastated room, the Underlayer soldiers remained as silent as graves. They stood exactly where they had been commanded, spears grounded, armor quiet.</p>
<p>They were a ring of dark, motionless figures, a circle of predators waiting for the signal to either feast or flee.</p>
<p>It was Rex who broke the silence.</p>
<p>It started as something small, a low, breathy sound of amusement that seemed to vibrate unnaturally through the debris. It was a sound that did not belong in a room filled with corpses and ruins.</p>
<p>Then, it grew.</p>
<p>It transformed into something almost fond, almost nostalgic, as if he were reminiscing about a cherished memory rather than standing in the wreckage of a family home. He shook his head slowly, a gesture of mock disappointment, as if at a joke that had taken far too long to land.</p>
<p>Then, he lifted one hand. His fingers traced the upper half of his face in a slow, deliberate caress.</p>
<p>Violet black energy began to hemorrhage from his skin.</p>
<p>It didn’t spark or crackle; it emerged from him the way thick smoke curls from a banked fire—unhurried, heavy, and suffocating.</p>
<p>The darkness spiraled up around his wrist, sliding over his knuckles like living oil. It pooled in the hollow of his palm before flowing outward across his brow and cheekbone in a smooth, spreading sheet of shadow.</p>
<p>Where the energy touched his flesh, it didn’t just cover him; it hardened. A pale, sickly line of violet light traced the leading edge of the transformation, marking the boundary between man and something else entirely.</p>
<p>When he finally lowered his hand, the transformation was complete. Half of his face was now encased in a sleek, terrifying mask of dark metal veined with pulsing violet light.</p>
<p>It was the same infernal visage that every demon, every bloodthirsty orc, and every mindless slime in the Underlayer had learned to kneel before. It was not a costume; it was a declaration of sovereignty.</p>
<p>The change in the room was immediate and physical.</p>
<p>The temperature seemed to plummet, sending visible puffs of breath into the air. The very pressure in the hall shifted, the atmospheric weight increasing until it felt as though the oxygen had been replaced by something heavier, something more toxic.</p>
<p>Helena felt the hair on her arms stand up as a primal instinct screamed at her to run, not because she was afraid of being killed, but because she was afraid of being claimed.</p>
