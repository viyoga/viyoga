<p>A flicker of realization, sharp and cold, passed through Elizabeth’s eyes. The pieces of the puzzle were clicking into place, forming a picture of immense power and immense risk.</p>
<p>"The Earthen authority you demonstrated yesterday..." she whispered, her voice a breath of awe and apprehension, "...the way the ground itself seemed to obey your very heartbeat..."</p>
<p>"It was partial," Rex interrupted, his voice cutting through her wonder like a blade.</p>
<p>He didn’t allow her to romanticize the power; he demanded she understand its limitations. "What a partial integration produces is a shadow of the full designation’s true capacity."</p>
<p>’It is a fraction of the godhead. That is why Tremor was able to wield it with such devastating, tectonic scale... and why I was only able to manifest the level you witnessed at the end."</p>
<p>He let the silence hang between them, heavy and thick with the implication of what he was truly becoming and the terrifying scale of the power he was still struggling to cage.</p>
<p>Elizabeth stood in the center of the room, the silence between them thickening like cooling lava. She was processing the sheer magnitude of his deception, not a deception of malice but a deception of necessity.</p>
<p>"You have been carrying this since ten days ago," she said, her voice low, vibrating with a mixture of disbelief and a sudden, sharp tension. "You have been walking among us, sitting at our tables, breathing our air... and you said nothing."</p>
<p>"I did not know the full scope of what I had acquired," Rex replied, his voice devoid of apology.</p>
<p>He spoke with the cold pragmatism of a man who viewed secrecy as a tactical resource. "The archives described the designation, yes, but they were vague on the full capability set."</p>
<p>"I spent the last ten days assessing the limits of my own biology."</p>
<p>"Yesterday’s engagement was simply the moment where controlled assessment became a luxury we could no longer afford."</p>
<p>"The situation forced the hand of integration."</p>
<p>"The assessment," she pressed, her eyes searching his, her intellect working at a fever pitch. "What did it tell you? What did you learn in that silence?"</p>
<p>"That the partial integration at the fourteen-day threshold produces two distinct phenomena," Rex said, his gaze hardening. "First, geological awareness at the scale of the island’s substrate."</p>
<p>"Second, directed force application at a construct building scale... But," he paused, a shadow of the physical strain crossing his face, "it does not produce full seismic authority."</p>
<p>"The earthquake protocol we witnessed yesterday was the absolute ceiling of what the partial integration could sustain."</p>
<p>"It was a violent, screaming limit... During the deployment, the feedback was so intense... I could feel the very ceiling of the world pressing against my consciousness."</p>
<p>Elizabeth went silent, her breath hitching.</p>
<p>"You could feel it," she whispered, the horror of the sensation dawning on her.</p>
<p>To feel the earth not as a platform but as a living, straining limb of one’s own body.</p>
<p>"The geological awareness is not an abstract sense, Elizabeth..."</p>
<p>"It is not a HUD or a mental map," Rex explained, his tone becoming clinical to mask the visceral memory. "It is direct, unmediated contact with the substrate’s state."</p>
<p>"When the output approaches the ceiling of what the integration can sustain, the awareness registers the strain the same way a muscle registers a tear."</p>
<p>"You don’t just ’know’ the limit; you feel the agony of the earth reaching its breaking point."</p>
<p>"You feel the tension in the stone as if it were the tension in your own tendons."</p>
<p>She looked at him then, her expression a complex, swirling vortex of emotion. She was the researcher, fascinated by the terrifying architecture of this new power; she was the analyst, calculating the massive, unspoken variables he was still withholding; and she was the woman, realizing the sheer isolation of the man standing before her.</p>
<p>"The gauntlets," she said, her voice cutting through her reverie. "Where are they currently?"</p>
<p>"Integrated," Rex said simply.</p>
<p>"Integrated?"</p>
<p>"The Earthen Authority’s physical expression at this stage of partial integration is no longer a matter of external tools," Rex said, his eyes flashing with a strange, mineral light. "The divine mineral material from Sable Island has woven itself into the integration architecture."</p>
<p>"The gauntlets are no longer separate objects to be donned or removed."</p>
<p>"They have been subsumed."</p>
<p>"They are in you," she breathed, the realization hitting her like a physical blow.</p>
<p>"They are part of the integration architecture," Rex corrected, though he allowed her the simplification. "Which is intrinsically linked to my designation framework."</p>
<p>"Which, in turn, operates through my biological systems..."</p>
<p>"So, in a practical, visceral sense... yes. They are part of my very marrow."</p>
<p>Elizabeth leaned back slightly, the weight of the revelation settling in her chest. She was no longer just looking at a man; she was looking at a living monument of divine minerals.</p>
<p>"And the elemental outputs," she said, her mind racing to connect the dots. "The fire... the way you manipulated the air with Ignivara."</p>
<p>"That wasn’t part of the Earthen Authority."</p>
<p>"Elemental Mastery is one of my system skills," Rex said, his voice steady as he laid out the final pieces of the puzzle. "So it’s not a rare thing because all reincarnators have this kind of shit, right?"</p>
<p>"Anyway... I have been refining it in the Academy’s practice facilities in secret."</p>
<p>"The synergy between Elemental Mastery and the Earthen Authority’s geological baseline is what produced the atmospheric combustion you saw."</p>
<p>"Your system... we haven’t talked about that much," she said, her voice rising slightly in incredulity.</p>
<p>"My system skills have a cost," Rex said, his gaze unwavering. "I had accumulated enough energy through the standard progression of my own power system that was called the Desire Authority to purchase both Elemental Mastery and Weapons Mastery within the same window of time."</p>
<p>A heavy silence descended upon the room. Elizabeth was no longer just listening; she was building.</p>
<p>You could almost see the mental gears grinding as she assembled the data, her eyes narrowing as she reached the inevitable, staggering conclusion.</p>
<p>"I see..." she said, her voice trembling with the weight of her deduction. "You had already accumulated sufficient energy from your system progression to purchase two major system skills... now I can see why you’re so strong..."</p>
<p>"A system that isn’t even matched in this world..."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And this... energy generation rate of the Desire Authority..." she began, her voice becoming carefully, dangerously precise, "is directly proportional to the depth and the sheer number of the relationships the holder has developed."</p>
<p>"The more profound the connection, the more massive the energy yield."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, his expression a mask of calm.</p>
<p>"I see it now," she whispered, her eyes locking onto his with a piercing, analytical ferocity. "To generate enough energy for two major, high-tier system skills in only...?"</p>
<p>"Hours or maybe days... depends..." he replied.</p>
<p>"The rate varied," Rex continued, his voice dropping into a low, enigmatic tone that offered no easy comfort. "Depending on the period."</p>
<p>Elizabeth stared at him, her eyes narrowing into slits of pure, unadulterated intellect. It was the specific expression of a woman who had reached the jagged edge of an answer, a boundary where the logic she possessed met the truth he was guarding and had decided, with a quiet, terrifying resolve, that she was not going to walk past it today.</p>
<p>She was marking the spot. She was letting him know that while he might escape the scrutiny for now, the boundary had been found.</p>
<p>The silence between them was a taut wire, vibrating with the unspoken weight of everything he had just admitted.</p>
<p>"Morwenna is going to ask about the Underlayer," Rex said, breaking the tension.</p>
<p>He didn’t wait for her to respond; he stepped forward, closing the distance between them. He moved with a predatory grace, knowing that standing still at the edge of her suspicion was less efficient than meeting her head on.</p>
<p>"She is going to ask," Elizabeth conceded, her voice flat and devoid of drama, "because you spoke about it with a level of specificity that is impossible to achieve through mere observation. You didn’t just describe the Underlayer, Rex. You spoke as if you had mapped its very veins."</p>
<p>"Because Valentina gave me access to six months of Academy resources," Rex countered, his voice hard and pragmatic, providing the structural support for his lie. "And the Academy’s restricted archives contain approximately forty years of compiled intelligence on the Underlayer’s structural evolution."</p>
<p>"I haven’t just been observing; I have been studying."</p>
<p>Elizabeth didn’t flinch. She accepted the movement of his advance with the practiced ease of a strategist, noting the new terrain he was presenting.</p>
