<p>The entity imploded, a momentary black hole that sucked in the surrounding air and stone before detonating outward in a shower of crushed matter.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even look at the explosion. He was already scanning for the next threat, his eyes flickering with a cold, predatory intensity.</p>

<p>The energy surging through him was too immense to be contained; he was a living conductor for the gacha’s raw power.</p>
<p>"I’m not a bum anymore, Rex!" Mordecai shouted back, his voice cracking with the sheer weight of the emotion he had bottled up for six long months. "I’m not the guy who signs the paperwork while you do the heavy lifting!"</p>
<p>"I’m the one who brought this here! I’m the one who opened the sky!"</p>

<p>"I can clearly see that you’re FUCKING shaking," Rex pointed out, his voice dripping with a cruel, challenging amusement. "And what makes the fun slowly disappear is that... you’re still terrified."</p>

<p>"What’s the matter? Too much reality for you?"</p>

<p>"I’ve been holding it in for so long... I don’t know where I end and this power begins."</p>
<p>Rex stopped, his cocky expression softening into something more complex, something that resembled a dangerous kind of empathy. He looked at the man across from him, the man he had mocked, the man he had dismissed.</p>
<p>"Then don’t let go," Rex said, his voice sudden and serious, the arrogance momentarily replaced by a grim, guiding authority. "Don’t you dare drop the leash!’</p>
<p>"You’ve jumped into the deep end, Mordecai!"</p>

<p>...</p>
<p>The silence that followed the collapse of the temporal bubble was unnatural. It was a heavy, ringing stillness that felt like the world was holding its breath, waiting for the final blow.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the center of the ruined courtyard, his chest heaving, his skin slick with sweat and the shimmering residue of raw mana. His clothes were shredded, his elemental constructs were flickering like dying candles, and his energy reserves were finally, for the first time in his life, feeling the genuine, gnawing ache of depletion.</p>
<p>He had been fighting for two hours straight, a relentless, high-octane dance of destruction that would have killed a lesser man ten times over.</p>
<p>He turned his head, his eyes landing on Mordecai. The other man was a wreck.</p>
<p>He was slumped against a fallen pillar, his eyes glazed, his hands trembling so violently he had to clench them into fists to keep them still. The violet glow in his eyes was dimming, flickering like a dying star.</p>
<p>"You look like hell, Mordecai," Rex said, though there was no mockery in his voice this time and it was a raspy, breathless observation.</p>
<p>He wiped a streak of blood from his jaw, a cocky, lopsided grin still fighting to surface through the exhaustion. "But hey, you did it."</p>
<p>"You broke the world..."</p>
<p>"You’re officially the most dangerous man in the Underlayer." Rex raised his arms. "How does it feel to finally be the center of the universe?"</p>
<p>Mordecai looked up, a hollow, haunting laugh escaping his lips. "It feels... heavy..."</p>
<p>"It feels like the weight of every choice I never made is finally pressing down on me."</p>
<p>"Is this what it means to be ’more’? To just be a bigger target for the gods?"</p>
<p>Rex stepped toward him, his movements heavy but still possessing that predatory grace. "It’s not about being a target."</p>
<p>"It’s about being the one who decides who survives the hit..."</p>
<p>"You stopped being a spectator, Mordecai."</p>
<p>"You stopped waiting for permission to exist."</p>
<p>"That’s the only difference between a man and a god."</p>
<p>"But there’s so much more coming," Mordecai whispered, his gaze drifting back toward the sky, toward the massive, weeping wound in reality that had been festering for two hours. "The pressure... it hasn’t stopped... It’s actually increasing."</p>
<p>Rex followed his gaze, and the grin on his face finally, slowly, died.</p>
<p>The largest rift, the one that had been pulsing like a wounded heart since the moment the limiter snapped, wasn’t just expanding. It was unfolding. It was a tear in the very concept of "where" and "when."</p>
<p>The dimensional pressure was so intense that the air around the rift began to crystallize, turning into shards of frozen time that drifted aimlessly through the courtyard.</p>
<p>Then, the SSS class emerged.</p>
<p>It didn’t fall, and it didn’t descend. It simply was.</p>

<p>It was an absence so profound that it felt like a physical weight on the soul. Looking at it was like trying to remember a dream that was being erased as you woke up.</p>
<p>It was the silence before the first word was spoken; the void before the first light was struck.</p>
<p>Rex’s foresight, his most reliable, most arrogant tool, went dead. It didn’t give him a warning; it didn’t give him a trajectory.</p>
<p>It simply returned a terrifying, absolute null. It was as if the entity were a blind spot in the eye of God.</p>
<p>Rex felt a coldness he had never known, not the cold of ice but the cold of nonexistence. His telekinesis reached out instinctively, but it felt like grasping at a shadow in a room with no light.</p>
<p>His elemental mastery, his absolute authority over the world’s forces, felt suddenly, pathetically small.</p>
<p>He stood there, the most confident man in the Underlayer, the master of all he could perceive, staring into the face of something that didn’t care to be seen.</p>
<p>"Lustful Villain..." Mordecai’s voice was a mere breath, filled with a sudden, existential dread. "What... what is that?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t answer immediately. He tightened his grip on the air, his knuckles white, his eyes wide and unblinking.</p>
<p>He forced the cocky smirk back onto his face, a desperate, beautiful act of defiance against the encroaching nothingness.</p>
<p>"I don’t know," Rex whispered, his voice low and dangerous, vibrating with a mixture of terror and a dark, manic excitement. "But it’s the first thing in this entire goddamn universe that actually looks like it might be able to kill me."</p>
