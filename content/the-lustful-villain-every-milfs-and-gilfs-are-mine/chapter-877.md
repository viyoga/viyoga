<p>Lustia didn’t flinch at the memory of his grip on her throat.</p>
<p>"I did nearly fail," she said simply. "Softening that truth would have been a lie..."</p>
<p>"And if I lied to you, you would have discovered it eventually; the truth always finds its way out, and then you would have had a reason to doubt every word I ever spoke."</p>
<p>"I would rather you be angry at the truth than comfortable with a lie."</p>
<p>"Most people would have chosen comfort," Rex said, stepping closer to her.</p>
<p>"I am not most people," Lustia replied, her chin lifting. "And as you have pointed out to me more times than I care to count, usually as a complaint, I am also not most gods."</p>
<p>"It wasn’t always a complaint," Rex murmured, the corner of his mouth twitching. "Tonight, it isn’t one at all."</p>
<p>Lustia watched him, waiting for the blade to drop.</p>
<p>"I mean that," Rex said, his voice gaining a rare sincerity. "I’ve spent a great deal of time treating you as a resource."</p>
<p>"Something to be managed, something useful, something occasionally frustrating but ultimately something to be handled correctly rather than something to actually listen to."</p>
<p>"But tonight..." he paused, searching for the words. "Tonight you sat on the floor of your own domain and handed me the truth, knowing exactly what my hand around your throat was going to feel like before you even opened your mouth."</p>
<p>"That isn’t what a resource does..."</p>
<p>"That is what someone does when they have decided the relationship matters more than their own comfort."</p>
<p>Lustia fell silent. For once, the goddess had nothing to say.</p>
<p>The power dynamic between them had shifted irrevocably; it was no longer master and tool, or goddess and apostle. It was something far more complicated.</p>
<p>Rex took one last look at her, his eyes reflecting a respect that was more terrifying than his wrath.</p>
<p>"I prefer this version of you," he said. "The serious one..."</p>
<p>"Not the warm, teasing thing you perform when you think I need to be managed gently."</p>
<p>"This... Someone who tells me the truth even when the truth makes her look like she nearly lost everything."</p>
<p>The silence that followed Rex’s declaration was heavy, charged with a new kind of understanding. But Rex, ever the predator who knew exactly when to shift tactics, decided to shatter the moment’s gravity with surgical precision.</p>
<p>He took two steps closer, invading her personal space until he could smell the faint, divine scent of her skin. His expression remained deadly serious, his eyes boring into hers.</p>
<p>"And another thing," Rex added, his voice dropping to a husky, dangerous growl. "I prefer this version because she doesn’t immediately start grinding against my thigh every time I finish explaining a tactical problem."</p>
<p>Lustia froze. The regal, composed goddess who had just stood firm against his wrath suddenly appeared as if struck by lightning.</p>
<p>"I... that is a gross mischaracterization of divine enthusiasm!" she stammered, her cheeks flushing a shade of crimson that rivaled the sunset. "I am a goddess of... of... of... profound cosmic energies!"</p>
<p>"What you perceive as... as inappropriate physical proximity is merely... residual mana discharge!"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. "Residual mana discharge... Right."</p>
<p>"Yes!" Lustia said, her hands fluttering uselessly in front of her as she tried to regain her dignity. "It is a very scientific phenomenon! "</p>
<p>"Very common in higher dimensions!"</p>
<p>"You wouldn’t understand; it’s... it’s... it’s statistically improbable for a mortal to experience such—" She tripped over her own words, her divine composure crumbling into a heap of nervous babbling. "The alignment of the stars suggests that physical contact is a fundamental necessity for maintaining dimensional stability during periods of high stress!"</p>
<p>"Is that why you were eyeing my belt buckle during the briefing on Celestina’s movements?" Rex asked flatly.</p>
<p>Lustia let out a sound that was halfway between a squeak and a cough. "I was assessing the... the structural integrity of your equipment!"</p>
<p>"For safety purposes! Purely for your protection, Rex!"</p>
<p>Rex simply stared at her for three more seconds of excruciating awkwardness before turning away. He didn’t offer a rebuttal, didn’t laugh, and didn’t acknowledge her increasingly desperate attempts to explain away her obvious arousal.</p>
<p>He simply dismissed her as one would dismiss a malfunctioning piece of equipment.</p>
<p>As he walked toward the edge of the domain’s platform, his mind retreated from her flustered stammering. The comedy of her reaction faded instantly, replaced by the cold, razor-sharp focus that defined his existence.</p>
<p>He left the flustered goddess behind, her nonsensical explanations echoing uselessly in the air.</p>
<p>’Kaeleth,’ he thought, his inner voice as cold as the void between stars. ’You think you can contain a storm by hiring a hunter.’</p>
<p>’You think you can cage time by contracting a killer... But you’ve forgotten what happens when you try to trap something that doesn’t belong to your timeline.’</p>
<p>His mind began to dissect the mechanics Lustia had described. He pictured the paradox load the way reality itself would recoil when forced to swallow its own contradiction.</p>
<p>He could almost feel the phantom pressure of it.</p>
<p>’If the domain punishes the user for breaking time,’ Rex mused, his mental fingers tracing the invisible ley lines of the threat ahead, ’then I don’t need to kill Kaeleth...’</p>
<p>’I just need to make him complicit in his own unraveling...’</p>
<p>’I will make his own authority the rope that strangles him.’</p>
<p>Rex looked at Lustia. "That’s enough of all the yap... you really are worth my respect after helping me with that information... make sure to stay like this if I want it serious."</p>
<p>Something shifted across Lustia’s features, a tremor in the divine porcelain of her composure that Rex had never witnessed before. In all their previous encounters, their conversations had danced between the cold calculus of strategic counsel and the intimate, almost playful teasing she employed when invoking his title as her ’dear apostle.’</p>
<p>But this was not that. This was not the practiced warmth of a goddess maintaining the equilibrium of her chosen vessel.</p>
<p>It was something raw. Something dangerously unguarded.</p>
<p>It was the look of a being who had been caught completely undefended, seen with terrifying accuracy by the one mortal whose perception had begun to matter more to her than the celestial hierarchies she commanded.</p>
<p>"Rex," she whispered.</p>
<p>His name didn’t carry its usual melodic cadence or the teasing lilt that usually rode alongside it. It came out fragile, stripped of its divinity, sounding almost human.</p>
<p>"You don’t have to say anything," Rex said, his voice cutting through the sudden, suffocating intimacy of the moment. "I’m not asking for a confession or a response."</p>
<p>"I’m stating a fact... Because you gave me the truth when it would have been easier to lie, and I believe that level of honesty deserves to be acknowledged."</p>
<p>Lustia turned her head away, unable to sustain his piercing gaze. She looked toward the amber glow of her domain’s light, the divine energy that usually felt like a comforting embrace but now seemed to expose every crack in her armor.</p>
<p>When her eyes finally returned to his, they betrayed her. They held that specific, shimmering brightness, the terrifying luminescence that precedes tears in a being who has spent millennia learning how to suppress every mortal weakness.</p>
<p>"Two thousand years..." she said, her voice cracking like thin ice under pressure. "Two thousand years of existing as something beyond understanding, and no one has ever said that to me."</p>
<p>"Not like that... Not with the weight you put behind it."</p>
<p>"Then it was overdue," Rex replied flatly. His tone wasn’t cruel, but it lacked the softness she clearly craved; he was a man who dealt in certainties, not comforts.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was absolute, a vacuum that seemed to pull the very air from the room. Lustia stood there, a goddess reduced to something achingly vulnerable, while Rex stood before her not as a supplicant but as an equal who had just dismantled her defenses without drawing a blade.</p>
<p>But even as the emotional tension reached its breaking point, Rex’s mind remained a fortress of cold calculation. Behind the rare moment of acknowledgment, the gears of his intellect were already grinding against the implications of what she had revealed.</p>
<p>’Alright... back to that fucking god I need to talk about...’</p>
<p>’Kaeleth,’ he thought, the name tasting like copper and old blood in his mind. ’You think you can play god with the fabric of time itself.’</p>
<p>’You think you can hide behind contracts and ’balance’ while you hunt down the anomalies you can’t control.’</p>
<p>His fingers flexed instinctively, as if already feeling the resistance of a temporal barrier. He could almost envision the domain of the Time God not as a sanctuary, but as a trap lined with paradoxes, waiting to snap shut on anything that moved out of sync with its rhythm.</p>
<p>’Let her weep, let her feel,’ he mused grimly, his inner monologue shifting back to the hunt. ’I’ll give her the truth she deserves...’</p>
<p>’But when I step into that domain, I’m not going there to negotiate or to understand.’</p>
<p>’I’m going there to break the clock. And if Kaeleth thinks he’s seen a monster before, he’s about to learn that some things can’t be erased; they can only be unleashed.’</p>
