<p>Kaeleth began to recount what his observation function had witnessed: a clinical dissection of a predator’s evolution.</p>
<p>He described Rex’s movements through the Nightwing household with terrifying precision. He spoke of the skill acquisition patterns that read as more than mere practice; they were a violent, exponential expansion of capability.</p>
<p>He detailed the integration of presence manipulation, and then he spoke of the second acquisition, the one that had slipped through the cracks. A secondary skill, which was fundamental and devastating, had been concealed from the divine due to the patron’s new, unknown technique.</p>
<p>Celestina listened, her mind a furnace of cold calculation. Each detail was a new variable in a problem that was rapidly becoming unsolvable.</p>
<p>"He is not merely learning to fight," Celestina said, her voice cutting through the divine static. "He is acquiring divine engagement capabilities..."</p>
<p>"This isn’t Reincarnator combat training; he’s not learning how to kill mortals more efficiently..."</p>
<p>"The Presence Manipulation is the first piece of a specific arsenal designed to operate within divine registers."</p>
<p>"He is learning how to wound you."</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>"He is planning to come for you," Celestina stated. It wasn’t a warning; it was a tactical inevitability.</p>
<p>The pause that followed was not the measured silence of a god processing data. It was something far more unsettling.</p>
<p>It was the stillness of an apex predator that has just heard the click of a trap snapping shut behind it.</p>
<p>"He is planning to," Kaeleth said at last. "Yes."</p>
<p>Celestina leaned forward, her eyes narrowing. "You do not sound like a god who considers that an idle threat."</p>
<p>"I do not consider anything about this reincarnator to be idle," Kaeleth snapped.</p>
<p>The temporal vibration beneath his words carried a timbre. Celestina had never heard, in twelve years of contact, a raw, grinding tension that made the stone beneath her throne tremble. "I want to tell you something, Celestina."</p>
<p>"I am not saying this to alarm you... I do not waste energy on unnecessary alarm."</p>
<p>"I am saying it because you hired me to help you manage a threat, and I am no longer certain I fully understand the nature of the enemy we are facing."</p>
<p>The shift in Celestina’s posture was immediate. Her spine straightened, and her breathing became shallow and controlled.</p>
<p>When the expert admits he is blind, the student must prepare for the dark. "Explain."</p>
<p>"My domain’s function includes the ability to observe a mortal’s temporal thread," Kaeleth began, his voice growing distant as he delved into the mechanics of his own failure. "Not their future, which is a chaos even I cannot fully map, but their past."</p>
<p>"The specific sequence of moments that forged the person standing before me now."</p>
<p>"For every reincarnator I have observed across two millennia, that thread has two distinct segments: the foreign sequence from their original world, a disconnected, alien string, and the native sequence from the moment they arrived here."</p>
<p>"And Rex’s thread?" Celestina asked.</p>
<p>"Rex’s thread has three segments," Kaeleth said, and there was a tremor in the divine frequency. "Somewhere between the foreign sequence and his arrival in this world, there is a section that defies categorization."</p>
<p>"It does not read as absent, which would simply imply a void where nothing happened..."</p>
<p>"It reads as obscured... Deliberately, surgically obscured in a way that no natural reincarnator arrival sequence could ever produce."</p>
<p>Celestina sat in silence, processing the impossibility of the statement. Her inner mind replayed every file she had on Rex Rexilion, searching for the crack, the wound, and the lie.</p>
<p>"Obscured by what?"</p>
<p>"I do not know," Kaeleth admitted, and the admission felt like a crack in the foundation of the world. "That is the horror of it."</p>
<p>"I have spent two thousand years being the entity who knows... Even when the truth is blood-soaked or blasphemous, I see it."</p>
<p>"I see the shape of every sin and every miracle. But when I looked at his thread, there was a piece of it I simply could not see."</p>
<p>"I cannot tell if something is actively hiding it from me or if something occurred there that exists entirely outside the categories my divine perception was built to recognize."</p>
<p>Celestina’s voice was slow, each word deliberate as she dragged the implication into the light. "You are telling me that a reincarnator entered this world with a portion of his own history already sealed against a god whose entire existence is built upon reading history."</p>
<p>"Yes."</p>
<p>"That has never happened before."</p>
<p>"Not in two thousand years," Kaeleth confirmed. "I have seen Apostles with protective wards over sensitive moments their patrons wish to keep private."</p>
<p>"But even then, I can see the shape of the protection... I can see the outline of what is being hidden. This is different."</p>
<p>"This is not a ward placed over a moment, but this is an absence with razor-sharp boundaries."</p>
<p>"A piece of his thread that has been excised... the front and back edges are clean, as though something reached into the fabric of his soul and cut it out."</p>
<p>"Excised," Celestina whispered.</p>
<p>"Or replaced," Kaeleth said. "I genuinely do not know which, and that uncertainty troubles me more than any threat I have faced."</p>
<p>"I built the counter frequency he used against my working through years of patient study of exactly this kind of temporal gap..."</p>
<p>"And yet, his patron found a seam in my architecture that should have been impossible to locate without intimate, pre-existing knowledge of how my workings are constructed."</p>
<p>"That is not the behavior of a minor deity managing a new puppet... but that is the behavior of something that has done this before, something that has dismantled workings far older, far more sophisticated, and far more dangerous than the one I built for you."</p>
<p>Celestina absorbed the revelation in a silence so profound it felt like the throne room itself had been hollowed out. The amber light from the crystal cast elongated, skeletal shadows across her face, making her features appear as carved from something colder than stone.</p>
<p>She didn’t flinch; she didn’t gasp; she simply existed in the space between the impossible truth and the tactical reality it created.</p>
<p>The silence stretched, becoming a physical pressure, until Kaeleth broke it, his voice carrying a rare, unguarded vulnerability that made the divine frequency waver.</p>
<p>"I am not telling you to abandon the plan," he said, the words cutting through the stillness like a blade. "I am telling you what I am actually afraid of."</p>
<p>"You asked for my help as a partner, not as a tool, Celestina."</p>
<p>"And a partner who withholds the true depth of his assessment out of pride is a liability, a liability you cannot afford."</p>
<p>"I am afraid that we have spent months treating Rex Rexilion as a difficult reincarnator when he may be something entirely different wearing the shape of one."</p>
<p>"I am afraid that the portion of his history I cannot see contains the exact blueprint for my downfall and that whatever lurks in that gap is older, hungrier, and more dangerous than either of us has dared to imagine."</p>
<p>Celestina finally raised her gaze, her eyes locking onto the pulsing light of the crystal. "You are a god who has existed for two thousand years, Kaeleth."</p>
<p>"You have witnessed the rise and fall of empires and the birth of pantheons, but now... you are telling me you are afraid of a mortal."</p>
<p>"I am telling you I am afraid of what I cannot read," Kaeleth corrected sharply, his voice regaining its terrifying authority. "Those are not the same statement, though I understand why your mortal mind would conflate them."</p>
<p>"I am not afraid of Rex Rexilion the way a human fears a rival or a predator, but I am afraid the way an architect fears a flaw in a foundation that shouldn’t exist."</p>
<p>"I am afraid in the same way an accountant fears a ledger that refuses to balance, as an unbalanced ledger indicates either a fatal error by the accountant or that someone has tampered with the system."</p>
<p>The divine vibration beneath his words grew more intense. "In two thousand years of keeping this particular ledger, I have never once found an entry I could not trace."</p>
<p>"Every sin, every miracle, every twisted thread of fate has had a source..."</p>
<p>"Until him... Until he walked into my domain and presented me with a void I could not fill."</p>
<p>Celestina sat motionless in the dying amber light, her mind working with the cold, relentless efficiency of a siege engine. She wasn’t thinking about fear; she was thinking about countermeasures.</p>
<p>She was thinking about the blood that would need to be spilled to reassert control over a reality that was beginning to slip through her fingers.</p>
<p>"You need to move first," she said. The words were quiet, but they carried the absolute finality of a death sentence.</p>
<p>"My operational options within the divine framework’s code are severely compromised," Kaeleth warned, his voice growing grim. "The violation of my working was noted by the other gods."</p>
<p>"Their eyes are on me now... Direct action, divine intervention, and celestial wrath are off the table."</p>
<p>"I cannot touch him directly without triggering a cascade of divine scrutiny that would expose everything we have built..."</p>
<p>"I am paralyzed, Celestina. For an indefinite period, I can only watch."</p>
<p>Celestina’s lips thinned into a hard, merciless line. The shadows in the room seemed to deepen around her, as if the darkness itself were acknowledging her intent.</p>
<p>"Then we do not use divine action," she said, her voice dropping to a lethal whisper. "We use an apostle."</p>
