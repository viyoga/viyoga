<p>They settled onto a low stone bench at the training yard’s edge, the cold granite seeping through their clothes, but neither noticed. Apollo began to speak, and as the minutes bled into an hour, the air between them grew thick and toxic with the weight of his words.</p>
<p>His voice didn’t just carry information; it carried the trauma of every atrocity. He spoke of the plaza fight against Tremor the way the ground had buckled like breaking bone as the earth itself turned hostile.</p>
<p>He described the wet, sickening sound of Calivara and Eryndra’s bodies being crushed under magical pressure and the spray of arterial blood across the cobblestones that had stained his own boots.</p>
<p>He recounted the aerial nightmare of Ignivara’s dragon form, from the searing heat that melted armor to flesh to the screams of soldiers being incinerated mid-air before Rex’s group had descended like vengeful gods to claim the kill.</p>
<p>Then, the cadence of his voice shifted. It dropped into a register so low and guarded that even the wind seemed too quiet to listen to.</p>
<p>He spoke of Theo.</p>
<p>"He blamed everything on Rex," Apollo said quietly.</p>
<p>He wasn’t looking at Elliot; his gaze was fixed on his own hands, which were unsteady, fingers twitching as if still feeling the phantom heat of the magical backlash.</p>
<p>"He was screaming it raw, guttural screams that sounded less like words and more like animal pain..."</p>
<p>"He was hysterical, Elliot. He was hovering over half the city while a temporal field tore through the streets like a scythe, aging children backward, turning their bones to powder and their minds to static."</p>
<p>Apollo swallowed hard, the motion visible in his Adam’s apple. "I didn’t believe him... I couldn’t."</p>
<p>"Rex has been our anchor. He’s been the one pulling us out of the fire time and time again."</p>
<p>"But..." Apollo’s voice wavered, "there’s a part of me that still hears the conviction in Theo’s voice."</p>
<p>"He didn’t sound like a man lying to save face... He sounded like a man who had seen the truth behind the curtain and was being driven mad by it."</p>
<p>Elliot sat motionless, his jaw tight enough to crack teeth. He turned the information over in his mind, dissecting it with the methodical cruelty of a surgeon.</p>
<p>He didn’t rush to judgment; he weighed the evidence against the human condition.</p>
<p>"Grief does strange things to certainty," Elliot said finally, his voice flat and controlled. "Theo was a man who had been hollowed out."</p>
<p>"He lost his standing, his purpose, his connection to Diana... he lost the very foundation of who he was before that final confrontation even began."</p>
<p>"When a person loses their entire world, they don’t seek understanding... They seek a target."</p>
<p>"They grasp for someone to blame because the alternative that the world is simply cruel and random is too terrifying to bear."</p>
<p>"That’s what I keep telling myself... Every single night..." Apollo finally looked up, meeting Elliot’s eyes.</p>
<p>The vulnerability there was startling the raw, exposed nerve of a man who had been holding back a floodgate of doubt. "But the doubt won’t die, Elliot."</p>
<p>"It just sits there in the dark. I keep thinking... if I had been faster."</p>
<p>"If I had noticed the cracks in his psyche before they became chasms..."</p>
<p>"If I hadn’t assumed the household would handle its own rotting corpse of a problem."</p>
<p>"Maybe he wouldn’t have ended up dead, and maybe the city wouldn’t be standing on the precipice of this Underlayer nightmare."</p>
<p>"You cannot carry the weight of every outcome you didn’t have the power to prevent," Elliot said.</p>
<p>The words felt hollow, tasting like ash in his mouth. He knew better than anyone that this was a lie that they would both carry these ghosts until their own bones broke.</p>
<p>He couldn’t forgive himself for being absent, so he couldn’t truly comfort Apollo for being present but helpless.</p>
<p>Elliot straightened his spine, the soldier eclipsing the grieving friend. The time for mourning was over; the time for blood was approaching.</p>
<p>"But we can’t change what’s already been written in blood," Elliot continued, his voice gaining a hard, metallic edge. "What matters now is what happens when we march into that hole."</p>
<p>"Tell me about the offensive... No more postmortems, Apollo."</p>
<p>"I need tactics... I need to know where the breaking points are..."</p>
<p>"I want to contribute something meaningful to this fight... I want to be the blade that cuts through the heart of this corruption, not just another body thrown into the meat grinder."</p>
<p>The shift in the air was immediate. As Elliot demanded specifics, as he demanded the language of war, the suffocating blanket of grief that had draped over Apollo’s shoulders began to fray and tear.</p>
<p>The raw vulnerability in his eyes hardened, replaced by something familiar—the sharp, analytical glint of a combatant.</p>
<p>For both of them, this was safer ground. Grief was an abyss with no bottom, but tactics? Tactics were something they could grasp, something they could wield like a sword.</p>
<p>"Heroic Strike hits hardest against a single concentrated target," Apollo said, his voice losing its tremor and gaining a rhythmic, military precision.</p>
<p>He began to pace the small space of the yard, his hands carving patterns in the air as if drawing invisible battle lines. "You’re the scalpel, Elliot."</p>
<p>"You punch through the center, shatter their primary defensive line, and create that momentary vacuum of chaos..."</p>
<p>"That’s when I come in."</p>
<p>His eyes sparked with the familiar green hum of his affinity. "My life affinity’s full activation isn’t just healing; it’s the reversal of localized entropy."</p>
<p>"When I flood the field, I can disrupt the undead constructs by forcing their cellular structure into a state of hyper regeneration that their rotting forms can’t sustain; they’ll literally burst from the inside out."</p>
<p>"I can stabilize our wounded in the split second after the impact, stitching flesh together while the blood is still warm..."</p>
<p>"If we time it right, your strike cracking their shell just before I bring the wave through behind you, we create a breach wide enough for the entire formation to pour through like water through a broken dam."</p>
<p>Elliot leaned forward, his mind already visualizing the carnage. "How long does that window stay open?"</p>
<p>"How long does the activation actually last at full output?"</p>
<p>"Four minutes," Apollo answered grimly. "At my current strength, I can hold the field for four minutes before the strain starts tearing my own capillaries apart."</p>
<p>"It’s longer than it used to be, but it’s still a razor’s edge..."</p>
<p>"If I burn it too early... if I ignite the field before the formation has properly positioned itself to exploit the breach, we lose the advantage before the rest of the assault can even capitalize on it."</p>
<p>"We’d be left standing in a field of corpses with no protection."</p>
<p>For the next hour, the training yard became their war room. They descended into the brutal, granular details of slaughter that only two veterans could truly appreciate.</p>
<p>They mapped out rotation schedules with the cold efficiency of clockwork: who would hold the flank when the first wave of horrors broke against them, which magical reserves would be tapped first, and how to coordinate their abilities to ensure maximum lethality.</p>
<p>’We can’t just fight,’ Elliot thought grimly as they debated the geography of the northern corridor. ’We have to become a machine of controlled violence.’</p>
<p>They discussed contingencies for every nightmare scenario: what if the corridor’s stone walls proved more claustrophobic than the scouts reported, turning their formation into a killing box?</p>
<p>What if the resistance wasn’t just a wave but a meat grinder of bone-crushing pressure that required immediate fallback positions?</p>
<p>They argued over shield walls, over the exact moment to commit their reserves, and over how to protect the healers when the first blood began to spray.</p>
<p>It was a return to the old ways, the familiar, grounding rhythm of shared purpose. Two rivals who had once measured themselves against each other now worked in perfect, deadly synchronization toward a singular, desperate goal.</p>
<p>By the time they finally stopped, the sun had begun to dip below the academy walls, casting long, blood-red shadows across the yard.</p>
<p>The earlier heaviness in Apollo’s posture had vanished. In its place was something far more dangerous: the focused, coiled energy of a man who no longer felt like a victim of circumstance but a predator preparing for the hunt.</p>
<p>He wasn’t bracing for the storm anymore; he was ready to become part of it.</p>
