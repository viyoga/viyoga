<p>Helena screamed.</p>
<p>It was not a human sound. It was a raw, primal shriek that tore from the depths of her shattered soul a sound of pure, unadulterated agony that seemed to vibrate in the marrow of everyone present.</p>
<p>Her vocal cords strained until they sounded like they were tearing, but she didn’t stop. The scream became a conduit, a bridge between her grief and the raw power she was about to unleash.</p>
<p>She flung both arms outward in a desperate, violent gesture.</p>
<p>"DEATH TO THE DEVIL!" she shrieked.</p>
<p>The atmosphere in the hall didn’t just change; it died. The air began to hum with an ominous, subsonic frequency that made the teeth of every soldier in the room ache.</p>
<p>A pressure like the eye of a hurricane rolled across the floor, rattling every remaining shard of glass in the windows until they exploded outward with a synchronized tink-tink-crash.</p>
<p>Then, the elements answered her call.</p>
<p>From her right palm, flame erupted not as a spark, but as a roaring, sentient coil of molten fury that hissed like a thousand serpents.</p>
<p>FROOOOOOSH.</p>
<p>From her left, frost spiraled upward in a glittering, deadly cyclone of razor-sharp ice crystals that screamed as they cut through the air.</p>
<p>Beneath her feet, the floor groaned as stone tore itself free from the foundation, rising in a jagged ring of boulders that pulsed with magical energy.</p>
<p>And threading through it all, veins of brilliant, crackling blue lightning crawled across the swirling chaos like living nerves.</p>
<p>She was invoking the Primordial Convergence, the forbidden ritual she had once sworn she would only use to save a life; now she was using it to end one.</p>
<p>The four elemental forces wound together above her head, coalescing into a towering, unstable vortex of impossible colors. Red, white, slate grey, and electric blue spun around each other in a lethal braid of energy.</p>
<p>The light was so intense it cast shifting, kaleidoscopic shadows across her tear-streaked face, making her look less like a woman and more like a vengeful goddess of destruction. The floor beneath her spiderwebbed with fresh cracks.</p>
<p>The remaining chandeliers groaned on their chains, swinging violently.</p>
<p>"MOTHER, NO! PLEASE STOP!" Diana’s voice shrieked from behind the stone barrier, muffled and terrified.</p>
<p>"YOU’RE KILLING US ALL!" Lily’s cry pierced through the magical static, but Helena was gone.</p>
<p>She had ascended beyond pain, beyond love, beyond humanity. The world had narrowed down to a single point of annihilation.</p>
<p>"THIS...!" Helena’s voice rose to a crescendo that cracked with divine madness, "...IS FOR MY CHILDREN!"</p>
<p>She thrust both hands forward with every ounce of her remaining life force. "DIE!!!"</p>
<p>The vortex descended.</p>
<p>It didn’t just fall; it struck Rex like the fist of a collapsing star. The impact was a cataclysmic explosion of sound and light.</p>
<p>KRA BOOOOOOOOM!</p>
<p>A shockwave of pure kinetic energy detonated from the center of the hall, punching outward with such force that the remaining walls buckled and groaned.</p>
<p>The massive marble pillars supporting the Starlight manor screamed as they split down their centers, sending spiderweb fractures racing toward the ceiling.</p>
<p>The ceiling of the second floor gave way first with a thunderous CRUNCH of splintering wood, followed immediately by the third. Tons of ancient oak beams, marble flooring, and plaster began to cascade downward in a deadly avalanche.</p>
<p>The crystal chandelier, already weakened, shattered completely, raining down in a glittering, deadly storm of glass shards.</p>
<p>A massive wooden joist, thick as a man’s torso, dropped like a guillotine blade directly onto the spot where Rex had been standing.</p>
<p>Behind it came the rest of a heaving, thundering mountain of debris that buried the center of the hall in a single, choking cloud of pulverized stone and ash. The sound was deafening, a continuous roar of destruction that seemed to last forever.</p>
<p>RUMBLE... CRASH... SHATTER...</p>
<p>The dust cloud rose high toward the broken roof, thick and grey, swallowing everything in its path. The only sound left was the settling of debris and the distant, terrified sobbing of the children.</p>
<p>Then, the rumbling slowly began to fade. The chaos ebbed.</p>
<p>The dust hung suspended in the air, swirling lazily in the moonlight filtering through the ruins.</p>
<p>Silence returned to the Starlight household a heavy, suffocating silence that felt like the end of the world.</p>
<p>When the dust finally began to settle, the silence that followed was not peaceful; it was absolute, heavy, and suffocating—the kind of silence that follows a massacre. The air was thick with the acrid stench of burnt ozone, pulverized limestone, and the copper tang of blood.</p>
<p>Somewhere deep in the wreckage, a single shard of crystal clinked against stone, and a massive oak beam groaned as it settled further into the debris.</p>
<p>Helena stood in the epicenter of the devastation, a ghost among ruins. Her chest heaved in ragged, desperate gasps that sounded like broken bellows.</p>
<p>Her arms hung limp at her sides, twitching uncontrollably from the magical backlash. Her hair, matted with sweat and ash, clung to her cheeks like spiderwebs.</p>
<p>She looked less like a powerful mage and more like something hollowed out, a shell of a woman who had poured her very soul into a single, catastrophic moment.</p>
<p>Behind the protective granite vault, the horror was visceral. Lily had both hands pressed so hard against her mouth that her knuckles were white, her eyes wide and glassy with trauma.</p>
<p>Valentina had collapsed to her knees, staring at the mountain of debris where her mother had just unleashed hell. Leonidas, still pinned to the floor by Rex’s residual gravitational pressure, could only stare at the wreckage.</p>
<p>His breath was trapped in his throat, his lungs burning, his mind screaming a warning that came too late.</p>
<p>Cassandra, Gorvasha, and the ring of Underlayer soldiers remained frozen. They were statues of flesh and steel, bound by the absolute authority Rex had exerted even in his apparent death.</p>
<p>Only Lilith broke the formation slightly, a small, involuntary half-step forward, her fingers twitching toward the rubble before she forcibly reined herself in, terrified of what she might find.</p>
<p>Helena’s magic was gone. She could feel it in the marrow of her bones a terrifying, hollow ache, as if someone had reached inside her chest and scooped out her very essence.</p>
<p>The volcanic rage that had fueled her for hours was evaporating, leaving behind a cold, creeping numbness. The adrenaline was receding, replaced by a dawning, horrific clarity.</p>
<p>She looked at the mountain of stone, timber, and twisted metal that had once been the heart of her home. She looked at the crater where Rex had stood.</p>
<p>A small, strangled sob escaped her throat, a sound of pure, unadulterated grief.</p>
<p>’What have I done?’ The thought echoed in her mind, louder than the explosion. ’I killed him.’</p>
<p>’I killed him, and I might have killed everything else.’ The realization hit her like a physical blow, more painful than any spell.</p>
<p>She had sought justice, but she had delivered only annihilation.</p>
<p>The silence stretched, taut as a bowstring. A single flake of ceiling plaster drifted down through the haze, shattering on the marble floor with a pathetic click. Helena’s shoulders began to shake.</p>
<p>Her knees buckled. She began to weep, not with anger, but with the broken whimpering of someone who had realized they were standing in their own grave.</p>
<p>Then, the mountain moved.</p>
<p>Grrrrrrrnd.</p>
<p>A sound like grinding teeth erupted from beneath tons of debris. A massive slab of marble shifted violently, sliding across the rubble with a wet, grinding screech.</p>
<p>A fist scarred, bloodied, and caked in grey ash punched through a gap between two broken beams, claws digging into the stone as it sought purchase.</p>
<p>’W-what...?! N-no way...!’</p>
