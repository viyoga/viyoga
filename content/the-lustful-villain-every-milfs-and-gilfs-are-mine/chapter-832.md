<p>The Knowledge System Holder narrowed his eyes, his gaze sharpening like a blade being honed against a whetstone. He didn’t recoil from Rex’s logic; instead, he leaned into it, his posture stiffening with the intellectual indignation of a man who prided himself on the purity of his inquiry.</p>
<p>"You are asking us to dismiss the question simply because the asking of it serves the Legion’s objective," he countered, his voice ringing with a cold, academic precision. "But a truth does not lose its validity just because a predator timed its reveal."</p>
<p>"If the Headmaster possessed intelligence regarding the Legion’s genesis and chose to bury it in the shadows of her own history, that is a legitimate crisis of accountability."</p>
<p>"The timing may be a trap, but the suspicion is a real, breathing thing."</p>
<p>"We cannot ignore the rot just because someone threw a handful of salt on it to make us notice."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. He met that intellectual ferocity with the immovable calm of a mountain facing a landslide.</p>
<p>"The concern is legitimate," Rex conceded, his voice low and steady, acting as the anchor for the room’s drifting tempers. "But the sequence you are demanding is a tactical error."</p>
<p>"You are trying to pass judgment before the evidence has even been laid on the altar."</p>
<p>"And what sequence would you prefer?" the knowledge system holder snapped.</p>
<p>There was a jagged edge to his tone, not quite outright hostility, but the dangerous friction of a man who felt he was being steered. He felt the subtle pull of Rex’s influence, and he wasn’t sure if he was being guided toward wisdom or being managed away from a truth he had a right to wield.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch at the edge in the man’s voice. He stepped into the tension, his presence expanding to fill the gap between the accusation and the answer.</p>
<p>"The sequence where we actually hear what she has to say before we decide what we think about it," Rex said, the words hitting the table with the finality of a hammer blow. "Right now, you haven’t built a case."</p>
<p>"You have built a reaction."</p>
<p>"You have taken a surname, a sudden tremor in a leader’s voice, and a moment of chaos, and you have called it ’evidence.’ That isn’t a case; it’s a lynch mob in the making."</p>
<p>"A real case requires the substance of the thing itself. It requires the meat, not just the scent of blood on the wind."</p>
<p>He turned his full, unyielding attention toward the Knowledge System Holder. He wasn’t trying to silence the man or patronize him; he was dissecting the very structure of his logic, treating the man’s doubt as a mathematical error that needed correction.</p>
<p>"Listen to me," Rex commanded, his voice dropping to a tone of lethal clarity. "If you allow Lady Valentina to speak, and the picture she paints, the one she has been carrying in the dark for eleven years, still aligns with the suspicion you are raising, then the concern will not just be heard!"</p>
<p>"It will have the full, crushing weight of this entire room behind it!"</p>
<p>"We will tear the truth out of her with our bare hands if we have to."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the visceral image of a collective interrogation settle in the air.</p>
<p>"But," Rex added, his eyes locking onto the man’s with a piercing intensity, "if the picture she produces is different from the one you have constructed in your head... if the reality is more complex than your suspicion... then by jumping the gun, you haven’t just made a mistake."</p>
<p>"You have handed the Legion exactly what they wanted."</p>
<p>"You will have turned a moment of scrutiny into a moment of schism."</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder fell silent. He looked at Rex, his mind visibly churning, caught between the pride of his intellect and the terrifying possibility that he was currently walking straight into the jaws of a trap.</p>
<p>"You are very confident in what the picture will show," the Knowledge System Holder remarked, his voice laced with a skeptical, almost weary cynicism.</p>
<p>He was testing the structural integrity of Rex’s bravado.</p>
<p>"I am not confident in the picture," Rex corrected, his voice dropping into a register of absolute, unshakeable gravity. "I am confident in the process."</p>
<p>"The outcome is a variable; the method is a constant."</p>
<p>"We do not gamble on the truth; we build a cage for it until it has no choice but to reveal itself."</p>
<p>The divination representative shifted in her seat, the silk of her robes rustling like dry leaves in a graveyard. The sound was unnervingly loud in the pressurized silence.</p>
<p>"Even if we grant you the sequence," she said, her voice trembling with the weight of her own logic, "even if we accept that we are jumping the gun... the blood does not wash away just because we wait for the right moment to count it."</p>
<p>"The fact remains that someone in this network has been withholding the very truth that could have altered the course of a war, all while the organization responsible for forty-three deaths continued to operate with impunity."</p>
<p>"The sequence does not make those forty-three ghosts any less real."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said, using the word as a blunt instrument. "It does not."</p>
<p>"Nothing makes that smaller."</p>
<p>"Nothing makes the grief of forty-three families any lighter."</p>
<p>"What I am asking you to do is not to minimize the tragedy, but to stop inflating the betrayal. Do not make the sin larger than it actually is before you have even heard the confession."</p>
<p>He turned his gaze, sharp and predatory, toward Morwenna.</p>
<p>"You have known Valentina for forty years," Rex said, his voice softening but losing none of its edge. "The reaction you read when Apollo spoke that name, the way her breath hitched, the way her eyes fractured for a split second—you were right."</p>
<p>"You identified a woman hearing information she already possessed."</p>
<p>"She did possess it. But the question we are actually fighting about isn’t whether she held a secret."</p>
<p>"The question is whether the act of holding it makes her the enemy of the people in this room."</p>
<p>Morwenna’s eyes met his, and for a moment, the two of them were the only two people in the world, locked in a silent, ancient combat of understanding.</p>
<p>"And you know the answer to that question," Rex pushed, his voice a low, rhythmic drumbeat. "You knew it before Apollo ever opened his mouth, and you know it now."</p>
<p>"The reason you are even entertaining this doubt isn’t because you truly believe Valentina has turned her back on us."</p>
<p>"It’s because forty years of bedrock trust is a quiet thing, and the chaos in this room is deafening."</p>
<p>"The chaos is louder right now, Morwenna."</p>
<p>"It’s screaming, and you’re just trying to hear it over the sound of your own heart."</p>
<p>Morwenna fell silent. The air in the room seemed to thicken, turning into something heavy and suffocating, like the atmosphere before a violent thunderstorm.</p>
<p>She didn’t look at Rex. She didn’t look at the council.</p>
<p>She looked at Valentina.</p>
<p>"Is he right?" Morwenna asked.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question directed at Rex. It was a demand, a plea, and a trial, all wrapped into one.</p>
<p>She was looking at the woman who had led them through hell, asking for the one thing a commander rarely gives: vulnerability.</p>
<p>Valentina held her gaze, her expression a mask of weathered granite, though her eyes burned with a fierce, internal light.</p>
<p>"About which part?" she asked, her voice steady despite the tectonic shifts occurring within her.</p>
<p>"That you are not the enemy," Morwenna said, her voice cracking just enough to reveal the agony of the question. "I know who you are."</p>
<p>’I have bled beside you for four decades..."</p>
<p>"I have seen you make the impossible look easy. But this room... this room is fracturing."</p>
<p>"They need to hear it from you, not from a student who has been in Aethelgard for not even a year, but from the woman who holds the weight of our lives in her hands."</p>
<p>"Say it. Directly."</p>
<p>The entire room turned.</p>
<p>The atmosphere had shifted once more. The frantic, jagged tension of the initial fracture and the sharp, reactive energy of people feeling threatened had bled away.</p>
<p>In its place was something older, heavier, and far more terrifying: the breathless, expectant silence of a people standing on the edge of an abyss, waiting to see if the ground beneath them would hold or shatter.</p>
<p>Valentina looked Morwenna dead in the eye, refusing to blink, refusing to flinch.</p>
<p>"I am not the enemy of this room," Valentina declared, her voice ringing out with the clarity of a clarion call. "I have never been the enemy of this room."</p>
<p>"The choices I made... the silence I kept... they were mistaken."</p>
<p>"They were heavy and flawed, and I will take responsibility for them."</p>
<p>"I will pay the price in blood or in service if that is what the math requires."</p>
<p>"But there is a distinction you all must grasp if we are to survive the night."</p>
<p>She leaned forward, her presence dominating the table, her shadow falling over the council like a shroud.</p>
<p>"The choices were wrong in the way that holding a shield too long instead of striking is wrong," she said, her voice dropping to a lethal, intimate whisper. "They were the mistakes of caution, not the mistakes of malice."</p>
<p>"They were not the choices of someone trying to destroy us but of someone trying to preserve the very thing we are currently tearing apart."</p>
<p>"The distinction matters. Not for my sake; my soul is already on the table, but for yours. Because if you decide that a mistake of silence is the same as an act of betrayal, you will build the wrong foundation for whatever comes next."</p>
<p>"And you will build it on sand."</p>
