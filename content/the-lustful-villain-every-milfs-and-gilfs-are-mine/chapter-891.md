<p>Kaeleth fell into a silence that seemed to drain the very color from the transmission. For a moment, the only sound was the faint, rhythmic hum of the crystal, a sound that felt unnaturally loud in the wake of their conversation.</p>
<p>When he finally spoke, his voice had lost its divine cadence, replaced by something colder, something that sounded like the grinding of tectonic plates.</p>
<p>"I will designate the Nightwing boy," he said, the words carrying the weight of a death warrant. "The designation will be delivered through the standard Apostle channel..."</p>
<p>"He will receive the blessing, and with it, the specific, devastating capabilities I described. But there will be no briefing..."</p>
<p>"There will be no manual... He will not know the true nature of the weapon he carries unless he encounters a situation that forces the power to manifest."</p>
<p>"He will know he is stronger," Celestina observed, her mind already mapping out the boy’s inevitable trajectory. "That is all he needs to know to maintain the facade."</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth agreed. "He will feel the surge of the designation’s power coursing through his veins, and he will mistake it for his own evolution."</p>
<p>"He will believe his system has simply broken through a new ceiling, and that is the standard experience for an early-stage apostle: the delusion of self-made strength."</p>
<p>"Good," Celestina said, a ghost of a smile touching her lips, a smile that held no warmth. "The deception is the most vital component."</p>
<p>"He must approach Rex believing he is merely an improved version of himself..."</p>
<p>"He cannot approach Rex as a weapon pointed at a target, because Rex would recognize the shape of the blade before it ever swung."</p>
<p>"He must be the dagger hidden inside the glove."</p>
<p>"This will not be instantaneous," Kaeleth warned, his image flickering as if the strain of the decision was affecting his connection to the mortal plane. "The delivery of a divine designation requires precise metaphysical conditions."</p>
<p>"I cannot simply force the blessing into a sleeping mind like water into a vessel..."</p>
<p>"The mortal’s consciousness must be in a receptive state, a vulnerability that exists between the layers of waking and dreaming."</p>
<p>"He sleeps," Celestina said flatly. "The boy is young; exhaustion is his constant companion."</p>
<p>"Not just any sleep," Kaeleth corrected, his eyes narrowing behind the crystal’s glow. "It must be the kind of sleep that occurs when the mind is desperate, searching for something in the darkness that it could never find in the light of day."</p>
<p>"A sleep born of longing or resentment..."</p>
<p>"Theo Morr Nightwing, based on every thread I have observed, is exactly that kind of person."</p>
<p>"He sleeps with a hunger that makes his soul vulnerable to intrusion."</p>
<p>"Then he is ready," Celestina said.</p>
<p>"The designation will find him," Kaeleth promised. "Like a predator finding a scent in the wind."</p>
<p>The transmission began to waver. The amber light dimmed, fading into a sickly, bruised purple as the connection strained under the gravity of what had been set in motion.</p>
<p>In that dying light, Celestina felt something shift. It wasn’t quite hesitation; she had long since burned that human weakness out of herself, and it wasn’t quite dread.</p>
<p>It was something else, something colder: a recognition that they had just stepped off the edge of a cliff and were now simply waiting to see if they would fly or shatter.</p>
<p>For the first time in twelve years of partnership, the silence between them felt wrong. It felt like the breath held before a scream.</p>
<p>Just as the light began to fail entirely, Kaeleth’s voice cut through the fading static one last time, sharp as a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>"One more thing."</p>
<p>"Say it," Celestina commanded, her voice cracking like a whip across the empty throne room.</p>
<p>The words were sharp, but beneath them lay a rare, almost imperceptible tension, the kind that precedes a storm that levels cities.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s image in the crystal wavered, his ancient features twisted into something resembling pity.</p>
<p>"If the boy fails," he began, each word dripping with the finality of a funeral dirge, "and the gap in Rex Rexilion’s thread proves to be what my darkest suspicions suggest, if that void is not a wound but a doorway, then you must understand something."</p>
<p>"I may not be able to help you a second time."</p>
<p>Celestina’s grip tightened on her armrest, but she remained motionless.</p>
<p>"This is not a threat, nor is it a lack of willingness," Kaeleth continued, his voice dropping to a gravelly whisper that seemed to vibrate through the crystal itself. "It is a warning of metaphysical exhaustion."</p>
<p>"Whatever force produced that gap in Rex’s history may already understand the architecture of my workings intimately enough to counter the next attempt before it even manifests..."</p>
<p>"I have given you my best effort tonight in describing the risk, so I am not certain my best effort will be enough to save you."</p>
<p>"And Celestina... I have not spoken those words about anything in longer than either of us has been alive."</p>
<p>The weight of that admission filled the room like toxic gas. The implications were staggering: they were gambling not just a boy’s life, but the last viable weapon in their arsenal.</p>
<p>"I understand," Celestina said. Her voice was steady, but her pulse hammered against the hollow of her throat.</p>
<p>"Do you?" Kaeleth challenged. His eyes pierced through the transmission, searching her soul through the amber glow. "I am not asking to insult you."</p>
<p>"I am asking because I need to know if I am speaking to a commander who has truly heard the tactical reality of this gamble or if I am speaking to a woman who has already decided the plan is settled and is simply waiting for me to finish my performance."</p>
<p>Celestina stared into the crystal. For several seconds, she did not breathe.</p>
<p>She looked at the flickering image of the god, seeing not a partner but a mirror reflecting the terrifying precipice they both stood upon.</p>
<p>"I have heard every word," she finally said, her voice regaining its iron edge. "Every syllable of your warning."</p>
<p>"And I am proceeding anyway... Because thirty years of my life, thirty years of blood, betrayal, and meticulous construction are built into what happens next."</p>
<p>"The alternative you describe to wait, to gather more intelligence, to hope that the gap in his history resolves itself into something less frightening before we strike, is not an alternative. It is suicide."</p>
<p>"In a world where Rex Rexilion grows stronger with every heartbeat he spends alone, waiting is merely choosing a slower way to die..."</p>
<p>"I would rather move against an uncertainty I understand imperfectly than wait for a certainty that may never arrive."</p>
<p>"That is either courage," Kaeleth said, his voice fading as the connection began to fracture, "or the specific kind of stubbornness that only looks like courage from the outside."</p>
<p>"I have not decided which you are, Celestina, even after twelve years."</p>
<p>"Neither have I," Celestina replied, her gaze unblinking. "I stopped trying to decide what I am a long time ago. It does not change what I must do next."</p>
<p>The crystal’s amber gold light shuddered violently before dimming into a cold, dead black. The silence that rushed in to fill the void was deafening.</p>
<p>Celestina set the artifact back onto its shelf with a precise, controlled motion and sank back into her throne. The afternoon light of the throne room spilled across her features, casting long, sharp shadows that made her look as ancient as the god she had just dismissed.</p>
<p>Her expression was flat and clear. The expression of a mathematician who had reached the end of a brutal thousand-page calculation and found that the result, however grim, was workable.</p>
<p>She was not afraid.</p>
<p>Fear was a luxury for those who still had options. She had been afraid once approximately fifteen years ago when she had first understood the true consequences of failure.</p>
<p>She remembered the cold sweat, the way her hands had trembled when she realized that the organization she had bled to build could be unmade in a single afternoon. But she had learned to dissect fear the way she dissected everything else: by turning it into data, by making plans to counter it, and by ensuring those plans were as adequate as humanly possible.</p>
<p>Whether these plans were adequate for Rex Rexilion was a question that could not be answered with logic or strategy. It could only be answered with blood.</p>
<p>She would find out.</p>
