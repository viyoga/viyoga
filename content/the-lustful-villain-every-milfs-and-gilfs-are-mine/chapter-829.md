<p>"Let her speak," Rex said.</p>
<p>The words were quiet, but they possessed a tectonic force. It was the first time he had opened his mouth since the name had been uttered, and the specific, heavy resonance of his voice acted like a gravitational pull in the room’s existing silence.</p>
<p>He had calculated the effect with surgical precision: he didn’t want the sharp, jarring cut of an interruption that would provoke more defensiveness; he wanted the low, settling quality of a landslide, something that reorients the entire landscape without having to scream for attention.</p>
<p>Every eye in the room snapped to him.</p>
<p>Rex was standing now. He hadn’t made a grand, theatrical gesture; he had simply risen during the chaotic fracturing of the council, a slow, inevitable movement like a tide coming in.</p>
<p>He placed both hands flat on the obsidian surface of the table, his palms pressing down with the patient, unyielding solidity of a man who had decided that the room’s temperature was far too volatile and intended to cool it with his own presence.</p>
<p>’They’re all bleeding,’ Rex thought, his eyes scanning the faces around the table. ’Elizabeth is bleeding pride, Morwenna is bleeding suspicion, and Valentina...’</p>
<p>’Valentina is bleeding secrets.’</p>
<p>’They’re tearing each other apart, and they don’t even realize they’re walking straight into the trap.’</p>
<p>He looked at them, his gaze steady and unnerving.</p>
<p>"We are doing exactly what they want us to do," Rex said.</p>
<p>The effect was instantaneous. The frantic, overlapping murmurs; the sharp, jagged undertones of anger; and the frantic agitation that had been building like steam in a pressure cooker since Apollo spoke the name—it all vanished.</p>
<p>It didn’t just die down; it was smothered.</p>
<p>The silence that followed wasn’t because Rex had demanded it. It was because the sheer, terrifying truth of his statement occupied the space where the noise had been.</p>
<p>The noise had no place to hide; it had nowhere to go.</p>
<p>He did not rush the silence. He let it sit, heavy and thick, allowing the realization to sink into their bones.</p>
<p>He looked at the room the way a master architect looks at a crumbling foundation, not with panic, but with the cold, analytical patience of someone giving the structure time to understand the shape of its own impending collapse.</p>
<p>"This room," he said, his voice dropping into a low, rhythmic cadence that demanded absolute focus, "was the target of last night as much as the plaza was the target of yesterday morning."</p>
<p>"Two different operations, two different strikes, both running toward the exact same objective."</p>
<p>The divination representative stared at him, her eyes narrowing. She looked like a woman who had been interrupted in the middle of a delicate ritual and was currently weighing whether Rex’s insight was worth the cost of her concentration.</p>
<p>"The Legion’s intelligence on Aethelgard has been festering and building for thirty years," Rex continued, his hands tightening slightly on the table, the skin over his knuckles turning white. "They don’t just know our borders."</p>
<p>"They know this network."</p>
<p>"They know the names of the people sitting at this table."</p>
<p>"They know how each of you thinks, how you react to pressure, and exactly where your specific, personal breaking point lies."</p>
<p>"The name Apollo delivered tonight wasn’t a piece of ’discovered’ intelligence. It wasn’t a lucky find in a dusty archive."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the tension coil tighter.</p>
<p>"It was prepared intelligence."</p>
<p>"Prepared for what purpose?" Morwenna asked, her voice a mere shadow, her eyes fixed on Rex as if she were seeing him clearly for the first time.</p>
<p>"For this," Rex said, his voice gaining a sharp, brutal edge. "For the exact conversation that has been tearing this room apart for the last fifteen minutes."</p>
<p>"For the fracturing... for the accusations... and for the desperate, frantic demand for immediate accountability before anyone in this room has even had the time to think past the first three words of the sentence Apollo delivered."</p>
<p>He leaned in, his shadow stretching across the table toward Valentina, a silent warning of the chaos to come.</p>
<p>"They didn’t just want to tell us the name," Rex whispered, the finality of his words chilling the air. "They wanted to watch us destroy ourselves with it."</p>
<p>Rex turned his gaze away from the center of the storm and locked it onto the knowledge system holder. The man was still bristling, his face flushed with the indignant heat of a man who felt his logic had been insulted.</p>
<p>"You have been in this network for twelve months," Rex said, his voice dropping into a tone of clinical, almost brutal deconstruction. "That means you have been a spectator to the history everyone else in this room has bled for."</p>
<p>"You haven’t lived through the thirty years of the wars, the sieges, or the quiet, desperate compromises that Valentina has had to make to keep this entire structure from collapsing into the dirt."</p>
<p>"You don’t have the operational record of what this network has endured under her administration."</p>
<p>"You have a surname, a sudden spike in your pulse, and a knee-jerk reaction, a reaction that is exactly the kind of predictable, emotional volatility the Legion calculated you would exhibit."</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder opened his mouth to retort, but Rex didn’t give him the space. He pivoted, his eyes shifting like a predator tracking a new scent, landing on the divination representative.</p>
<p>"And you," Rex said, his voice softening but gaining a terrifying, piercing intensity, "you have been here long enough to see the cost of her leadership."</p>
<p>"You have seen the specific, agonizing choices she has made—the ones that kept this network intact when the cold mathematics of battle suggested we should have all been slaughtered decades ago."</p>
<p>"You know the weight of the lives she has traded to buy us another day of survival."</p>
<p>"You know that record because you were there to witness the scars."</p>
<p>He stared her down, refusing to let her look away.</p>
<p>"You are not wrong to have questions," Rex conceded, the concession feeling like a heavy weight being placed on the table. "The questions you are asking are legitimate."</p>
<p>"They are vital... The problem is the order in which you are asking them..."</p>
<p>"You are demanding accountability before you have the information that makes accountability possible."</p>
<p>"You are running toward a verdict before the evidence has even been presented, and you are doing it in the exact, surgical sequence the Legion designed this delivery to produce."</p>
<p>A chill seemed to settle over the divination representative. She leaned back, her eyes searching his, her voice trembling with a mixture of skepticism and a dawning, horrific realization.</p>
<p>"How do you know they designed it?" she whispered. "How can you be so certain it isn’t just... a coincidence?"</p>
<p>"Because the information is thirty years old!" Rex’s voice finally broke its calm, ringing through the chamber with the force of a hammer striking an anvil. "Celestina Von Starlight founded the Legion thirty years ago."</p>
<p>"The connection between her and Valentina has been a festering wound for thirty years."</p>
<p>"If this were true intelligence, if it were a gift intended to strengthen us, it would have been delivered through the standard channels."</p>
<p>"It would have come with dossiers, with documentation, with context... It would have been a slow burn of discovery."</p>
<p>He leaned over the table, his shadow looming large against the obsidian.</p>
<p>"It wouldn’t have been dropped like a thermal detonator at the end of a meeting on the single worst day this network has endured in a decade."</p>
<p>"It wouldn’t have been timed to hit us when we were already bruised, bleeding, and looking for someone to blame."</p>
<p>He turned his head toward Apollo. The Apostle of Life sat motionless, his expression unreadable, but his eyes were burning with a quiet, intense focus.</p>
<p>"I am not saying you were wrong to raise it, Apollo," Rex said, his voice returning to a steady, grounding hum. "The network needed to know."</p>
<p>"But the how and the when of how it arrived here is not incidental. It is the entire point. The timing is the weapon."</p>
<p>Apollo looked at him, his gaze steady, unblinking.</p>
<p>"You are saying someone controlled the hand that fed us this intelligence," Apollo said, the implication hanging in the air like the scent of ozone before a lightning strike. "That someone decided exactly when we were most vulnerable to the truth."</p>
<p>"I am saying the intelligence was delivered in a form designed to produce this exact conversation," Rex corrected. "Whether that delivery was a masterstroke of intent or simply the inevitable result of the Legion’s operational design, the result is the same: we are being manipulated into a civil war."</p>
<p>"And if you are wrong?" the combat-adjacent representative challenged, his hand instinctively twitching toward the hilt of the blade at his hip, his body coiled for a fight that hadn’t yet begun. "If this isn’t a designed weapon?"</p>
<p>"If it’s just a piece of truth that happened to arrive when it did?"</p>
<p>Rex met his gaze with a grim, knowing smile, the smile of a man who had already seen the end of the battle.</p>
<p>"Then we answer Valentina’s questions the same way we would answer them regardless," Rex said, his voice final and absolute. "With the same process and with the same brutal accuracy."</p>
<p>"The only difference is whether we answer them because the Legion scheduled us to... or because we chose to."</p>
