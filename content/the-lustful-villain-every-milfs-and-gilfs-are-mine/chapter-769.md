<p>The dragon moved.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a mere shift in weight; it was a tectonic event. The beast lunged, its massive, clawed limbs churning the earth with a violent CRUNCH SLURP as it tore through the soil and stone.</p>
<p>Its target was the open expanse of the eastern market district, the killing field where Iris and Aurelia stood, caught in the most precarious position possible: the exposed, unshielded gap between the retreating combatants and the safety of the inner walls.</p>
<p>To the dragon’s primal, territorial mind, they weren’t people; they were obstacles in the path of its dominion.</p>
<p>Iris saw it. Her assassin-trained mind didn’t scream or panic; it processed.</p>
<p>It stripped the terror away to reveal the cold, hard geometry of death.</p>
<p>’Threat assessment... Mature scale dragon.’</p>
<p>’Velocity... Accelerating...’</p>
<p>’Approach vector... Direct...’</p>
<p>’Capability set... Insufficient...’</p>
<p>Her eyes darted, scanning for an exit, but the math was a death sentence. To the east, a sheer building face offered no entry.</p>
<p>To the south, the market street was a funnel that would lead them directly into the beast’s maw. To the north lay the rubble zone, a jagged, chaotic graveyard of shattered golem stone.</p>
<p>"Aurelia," Iris said, her voice unnervingly flat, the sound of a blade being drawn.</p>
<p>"I see it," Aurelia replied.</p>
<p>The air between them felt heavy, thick with the ozone of approaching magic and the musk of the beast.</p>
<p>"The northern rubble," Iris commanded, her eyes fixed on the jagged mounds of debris. "The golem strike zones... the heights are uneven."</p>
<p>"If we can reach the debris, its wingspan will force it to decelerate to navigate the terrain."</p>
<p>"We won’t make it," Aurelia said.</p>
<p>The words were a gut punch. Her voice lacked the frantic pitch of fear; instead, it held the hollow, devastating clarity of someone who had just finished a calculation and found it wanting.</p>
<p>"Iris, we won’t reach the rubble before it reaches us."</p>
<p>"I know," Iris whispered.</p>
<p>Without a word of command, Aurelia stepped forward. It was a primal, instinctive movement, the parent shielding the child, the protector stepping into the path of the storm.</p>
<p>She braced herself, her hands trembling slightly as she prepared to face a god of scale and fire with nothing but her own flesh.</p>
<p>It wouldn’t have mattered. The dragon was a landslide of muscle and malice, and they were merely pebbles in its path.</p>
<p>WHHHH OOM!</p>
<p>Then, the world blurred.</p>
<p>A shadow tore through the air, faster than a human eye could track.</p>
<p>SHHH WIP!</p>
<p>Rex materialized.</p>
<p>He didn’t arrive as the Avatar, a shimmering god of light and power. He didn’t arrive as a stone construct of the Tremor form.</p>
<p>But he arrived as Rex Rexilion. He stood between the dragon and the women, his feet planted in a wide, heavy base stance that spoke of centuries of martial discipline.</p>
<p>His academy student clothes were wrinkled and stained with the dust of the morning’s battles, and his fists hummed with a low, menacing vibration.</p>
<p>Iris stared, her breath hitching in her throat, her eyes locked onto the back of his jacket.</p>
<p>It was a surreal, terrifying sight. There was no divine armor.</p>
<p>There was no impenetrable shield of energy. There was only the unremarkable, creased fabric of a second-year student’s jacket, a piece of clothing that had seen a long, exhausting morning of training and combat.</p>
<p>And that jacket, that thin, mundane layer of cloth, was the only thing standing between a mountain of prehistoric fury and the lives of everyone behind him.</p>
<p>Aurelia let out a sound, a strangled, half-formed sob that died in her throat, a sound of pure, helpless realization.</p>
<p>The dragon hit him.</p>
<p>KRA BOOOOOOOOM!</p>
<p>The impact was a symphony of violence. The sound was a deafening, bone-shaking explosion of kinetic energy, the sound of a mountain colliding with a wall.</p>
<p>Dust erupted in a massive, choking cloud, and the ground beneath Rex’s feet shattered into a spiderweb of deep, jagged cracks.</p>
<p>CRACK! THUD!</p>
<p>The air itself seemed to scream as the pressure wave rippled outward, flattening the nearby debris.</p>
<p>But as the dust began to swirl and settle, the expected sound of breaking bone, of a body being pulverized into red mist, never came.</p>
<p>Rex did not move.</p>
<p>The dragon was a force of nature, a living avalanche that had spent the last fifteen meters of its approach building a terrifying, kinetic momentum.</p>
<p>It hit the man standing in front of Iris and Aurelia with the catastrophic, full-body mass of a creature that weighed far more than two hundred kilograms of stone-armored muscle and the absolute, unyielding geological anchor of the Earthen Authority combined.</p>
<p>The sound was a sickening, deafening THOOOOM!</p>
<p>The sound of a world being struck by a hammer.</p>
<p>Rex still did not move.</p>
<p>The dragon’s charge didn’t just stop; it arrested. It slammed into his extended arms with a violent, jarring deceleration that shook the very bedrock.</p>
<p>His hands, glowing with a dull, subterranean heat, pressed deep into the dragon’s chest scales, the metal groaning under the pressure CREEEEEEAK as the two forces fought for dominance.</p>
<p>It was the impossible friction between something that was meant to move and something that was fundamentally, cosmically immovable.</p>
<p>The dragon, driven by a primal, confused rage, leaned into it. It dug its massive talons into the dirt, its hind legs straining, muscles bulging and rippling beneath its hide as it tried to shove the obstacle aside.</p>
<p>SKREEEEE!</p>
<p>The sound of its claws tearing through the earth was like a thousand knives scraping against stone.</p>
<p>Rex pushed back.</p>
<p>The mechanics of the Earthen Authority’s geological anchor were absolute. By defining his position relative to the island’s very substrate, Rex had ceased to be a man and had become a fixed point in the universe.</p>
<p>A fixed point under geological-scale force does not budge. The dragon’s charge, while immense and terrifying to any mortal, was still merely large animal force, a significant, violent weight, but one that fell short of the tectonic power Rex was currently channeling.</p>
<p>He held.</p>
<p>Behind him, the air was thick with the suffocating tension of a moment stretched to its breaking point. Aurelia was still making that sound, that hollow, strangled, nonverbal cry.</p>
<p>It hadn’t transitioned into a scream or a word because the reality of the situation was still too surreal to be processed by the human mind. It was the sound of a soul caught in the throat.</p>
<p>Iris said nothing. She was a statue of focused, terrifying intensity.</p>
<p>Her eyes weren’t on the dragon’s head but on its legs. She watched the way the beast’s massive hindquarters were forced outward, the lateral spread of its body as the forward momentum had nowhere to go.</p>
<p>The dragon’s legs were trembling, the muscles twitching with the effort of a creature trying to push a mountain.</p>
<p>THUD. THUD. THUD.</p>
<p>The dragon’s feet pounded the earth in a desperate, rhythmic attempt to regain its footing, but the ground simply gave way beneath it, unable to provide the leverage needed to overcome the man.</p>
<p>For three agonizing seconds, the dragon pushed with everything it had, the full, committed, murderous pressure of a predator that had decided this was its path and would destroy anything in its way. And for all three of those seconds, Rex Rexilion held the line.</p>
<p>Then, the energy shifted.</p>
<p>The dragon’s frantic, violent pushing suddenly faltered. The rage in its eyes flickered, replaced by a sudden, jarring confusion.</p>
<p>The bond disruption’s recovery was finally taking hold, and the beast’s instinctual, predatory brain was beginning to reboot. It was the look of an animal encountering a glitch in reality, a predator finding something it could not process because its entire biological programming assumed that anything in its path would eventually yield, break, or bleed.</p>
<p>The dragon had expected a collision. It had expected the sound of crunching ribs and the spray of hot blood.</p>
<p>Instead, it found a wall.</p>
<p>Rex slowly lifted his head. His eyes, burning with a quiet, subterranean intensity, locked onto the beast’s massive, reptilian eye.</p>
<p>The dragon froze, its breath coming in hot, sulfurous puffs of steam. HAAAAH... HAAAAH... as it stared back at the boy who had just defied its very existence.</p>
<p>Rex looked at it.</p>
<p>The dragon looked at him.</p>
