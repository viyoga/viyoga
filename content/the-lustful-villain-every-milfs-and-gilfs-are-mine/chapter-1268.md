<p>Outside, the morning sun rose like a bleeding wound over the rooftops of Aethelgard. The city was transformed.</p>
<p>Where once the proud azure and silver of the old regime had flown, now hung heavy banners of deep purple and obsidian black, the colors of Rex’s new reign.</p>
<p>Below, in the cobblestone arteries of the city, patrols of demons moved with unsettling silence, their armored forms catching the light as they stalked the streets.</p>
<p>The city looked peaceful, a hollow, synthetic calm, as if the entire population had been collectively commanded to hold their breath.</p>
<p>No one in the streets, no one in the taverns, no one in the markets knew that this fragile serenity was a lie. The clock was ticking.</p>
<p>This was the last day of the world they knew.</p>
<p>By late afternoon, the Starlight household, a place that had been a fortress of aristocratic dignity, felt unnervingly alive. The reason for this sudden pulse of energy was simple: Rex had gutted it.</p>
<p>He had done it with the terrifying efficiency of a predator. There was no shouting, no grand displays of cruelty, just a few quiet words delivered in a tone so reasonable, so utterly calm, that it felt like inevitability itself.</p>
<p>The servants, the cooks, the gardeners, and the seasoned guards who had worn the Starlight colors for decades had all been given the same directive: the family required absolute privacy to recover from the trauma of the war. The household would function alone.</p>
<p>Nobody fought him. Nobody dared.</p>
<p>Every soul in the building had spent the last week whispering about the monster who had stood beside Apollo until the very end, the man who had rewritten the fate of Kaeleth.</p>
<p>To oppose Rex was to oppose destiny itself. They left with small bags and bowed low, their eyes averted, scurrying away like rats fleeing a sinking ship.</p>
<p>By the time the sun began its slow, orange slide toward the horizon, the great house was silent. But it wasn’t the silence of emptiness; it was the silence of a trap being set.</p>
<p>’Let them leave,’ Rex thought as he watched the last carriage disappear beyond the gates. ’The more witnesses there are, the more chances there are for a mistake.’</p>
<p>’Tonight, there will be no witnesses... Only victims.’</p>
<p>Rex spent the next two hours transforming the manor from a home into a stage.</p>
<p>He performed the labor himself. He found a perverse satisfaction in it, the physical act of rearranging the space where he would soon break them.</p>
<p>He carried heavy oak chairs with effortless strength, moving the long dining table closer to the tall windows so that the dying amber light of the day would spill across its surface like honey. His muscles coiled and relaxed with each movement, a predator preparing his den.</p>
<p>Then, he descended into the family cellar. The air down there was thick with dust and the smell of centuries-old earth.</p>
<p>With a single lantern casting dancing, monstrous shadows against the stone walls, he prowled through the racks. He wasn’t looking for sustenance; he was looking for ritual.</p>
<p>Deep in the back, hidden behind cobwebs that clung like funeral shrouds, he found it: a dusty rack containing a vintage lamp laid down before Elizabeth was even born.</p>
<p>He retrieved the bottle, wiped the grime away with his sleeve with the tenderness of a man handling a sacred relic, and carried it upstairs like a trophy of war.</p>
<p>In the kitchen, he worked with a detached, culinary precision. He prepared a meal that was deceptively simple: a roast rubbed with bitter herbs and left to slow cook until the meat was tender enough to fall apart at a touch; vegetables glazed in honey and salt; and fresh bread still warm from a baker who had been too terrified to ask for payment.</p>
<p>The aroma of savory meat and roasting herbs began to permeate the lower floors, filling the house with an illusion of domestic comfort. It was a sensory deception designed to lower their guard.</p>
<p>Finally, he moved to the dining room. The shadows were lengthening now, stretching across the floor like grasping fingers.</p>
<p>One by one, he began lighting candles. The flame flickered, casting amber light across the polished wood and the silver cutlery.</p>
<p>The glow reflected off the crystal glasses, creating tiny prismatic stars that danced on the walls.</p>
<p>It looked beautiful. It looked inviting.</p>
<p>It looked like a home where a family might gather to share stories and laughter.</p>
<p>Rex stood back, surveying his work. The table was set and the wine was breathing.</p>
<p>The fire was warm. The trap was laid, the bait was sweet, and the hunters were less than twenty-four hours away from walking directly into the jaws of the beast.</p>
<p>By the time Rex finished, the dining room had been transformed into something sacred and sinister. Dozens of candles burned in unison tall silver pillars lining the center of the table, brass sconces casting warm halos against the walls, and tiny tea lights scattered across the windowsills like fallen stars.</p>
<p>As the last bruised purple of twilight bled from the sky, the flames took possession of the room, bathing everything in a rich, honeyed amber glow.</p>
<p>The polished wood of the table gleamed like dark water; the silverware caught the firelight, flashing like warning signals; and the shadows in the corners softened, swaying gently as if the house itself were breathing.</p>
<p>The entire space felt like the inside of a lantern, prepared specifically to lure its guests into its warm, deadly light.</p>
<p>Rex stood at the head of the table, his broad shoulders casting a massive shadow across the empty chairs. He stared at the tableau he had constructed, his mind working with the cold, relentless precision of a chess master.</p>
<p>He didn’t move until he had run through his calculations, because Rex never acted on impulse. He acted on purpose.</p>
<p>And for tonight, there were three distinct reasons for this performance.</p>
<p>’First,’ he thought, his gaze lingering on the empty seat where Lily would sit, ’there is the debt.’</p>
<p>His mind flashed back to the Starlight steps weeks ago, the smell of jasmine, and the weight of Lily’s smaller hand in his left and Diana’s in his right. He remembered the way their eyes had shone with a desperate kind of hope when he made the promise: a full, uninterrupted night after the war.</p>
<p>No missions, no duties, no interruptions—just the three of them, surrendered to each other.</p>
<p>At the time, it had been a tactical concession, a calculated lie to buy their loyalty and calm their nerves. He had expected to pay that debt in months, perhaps years. But the war had ended with terrifying speed, and the debt had come due.</p>
<p>Rex found he didn’t mind paying it. Honoring his word to the women who had already given him their souls served a dual purpose: it kept the foundation of his new empire solid while the world above it crumbled.</p>
<p>’Second,’ his eyes shifted to the seat beside Valentina’s, ’there is Elizabeth.’</p>
<p>Elizabeth was the anomaly. She was the only one in this entire household who had seen behind the mask, who had looked at the raw, unvarnished monster beneath the hero and chosen not to recoil.</p>
<p>That kind of loyalty was a rare currency, but it was dangerously fragile. It was a choice she had to remake with every sunrise.</p>
<p>Rex knew that a single night of true inclusion, a night where she wasn’t the observer but a participant who sat at the same table as her mother and sisters, sharing the same wine and the same secrets, would bind her to him more tightly than any oath of blood.</p>
<p>He needed her inside the circle, not standing in the doorway watching with a conscience that might one day betray them both.</p>
<p>’And third,’ his jaw tightened as his thoughts turned to the approaching storm, ’there is Valentina.’</p>
<p>This was the most practical reason of all, the one that required the most ruthless calculation. Helena was arriving tomorrow. When she crossed that threshold, she wouldn’t just be walking into a house; she would be walking into a fait accompli.</p>
<p>She would see her mother, her sisters, and her daughters all gathered in one place, under one roof, surrendered to the same man. A scene like that could not be explained away with excuses or lies. It could only be absorbed.</p>
<p>’If I want Helena to accept this new reality without breaking everything to pieces,’ Rex reasoned, ’I must ensure there are no cracks for her to exploit.’</p>
<p>If Valentina’s pride wavered, if Elizabeth’s conscience flickered, or if Lily’s honesty cracked at the wrong moment, Helena would sense the weakness immediately. She would find the gap in their united front and pull until the entire structure collapsed.</p>
<p>He needed them all inside the walls tonight, bound together by the same warmth, the same wine, and the same complicity. He needed to forge them into a single, unbreakable unit before the matriarch could arrive to challenge them.</p>
<p>Tonight, they would become a family.</p>
<p>And tomorrow, they would become his fortress.</p>
