<p>Behind the cold obsidian of his mask, Rex allowed himself a flicker of dark amusement. ’Technically true, utterly infuriating, and perfectly ambiguous.’</p>
<p>It was a masterpiece of linguistic manipulation. He watched the immediate fallout: the way the Aethelgard soldiers’ grip tightened on their spears, the way their eyes darted with newfound suspicion.</p>
<p>He had given them exactly what they needed to fuel their righteous fury: a confirmation of guilt wrapped in a shroud of plausible deniability.</p>
<p>But then, the fragile equilibrium of the standoff shattered.</p>
<p>Mordecai Vrael chose that exact moment to break.</p>
<p>He stepped forward, stumbling half a pace past the protective flanks of Cassandra and Gorvasha.</p>
<p>His movement was clumsy, lacking the lethal grace of the others, and his voice when it finally came was pitched higher than Rex’s, cracking with a genuine, desperate distress that cut through the oppressive silence.</p>
<p>"That isn’t... that isn’t a fair characterization!" Mordecai cried out, his hands lifting in a gesture of helpless protest. "I am the Demon Lord of this territory!"</p>
<p>"I never ordered an attack called Tremor!"</p>
<p>"I never sent the Undead Bringer to slaughter your people!" His chest heaved, his breathing shallow and audible even to the front ranks. "I heard those names the same way you did!"</p>
<p>"I read the reports with horror, just as you did!"</p>
<p>"Whatever those monsters were, whatever carnage they wrought... they did not come from my throne!"</p>
<p>The declaration landed like a grenade in the center of the corridor. An immediate, visible ripple of confusion surged through both armies. On the Aethelgard side, the rigid formation wavered as soldiers exchanged uncertain glances.</p>
<p>Apollo Brightsoul didn’t move, but his entire posture shifted; his brow drew together in a hard furrow, one eyebrow lifting as his mind raced to process the contradiction. The Demon Lord was denying everything, while the man supposedly ruling him was frantically claiming innocence.</p>
<p>’Is this a civil war?’ Apollo wondered, his inner voice sharp with calculation. ’Or is he lying? Is this a performance to sow doubt?’</p>
<p>Behind his mask, Rex savored the exquisite irony. He watched Mordecai deliver, with complete and terrifying sincerity, the single most valuable piece of misdirection he had ever orchestrated.</p>
<p>The idiot genuinely believed he was defending his honor. He had no conception that his bewildered honesty was the final brushstroke needed to complete the lie.</p>
<p>By appearing out of the loop, Mordecai made the Underlayer look exactly as Rex intended: a fractured, chaotic ecosystem where independent monsters could emerge without central command.</p>
<p>A frightened, confused scapegoat who believed in his own innocence was worth more than ten thousand trained liars.</p>
<p>Valentina, however, was not so easily swayed by the theatrics of a panicked demon. Her instincts, honed by years of hunting shadows, screamed that something was wrong.</p>
<p>She caught the discrepancy immediately—the gap between Xerollion’s cold evasion and Mordecai’s frantic denial—and she latched onto it like a predator.</p>
<p>Her gaze shifted away from Apollo, her eyes narrowing into slits of icy accusation as she fixed them on Rex. The air around her seemed to drop several degrees as her magical aura began to prickle.</p>
<p>"Interesting," she said, her voice dropping to a dangerous, razor-edged whisper that carried a lethal weight. "Your Demon Lord denies the very atrocities you refuse to disavow."</p>
<p>She took a step forward, her hand closing around the hilt of her blade with enough force to whiten her knuckles. "So let’s cut through the theater, Lord Xerollion."</p>
<p>"Which is it? Do you hold absolute authority over every drop of blood spilled in this realm, or is there something else moving in these tunnels—something so monstrous that even you cannot leash it?"</p>
<p>The masked head tilted just a fraction, a slow, deliberate motion that felt more like the assessment of a specimen than the reply of a man.</p>
<p>When the answer finally came, it carried the faintest, most dangerous edge of amusement—the sound of someone who had already won a game their opponent hadn’t even realized they were playing.</p>
<p>"True authority is never singular, Headmaster," Xerollion said, the title tasting like poison on his tongue. "Anyone who has truly wielded power knows that."</p>
<p>"A crown does not control every shadow it casts."</p>
<p>He took a single step forward, the obsidian plating of his armor catching the sickly blue light, making him look like a vengeful god carved from the abyss itself. "There are powers moving in this realm with purposes of their own."</p>
<p>"Some align with my interests; some are indifferent; and some would gladly see me buried alongside every last one of your soldiers if they believed the opportunity presented itself."</p>
<p>"But you are missing the point."</p>
<p>His voice dropped lower, becoming something intimate and terrifying. "What matters for you, Valentina, is not identifying which hand held which knife."</p>
<p>"What matters is that you have marched several thousand armed souls through a dimensional gate into a corridor where no amount of moral certainty or bureaucratic investigation will protect a single one of them."</p>
<p>"You have gone too far to turn this into an inquiry..."</p>
<p>"You have crossed the threshold."</p>
<p>"But now, you simply have to survive it."</p>
<p>The threat hung in the air like the smell of ozone before a lightning strike. The tension between the two armies was so visceral it felt as though the stone walls themselves might crack under the pressure.</p>
<p>Cassandra chose that moment to shatter the standoff. Her voice cut across the corridor with the sharp, contemptuous clarity that had once commanded entire divisions and sent lesser men fleeing in terror.</p>
<p>"It’s remarkable, honestly," she spat, her eyes burning with centuries of accumulated resentment. "Eight hundred years of Aethelgard treating this place as beneath their notice."</p>
<p>"Eight hundred years of dumping their exiles, their criminals, and their inconvenient problems down these shafts without so much as a second thought."</p>
<p>"And now that the consequences have finally crawled up to your doorstep, you arrive with your polished banners and your sanctimonious speeches about justice."</p>
<p>She took a step forward, her weapon humming with restrained energy. "Where was your justice when the first Underlayer settlements starved because your city sealed the trade shafts to ’purify’ the district?"</p>
<p>"Where was your justice when you cut off our oxygen to suffocate rebellion before it could even breathe?"</p>
<p>"Where was your justice," Gorvasha added, her voice a low, guttural growl that seemed to vibrate up from the very bedrock of the corridor, "when you sent your pathetic hunting parties down here to slaughter demons for sport and called it ’Apostle training’?"</p>
<p>The massive demoness loomed like a mountain of muscle and scarred flesh, her presence alone enough to make the front ranks of Aethelgard visibly recoil. "You march into a corridor you do not understand, in a realm whose geography you have never mapped, and you have the audacity to call it courage."</p>
<p>"I call it walking into a den because you thought the door was left open."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was different than before. It wasn’t the silence of fear; it was the silence of a fuse burning down to the powder keg.</p>
<p>The Aethelgard soldiers began to shift their weight, shields rising, spear tips lowering. The Underlayer warriors behind Xerollion didn’t move, but their energy shifted, a coiled spring of violence waiting for the first drop of blood to hit the floor.</p>
