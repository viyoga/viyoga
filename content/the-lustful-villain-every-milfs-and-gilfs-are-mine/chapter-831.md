<p>"I was trying to understand her," Valentina countered, her eyes snapping up, flashing with a momentary, defensive fire. "I was trying to understand what she was doing, why she was doing it, and why the Legion was built upon the foundation of a secret my own blood had discarded."</p>
<p>"Those are related objectives, Apollo, but they are not identical."</p>
<p>"One is a search for a sister; the other is a search for the truth of our existence."</p>
<p>"And the network?" Morwenna’s voice cut through the tension, sharp and jagged as a broken blade. "You held this truth in your hands, and you did not bring it to us."</p>
<p>"You kept us in the dark while we fought in the light."</p>
<p>"Because I did not have enough to bring!" Valentina’s voice finally broke, a sudden, violent crack like a whip that made several council members flinch.</p>
<p>The sudden outburst was a release of years of suppressed tension, a momentary lapse in the commander’s iron mask. "What I had were fraying threads, Morwenna!"</p>
<p>"Frayed, inconclusive threads that pointed toward a conclusion so massive, so potentially catastrophic, that to speak it without certainty would have been an act of madness."</p>
<p>"If I had brought incomplete intelligence about the Legion’s founding to this room, this council would have torn itself apart before the first piece of evidence was even laid bare."</p>
<p>"You would have been fighting ghosts before you even knew they were real."</p>
<p>She leaned forward, her shadow stretching long and imposing across the obsidian table, her gaze sweeping over every face in the room, searching for judgment, for understanding, for anything at all.</p>
<p>"I was wrong to hold it as long as I held it," she said.</p>
<p>The words didn’t sound like a politician performing a calculated act of accountability. They didn’t sound like a leader trying to mitigate damage.</p>
<p>They sounded like the flat, honest acknowledgment of a woman who had done the grim arithmetic of her own life and found that the sum was a deficit. It was the sound of a soul admitting its own weight.</p>
<p>"The specific morning we buried our dead in today’s ceremony..." Valentina’s voice wavered, just for a fraction of a second, before hardening into steel. "That is a cost. A debt of silence."</p>
<p>"It is a weight I am going to carry for every remaining second that I sit at the head of this table."</p>
<p>"I am not standing here asking you to absolve me. I am not asking for your forgiveness."</p>
<p>"I am telling you what I knew, when the knowledge began to bleed through, and what a leader chooses to do when the truth threatens the very stability of the people she protects."</p>
<p>The room fell into a profound, heavy quiet.</p>
<p>Morwenna stared at her for a long, agonizing moment. In her eyes, the forty years of shared blood, shared wars, and shared victories played out like a fever dream.</p>
<p>The accusation had died, replaced by something far more complex and far more painful: the realization that even after four decades, the person standing before her was still, in some fundamental way, a mystery. She was navigating the treacherous, uncharted territory of realizing she had been mistaken about the very essence of her closest ally.</p>
<p>"What do we do now?" the divination representative asked, her voice small, almost lost in the vastness of the tension.</p>
<p>The question was a plea for direction, a desperate need to find a foothold in a world that had just shifted on its axis.</p>
<p>Rex finally moved, watching the room with the detached, predatory intensity of a man observing a tectonic shift.</p>
<p>He didn’t look at the maps, the dossiers, or the empty seats. He looked at the faces of the people who were now standing on the precipice of a civil war.</p>
<p>"Now," Rex said, his voice low, resonant, and chilling.y certain, "we find the person who can actually answer that question."</p>
<p>The council turned toward Rex with a singular, jarring motion. It was the collective reorientation of a predator pack that had suddenly noticed a new, much larger threat emerging from the tall grass.</p>
<p>The conversation hadn’t just changed direction; the entire landscape of their reality had been violently upended, and Rex was the only one standing with his feet planted firmly on the new ground.</p>
<p>Rex did not rush to fill the void. He was a man who understood the utility of a vacuum.</p>
<p>He stood there, letting the silence act as a whetstone, sharpening the tension until it was thin enough to cut. He let them sit in the wreckage of the last few minutes: the stinging salt of the accusations, the suffocating weight of Valentina’s confession, the jagged edges of Morwenna’s betrayal, and the hollow ache of Apollo’s quiet wisdom.</p>
<p>He forced them to feel the exact shape of the chaos they were currently drowning in before he gave it a name.</p>
<p>"Aethelgard was attacked yesterday," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice was a low, rhythmic tolling of a funeral bell, cutting through the emotional haze like a cold wind through a fever dream. "But you are all looking at the scars on the walls and the blood in the streets."</p>
<p>"You are looking at the physical destruction."</p>
<p>"You are treating the symptoms while the infection is already in your veins."</p>
<p>He leaned forward, his eyes scanning the table, locking onto each member with a gaze that felt like a physical interrogation.</p>
<p>"The physical assault was merely the visible portion of a much more surgical operation," Rex continued, his tone hardening into something clinical and brutal. "The actual attack was designed to do exactly what is happening in this room right now."</p>
<p>A murmur of confusion rippled through the council, but Rex silenced it with a single, sharp glance.</p>
<p>"A geological apostle, an entity we have zero clean intelligence on, manifests in the heart of our city and systematically deconstructs half of our operational capacity," Rex said, his voice rising in intensity. "And as if that weren’t enough of a hammer blow, two Legion operatives descend simultaneously on a dragon, specifically designed to finish the work the apostle started."</p>
<p>"Both attacks failed..."</p>
<p>"Both attacks were repelled at a staggering, bloody cost to our people. And yet..."</p>
<p>He paused, his eyes landing on Valentina, then sweeping back to the rest of the room.</p>
<p>"And yet, here we are."</p>
<p>"Sitting in this room, breathing this heavy air, and doing the exact thing those attacks were engineered to provoke."</p>
<p>"Which is?" the divination representative whispered, her voice trembling as the realization began to dawn on her, a cold dread creeping up her spine like frost.</p>
<p>"Turning on each other," Rex said, the words landing like a heavy, iron slab. "Specifically, turning on the person at the head of this table."</p>
<p>"The person whose leadership is the singular, unbreakable cord that has held this entire network together while the Legion has been tearing at our throat for years."</p>
<p>He let that sink in. He let them see the trap.</p>
<p>"The Legion knows they don’t need to burn every building in Aethelgard to win," Rex said, his voice dropping to a lethal, predatory whisper. "They don’t need to kill every soldier."</p>
<p>"Because if they can break the trust between the commander and the council... if they can turn the leadership into a target of suspicion... then the network doesn’t need an external enemy to destroy it."</p>
<p>"It will simply collapse under its own weight, tearing itself apart from the inside out until there is nothing left but the dust of what we used to be."</p>
