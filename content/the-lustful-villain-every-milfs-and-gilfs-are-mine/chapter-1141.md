<p>Apollo stood frozen at the tactical overlook, his knuckles white as he gripped the hilt of his sword. Forty meters away, the southern shelf’s brilliant flanking maneuver hadn’t just failed; it had been systematically dismantled.</p>
<p>He watched through the haze of dust and magical discharge as the last of his hopeful soldiers were cut down in that godforsaken stone bowl.</p>
<p>A cold, oily suspicion slithered up his spine, settling in his gut like lead.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a battle.</p>
<p>This was a harvest.</p>
<p>’Every move I make... every tactical shift... they’re already there waiting.’</p>
<p>’It’s like they’ve seen this dance a thousand times before.’</p>
<p>"They knew," Apollo whispered, the words lost to the wind until he forced them out. "Gods above, they knew."</p>
<p>"Knew what, Apollo? Speak up!" Elliot roared as he charged past, his divine radiance flickering like a dying candle.</p>
<p>Blood ran freely from a jagged gash above his eyebrow, streaming down his face in crimson rivulets that stained his golden armor. He swung his blade in a desperate arc, severing a demon’s head with a wet squelch, but his eyes remained fixed on the chaos.</p>
<p>"Why aren’t you commanding?! The line is buckling!"</p>
<p>Apollo turned to him, his face pale beneath the grime. "They knew we were coming through that Key, Elliot!"</p>
<p>"Every angle, every choke point, every contingency—it was all laid out for us!"</p>
<p>"This entire corridor wasn’t built to defend the Underlayer! It was built to receive us!"</p>
<p>The grin that had sustained Elliot through three hours of carnage faltered. For the first time since the first demon drew blood, the light in his eyes dimmed, replaced by a hollow, gnawing dread.</p>
<p>"That’s impossible... We had intelligence... we had months of planning—"</p>
<p>"Intelligence we provided?!" Apollo snapped, though his voice lacked conviction.</p>
<p>A blur of motion announced Evelyn’s arrival. She appeared at Apollo’s shoulder with supernatural speed, her breathing barely elevated despite having sprinted across half a battlefield.</p>
<p>Her armor was splattered with black ichor and arterial spray.</p>
<p>"The eastern side is compromised too," she said, her voice clipped and urgent. "Every time I attempt a pincer, they have a secondary formation waiting in the shadows."</p>
<p>"They aren’t reacting to our movements, Apollo... They’re executing a script."</p>
<p>She grabbed his pauldron, forcing him to meet her gaze. "I’ve been in six major engagements against fortified positions."</p>
<p>"None of them looked like this... This isn’t defense; this is a trap that has been sprung from the moment we stepped through the portal."</p>
<p>Apollo looked back at the slaughter.</p>
<p>"Then we push harder," he said, though the words felt brittle, like ice cracking underfoot. "We overwhelm their preparations!"</p>
<p>"We break their plan by sheer force of will!"</p>
<p>But even as he said it, his mind screamed at him that they were merely feeding more meat into the grinder.</p>
<p>Behind them, cutting through the center of the formation like a scalpel through silk, Rex Rexilion moved with terrifying, measured competence. He was the eye of the storm.</p>
<p>A demon lunged at him, claws extended to rake his throat; without breaking stride, Rex pivoted, his blade finding the demon’s sternum with a sickening crunch of vertebrae.</p>
<p>He didn’t waste movement. And he didn’t waste breath.</p>
<p>Thwack. Slice. Step.</p>
<p>"Fall back to the secondary line!" Rex commanded, his voice calm, almost conversational amidst the screaming and the grinding of steel.</p>
<p>He grabbed two wounded soldiers by their harnesses, hauling them clear of a collapsing stone archway just as it shattered into lethal debris.</p>
<p>"Captain, flank left now!" he barked at a Nightwing officer, saving the man’s life seconds before a demon’s mace would have turned his skull into paste.</p>
<p>He was exactly where a legendary hero should be. He was the anchor, the reliable ally, the symbol of hope. He moved with a practiced grace that made the impossible look routine.</p>
<p>’But why does he look so bored?’ Apollo thought bitterly, watching Rex’s back. ’He’s fighting a war for survival, and he’s moving like he’s practicing a routine in a training hall.’</p>
<p>’His blade finds the heart, his footwork is flawless, but there’s no fire.’</p>
<p>’No desperation. Just... execution.’</p>
<p>Rex Rexilion continued his perfect work, a flawless soldier in a doomed army, while around him, the screams of Aethelgard rose higher, each one a note in a symphony of slaughter that he seemed to have written himself.</p>
<p>On the opposite side of the corridor, where the air tasted of copper and burnt ozone, the Underlayer unleashed its true answer.</p>
<p>Cassandra Vexmoor had been a patient predator. For eight agonizing minutes, she had stood like a statue of marble amidst the carnage, watching Aethelgard’s formations ripple and break, letting her standard soldiers absorb the initial shockwave of the invasion.</p>
<p>She was reading them not their numbers, but their souls. She was cataloging their desperation, their tactical weaknesses, and the exact rhythm of their slaughter.</p>
<p>Then, she moved.</p>
<p>The sudden shift in her posture made the nearest defenders flinch instinctively. With a fluid, almost ritualistic motion, she drew her blade across her own forearm.</p>
<p>But the blood that welled from the wound did not drip. It defied gravity, suspended in the air like liquid rubies.</p>
<p>It spread outward in a dark, pulsating corona that seemed to drink the very light from the corridor.</p>
<p>"Let the debt be paid in crimson," she hissed, her voice distorting into something ancient and terrifying.</p>
<p>The transformation didn’t just change her appearance; it rewrote reality around her. The Blood Oath Demon Queen erupted from the shell of the woman.</p>
<p>Her claws elongated into obsidian talons, and her blade ignited with a malevolent crimson energy that left searing afterimages hanging in the air like ghostly scars.</p>
<p>She moved with a speed that shattered the laws of physics. She didn’t run; she blurred.</p>
<p>Her first pass through Aethelgard’s left flank was less of a charge and more of a harvesting.</p>
<p>Squelch. Crack. Tear.</p>
<p>Eleven soldiers were decapitated or disemboweled in a single heartbeat. By her second pass, nine more were reduced to screaming heaps of severed limbs and shattered armor.</p>
<p>"Bind her! For the Light, bind her now!" an Apostle-ranked mage screamed, his hands weaving intricate geometric patterns.</p>
<p>A lattice of golden energy erupted toward Cassandra, designed to freeze a god in place.</p>
<p>Cassandra didn’t even slow down.</p>
<p>"Pathetic," she spat.</p>
<p>The blood energy bleeding from her skin dissolved the binding spell on contact, eating through the magical structure like acid through parchment. Before the mage could even register his failure, she closed the distance.</p>
<p>With a vicious backhanded strike, she caught him mid-incantation.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of his ribs folding over her forearm was sickeningly wet. She didn’t stop there; she hurled his broken body into three of his own soldiers, using the dying man as a fleshy projectile to shatter their formation.</p>
<p>Amidst the wet sounds of evisceration, Cassandra’s crimson eyes flickered toward the center of the corridor. Through the haze of blood and magical discharge, she saw him, the masked figure, standing motionless, an island of eerie calm in a sea of screaming death.</p>
<p>’Is this what it means to be truly dangerous?’ she wondered, a flash of something complicated pride mixed with something darker stirring behind her eyes. ’To stand in the center of a hurricane you created and not even blink?’</p>
<p>She remembered the day she had knelt to him. She, the Demon Queen who had once brought entire military structures to their knees, had submitted to a mortal’s authority.</p>
<p>There had been nights of bitter reflection, wondering if she had traded her sovereignty for a gilded cage, if her choice had been a surrender dressed up as strategy. But watching him now, watching him orchestrate this genocide from the center of a grinder he had built with flawless precision, she knew.</p>
<p>It hadn’t been weakness. It had been the single sharpest strategic decision of her entire existence.</p>
<p>"Die for nothing," she growled, turning back to the slaughter and cleaving through four more soldiers before the thought could even fully form.</p>
<p>Not far from her, Gorvasha Bloodtusk was conducting her own symphony of destruction. If Cassandra was a scalpel, Gorvasha was a sledgehammer. She fought with a primordial brutality that made even the most battle-hardened Aethelgard veterans freeze in terror.</p>
<p>Her warhammer didn’t just hit shields; it obliterated them, sending splinters of wood and fragments of bone flying like shrapnel.</p>
<p>BOOM. CRUNCH. SHATTER.</p>
<p>Every strike was calculated. Gorvasha wasn’t just killing; she was engineering. She would deliberately drive a knight backward, using the momentum of her hammer to slam him into specific sections of the corridor wall.</p>
<p>She knew exactly which stones were weakened, which supports were frayed, and which sections would collapse inward under a specific amount of force. She was fighting a battle and reshaping the battlefield simultaneously.</p>
<p>"Third rank, fall back six meters! "Gorvasha," she bellowed, her voice cutting through the cacophony of screams like a war horn. "Give them the gap!"</p>
<p>"Let them think they have won!"</p>
<p>"The stone will do the rest!"</p>
<p>As the Aethelgard soldiers rushed into the perceived opening, eager for a kill, Gorvasha slammed her hammer into the ground. The impact sent a shockwave through the floor that coordinated perfectly with the structural weaknesses she had spent weeks cultivating.</p>
<p>The corridor began to groan, the ancient stone weeping dust as it prepared to swallow the invaders whole.</p>
