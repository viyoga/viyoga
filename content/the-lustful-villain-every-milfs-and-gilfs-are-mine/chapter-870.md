<p>"She knew exactly what the fuck I was building!" Rex’s voice didn’t just rise; it tore through the air, a jagged, violent sound that seemed to physically bruise the silence.</p>
<p>He began to pace, his movements predatory and restless, like a caged beast sensing the bars of its cell. "She wasn’t just playing politics!"</p>
<p>"She wasn’t just ’managing’ things!"</p>
<p>"She knew the Underlayer’s reconstruction was the single most significant strategic goddamn development on this entire island in a generation!"</p>
<p>"She knew we were finally gaining real, tangible ground!"</p>
<p>He stopped, spinning on his heel to face Lustia, his eyes burning with a terrifying, incandescent light. "And she went and contracted with the God of Time to produce a reset that would wipe it all out!"</p>
<p>"She was going to hit the fucking ’undo’ button on our lives just to keep her own goddamn hands clean!"</p>
<p>"She was very close, Rex," Lustia said, her voice a calm, chilling contrast to his volcanic fury.</p>
<p>She didn’t flinch as his aura lashed out, a psychic whip of pure resentment. "The Ignivara operation, the geological attack, the combined pressure of the Underlayer’s war declaration and the Legion’s surface operation... you saw them as separate fronts in a war."</p>
<p>"But they weren’t... They were the ingredients..."</p>
<p>"They were the specific, calculated combination designed to produce the exact emotional and strategic crisis state that the reset condition required."</p>
<p>Rex felt a cold sweat break across his brow. His mind was a whirlwind of fury and sudden, sickening clarity. "What was the fucking reset condition, Lustia?!"</p>
<p>"Don’t dance around it! Tell me the goddamn trigger!"</p>
<p>"The temporal working’s reset anchor was tied to the Apostle network’s psychological state," Lustia explained, her eyes fixed on his, refusing to let him look away from the horror. "Specifically, the collective despair of the network’s senior members."</p>
<p>"The working was programmed to monitor the emotional threshold of the leadership."</p>
<p>"When that despair reached a point defined as the ’point of no return’ for Aethelgard’s survival... the reset would have activated automatically."</p>
<p>"It was a failsafe for her... A way to wipe the slate clean the moment the pressure became too much to control."</p>
<p>Rex’s mind suddenly flashed back to the meeting room. The air in his memory felt just as heavy as the air in the divine domain.</p>
<p>He saw Valentina’s face, that hollow, haunting silence when Apollo uttered that name. He saw Morwenna, her eyes brimming with a betrayal so deep it looked like physical pain as she looked at her oldest friend.</p>
<p>He remembered the knowledge system holder, the sheer, agonizing tension of a man ready to argue that thirty years of history might be a goddamn lie.</p>
<p>’We were the fuel,’ Rex thought, a bitter, hysterical laugh bubbling in his throat. ’Our grief, our confusion, our heartbreak... we were just the fuel meant to ignite the reset.’</p>
<p>"We were close," Rex whispered, the realization hitting him like a hammer to the ribs.</p>
<p>"Very close," Lustia confirmed, her voice heavy with the weight of the ’almost.’ "If that meeting had continued in the direction it was moving before you stepped in, before you spoke, the threshold would have been reached within approximately forty minutes."</p>
<p>"You were standing on the edge of a precipice, Rex..."</p>
<p>"One more minute of tension, one more minute of despair, and the world would have folded in on itself."</p>
<p>"Forty fucking minutes," Rex growled, his voice dropping to a low, lethal vibration.</p>
<p>He slammed a fist into his palm, the sound like a bone snapping. "Forty minutes... I stood in that room and talked..."</p>
<p>"I played the diplomat because I didn’t want to watch a network I had spent a goddamn year building leverage over tear itself apart over a single name."</p>
<p>"I was trying to save the network..."</p>
<p>He paused, his breath hitching, a raw, guttural sound of pure agony escaping his chest. "I didn’t know... I didn’t know I was also talking a goddamn deity out of erasing my own bloodline before they were even fucking born to conquer!"</p>
<p>"I was fighting for stability while she was waiting for us to break so she could erase the evidence!"</p>
<p>"You did not know," Lustia said, stepping closer, her voice filled with a desperate need to bridge the chasm of his rage. "That does not make what you did any less the thing that mattered, Rex."</p>
<p>"Intention is not the only measure of an action’s weight."</p>
<p>"You would not have phrased it that way if you had known, but you would have done the same thing."</p>
<p>"You would have stepped into that fire just the same, because the truth was correct for reasons that had nothing to do with the God of Time."</p>
<p>Rex turned his gaze to her, and for a moment, the sheer intensity of his stare was enough to make the divine realm tremble. His eyes weren’t just angry; they were wounded, bleeding with the fury of a man who had been cheated out of his very existence.</p>
<p>"Do not tell me what the fuck I would have done," Rex hissed, the words dripping with a lethal, condescending venom. "Do not you dare try to hand me comfort dressed up as certainty."</p>
<p>"Do not try to soothe the beast, Lustia... Not tonight... Not when the world is a lie and the gods are nothing but fucking thieves."</p>
<p>Lustia held his gaze, her eyes steady and unflinching. She didn’t offer a hollow apology, didn’t cower from the sheer, violent pressure of his presence.</p>
<p>She didn’t try to smooth over the jagged edges of his fury with platitudes. Rex noted this; he noted the way she stood her ground, the way she accepted his venom without letting it poison her own resolve.</p>
<p>It was a rare, brutal kind of honesty, the kind he respected, even if he currently wanted to tear the world apart to find it.</p>
<p>"Fair," she said, her voice a calm anchor in the middle of his hurricane. "I withdraw the certainty."</p>
<p>"But I keep the comfort..."</p>
<p>"You can refuse both if you want, Rex."</p>
<p>"You can stand in this wreckage alone if that’s what you need."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t answer. He couldn’t.</p>
<p>He just turned his gaze downward, staring at his own hands. They were trembling not with weakness, but with a frantic, kinetic hunger for destruction.</p>
<p>The Mark on his skin, that divine tether that had been screaming in his blood, was finally beginning to settle. The violent, erratic pulsing had slowed, receding back to its baseline rhythm, leaving behind a dull, throbbing ache that felt like a brand of shame.</p>
<p>’A failure,’ he thought, the word tasting like bile and copper in his mouth. ’A goddamn, monumental failure.’</p>
<p>He was Rex Rexilion. He was the man who calculated every variable, the man who saw the board three moves ahead of everyone else, the man who had built an empire out of chaos and blood.</p>
<p>He had planned for wars, for betrayals, for the total collapse of entire political structures. He had accounted for the greed of men and the desperation of kings.</p>
<p>He had mastered the art of the long game.</p>
<p>But he had missed this. He had been so focused on the pieces on the board that he hadn’t realized the board itself was being manipulated by a hand from the heavens.</p>
<p>’How fucking could I have been so blind?’ The thought was a serrated blade twisting in his gut. ’I was playing chess while she was rewriting the rules of the fucking universe.’</p>
<p>’I thought I was the architect, but I was just a tenant in a house she could demolish whenever she felt a draft.’</p>
<p>The humiliation was more visceral than any physical wound. It was a deep, structural rot in his pride.</p>
<p>For the first time in his life, his meticulous planning and the sheer, unadulterated willpower he had used to sculpt his destiny had been rendered moot by a celestial whim. He had been a victim of a cosmic joke, a puppet dancing to a rhythm he couldn’t even hear.</p>
<p>But as the humiliation settled, it began to transmute. The shame didn’t vanish; it hardened.</p>
<p>It condensed into a singular, crystalline point of absolute, murderous intent.</p>
<p>’Fine,’ he thought, his eyes narrowing until they were nothing but slits of burning amber. ’That’s fucking fine...’</p>
<p>’If she wants to play with time, if she wants to treat our lives like a fucking draft that can be erased and rewritten... then let her.’</p>
<p>’Let her think she’s won...’</p>
<p>’Let her think the loop is closed...’</p>
<p>A dark, predatory grin tugged at the corner of his mouth, a smile that held no warmth, only the promise of a reckoning.</p>
<p>’She thinks she owns the clock.’</p>
<p>’She thinks she can reset the board when the game gets too messy.’</p>
<p>’But she forgot one thing about the pieces she’s playing with.’</p>
<p>He clenched his fist so hard that the tendons in his forearm stood out like steel cables, and he felt the familiar, delicious sting of his nails digging into the meat of his palm.</p>
<p>’I’m not just a piece on her board...’</p>
<p>’I’m the fucking glitch in her system...’</p>
<p>’And if the God of Time thinks he can just ’undo’ what I’ve built, then I’m going to find a way to reach up into the heavens and choke the fucking seconds out of his throat...’</p>
<p>’I won’t just survive her reset...’</p>
<p>’I will break the cycle...’</p>
<p>’I will find the seam in her perfect little timeline, and I will rip it wide open until time itself bleeds gold and screams in agony...’</p>
<p>He looked up at Lustia, his eyes no longer just angry but possessed by a terrifying, divine purpose.</p>
<p>"She wants a crisis?" Rex whispered, his voice a low, guttural promise of carnage. "She wants a point of no return?"</p>
<p>"I’ll give her a fucking apocalypse she can’t erase."</p>
