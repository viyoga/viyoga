<p>The air in the drawing room became a pressurized chamber of violence. Theo wasn’t just angry anymore; he was unraveling.</p>
<p>The psychological wound inflicted by Rex’s cold pragmatism had torn a hole in his psyche that no amount of grief could patch. His mind was no longer his own; it was a battlefield where reality and hallucination fought for dominance.</p>
<p>’Kill her,’ a voice hissed in the back of his skull.</p>
<p>It wasn’t his own. It was ancient, oily, and hungry.</p>
<p>’She is the vessel of his corruption.’</p>
<p>’End the lineage.’</p>
<p>’End the taint.’</p>
<p>"Theo, please, you’re scaring me." Aurelia’s plea was the wrong thing to say.</p>
<p>To Theo’s fractured mind, her fear sounded like condescension. It sounded like she was patronizing him from her position of sinful surrender.</p>
<p>"Scaring you?" Theo’s laughter was a wet, broken sound. "You should be terrified!"</p>
<p>"You should be screaming until your lungs burst!"</p>
<p>Suddenly, his movements became a blur of unnatural speed. The temporal energy bleeding from his pores distorted the space around him, making him appear to stutter through reality like a broken film reel.</p>
<p>Before Aurelia could even gasp, his hands were around her throat.</p>
<p>"You gave him everything!" he screamed directly into her face, his grip tightening with bone-crushing force. "You gave him our dignity, our blood, our future!"</p>
<p>"If you want to be his toy, then die like one!"</p>
<p>Aurelia’s hands clawed uselessly at his wrists. Her face began to flush a deep, bruised purple, her eyes bulging as she fought for oxygen.</p>
<p>"Th-Theo," she managed to wheeze, the sound barely audible over the roaring of the temporal distortions.</p>
<p>’More power,’ Kaeleth whispered, the voice now a deafening crescendo in Theo’s mind. ’Feed the distortion.’</p>
<p>’Let time fracture.’</p>
<p>’Break the vessel, and you break the curse.’</p>
<p>Theo’s vision began to bleed crimson. The room started to warp; the walls seemed to stretch into infinite corridors of shadow, and the clock on the mantel began to tick backward with the sound of grinding teeth.</p>
<p>He could feel the power raw, chaotic, and terrifying surging through his veins. He felt like he could reach out and tear the seconds from the air.</p>
<p>He wanted to tear the very concept of ’now’ apart.</p>
<p>"I’ll end it!" Theo howled, his fingers sinking deeper into Aurelia’s flesh. "I’ll end the bloodline before his bastard can breathe!"</p>
<p>"THEO! STOP!"</p>
<p>The door burst open with enough force to crack the frame. Morwenna charged into the room, her face a mask of desperate horror.</p>
<p>She didn’t hesitate; she threw herself forward, her hands grasping Theo’s wrists.</p>
<p>The contact was like touching live electricity. The temporal energy lashed out at her, stinging her skin, threatening to age her bones decades in a single second.</p>
<p>With a strength born of pure terror, Morwenna wrenched his hands away from Aurelia’s throat. Aurelia collapsed to the floor, gasping and retching as she fought to reclaim her breath.</p>
<p>"Theo, look at me!" Morwenna commanded, grabbing his shoulders, trying to anchor him to the present. "This isn’t you!"</p>
<p>"This is what he wanted! He wanted you to break!"</p>
<p>But Theo didn’t look at her. His eyes were rolled back, showing only bloodshot whites.</p>
<p>His body was vibrating with such intensity that the floorboards beneath him began to splinter and splinter further.</p>
<p>"It’s too late, Morwenna," a voice whispered, but it wasn’t Kaeleth.</p>
<p>It was the realization dawning in Morwenna’s own heart.</p>
<p>She looked at her grandson. She saw the way his shadow moved independently of his body, twisting like something alive.</p>
<p>She saw the way the air around him cracked like broken glass. She saw the hollowed-out shell of the boy she loved, replaced by a weapon designed by Rex Rexilion.</p>
<p>Rex hadn’t just manipulated events; he had orchestrated a symphony of destruction where every note was a tragedy. He had provoked the trauma, fed the resentment, and now he sat back while the pieces tore each other apart.</p>
<p>"Theo..." Morwenna’s voice broke. "Please, my great-grandson..."</p>
<p>"Great-grandson?" Theo’s head snapped toward her. When he spoke, his voice sounded layered, as if three people were speaking at once his own, Kaeleth’s, and something ancient and dead. "There is no grandson here, Grandmother."</p>
<p>"Only survivors. And only victims."</p>
<p>The temporal field expanded. A shockwave of distorted time rippled outward from Theo, shattering the windows and sending furniture flying. Morwenna fell back, shielding her face as the room began to dissolve into a kaleidoscope of fractured moments.</p>
<p>As she watched Theo’s face contort into something inhuman, something utterly lost to the darkness, Morwenna realized the terrible truth. Rex hadn’t just won the battle; he had won the war for their souls.</p>
<p>Hope wasn’t just dying; it had been systematically dismantled, leaving nothing behind but the cold, inevitable victory of the monster.</p>
<p>"I’LL END IT ALL!!!" Theo let out a massive rage that caused an explosion.</p>
<p>The explosion did not come from fire or gunpowder; it came from the fundamental unraveling of causality itself.</p>
<p>Inside the drawing room, Theo’s scream transcended the audible spectrum. It became a physical force, a psychic shriek that tore through the fabric of the moment.</p>
<p>His rage was no longer a human emotion; it had become a gravitational event. The temporal energy hemorrhaging from his body reached critical mass, feeding off the hatred that burned in his marrow, the disgust he felt for his mother’s complicity, and the murderous fury directed at the man who had orchestrated their ruin.</p>
<p>"I WILL TEAR THE SECONDS FROM YOUR BONES!" Theo roared, but the words didn’t travel through the air; they vibrated through time itself.</p>
<p>The world convulsed. A sphere of distorted reality erupted from Theo’s center, expanding outward with the inevitability of a supernova.</p>
<p>In an instant, the screaming stopped. The shattering glass froze midair, each shard suspended like a diamond in amber.</p>
<p>Morwenna’s outstretched hand remained locked in space; Aurelia’s gasping mouth was stilled in a silent plea for mercy. The very dust motes in the air became motionless stars.</p>
<p>Time did not simply slow down; it died. The universe within the radius of Theo’s wrath became a tomb of frozen moments.</p>
<p>Miles away, or perhaps mere seconds away, for time had lost all meaning, Rex Rexilion stopped mid-stride in the academy corridors. He did not flinch, and he did not panic.</p>
<p>Instead, he tilted his head slightly, a faint, almost imperceptible smile touching his lips. He could feel it.</p>
<p>The sudden, violent stagnation in the temporal flow. It felt like a pulse of pure, concentrated venom cutting through the ether.</p>
<p>’Magnificent,’ Rex thought. ’The variable has exceeded expectations.’</p>
<p>’The rage has achieved transcendence. ’</p>
<p>Back in the courtyard, the frozen world was a grotesque gallery of suffering. But at the epicenter, Theo was not frozen.</p>
<p>He was moving through the stillness like a god of calamity. His eyes were twin vortexes of chronological decay, swirling with gold and black energy.</p>
<p>Every step he took left cracks in the air itself, visible fissures where reality had been torn apart by his sheer presence.</p>
<p>He was a creature of pure, unadulterated hatred. His mind was a storm of images: Rex’s face, the pregnant belly of his mother, the broken walls, the blood on the cobblestones.</p>
<p>Every memory was fuel. Every tear he had shed was now a weapon. He wasn’t just angry at Rex; he was angry at the very concept of a world where Rex Rexilion was allowed to exist.</p>
<p>He wanted to reach into the clockwork of the universe and grind the gears to dust.</p>
<p>The temporal freeze was not a shield; it was his domain. In this silent, motionless wasteland, Theo was the only thing that was truly alive, and his life was defined entirely by the desire to destroy.</p>
<p>Eventually, the pressure began to subside, not because the rage had faded, but because it had exhausted the immediate capacity of the local reality to bend further.</p>
<p>The distortion began to contract, pulling back toward the source like a dying star.</p>
<p>Theo emerged from the household’s interior approximately ninety minutes after Aurelia had steered him inside.</p>
<p>The emergence was the specific emergence of someone who had spent ninety minutes in a space with people who cared about them and had received what the caring people had to give and had found that what they had to give was real and insufficient simultaneously.</p>
<p>He had been met with tears, with desperate embraces, with hollow apologies that could not undo the violation of their bloodline. He had processed the grief; he had processed the betrayal, and he had found that the residue, the raw, jagged hatred, was still there, stronger than before.</p>
<p>He stepped out into the courtyard, his boots crunching on the frozen debris. He stood in the specific state of someone who had processed what was processable and had found that the residue was still there.</p>
<p>His face was no longer contorted in rage; it was something far more terrifying: it was cold. It was the stillness of a blade before the strike.</p>
<p>He stared toward the Academy, his gaze piercing through the walls, seeking the one man whose existence was an insult to every heartbeat Theo possessed.</p>
