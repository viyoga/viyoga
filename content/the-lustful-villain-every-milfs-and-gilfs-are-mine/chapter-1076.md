<p>Rex moved through the chaos with the terrifying, unhurried gait of a reaper approaching a harvest already completed. The domain around him was a nightmare of fractured reality; the floor buckled like wounded flesh, and the amber gold air screamed as it tore apart without its anchor.</p>
<p>Shards of the divine throne and crystallized time whipped through the air like shrapnel, but Rex walked through the storm as if it were nothing more than a light breeze. His boots crunched on debris that used to be the foundation of a god’s power.</p>
<p>Kaeleth lay broken on the heaving floor. Without the throne, his divine coherence was hemorrhaging.</p>
<p>His form flickered like a dying hologram, translucent patches of his body revealing the raw, bleeding void beneath.</p>
<p>Every time his essence tried to reassert itself, the chaotic domain rejected it, sending fresh waves of agony through his nerves. He looked less like a god and more like a corpse made of fading light.</p>
<p>Rex reached him and crouched, bringing himself level with the fallen deity. The proximity was intimate, almost tender in its brutality.</p>
<p>"The throne," Rex said, his voice cutting through the howling temporal winds with chilling clarity. "Your anchor."</p>
<p>"The singular point around which your two thousand-year reign has spiraled."</p>
<p>"The lynchpin that held every law, every moment, and every breath of this domain together."</p>
<p>"It is gone, Kaeleth... You are untethered."</p>
<p>Rex leaned closer, studying the way Kaeleth’s essence leaked onto the cracked floor in shimmering, viscous pools of gold.</p>
<p>"I’ve dissected your little gambit," Rex continued, his eyes cold and analytical. "Your plan was elegant in its cruelty."</p>
<p>"You sought to manufacture a mortal vessel, a puppet forged from trauma, and point it at me."</p>
<p>"You banked on the idea that the vessel’s personal history with me would grant it the intimate access a stranger could never achieve."</p>
<p>"You wanted the vessel’s designation to carry enough borrowed authority to bypass my defenses."</p>
<p>"And if the puppet broke? Well, puppets are expendable."</p>
<p>Rex paused, letting the silence of the dying domain underscore his words. He watched a spark of gold blood bubble at Kaeleth’s lips.</p>
<p>"Your plan suffered two catastrophic failures," Rex said. "The first was your failure to understand the human variable."</p>
<p>"The vessel chose to surrender its designation rather than remain your weapon."</p>
<p>"That wasn’t a flaw in your mathematics, Kaeleth; it was a flaw in your selection."</p>
<p>"You thought Theo Morr Nightwing was a man stripped of everything."</p>
<p>"But there is a difference between having no attachments and being convinced you have none. When his mother appeared, the illusion shattered."</p>
<p>"The weapon became a man again."</p>
<p>Rex’s expression shifted to a slow, predatory smile that didn’t reach his eyes.</p>
<p>"The second failure," Rex whispered, "was me."</p>
<p>"But I think you’ve been feeling that failure in your bones for the last hour."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s breathing was wet and labored. He lifted his head, his eyes glassy with pain and the dawning horror of his situation.</p>
<p>Even as a broken thing, his voice carried the fading echo of command.</p>
<p>"You are going to take it," Kaeleth rasped, each word costing him a fresh spasm of agony. "You’re going to take the authority."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex answered simply.</p>
<p>"The temporal authority over Aethelgard," Kaeleth said, his voice cracking. "Not the general domain."</p>
<p>"Don’t be arrogant, mortal."</p>
<p>"You cannot absorb the general temporal domain... Its scale exceeds your current capacity, and it would incinerate your soul before you could grasp it."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. "I know what I can handle."</p>
<p>"I’m not reaching for the ocean, Kaeleth... I’m not trying to swallow the general domain."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes burned with a focused, terrifying hunger. "I am taking the operational component."</p>
<p>"The specific, concentrated expression of temporal authority that you have been bleeding into Aethelgard’s foundational layer for two millennia."</p>
<p>"The part you actually use."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s jaw tightened. "And with it..."</p>
<p>"Control over the temporal substrate of this specific location," Rex finished for him. "I won’t own time itself."</p>
<p>"But I will own the time that happens here."</p>
<p>"I will own every second that passes within these borders... I will own you."</p>
<p>Kaeleth fell silent. The only sound was the grinding of the collapsing domain and the wet, fading heartbeat of a god who had finally run out of miracles.</p>
<p>"Many things I do have not existed before," Rex said, his voice a low rumble that seemed to vibrate through the very marrow of the crumbling reality. "And the thing that exists now is your end."</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, there was only the sound of the dying domain, the wet dripping of divine ichor, and the distant crackle of fractured time. Then, the atmosphere changed.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s compressed, broken form did not merely heal; it underwent a violent metamorphosis. This was not the measured recovery of a warrior stitching wounds or a mortal catching their breath.</p>
<p>This was something far more terrifying. It was the instantaneous decision of a cornered apex predator that had decided to stop playing with its prey and begin its slaughter.</p>
<p>It was the choice of a god who realized that half measures had already cost him his throne and that survival now demanded he become everything—every law, every force, every nightmare—all at once, or die in the attempt.</p>
<p>Rex felt it first through his Divine Perception. The sensation was like a sudden, crushing pressure behind his eyes—the unmistakable feeling of an entity abandoning all restraint.</p>
<p>For two thousand years, Kaeleth had ruled with the careful hand of an administrator, holding back the true tide of his power because he had never needed to see what lay beneath the surface.</p>
<p>But now, with the throne shattered and his authority bleeding out, there was no more reason to be gentle. There was no longer any posture to protect.</p>
<p>The domain’s ambient medium, which had been swirling in a chaotic, mindless maelstrom since the throne’s destruction, suddenly froze.</p>
<p>The turbulence didn’t dissipate; it obeyed.</p>
<p>Every shard of fractured time, every floating piece of amber gold debris, and every scream of displaced energy ceased its random flight. They stopped midair, suspended in a terrifying stillness, before snapping into alignment.</p>
<p>The chaos didn’t vanish; it reorganized itself. The entire reality of Aethelgard suddenly pivoted on a new axis, and that axis was Kaeleth.</p>
<p>Rex’s inner mind raced, his thoughts a frantic calculation of power scales.</p>
<p>’He’s not trying to rebuild his throne,’ Rex realized, his teeth grinding together as the air began to hum with lethal frequency. ’He’s turning himself into the throne.’</p>
<p>A sound began to rise from the floor, a low-frequency growl that wasn’t sound so much as it was the universe protesting a violation of its fundamental laws.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s blood, that luminous gold ichor, began to boil. It didn’t drip; it levitated, spiraling upward around his body in angry, molten ribbons.</p>
<p>The wounds in his divine form didn’t just close; they fused with searing heat, cauterizing his essence with raw power that smelled of burnt ozone and ancient copper.</p>
<p>A shockwave of pure, unfiltered rage erupted from Kaeleth’s center. It hit Rex like a physical blow, forcing him to plant his feet as the ground beneath him spiderwebbed from the sheer pressure.</p>
<p>The light in the room shifted from amber gold to a blinding, apocalyptic white. The temperature skyrocketed, then plummeted to absolute zero in the span of a second, each fluctuation threatening to tear Rex’s molecular structure apart.</p>
<p>Kaeleth wasn’t just angry; he was a supernova contained within the shape of a man. His eyes weren’t eyes anymore; they were twin voids of temporal fury, seeing every possible moment of Rex’s death simultaneously.</p>
<p>The pressure became unbearable. Bones creaked audibly in Rex’s body as the gravity around Kaeleth intensified to impossible levels.</p>
<p>The divine ichor spiraling around him hardened into jagged, razor-sharp armor of solidified time, each spike humming with enough energy to cleave through dimensions.</p>
<p>Through the blinding glare and the deafening shriek of reality bending to his will, Kaeleth rose. He didn’t climb to his feet; he ascended, his body unfolding with a terrifying, unnatural grace as he reclaimed his height.</p>
<p>He stood tall, silhouetted against the collapsing sky of his own domain, no longer a wounded king, but a vengeful god made flesh.</p>
