<p>"I would like," she said, her voice dropping into a low, intense register, "to understand that system..."</p>
<p>"In detail... Every single variable."</p>
<p>"The Foresight’s predictive window is twelve seconds," the avatar replied, his voice devoid of any boastfulness, stating it as a simple, unyielding law of nature. "Everything within a twelve-second radius of the present moment is calculated before the first action is even taken."</p>
<p>"Because the calculation is continuous, the dragon’s deflected arc was already a solved certainty before the first construct had even left its housing."</p>
<p>"Twelve seconds," Talyra whispered, the number feeling impossibly long, an eternity in the heat of a kill.</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>Talyra turned her head, looking toward the northern rubble. Above the jagged, broken roofline of the city, the dragon’s massive, motionless form lay sprawled like a fallen god.</p>
<p>The beast’s scales were cracked, its blood thick, steaming, and smelling of sulfur seeping into the stone, staining the ruins a dark, visceral crimson.</p>
<p>"I have spent six years honing my predictive window," she said, her voice tight with the memory of a thousand failed shots and narrow escapes. "I can read approximately two seconds of a target’s movement pattern with enough precision to guarantee impact through a closing gap."</p>
<p>"Two seconds is all the world gives you."</p>
<p>"Two seconds at your designation’s level of accuracy is significantly more useful in a practical engagement than twelve seconds of low-probability estimation," the Avatar countered.</p>
<p>It was a quintessential Rex response, a calculated, comparative truth that offered a compliment wrapped in the cold armor of logic.</p>
<p>Talyra turned back to him, and the expression on her face shifted. The relief of the victory faded, replaced by a sharp, gleaming smile, the look of a warrior who had just found a peer, someone whose intellect was as sharp as her arrows.</p>
<p>"That is a cautious compliment," she said, her eyes dancing.</p>
<p>"It is an accurate one," the Avatar replied.</p>
<p>She stepped back into her position, but the smile remained, a lingering spark of warmth in the cooling air of the battlefield.</p>
<p>Nerith, however, remained a silhouette of quiet intensity. She stood apart from the group, her presence less like a soldier and more like a part of the landscape itself.</p>
<p>Her expression was shifting, moving through the amber leaf and heart moss spectrum of her druid channel, a state where emotion was not a feeling but a fundamental force of nature. She wasn’t just looking at the Avatar; she was sensing him, as if trying to read the vibration of his very essence.</p>
<p>She did not move toward him. She didn’t need to.</p>
<p>The distance between them was filled with the heavy, unspoken weight of the earth itself.</p>
<p>The Avatar looked across the gap, his gaze locking onto hers, waiting in the silence.</p>
<p>"The roots," she said finally, her voice carrying the mournful weight of a funeral dirge.</p>
<p>"Yes," the Avatar said.</p>
<p>"The old roots," she pressed, her eyes shimmering with a sudden, painful light. "The arterial ones."</p>
<p>"The ancient, deep substrate veins that run beneath the residential district..."</p>
<p>"They are eight hundred years old, Rex... They were not meant to move because they are the bones of the forest, the anchors of the world."</p>
<p>"I know," the Avatar said, his voice softening, acknowledging the cost.</p>
<p>"I asked them to move," she said, her voice trembling with the memory of the strain. "Because the dragon was coming, and because you asked Lily to contain it, and because Lily was drowning in the chaos, and the roots were the only strength I had left to offer."</p>
<p>She fell silent, the weight of the command pressing on her chest. "They moved because of the relationship."</p>
<p>"The druid channel isn’t a command; it’s a conversation. And a relationship that has endured for eight centuries has a depth of trust that defies logic."</p>
<p>"They moved because they loved the land." Her gaze hardened, turning toward the ground. "And they moved so violently that two of them snapped."</p>
<p>"I know," the Avatar said, the words heavy with the burden of his own foresight.</p>
<p>"They cannot simply grow back," she said, her voice cracking like dry wood. "Not in my lifetime. Not in several lifetimes."</p>
<p>"The arterial structure of the residential district is wounded."</p>
<p>"The great network that has breathed life into the organic foundation of this island for eight hundred years... it has two massive, gaping voids in it now."</p>
<p>"The heart of the island is bleeding, and it won’t heal."</p>
<p>The Avatar remained silent, a monolith of stillness amidst the settling dust and the scent of ozone and pulverized stone.</p>
<p>Nerith watched him, her eyes glowing with that strange, amber druidic light, a hue that signaled she wasn’t looking for a hollow apology, nor was she casting a sentence of judgment. She was simply witnessing a truth.</p>
<p>"I want you to know," she said, her voice steady despite the tremor of grief vibrating through the earth beneath her, "that I do not regret asking."</p>
<p>"If the world were ending again tomorrow, I would make the same choice."</p>
<p>"The dragon would have pulverized this entire district, leaving nothing but a crater of bone and ash, if we hadn’t held it." She looked down at the deep, dark fissures in the stone, her gaze piercing the layers of dirt to the wounded veins below. "But there is a cost to survival."</p>
<p>"I want someone to know that the roots broke... that something ancient, something that had breathed for eight centuries, is simply... gone."</p>
<p>"The island is scarred. It is different now than it was yesterday. It is less whole."</p>
<p>The Avatar turned his gaze to her. The weight of her words seemed to press against the very air between them.</p>
<p>"I know," he said.</p>
<p>For the third time, the words left his lips, but the register had shifted. It was no longer the clinical observation of a strategist; it was the heavy, muted acknowledgment of a construct, a being that understood the mathematics of loss but lacked the biological machinery to weep for it.</p>
<p>He knew he was offering a fraction of the emotional depth the moment deserved, and he knew it.</p>
<p>Nerith seemed to accept the limitation. She didn’t demand more.</p>
<p>"Thank you," she whispered, directed perhaps at the Avatar, perhaps at the wounded island itself, or perhaps at the gods who had allowed them to survive at all.</p>
<p>Then, the silence was shattered.</p>
<p>A roar of cheering began to swell from the edges of the plaza. The civilians, who had been huddling in doorways and peering nervously from shattered windows since the earthquake protocols ended, were pouring into the open space.</p>
<p>They were the survivors of a nightmare, their faces streaked with soot and tears, but their voices were thunderous.</p>
<p>The academy staff were already moving in a disciplined, professional manner, setting up perimeters and assessing the structural carnage of a city-scale engagement. But their organized efficiency had not yet managed to impose the sterile, controlled silence of a post-battle zone.</p>
<p>In that raw, chaotic gap between the violence of the fight and the bureaucracy of the aftermath, the crowd’s voice filled the vacuum.</p>
<p>They were screaming a single name, a rhythmic, primal chant that shook the very air: "Rex! Rex Rexilion!"</p>
<p>The Avatar stood in the center of the broken plaza, a solitary figure amidst the wreckage of stone and dragon scale, and he received it. He didn’t bask in it like a conqueror; instead, he wore the expression Rex would have worn, a warm, slightly overwhelmed aura.</p>
<p>It was the look of a man who had stared into the abyss of a thousand different death scenarios, calculated the odds, and realized how narrowly they had escaped the worst of them. It wasn’t false modesty; it was the quiet, internal friction of a man who knew how close they had come to total annihilation and found the crowd’s absolute certainty a beautiful, jarring contrast to his own memory of the chaos.</p>
<p>The avatar was performing the role of Rex perfectly.</p>
