<p>The transformation did not arrive as a sudden erasure of thought but as something far more insidious: a corruption of feeling. It came in layers, weaving itself through the psyche so seamlessly that it felt less like being brainwashed and more like being understood.</p>

<p>In the plaza, the Aethelgard soldiers who had been standing with their heads bowed in shame and horror slowly raised them.</p>

<p>Their faces were still streaked with tears; the salt from their grief was still wet on their cheeks, but the quality of those tears had shifted.</p>

<p>The sorrow was gone, replaced by an overflowing, terrifying gladness. Their eyes didn’t hold the clarity of Evelyn’s truth anymore; they held the ecstatic madness of the devout.</p>

<p>An old baker in a narrow back lane, mid-prayer for a city in ruin, sank to his knees in the middle of the cobblestones.</p>

<p>His flour-dusted hands folded over his chest in a gesture of surrender that looked suspiciously like worship. A small child, who moments before had been screaming for her mother amidst the smoke, suddenly stopped.</p>

<p>She stared up at the pulsing violet light threading through the cracks in the street, and her lips curled into a smile, a pure, innocent smile, as though she had finally seen the face of something she had been waiting for her entire life.</p>

<p>On the high balconies of the Grand Academy, the instructors, men and women, who had spent their lives cultivating reason and logic, straightened their shoulders.</p>

<p>The shock that had drained the color from their faces evaporated, replaced by a terrifying calm, like people who had finally been relieved of the unbearable burden of thinking for themselves.</p>

<p>All across the city, the infection spread in staggered waves of devotion.</p>

<p>THUD. THUD. THUD.</p>

<p>The gate guards struck their armored chests with their fists in a rhythmic salute. Merchants in the market district dropped their crates of fruit and grain, letting them shatter on the ground, and knelt in the dirt without hesitation.</p>

<p>The Underlayer troops, whose loyalty to Rex as Xerollion had already been absolute, found themselves caught between two conflicting impulses: they sobbed with the terror of what they had witnessed and laughed with the ecstasy of their master’s return.</p>

<p>Mordecai, who had just reached the edge of the plaza, his sword drawn and his jaw set for a fight, suddenly felt the air leave his lungs.</p>

<p>His legs gave way, and he sat down hard on the blood-soaked stones, his mouth hanging open, his eyes glistening with a sudden, unbidden moisture.</p>

<p>Cassandra drew a sharp, ragged breath. Her face flushed crimson, her heart hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird.</p>

<p>For a second, her mind screamed against it, ’This is wrong, this is madness!,’ but the violet pulse beneath her feet answered her fear with a promise of belonging.</p>

<p>Her knees hit the ground. Her hand pressed against her heart.</p>

<p>"God help us," she whispered, but there was no fear in the words.</p>

<p>Only awe.</p>

<p>Gorvasha growled once a sound of primal recognition and dropped beside her, her massive frame lowering to the earth.</p>

<p>Lilith Nocturne wept without making a sound, her tears falling silently as her lips twisted into a serene, terrifying smile.</p>

<p>Then, from somewhere far off in the gathering crowd, a single voice rose.</p>

<p>It was hoarse, shaking with an emotion that defied description, barely audible over the crackling flames.</p>

<p>"Rex Rexilion."</p>

<p>It was a test. A tentative probe into the new reality.</p>

<p>Then, another voice joined it.</p>

<p>And another.</p>

<p>"Rex Rexilion."</p>

<p>Within heartbeats, the sound swelled. It was no longer a collection of individual voices; it was a tide.</p>

<p>A tsunami of sound that rolled from one district to the next, surging from the northern cliffs to the southern docks, echoing from the streets of Aethelgard down into the deepest, darkest corridors of the Underlayer that ran beneath the bedrock.</p>

<p>The city was no longer screaming in terror; it was chanting in ecstasy.</p>

<p>"HAIL REX REXILION!"</p>

<p>"HAIL REX REXILION!"</p>

<p>"HAIL REX REXILION!"</p>

<p>The roar of the crowd escalated until it was no longer a sound but a physical force.</p>

<p>Hundreds of thousands of throats screamed in unison, the vibration so profound that dust shook free from the crumbling battlements and the last remaining panes of glass in the Grand Academy shattered inward with a cascading CRASH.</p>

<p>"Protector!" they cried. "Guardian! Lord of Aethelgard! Our God!"</p>

<p>The words were not shouted with the hollow cadence of a mob; they were wept with the raw, desperate conviction of the saved.</p>

<p>An old soldier, whose face was a roadmap of scars and fresh tears, knelt in the rubble. Only days before, he had watched his son die in the northern corridor, slaughtered by the very man now being hailed as his savior.</p>

<p>But as the violet pulse of [Idolatry Dominion] washed over him, his grief twisted.</p>

<p>Through his sobbing, he whispered to the sky, "It doesn’t hurt anymore... I don’t feel the pain anymore... because he is watching over us now."</p>

<p>The horror of it lay in that sincerity. It was not a lie being forced into their minds; it was a truth being rewritten in their souls.</p>

<p>That was the true cruelty of the curse; it made the victim believe they had found salvation in their own chains.</p>

<p>In front of Rex, the air fractured again as new lines of text hemorrhaged through the space. But this time, the numbers didn’t bleed out.</p>

<p>They surged upward, climbing with violent speed.</p>

<p>Rex watched the numbers lock into place with the satisfaction of a craftsman seeing a perfect weld. The exclusions were calculated cruelty.</p>

<p>He did not want Helena or Leonidas to become mindless drones; he wanted them to witness the collapse. And he wanted Evelyn above all others to remain untouched by the ecstasy.</p>

<p>He wanted her to stay trapped in her own clarity, to feel every ounce of her righteous fury curdling into helpless despair. A victory where the loser sees nothing is a hollow triumph; he wanted her to taste every second of her defeat.</p>

<p>Evelyn lay broken in the center of her crater, watching the apocalypse of the soul unfold with eyes that remained terrifyingly clear.</p>

<p>The golden ring of her [Open Court] was gone, reduced to dead ashes scattered by the wind. The divine mark on her chest flickered weakly, its light dimming like a candle drowning in oil.</p>

<p>She watched the faces she had tried to save—the same faces that had just moments ago been waking up to the truth, now plunging back into the abyss like swimmers caught in a lethal undertow.</p>

<p>The [Verdict] she had screamed with the last of her shattered strength melted away. Her words, her truth, her sacrifice—it all dissolved like ink in a storm.</p>

<p>Her breathing hitched, a wet, broken sound in her throat.</p>

<p>For the first time since their battle began, through forty minutes of agony, through shattered ribs and punctured lungs, the fear she had never permitted herself to show finally surfaced.</p>

<p>Her pupils dilated until her eyes were almost entirely black. Her lips trembled violently against the blood staining them.</p>

<p>"No..." she whispered, the word so thin and hoarse it was almost lost to the thunder of the chanting crowd. "No..."</p>
