<p>The air around Evelyn Astralight didn’t just vibrate; it screamed.</p>

<p>Blue white lightning crawled over her body like a living, hunting thing, racing from her head down to her fingertips in a frantic, jagged dance.</p>

<p>It wasn’t just a glow; it was raw, unbridled energy that wrapped her limbs in a crackling net of lethal threads.</p>

<p>Everywhere her boots touched the earth, the grass didn’t just burn; it vaporized, leaving scorched, smoking footprints in the blood-soaked clay.</p>

<p>The lightning cast grotesque, stroboscopic shadows across her features, painting her face in harsh pulses of brilliance that made her look less like a woman and more like an ancient force of nature incarnate.</p>

<p>Her hair defied gravity, standing in electrified arcs around her head, and her eyes, once familiar, were now twin voids of sheet lightning burning behind a storm cloud.</p>

<p>The speed she was known for, that legendary blue streak that marked the end of a fight, had mutated. It had become something heavier, more dangerous.</p>

<p>The atmosphere around her sang with a constant, low-frequency rumble of miniature thunderclaps, a sound that vibrated in Rex’s very marrow.</p>

<p>’That’s how she did it,’ Rex realized, the revelation cutting through his pain sharper than any bone fracture. ’The impossible rescue... The impossible strike...’</p>

<p>In the span of a single heartbeat, she had crossed the entire devastation of the garden, scooped up two grown adults, deposited them safely within the circle, and accelerated to a velocity that bypassed his reaction time entirely.</p>

<p>And most terrifyingly, Foresight had seen nothing. His divine advantage, his ability to read the immediate future as if it were an open book, had been utterly blind.</p>

<p>’Why?’ His mind raced, even as his cells screamed during regeneration. ’Why couldn’t I detect her?’</p>

<p>Then it clicked. Her system had been a black hole in his perception since the moment she stepped foot in Aethelgard.</p>

<p>She had been running a perfect, silent veil, a cloaking mechanism so sophisticated that it didn’t just hide her presence; it erased her from the fundamental calculations of his power. She hadn’t just been hiding; she had been waiting.</p>

<p>There was no fear in her expression.</p>

<p>No hesitation.</p>

<p>No righteous anger.</p>

<p>There was only a terrifying, crystalline calm. Her eyes met his with a clarity that felt like being stared down by the universe itself.</p>

<p>"I suspected you from the corridor," Evelyn said.</p>

<p>Her voice wasn’t loud, but it possessed an unnatural authority that carried across the silent, traumatized garden without effort, cutting through the crackle of lightning like a blade through silk.</p>

<p>"I kept telling myself I was wrong..."</p>

<p>"I kept looking for reasons to believe in the man I thought I knew!"</p>

<p>"I don’t know why I fought it so hard...!"</p>

<p>A pause hung between them, heavy as lead.</p>

<p>"You are not just Xerollion," she continued, her voice dropping to a whisper that somehow felt more dangerous than a shout. "You are also the man who stood beside us."</p>

<p>"The man who shared our bread... The man who pretended to care."</p>

<p>Her gaze hardened. The storm behind her eyes intensified.</p>

<p>"But the mask has slipped, Rex... And now, there is nothing left to protect."</p>

<p>She lifted her right fist.</p>

<p>CRACK BOOM!</p>

<p>The lightning didn’t just flare; it detonated around her knuckles in a sphere of azure wrath. The sheer pressure of the gathering energy caused the earth beneath her feet to surrender, splitting outward in a perfect, concentric ring of jagged cracks that radiated toward Rex like the petals of a deadly flower.</p>

<p>The air itself began to ionize, the smell of ozone and burnt air filling Rex’s lungs as the sky above them seemed to darken in response to her power.</p>

<p>She wasn’t just preparing to fight. She was preparing to erase him.</p>

<p>"You killed Apollo," Evelyn said, and the words landed with more force than any physical blow.</p>

<p>Each syllable was a condemnation, dripping with the agony of a broken trust. "You put your hands on Elliot’s family."</p>

<p>"You dismantled our lives while sitting at our tables...!"</p>

<p>"You made every single one of us believe you...!"</p>

<p>She stopped. For a heartbeat, the only sound in the ruined garden was the ferocious, hungry crackle of her lightning and the wet, labored breathing of the wounded behind her.</p>

<p>When she spoke again, her voice had lost all its human tremor. It was hollow.</p>

<p>It was the stillness of a blade already halfway through its swing, the absolute, terrifying calm of a decision that had been made and could never be undone.</p>

<p>"I am going to kill you, Rex Rexilion... Or should I say... Lord Xerollion!"</p>

<p>"Or whatever hollow name you wear underneath that skin!"</p>

<p>Rex didn’t flinch.</p>

<p>Instead, he raised his hand to his mouth, wiping a thick smear of crimson from his lips with the back of his scarred knuckles. His eyes didn’t meet hers with remorse; they met hers with something far more dangerous: recognition.</p>

<p>A low, guttural chuckle rumbled in his chest, startlingly out of place amidst the carnage.</p>

<p>"Finally," Rex said, his voice carrying a cruel, mocking edge that sliced through the ozone. "The little mouse finally decides to leave her hole."</p>

<p>"Do you have any idea how tedious it’s been, Evelyn?"</p>

<p>"Watching you play house? Watching you pretend to be one of these pathetic, weeping mortals?"</p>

<p>His smile sharpened, becoming something predatory and grotesque.</p>

<p>"I was wondering when you’d stop hiding behind that pathetic veil of yours."</p>

<p>"You think you’re special because your system doesn’t register on their primitive scans?"</p>

<p>"You think you’re the only one who remembers a life before this charade?" His eyes gleamed with a sadistic intelligence. "I knew you were different."</p>

<p>"I knew you had that same... stink of another world clinging to your soul."</p>

<p>"A reincarnator playing hero... How quaint... and how utterly exhausting."</p>

<p>His gaze swept over her, dismissive despite the power she radiated. "You’re the last one, Evelyn."</p>

<p>"The last piece of debris I need to sweep away before this world becomes mine to play with...!"</p>

<p>"You’re not a threat; you’re just the final chore...!"</p>

<p>He stood fully upright now, the broken bones in his ribs knitting together with audible, wet clicks and pops as Infinite Regeneration forced his frame back into its perfect, muscular alignment.</p>

<p>The red and violet spirals of his own authority began to writhe around his arms again, more violent and agitated than before, responding to his rising bloodlust.</p>

<p>He looked at her; he really looked at her, not as a comrade, but as a worthy obstacle. He saw the potential in her lightning, the way her power didn’t just flow but seemed to bend the very fabric of reality around her.</p>

<p>She was a variable he hadn’t fully accounted for, a player who had been holding her cards close to her chest.</p>

<p>The burn in his ribs flared one last time before fading into a dull, manageable ache. Behind his eyes, the cold, calculating predator that had survived a thousand lifetimes took full control.</p>

<p>The mask wasn’t just off; it was incinerated.</p>

<p>A slow smile spread across his face, wider than any he had worn that day. It was a smile full of teeth, hungry and devoid of any shred of humanity.</p>

<p>"Try," he said, the single word a challenge that shook the very air between them. "Make sure that it’s your..."</p>

<p>"...hardest!"</p>
