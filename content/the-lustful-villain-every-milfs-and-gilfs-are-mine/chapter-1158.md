<p>The air didn’t just move; it shattered.</p>
<p>Apollo didn’t run; he detonated. One moment he was a figure of radiant stillness twenty meters away, and the next, he was a streak of white gold lightning tearing through the space between them.</p>
<p>He crossed the distance faster than any mortal eye could track, his movement bypassing the concept of velocity entirely.</p>
<p>Xerollion, the man beneath the mask, the predator who had dominated this duel with terrifying precision, felt something he hadn’t experienced since his first day of combat: the sickening lurch of being surprised.</p>
<p>For approximately one and a half seconds, his mind had been divided, pulled between the immediate threat of Apollo and the incomprehensible reality of the Goddess’s presence.</p>
<p>That hesitation was a death sentence. For the first time all day, his reaction was half a beat behind his instinct.</p>
<p>His defensive posture formed, but it was a ghost of a shield, already crumbling before it could solidify.</p>
<p>The punch got through.</p>
<p>BOOM.</p>
<p>Apollo’s fist, armored in two overlapping layers of divine light that hummed with the frequency of a dying star, connected with Xerollion’s sternum. The sound was wet, metallic, and bone-deep, a sound like a sledgehammer striking a slab of marble wrapped in raw meat.</p>
<p>The impact didn’t just push Xerollion; it launched him. He was driven backward four meters across the unforgiving stone floor.</p>
<p>His boots screamed against the masonry, carving twin grooves into the solid rock as he struggled to find purchase, but the momentum was absolute. His spine buckled under the sheer kinetic force, and the sound of his ribs cracking sharp, staccato pops like dry kindling snapping echoed through the corridor.</p>
<p>Rex, deep within the analytical fortress of his mind, noted the impact. He didn’t feel alarm; alarm was too primitive, but he felt something dangerously close to it.</p>
<p>The data was undeniable: ’The strike registered.’</p>
<p>The divine sheath hadn’t just enhanced Apollo’s speed; it had added physical mass to his very concept. The force behind that fist was no longer measured in Newtons but in inevitability.</p>
<p>The silence of the divine presence was instantly obliterated as the Aethelgard formation exploded into a cacophony of panicked noise. Steel scraped against scabbards, boots scrambled for position, and shouts of terror and confusion rose like smoke.</p>
<p>"Move! Protect the flanks!" someone screamed.</p>
<p>Cassandra’s arms, previously folded in a display of controlled tension, snapped open. Her entire body coiled like a spring, her eyes blazing with the need to intervene.</p>
<p>Beside her, the massive Gorvasha’s grip shifted on her war hammer, the leather wrappings creaking under her tightening fist. The demon blooded warrior took one thunderous step forward, her muscles bunching to charge into the chaos.</p>
<p>Before she could launch, Cassandra’s hand shot out, catching Gorvasha’s shoulder with bruising force. The grip was iron.</p>
<p>"Not yet," Cassandra hissed.</p>
<p>Her voice was tight, strained through clenched teeth as she fought to maintain her own composure.</p>
<p>"He was pushed back, Commander!" Gorvasha growled, her breath coming in ragged bursts. "He’s vulnerable! If we strike now,</p>
<p>"I know he was pushed back, you fool!" Cassandra snapped back, her eyes never once wavering from the center of the storm where the two combatants stood. "Look at the ground! Look at the air!"</p>
<p>"He hasn’t given the signal yet."</p>
<p>"If we charge in there now, we won’t be fighting the enemy; we’ll be dying in the crossfire of gods."</p>
<p>"We will be collateral damage in a war we cannot comprehend."</p>
<p>She squeezed Gorvasha’s shoulder harder, forcing the warrior to remain rooted.</p>
<p>"He is still in control," Cassandra whispered, though her own heart was hammering against her ribs. "Trust him... Trust Lord Xerollion."</p>
<p>Apollo did not merely press the advantage; he unleashed a cascade of divine violence.</p>
<p>The rhythm of the duel shattered. Previously, their exchange had been a predictable dance of feints and counters, but now Apollo moved with a terrifying, multilayered complexity that defied the very laws of combat.</p>
<p>The strikes came faster than the human eye could process a blur of white gold motion that turned the corridor into a pressure cooker of kinetic energy.</p>
<p>Rex’s mind screamed warnings as his Full Potential Adaptation labored at its absolute limit. His ability was designed to solve problems, but it was being asked to solve an equation that changed its variables mid-calculation.</p>
<p>The first component of Apollo’s attack was familiar: the warm, golden torrent of his own life affinity. Rex had already mapped its cadence; he knew its weight and its reach.</p>
<p>But the second component, the divine sheath, was an unpredictable nightmare.</p>
<p>SHING CRACK THUD.</p>
<p>The second layer shifted its nature with every impact. One moment, it was a pure disruption of matter, turning Rex’s reinforced armor into brittle glass that shattered under the slightest pressure.</p>
<p>The next, it wasn’t even touching his body physically but was instead a crushing pressure on his soul register, making his consciousness waver as if his very essence were being squeezed by a titan’s fist.</p>
<p>Occasionally, it manifested as something more insidious: a metaphysical poison that forced Rex’s own regeneration to hesitate, causing wounds that should have healed instantly to linger like festering injuries.</p>
<p>’Adaptation failing to sync... recalculating... recalculating...’ Rex’s inner monologue was a frantic stream of data.</p>
<p>His ability kept falling exactly one contact behind. To adapt to a mechanism, one had to encounter it cleanly, to understand its fundamental pattern.</p>
<p>But the divine layer refused to hold still. It was a liquid godhood, shifting its properties faster than his mind could catalog them.</p>
<p>The cost was written in blood.</p>
<p>SPLAT.</p>
<p>A clawing strike from Apollo’s palm tore through Rex’s forearm. Normally, the wound would have vanished before the blood could even bead.</p>
<p>Now, the gash stayed open for three agonizing seconds, crimson arterial spray painting the stone floor. A deeper slash across his shoulder left a jagged crater in his flesh that took nearly five seconds to knit back together.</p>
<p>It was a marginal degradation—the difference between instantaneous and almost instantaneous—but in a fight where Apollo was landing three divine enhanced strikes every second, that margin was a death sentence. The damage was accumulating and the blood was pooling.</p>
<p>Rex began to give ground.</p>
<p>He retreated in measured, deliberate steps, his boots skidding through his own blood. He wasn’t retreating in defeat; he was retreating with the calculated precision of a master duelist trading space for time.</p>
<p>Each step back bought his analysis another millisecond of exposure, another chance to glimpse the pattern behind the chaos. Behind the porcelain mask, his brain was burning through glucose at an impossible rate, his neurons firing with enough intensity to cook his own flesh.</p>
<p>’I can end this,’ Rex thought, the cold pragmatism of a killer overriding the strain of survival. ’I can end this right now.’</p>
<p>The options laid themselves out before him like weapons on a rack:</p>
<p>First: Gravity Manipulation.</p>
<p>At full output, he could increase the gravitational constant within three hundred meters to catastrophic levels.</p>
<p>He would flatten Apollo into a crimson smear against the floor, crushing everything in the corridor, including himself, regardless of whatever divine reinforcement Apollo carried.</p>
<p>Fundamental physical force wasn’t concerned about divine layering; gravity was the universe’s ultimate truth.</p>
<p>Second: Sovereign Necromancy.</p>
<p>With a single word, he could rip the dormant souls from the thousands of corpses scattered across the battlefield and funnel them into this corridor. A tide of undead fury would overwhelm Apollo in seconds.</p>
<p>Third: Temporal Authority.</p>
<p>He could reach into the clockwork of reality and simply stop time. The engagement would freeze between one heartbeat and the next, allowing him to dissect Apollo while he stood like a statue.</p>
<p>But each solution was a poisoned chalice.</p>
<p>’Tactical victory, strategic annihilation,’ Rex mused bitterly.</p>
<p>If he used any of them, he would reveal everything. Ten thousand witnesses would see the true monster behind the mask. A goddess was watching through the channel she had opened, her divine attention focused directly on him.</p>
<p>And worst of all, forty meters away stood the Aethelgard front rank. There, wearing his own face, stood the version of himself that had to remain an unsuspected ally.</p>
<p>If Rex unleashed his true nature here, he would destroy the very foundation of the deception that kept his army alive.</p>
<p>The corridor groaned as Apollo lunged again, the air screaming as the divine light tore through it.</p>
<p>Rex braced himself, his ribs aching, his blood warm on his skin, trapped between the need to survive and the need to remain hidden.</p>
