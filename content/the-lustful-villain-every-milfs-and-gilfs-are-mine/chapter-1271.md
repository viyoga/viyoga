<p>Over Lily’s shoulder, Valentina’s eyes found his. The connection was instantaneous and icy, cutting through the warm amber glow of the dining room like a blade through silk.</p>
<p>The look lasted for less than a heartbeat, but in that fragment of time, a silent conversation unfolded between them, one stripped of all pretense.</p>
<p>’I know what you want from me tonight,’ her gaze screamed with terrifying clarity. ’I know the price of this dinner.’</p>
<p>’I will play my part... I will sit at this table, I will cradle these girls, and I will hold this lie together with everything I have left.’</p>
<p>’But understand this, Rex: this is not a gift.’</p>
<p>’This is a transaction.’</p>
<p>She was reminding him that her compliance was not surrender; it was a calculated concession, a performance maintained by sheer force of will. She was keeping her end of the arrangement, ensuring the foundation remained solid for his coming storm, but she wanted him to know she was doing it with eyes wide open.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He didn’t blink.</p>
<p>He simply inclined his head by the smallest possible degree, a gesture of acknowledgment between two predators who understood the necessity of the hunt.</p>
<p>’Thank you,’ his mind answered, the thought brief and cold.</p>
<p>Then, the performance began in earnest. They sat down to eat.</p>
<p>The evening could have spiraled into disaster. Rex had run every permutation of failure in his mind before the first candle was lit; he had prepared contingencies for poisoned words, for sudden tears, for the explosive breaking of silence.</p>
<p>Yet, as the wine flowed and the first courses were served, the evening unfolded with a deceptive ease that surprised even him. It worked because, beneath the layers of trauma and deception, every person at that table shared a desperate, starving need for this moment of normalcy.</p>
<p>They were all complicit in the illusion.</p>
<p>Rex took his place at the head of the table, the candlelight positioned behind him so that his features remained partially cast in shadow, lending him an air of casual authority. He activated the persona that had earned him his reputation, the Silver Tongue.</p>
<p>He didn’t dominate the conversation; he orchestrated it, weaving an easy, unforced rhythm that allowed each woman to breathe without feeling the weight of the world pressing down on them.</p>
<p>He offered a joke here, a thoughtful question there, and when the tension threatened to thicken, he deployed a story from his past.</p>
<p>He recounted the time Garrett had attempted to teach him the proper technique to gut a fish, only for Rex to misjudge the tension of the blade and send the entire slippery creature flying across the dock, where it landed with a wet slap atop a stack of wooden crates.</p>
<p>The effect was immediate. Lily laughed—not the polite, strained sound she had been making for weeks, but a genuine, unguarded peal of laughter that forced her to set her fork down.</p>
<p>It was the first time he had heard that sound since the war began—a bright, musical note that seemed to chase the shadows from the corners of the room.</p>
<p>Beside her, Diana, who had spent the entire evening sitting with her spine as straight as a sentry’s spear, finally allowed the armor to crack. Her shoulders dropped, her breathing steadied, and she leaned back in her chair with a sigh that sounded like surrender.</p>
<p>Elizabeth provided the perfect counterpoint. She contributed in her signature style: dry, precise, and delivered with a deadpan expression that made her words land like stones dropped into still water.</p>
<p>When Diana, emboldened by the wine and the rare levity, began describing a ridiculous incident where a recruit had somehow managed to shoot an arrow through a training dummy, then through a hanging banner, and finally through the hat of an instructor in one continuous draw, Elizabeth didn’t miss a beat.</p>
<p>Without shifting her expression or pausing her eating, Elizabeth remarked, "Then he was clearly aiming at the hat."</p>
<p>The timing was flawless. Diana burst into a genuine laugh, the sound mingling with Lily’s as they shared the moment.</p>
<p>For a few precious hours, the war was a distant memory, the lies were invisible, and the house felt less like a fortress under siege and more like the home they had once believed they would have.</p>
<p>Valentina remained the silent anchor of the evening. She ate with a measured, almost ritualistic slowness, each bite small and deliberate, sipping her wine as if she were tasting something ancient and bitter.</p>
<p>She followed the conversation with a calm, steady attention that felt both comforting and unnerving. Every so often, when Lily would spill over with some bright, unguarded anecdote, Valentina would offer a small smile, a genuine flicker of warmth that seemed to surprise even Lily herself.</p>
<p>She never interrupted. She never contradicted.</p>
<p>She never imposed her authority. She simply sat there as the quiet, unshakable center of a table teeming with noise, and for several precious minutes, Rex found himself almost deceived.</p>
<p>He almost forgot that he was watching a masterclass in deception, a symphony of carefully maintained illusions.</p>
<p>For that stretch of time, the family gathered around the mahogany table and looked entirely ordinary. They looked like the kind of people who worried about trivialities, who shared meals without the shadow of death looming over their shoulders.</p>
<p>A grandmother and her daughters, sisters sharing old stories; a young man who had emerged from the fires of war to claim his place at the head of the table, not as a conqueror, but as a protector.</p>
<p>The candles flickered in the gentle draft, casting dancing shadows across the walls, and the wine was rich and intoxicating. The roast had been perfect, tender enough to melt, a rare victory in a world that had given them nothing but hardship.</p>
<p>Nobody mentioned Apollo.</p>
<p>Nobody mentioned the blood-soaked banners now fluttering in the streets.</p>
<p>Nobody mentioned the new order rising from the ashes. It was a warm room on a quiet evening, and for one fragile moment, the people in it were very nearly happy.</p>
<p>But the irony of it all was a poisoned blade, and only two people in that room could feel its edge. Rex looked down the length of the table, his gaze cutting through the lamplight to meet Elizabeth’s eyes across the twin silver candlesticks.</p>
<p>She didn’t flinch. She looked back at him with an expression that defied easy categorization; it was not quite a smile of shared understanding, nor was it quite a warning.</p>
<p>It was something colder: a recognition of the grotesque absurdity of their situation. She knew exactly how surreal this charade was—a feast held in the mouth of a wolf.</p>
<p>So did he.</p>
<p>They shared that recognition in a loaded silence, a private, twisted joke that no one else could be allowed to hear, lest the entire fragile reality shatter like thin ice.</p>
<p>When the plates had finally been cleared of their remnants and the last of the wine had been poured into crimson pools at the bottom of their glasses, Rex set his linen napkin on the table.</p>
<p>The soft rustle of fabric against wood seemed unnaturally loud in the sudden lull of conversation.</p>
<p>He stood.</p>
<p>The shift in atmosphere was instantaneous. The room quieted as if the air itself had been sucked out of it.</p>
<p>Lily looked up at him, her chin resting in her palm, her eyes soft and slightly glassy from the wine, pupils dilated with a dangerous vulnerability.</p>
<p>Diana set her glass down with a controlled click, her body instinctively tensing as she reverted to her default state of alertness.</p>
<p>Elizabeth watched him with a careful, analytical calm, her mind already calculating what came next.</p>
<p>Valentina simply dropped her gaze to the table, her silence returning to that impenetrable, watchful stillness.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t move to dominate them. He didn’t raise his voice.</p>
<p>Instead, he held out his hand toward the room, palm upturned in an invitation that felt both generous and inevitable. He spoke in the same quiet, velvet tone he had maintained all evening, the tone of a man who had already won.</p>
<p>"The night is long," he said, his dark eyes catching the firelight as they swept across each of their faces. "And we have all of it."</p>
