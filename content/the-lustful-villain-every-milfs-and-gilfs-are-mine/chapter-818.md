<p>Rex met her gaze, his eyes steady and unblinking.</p>
<p>"I try to do what I say I will do," he said, his voice carrying the weight of a fundamental law. "When I say yes, the variable is set..."</p>
<p>"When I say yes, it means it."</p>
<p>Iris studied him. Her internal ’read’—that razor-sharp, assassin-class perceptive function—was running at full capacity, scanning the architecture of his presence.</p>
<p>What she found was a glitch in her own mental mapping. It was the specific, unsettling experience of reading a person you thought you had modeled, only to realize the model was missing an entire dimension, a depth, or a darkness you hadn’t even known was absent.</p>
<p>"I know," she said, her voice dropping into a contemplative register. "That is the thing about you that I have been trying to understand since the canyon, or perhaps since the first time we met in the village."</p>
<p>She turned her gaze back to the stone path, her profile sharp against the fading light. "Most people’s consistency is conditional."</p>
<p>"They are reliable within a certain temperature range, but once the pressure rises or the heat becomes too intense, they fracture..."</p>
<p>"They become something else entirely. But you... you are consistent at temperatures most people cannot even survive."</p>
<p>Rex remained silent, letting the observation hang in the air between them like a dormant charge.</p>
<p>"That is not a compliment, exactly," she added, her tone clinical. "It is an observation."</p>
<p>"The implications of that kind of stability depend entirely on the purpose of that consistency." And that is the part of you that I still do not have complete access to."</p>
<p>"No," Rex replied simply.</p>
<p>"You are not going to give me access to it, are you?"</p>
<p>"I am not in the habit of granting complete access to anything," Rex said, and for the first time, there was a hint of something almost like a shared understanding in his voice. "That is a universal truth of the people in my life... It is not specific to you."</p>
<p>"I know it isn’t specific to me," she countered, her eyes flashing with a brief, sharp intelligence. "What I am telling you is that I notice it more with you than with anyone else."</p>
<p>"Which is, in itself, a significant piece of information."</p>
<p>Rex held her gaze for a heartbeat longer than necessary, then turned his eyes to the path ahead. He processed the data in the specific register he reserved for unexpected, high-value results: ’Iris Morr Nightwing is significantly more perceptive than the combat designation the Academy has assigned her.’</p>
<p>He filed that observation alongside the [40/100] and continued walking.</p>
<p>They reached the junction where the residential corridor split. Iris stopped at the division, her body turning slightly toward him, her eyes narrowing with the gold-toned intensity of her perceptive function.</p>
<p>"The training offer," she said, cutting straight to the tactical core. "Aurelia’s offer."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her, waiting.</p>
<p>"Is it open to family?" she asked.</p>
<p>Rex understood the subtext immediately. She wasn’t just asking about a curriculum; she was asking about a tether.</p>
<p>She was asking for a way to integrate herself into the orbit of the man who had fundamentally altered the mathematics of her world. A subtle, genuine warmth touched his expression, not a social mask, but the quiet satisfaction of a strategist watching a long-term plan coalesce at the exact level required.</p>
<p>"Yes," he said.</p>
<p>She held his gaze for one second longer than was strictly necessary for confirmation, the precise duration of someone who has received a vital answer and is spending one final moment memorizing the face of the person who gave it before filing it away.</p>
<p>"Alright," she said.</p>
<p>She gave a single, sharp nod, the quintessential Iris acknowledgment of reception without the need for elaboration, and stepped onto the left path.</p>
<p>She had taken approximately four steps when she stopped dead.</p>
<p>She didn’t turn around fully, but she pivoted enough that the hard, elegant line of her jaw was visible, and he could see the tension in her profile, the telltale sign of a thought that had escaped her control.</p>
<p>"Rex," she said.</p>
<p>"Yes," he replied.</p>
<p>"I do not know what to do with the fact that you were the one who stopped it," she said, her voice suddenly raw, the assassin’s composure fraying at the edges. "The meteor..."</p>
<p>"What the world would have been if you had not been there." She fell silent, the weight of the hypothetical carnage pressing down on the corridor. "I keep running the simulation... the version where you were not there."</p>
<p>"And in every version, the number of coffins is significantly larger than the one we left in the garden today."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing. He gave her the space to exist in that terrifying math.</p>
<p>"I do not know," she said, her voice trembling almost imperceptibly, "what that means for what I feel about you."</p>
<p>"It does not cancel anything... It doesn’t erase the grief or the history..."</p>
<p>"But it is sitting there, right beside everything else, and I have not been able to separate it from the rest."</p>
<p>She didn’t wait for him to solve the equation. She didn’t wait for an answer.</p>
<p>She simply stepped back onto the left path and vanished into the shadows of the corridor.</p>
<p>Rex stood at the junction of the paths for exactly the amount of time required to let the weight of her confession settle into its proper place in his mind.</p>
<p>Then, he filed it and walked on.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Two minutes later, Aurelia and Elaris approached in perfect synchronization, breaking the silence of the corridor.</p>
<p>Aurelia walked with the measured, heavy bearing of a woman who had been architecting this specific conversation since the last moment of the ceremony. Every step was a calculation; she had organized her thoughts, her words, and her intent into a structure as solid as the stone walls around them.</p>
<p>Elaris, by contrast, moved with a lighter, more fluid grace. She was there to support, yes, but there was a glint in her eyes, the specific, private satisfaction she derived from witnessing moments of profound, unvarnished truth.</p>
<p>"Rex Rexilion," Aurelia said. Her voice was clear, devoid of the performative grief of the afternoon, replaced by a stark, formal gravity.</p>
<p>"Aurelia," Rex replied.</p>
<p>"I said it yesterday, and I will say it again here," she continued, her eyes locking onto his with unyielding focus. "In a place where there are no ruins to stand in and no emergency to justify the necessity of directness... the Nightwing household owes you an accounting."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. "You don’t."</p>
<p>"We do," she countered, her voice sharpening. "What Theo did in the street..."</p>
<p>"The way we allowed the arranged marriage situation to spiral into a farce of politics and pride..."</p>
<p>"The collective, unthinking behavior of this household toward you since the moment you stepped foot in Aethelgard." She held his gaze with the terrifying, flat directness of a woman performing a difficult task and intending to execute it with surgical precision. "None of it reflects a household that deserves the regard of a man who has repeatedly demonstrated the caliber of his soul."</p>
<p>"The household’s regard was never a variable I required for my own function," Rex said, his tone neutral, dismissing the social weight of her words.</p>
<p>"You should have," Elaris interjected.</p>
<p>She stepped forward, her voice carrying that signature warmth, a teasing, affectionate lilt that served as a velvet sheath for a blade of absolute sincerity. "Because you are undeniably deserving of it."</p>
<p>"I said so the very first time we met and was, unfortunately, overruled by people who possess significantly less perception than I do."</p>
<p>She cast a sidelong glance at Aurelia as she spoke, a sharp, triumphant look, the unmistakable expression of someone scoring a long-awaited point in a game that had been played in silence for months.</p>
<p>Aurelia received the look with the dignified composure of a commander accepting a hard-won victory on the battlefield. She did not contest it; she had earned the concession.</p>
<p>"You were right, Mother..." Aurelia said to Elaris, her tone carrying the heavy, humbling weight of an admission made by someone who had been wrong for a long time and finally had the courage to own it.</p>
<p>She turned her attention back to Rex, her expression softening into something more human. "My mother was right... And I was not."</p>
<p>Rex watched the exchange, the data points of their interpersonal dynamics settling into place.</p>
<p>"Elaris has a talent," Rex observed, his voice steady, "for reading a situation correctly long before the evidence has even manifested to confirm the reading."</p>
<p>"I first noted the accuracy of that talent at Silver Rest."</p>
