<p>The air in the tower seemed to solidify, turning from a gas to something viscous and oppressive, like breathing liquid mercury. Celestina could feel the cost of her proposal settling in her lungs.</p>
<p>She began to lay out the true mechanics of the slaughter, not of bodies but of causality itself.</p>
<p>She described how she would use the loop’s duration to plant Legion assets like poisonous seeds within Aethelgard’s power structure. She spoke of cultivating specific social and political pressures that would inevitably drive the Apostle network toward the precise psychological state required to trigger the Reset.</p>
<p>The end goal was not salvation; it was a controlled collapse. At the moment of maximum vulnerability, the working would reach its full expression, unraveling every achievement, every alliance, and every scrap of progress the reincarnators had clawed from this world, snapping them back to the sterile starting point she had defined.</p>
<p>"Not all of them will survive the reset," Kaeleth interrupted. His voice wasn’t a sound; it was a seismic shift that rattled Celestina’s ribcage. "The reset will not merely erase their progress..."</p>
<p>"It will erase their existence."</p>
<p>"No," Celestina agreed, her voice flat, stripped of all pretense.</p>
<p>"Those whose survival in the current timeline is contingent upon developments the reset will undo," Kaeleth continued, the divine pressure intensifying until Celestina felt a sharp, stabbing pain behind her eyes. "Their children... Their legacies... The very foundations of their lives..."</p>
<p>"They will be snuffed out like candles in a hurricane."</p>
<p>"Yes," she said.</p>
<p>"Say their names to me now," Kaeleth commanded.</p>
<p>The command was absolute, a physical force that demanded submission. "Not because I require it for my records."</p>
<p>"But because I want to witness the moment you confront the reality of what you are asking..."</p>
<p>"I want to know if you have truly considered the specific souls this mechanism will annihilate or if you have merely reduced them to convenient abstractions."</p>
<p>Celestina did not answer immediately. For the first time since she had stepped into this impossible space, the silence was hers.</p>
<p>It was not a divine pause but a human one, a moment where her mind raced through the terrifying calculus of her own ambition. A flash of memory struck her: the face of a young girl she had hunted three years ago, the way her bones had snapped like dry kindling under the pressure of a Legion operative’s strike, the wet, gurgling sound of her final breath.</p>
<p>That girl had been a person. In Aethelgard, there would be thousands more.</p>
<p>"I do not know their names yet," Celestina said finally.</p>
<p>Her voice was hoarse, but it did not waver. "The working has not begun, and the timeline is still fluid."</p>
<p>"I do not know who will be born within its radius by the time the reset condition is met..."</p>
<p>"I do not know which innocents will build lives that depend on a sequence of events that the loop will inevitably erase, so I am asking you to help me eliminate people I cannot currently name."</p>
<p>"That does not trouble you," Kaeleth said. It wasn’t an accusation; it was a diagnosis.</p>
<p>"It troubles me exactly as much as it should," Celestina snapped back, the fire in her blood momentarily overriding her caution. "Which is to say, it does not stop me."</p>
<p>"Because twelve years ago, I made a choice. I decided that the alternative, a world where the disruption continues to compound, where your observation function falls further behind the curve, where the imbalance between this reality and the infiltrators grows until the fabric of existence itself tears—that troubles me more."</p>
<p>"I am not asking you to make this comfortable for me, Kaeleth..."</p>
<p>"I am not asking for absolution... but I am asking you to make it possible."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavier than anything she had experienced. It felt as though the tower itself were holding its breath, waiting for the verdict of a god.</p>
<p>"You are asking me to perform a sustained temporal working on a city of forty thousand people," Kaeleth said, his voice dripping with the cold irony of eternity. "All to facilitate the elimination of twenty to forty reincarnators."</p>
<p>"I am asking you to perform a working that addresses the systemic threat those twenty to forty reincarnators represent," Celestina corrected, leaning into the danger. "You have been trying to solve this problem through legitimate channels for two hundred years."</p>
<p>"You have been playing at chess while the board itself is being eaten by termites."</p>
<p>"The population is growing faster than your ability to count them."</p>
<p>"My approach is not elegant... It is not pretty either, but... it is effective."</p>
<p>"What do you offer in exchange for this violation of causality?" Kaeleth asked. "What could a mortal possibly provide to a god who has seen everything?"</p>
<p>"My organization’s continued operation," Celestina said, her eyes locking onto the void with terrifying certainty. "Not just in Aethelgard, but everywhere..."</p>
<p>"The infection you see in Aethelgard exists in every major population center in this world."</p>
<p>"You are drowning in details, Kaeleth..."</p>
<p>"I am offering to be the scalpel that cleans the wound at scale, indefinitely, and... I am offering you the one thing you lack: control."</p>
<p>The silence that followed her offer was not merely quiet; it was a physical phenomenon. The air in the tower grew dense, charged with an ancient, terrifying energy that made the fine hairs on Celestina’s arms stand on end.</p>
<p>It felt as though she were standing at the epicenter of a collapsing star. Kaeleth was processing not with the linear deliberation of a human mind but with the vast, multithreaded calculation of a deity who perceived all possible futures simultaneously.</p>
<p>"The divine framework’s code prohibits direct intervention on behalf of individual mortals," Kaeleth said finally.</p>
<p>His voice didn’t just echo; it resonated through her very marrow, each syllable carrying the weight of unchangeable law. "You ask for a weapon of mass erasure, Celestina."</p>
<p>"You ask for a violation of the natural flow that borders on heresy."</p>
<p>"I am not asking you to act on my behalf," Celestina countered immediately, her voice cutting through the divine pressure like a scalpel through necrotic flesh. "I am asking you to perform a temporal management function that is fundamentally consistent with your divine mandate."</p>
<p>She took a step forward, refusing to let the intimidation break her stride. "The management of timeline disruptions is your domain."</p>
<p>"Aethelgard’s concentrated reincarnator population is not a community; it is a tumor..."</p>
<p>"It is a concentrated, destabilizing disruption in the causality of this world."</p>
<p>"My proposed working does not create a new problem; it addresses an existing one."</p>
<p>"That interpretation is creative," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>The words were laced with something dangerously close to amusement, the kind of amusement a child might show before pulling the wings off an insect.</p>
<p>"It is accurate," Celestina said. Her pulse was hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird, but her expression remained as cold and unyielding as marble.</p>
<p>"Those two things are not always the same," Kaeleth observed.</p>
<p>The divine aura around him flared briefly, a flash of gold and shadow that made the edges of Celestina’s vision blur. "Though I notice you have arranged your language so that they appear to be every single time you have spoken tonight."</p>
<p>"You describe personal favors as management functions..."</p>
<p>"You describe mass extermination as addressing disruptions."</p>
<p>"You have a remarkable talent for finding the precise architecture of a sentence that allows both of us to agree to it for entirely different reasons."</p>
<p>Celestina felt a bead of sweat trail down her spine, cold as ice. Her mind flashed to the countless briefings she had given to frightened senators and horrified council members, the years spent twisting the truth until it became a weapon she could wield without breaking it.</p>
<p>"I have had twelve years of practice explaining what my organization does to people who would revolt if I used less careful language," Celestina said, her voice hardening. "I am not going to apologize for the practice showing."</p>
<p>"I did not ask for an apology," Kaeleth said, and suddenly the pressure vanished, replaced by a stillness so absolute it was terrifying. "I asked you to notice that I see exactly what you are doing."</p>
<p>"There is a profound difference between a mortal who manipulates language because she believes I am too blind to notice and a mortal who manipulates language because she knows I see through every syllable and she does it anyway, trusting that I will find the arrangement useful enough to accept regardless."</p>
<p>He leaned into her space, the void of his presence overwhelming her senses. "I want to know which one you are."</p>
<p>The question hung between them like a guillotine blade suspended by a single thread. Celestina didn’t blink.</p>
<p>She didn’t hesitate. The time for posturing had ended; now there was only the raw truth of her ambition.</p>
<p>"The second," she said, each word precise and unshakeable. "I would not insult you by assuming a god who has managed a domain for two thousand years cannot see through careful phrasing."</p>
<p>"I am not hiding the nature of this arrangement from you."</p>
<p>She met the void with everything she had. "I am giving you language you can use with the rest of the divine community if this is ever questioned and we both know it will be."</p>
<p>"I am not just making a request, Kaeleth..."</p>
<p>"I am doing you the courtesy of preparing your legal defense at the same moment I am offering you the solution."</p>
