<p>There was one final, excruciating detail: one small crack in the flawless porcelain of Rex’s new world. The tears streaming down Lily’s and Diana’s faces were not illusions.</p>

<p>They were not conjured by magic. They were real.</p>

<p>Their hands trembled against Leonidas’s arms, their knuckles white with the effort of holding on.</p>

<p>Their mouths remained curved in those terrifying, serene smiles, chanting his name with ecstatic fervor, but behind those smiles, buried under a spiritual weight they could never hope to lift, lay something else.</p>

<p>Deep beneath the violet euphoria, in the microscopic fissures of their consciousness, something was screaming. It was the raw, primal shriek of their true selves, their actual desires, and their actual fears being crushed into submission.</p>

<p>The [Idolatry Dominion] was not a perfect erasure; it was a violation.</p>

<p>It lay over them like a suffocating shroud, forcing them to perform devotion while their souls wept in the dark.</p>

<p>Rex began to walk across the plaza.</p>

<p>TAP. TAP. TAP.</p>

<p>His boots struck the stones in deliberate contrast to the thunderous, rhythmic chanting of the city. He moved through the haze of smoke and ash, his torn coat billowing in a wind that carried the golden flakes of the dying [Verdict].</p>

<p>He looked like a conqueror walking through the ruins of a fallen temple, utterly at home amidst the devastation.</p>

<p>He stopped two steps away from them, towering over the broken form of Evelyn, who lay like a discarded weapon between the three witnesses.</p>

<p>He let the silence stretch. He let the weight of his presence settle over Helena, Leonidas, and Elizabeth like a guillotine blade hovering at the neck.</p>

<p>"I’ve won," Rex said finally.</p>

<p>His voice wasn’t loud, but it cut through the chanting like a scalpel. "I won a long time ago... You’re all just finally noticing the score."</p>

<p>He turned his gaze slowly, methodically, savoring the torment. He looked at Helena, whose face was a mask of pale, frozen hatred.</p>

<p>He looked at Leonidas, whose jaw trembled with the effort to speak, his teeth clicking together involuntarily.</p>

<p>Finally, he looked down at Evelyn. Half of her face was a crimson ruin, her eyes bloodshot and swollen, yet they remained open, fixed on him with a defiance that refused to die.</p>

<p>"Which means," Rex continued, a slow, hungry smile spreading across his lips, "that all three of you are going to receive a special punishment from me."</p>

<p>A shudder ran through the air. Even the chanting seemed to falter for a heartbeat.</p>

<p>"Not now," he promised, his tone almost gentle, like a father speaking to a child. "Not today..."</p>

<p>"But I’ve given it a great deal of thought... I’ve been very creative."</p>

<p>"I’ve made it different for each of you, tailored to exactly what you value most."</p>

<p>He did not elaborate. He didn’t give them the mercy of knowing what to fear.</p>

<p>He let the threat hang in the air like a poisoned fog, letting their own imaginations do the work of breaking them.</p>

<p>He took his time, studying their reactions with the detached interest of a scientist observing insects in a jar. He was in no hurry at all; he had already won, and now he was simply savoring the aftertaste.</p>

<p>Evelyn’s split lips moved. A bubble of blood popped against her teeth.</p>

<p>Her voice was so thin, so fragile, that it seemed as though the next gust of wind would carry it away forever. Yet, she forced her broken neck to move.</p>

<p>She forced her eyes to find his, not with the defiance of a warrior now, but with the desperate plea of a mother.</p>

<p>"Elliot..." she whispered, the name breaking on her lips. "Please... Rex... I’m begging you."</p>

<p>A fresh tear tracked through the blood on her cheek.</p>

<p>"Don’t hurt Elliot...! He has nothing to do with this!"</p>

<p>"He’s innocent... he isn’t even awake yet!"</p>

<p>"Do anything to me! Break every bone in my body again! Take my soul!"</p>

<p>"Destroy everything I am. But please..." Her voice cracked into a sob that tore through her chest. "Not him... Anything but him."</p>

<p>Rex stared down at her for several seconds. His face was a void—no anger, no triumph, no cruelty.</p>

<p>He simply looked at her with the detached interest one might show an insect twitching on a pin.</p>

<p>Then, with deliberate, agonizing slowness, he lifted his right foot.</p>

<p>SHHHH...!</p>

<p>The sole of his boot made contact with her temple. He did not slam it down.</p>

<p>He did not seek to crush her skull. Instead, he placed it there with terrifying gentleness, applying pressure millimeter by millimeter.</p>

<p>He pressed until her cheek was ground flat against the jagged stone, until the blood beneath her face spread in a thin, crimson halo around her head, and until her breath came in wet, shallow gasps that sounded like tearing parchment.</p>

<p>"You speak of him as if he ever mattered," Rex said.</p>

<p>His voice had dropped to a register that was almost intimate, almost tender.</p>

<p>It was the tone a predator uses when it has already cornered its prey and no longer needs to growl. "Listen carefully, Evelyn..."</p>

<p>"Let the words sink through the pain."</p>

<p>Evelyn’s eyes rolled back slightly, her pupils contracting as she struggled to maintain consciousness through the grinding pressure against her skull.</p>

<p>"Elliot Von Starlight is a very lucky man today," Rex continued. "Not because of your prayers..."</p>

<p>"Not because of your sacrifice..."</p>

<p>"He is lucky because he stayed in his bed."</p>

<p>"He stayed warm, safe, and ignorant while other people bled to pay for his existence..."</p>

<p>"He didn’t lift a finger... He let you carry the weight of the world."</p>

<p>"That passivity... that cowardice... that is the only reason his life is counted as safe."</p>

<p>CREAK.</p>

<p>The sound of Evelyn’s jaw grinding against the stone echoed in the sudden lulls between the city’s chants. Rex pressed a fraction harder.</p>

<p>A stifled, broken whimper escaped her throat, a sound of pure, unadulterated humiliation that hurt worse than any broken bone.</p>

<p>"And you should be thankful," he whispered, leaning down slightly so only she could hear the venom beneath the honey, "that I didn’t drag him here to watch you break."</p>

<p>"I could have made him kneel beside you..."</p>

<p>"I could have made him watch as I unmade everything you ever loved."</p>

<p>"I could have made him join the chant!!!"</p>

<p>He left the threat hanging there, a poisoned blade suspended between them. He didn’t need to finish it.</p>

<p>He knew the image would fester in her mind long after her body healed.</p>

<p>Rex remained there, standing motionless with his boot planted on her broken skull. He stood in the center of a ruined empire, a god of ash and violet light.</p>

<p>Around him, a city of hundreds of thousands remained on its knees, their voices rising in a hypnotic, endless tide of worship: "HAIL REX REXILION!"</p>

<p>The violet mark of his dominion and the crimson mark of his wrath twisted together across his skin like living serpents, pulsing in time with the city’s heartbeat.</p>

<p>Helena and Leonidas could only watch, their eyes wide with the horror of the living dead, helpless to intervene, helpless to even weep properly.</p>

<p>Over the entire tableau of devastation, the autumn sky of Aethelgard began to bleed into evening, the gold of sunset mixing with the violet haze of the corruption, casting long, distorted shadows across the carnage.</p>

<p>But Rex was not alone in his victory.</p>

<p>Far beyond the borders of Aethelgard, far across the vast and shifting continents of Erosyne, the air itself seemed to grow cold.</p>

<p>In distant temples, in hidden sanctums, and in realms beyond mortal comprehension, a few select pairs of eyes divine, ancient, and terrifyingly silent had been watching.</p>

<p>They had witnessed the fall of the Light, the breaking of the Queen, and the birth of something new.</p>

<p>And they were not pleased.</p>
