<p>In this house, belonging to Alexander and Elizabeth, only the two of them were present, along with the documents and the morning light streaming through the windows of Elizabeth’s sitting room. This light was different from the windows and light she had woken up to for the past three days.</p>
<p>Rex reached across the table to indicate something on the document, and this time he kept his hand there instead of moving it back immediately, while Elizabeth continued to avoid looking at the document.</p>
<p>She looked at his hand.</p>
<p>"We’re not going to get much more work done today," she said.</p>
<p>"No," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I knew that when I suggested coming here," she said.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said.</p>
<p>She looked up at him.</p>
<p>"I wanted to be somewhere that was mine," she said. "Where I could make whatever choices I was going to make in my own space."</p>
<p>Rex watched her.</p>
<p>"That probably sounds strange," she said.</p>
<p>"No," he said.</p>
<p>She stood up from the table and came around it, which was a decision that had no hesitation in it, the decision of someone who had finished deciding and was now simply doing, and she put her hand against his chest the way she had put her hand against his face that morning, just there, and looked at him.</p>
<p>"I’m not who I was a week ago," she said. "I don’t entirely know what to do with that."</p>
<p>"You don’t have to know yet," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Alexander—"</p>
<p>"Elizabeth," Rex said.</p>
<p>She stopped.</p>
<p>"You called Alexander’s name twice yesterday and both times you stopped yourself," he said. "You don’t have to stop yourself."</p>
<p>"Say what you’re going to say."</p>
<p>She was quiet for a moment.</p>
<p>"He loves me," she said. "That’s what I was going to say. He loves me genuinely and I know it and I am standing in the sitting room of our house and I am not thinking about him."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>"I don’t know what that makes me," she said.</p>
<p>"Human," Rex said.</p>
<p>She looked at him for a long moment. Then something in her expression shifted, the last of the management giving way to whatever was underneath it, and she said, "Stop being reasonable about it. It doesn’t help."</p>
<p>And then she kissed him, harder than that morning, the way someone kissed when they were done with the conversation and had been done with it for longer than they had admitted.</p>
<p>The documents stayed on the table. The ledgers stayed closed. The afternoon light moved across the sitting room in the slow way afternoon light moved, and the house was quiet, and nobody interrupted.</p>
<p>By evening the light in the sitting room had changed completely and the cross-reference was still unfinished on the table.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was sitting on the sitting room floor with her back against the sofa, and Rex was beside her. She had her head against his shoulder in a way that she was aware was not how she normally occupies space, which she was not going to correct right now.</p>
<p>"Lily is going to know the cross-reference wasn’t what we were doing," she said.</p>
<p>"Lily already knows," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Diana too," she said.</p>
<p>"Diana knew before we left," Rex said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was quiet.</p>
<p>"I don’t mind," she said, which surprised her slightly when she heard herself say it. "I thought I would. But I don’t."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>"That’s new," she said. "I mind everything..."</p>
<p>"I am constitutionally disposed to minding things."</p>
<p>"You mind them when they matter," Rex said. "You’ve decided this doesn’t."</p>
<p>She thought about that.</p>
<p>"Or I’ve decided it matters differently than I expected it to," she said.</p>
<p>Outside, Aethelgard was settling into the specific quiet of early evening, as people returned home and the streets buzzed with the ordinary hustle of the day winding down.</p>
<p>Inside the sitting room, it was warm and still, and Elizabeth felt a heightened awareness of everything around her, as if she were truly present in the moment rather than just managing her place within it.</p>
<p>Neither of them moved for another minute.</p>
<p>Then Elizabeth stood, straightening her clothes with the automatic precision that came naturally to her, while Rex also rose to his feet. She gathered the documents from the table, arranging them in a way that suggested she had spent her day productively.</p>
<p>Rex observed her with a specific expression that hinted at amusement, though he remained silent about it.</p>
<p>"Don’t," she said.</p>
<p>"I didn’t say anything," Rex said.</p>
<p>"You were about to," she said.</p>
<p>"I wasn’t," he said.</p>
<p>She looked at him. The corner of his mouth was doing the thing it did.</p>
<p>"You were," she said, and turned toward the door.</p>
<p>Rex followed her into the corridor. She paused for a moment, her hand resting on the door frame as she gazed at the staircase.</p>
<p>The house held a particular quiet that suggested it was aware of its emptiness, distinct from the silence of a place devoid of sound.</p>
<p>She turned to him.</p>
<p>"I want to show you the study," she said.</p>
<p>This was true, yet it didn’t encompass the entirety of what she meant, and they both recognized this, as Elizabeth had ceased to pretend that the partial truth was all there was to say sometime in the middle of the afternoon.</p>
<p>Rex remained silent, simply looking at her, allowing her the space to navigate toward whatever she was reaching for on her own terms.</p>
<p>She ascended the stairs.</p>
<p>He followed.</p>
<p>The study was the second door. She opened it, and as he peered inside, it was exactly as he had anticipated: organized with the specific logic of someone who thrives when everything is visible, bookshelves marked with her own notation system, a desk featuring a lamp that indicated she often worked late, and correspondence arranged by priority rather than date.</p>
<p>"It’s a good room," Rex said.</p>
<p>"It is," she said.</p>
<p>She was standing beside him in the doorway and not going into the room. "I do most of my serious work here when I’m home."</p>
<p>"More serious than the household study room?"</p>
<p>"Different," she said. "The Starlight study room is where I work in front of people."</p>
<p>"This one is where I work when I don’t want anyone to know what I’m working on."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her.</p>
<p>"It’s an important distinction," she said.</p>
<p>He said, "I know."</p>
<p>She was quiet for a moment. Then she moved away from the study door and stopped at the next one down the corridor, which was closed, and she put her hand on the handle and looked at him over her shoulder.</p>
<p>"This is the other room," she said.</p>
<p>The other room was not the study.</p>
<p>Rex held her gaze.</p>
<p>She opened the door, stepped inside, and left it ajar, creating a clear invitation that required no explicit words.</p>
<p>Rex went in. ’Here it is... the one that I’ve been waiting for.’</p>
<p>’I can’t believe we’re going to use his room as a new love nest for today.’</p>
<p>The room was hers in the way that spaces belong to those who have lived in them long enough to move beyond decorating and simply exist within them.</p>
<p>A book lay on the table beside the bed, a marker tucked inside. A second lamp stood nearby.</p>
<p>This specific arrangement of items suggested that the room was occupied by someone when they were not performing for anyone else.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was standing near the window with her arms crossed, not in a defensive manner, but in the way she typically held herself when she was in a situation she had not yet fully decided how to engage with.</p>
<p>"I don’t know how to do this part," she said.</p>
<p>"I mean the—" She paused. "I know what happens..."</p>
<p>"I meant I don’t know how to be the version of myself in this room that I’d like to be."</p>
<p>"What version is that?" Rex said.</p>
<p>She looked at him.</p>
<p>"The one that’s not managing anything," she said.</p>
<p>Rex crossed the room and stopped in front of her, close enough that she had to tilt her chin up slightly to hold his gaze, which she did.</p>
<p>"Then stop managing," he said.</p>
<p>She looked at him for a long moment, then uncrossed her arms and reached up to take his face in both hands, just as she had that morning. She leaned in, her voice quiet, so that her words were meant only for the space between them.</p>
<p>"Give me one good night today... Without holding back."</p>
<p>"I don’t care if this is my and his bedroom..."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her.</p>
<p>"Don’t be careful with me," she said. "I don’t need any gentleness today...’</p>
<p>He placed his hands over hers, gently holding them for a brief moment.</p>
<p>Then he said, "I know."</p>
<p>She kissed him, and this time it was free of management, devoid of the careful architecture of someone weighing the decision to act. It was simply the act itself.</p>
<p>Outside the window, Aethelgard transitioned into evening, and the house remained quiet around them. Neither of them thought about anything beyond the confines of the room they occupied.</p>
<p>The book on the side table remained untouched. The corridor beyond the closed door lay empty. The lamp on the table emitted a warm glow characteristic of rooms being used as intended.</p>
<p>And Elizabeth Von Starlight, who had meticulously planned and managed every detail of her life for so long that the act of management had become indistinguishable from her very identity, chose not to oversee anything for the rest of the night.</p>
<p>That, too, was new.</p>
<p>And she didn’t mind it at all.</p>
