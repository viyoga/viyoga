<p>A cold, predatory satisfaction settled deep in Rex’s chest. It wasn’t the simple pleasure of winning a fight; it was something far more complex: the intoxicating euphoria of a grandmaster who had executed a multi-layered gambit.</p>
<p>His plan hadn’t just succeeded; it had succeeded on three simultaneous planes: he had secured power, he had eliminated a threat, and he had erased the evidence. A single-purpose plan was a hammer; this was a scalpel made of starlight.</p>
<p>He turned his back on the crumbling god and stepped through the domain’s boundary.</p>
<p>FWOOSH.</p>
<p>The transition was violent. The pressurized, divine atmosphere of the domain shattered as the standard physical plane rushed in to fill the vacuum.</p>
<p>The sudden return of gravity and normal air pressure made his muscles tense, but Rex didn’t stumble. He had become accustomed to walking between worlds.</p>
<p>But before he allowed himself to simply walk away, he had one final debt to settle with reality.</p>
<p>"One last correction," Rex muttered, his voice carrying a new, terrifying weight.</p>
<p>He didn’t just walk toward the settlement; he reached back into the temporal stream one more time.</p>
<p>His mind dove into the messy, corrupted data left behind by Theo’s temporal distortion field, the chaotic aftermath that had left children screaming in agony as their very bodies forgot how to grow.</p>
<p>He remembered the scene: children convulsing on the ground, their skin rippling unnaturally as their cellular timelines spun out of control.</p>
<p>Apollo had been there, desperately trying to use Life affinity to patch the holes, but Apollo was trying to heal the wound. Rex was going to rewrite the injury.</p>
<p>Rex activated the temporal substrate’s management function with surgical precision. Guided by the stolen operational knowledge, he didn’t just apply ’healing’; he applied Restoration.</p>
<p>In his mind’s eye, he saw the children’s cellular timelines twisted, knotted threads of biological time that were fraying and breaking. He saw the cellular decay, the way their bodies were aging and de-aging in microsecond spasms, tearing through membranes and snapping DNA strands.</p>
<p>"Straighten out," Rex commanded, his will flowing into the substrate. "Return to the correct sequence."</p>
<p>"Now."</p>
<p>He channeled the authority downward, past the flesh, past the bone, into the very quantum foundation of their biology. He felt the resistance to the universe’s natural tendency toward entropy, but he crushed it.</p>
<p>He forced the disrupted cellular development timelines to snap back into alignment.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a flash of light; it was a quiet, terrifyingly efficient correction. Inside the bodies of the afflicted children, the chaotic spinning stopped. The microscopic tearing ceased.</p>
<p>The cellular ’noise’ smoothed into a perfect, silent hum. The children who had been prematurely aged, their bones brittle, their skin lined with decades of false time, suddenly felt the bones soften and reset.</p>
<p>The children who had been regressed, their organs failing because they lacked the development to function, felt their systems solidify and mature in a heartbeat.</p>
<p>It was the specific, quiet completeness of a broken machine suddenly finding its perfect gear. They weren’t just ’healed’; they were correct.</p>
<p>Rex exhaled sharply as he severed the connection. The strain left him momentarily lightheaded, but the work was done.</p>
<p>Turning his attention inward, he began the process of administrative consolidation. He couldn’t leave such a volatile power exposed in his active consciousness.</p>
<p>With practiced movements of his mind, he accessed the Soul Register’s organizational structure. He found the specific, encrypted vault designed for long-term storage of high-tier capabilities, the kind of power that was too dangerous to wield casually but too precious to discard.</p>
<p>[TEMPORAL AUTHORITY: ARCHIVING...]</p>
<p>[LOCATION: SECURE SECTOR SUB LAYER 9]</p>
<p>[STATUS: ENCRYPTED AND STABILIZED.]</p>
<p>"And the lie," Rex whispered. "Keep it running."</p>
<p>He set the perceptual overlay to its minimum expression. He programmed it to run as a background process, a passive, automatic maintenance loop that would continue to project the image of a peaceful Aethelgard to any divine observer, requiring zero conscious effort from him.</p>
<p>The deception would become as natural as breathing.</p>
<p>Then, he put it all away. The amber glow in his eyes died completely, replaced by the sharp, calculating steel of a man who knew exactly who he was.</p>
<p>A lesson had been burned into his mind today: The first principle of power was not to use it. To wield power constantly was to show weakness; it was an admission that you were still reacting to the world around you.</p>
<p>True power was having the ability to unmake reality and choosing to let reality exist exactly as you commanded. The people who burned through their authority to solve every problem were the desperate people who weren’t yet certain of their position.</p>
<p>Rex’s position in Aethelgard was no longer in question. He was no longer a pawn moving across the board; he was the hand that held the pieces.</p>
<p>He didn’t need the temporal authority for the daily grind of survival. He didn’t need it to eat, to sleep, or to fight petty battles.</p>
<p>He needed it for the moment when the daily operation of the world required something that only the fabric of time itself could provide.</p>
<p>That moment was not today. But when it came, he would be ready.</p>
<p>Rex turned away from the echoes of divine war and began the long walk back toward the heart of Aethelgard.</p>
<p>The city was different now. The air didn’t carry the sharp, metallic tang of fresh blood from his battle with Kaeleth; his overlay had scrubbed that away, but it carried something else: the heavy, suffocating stench of collective trauma.</p>
<p>The mourning that clung to the streets had a specific, poisonous quality. It wasn’t the sharp, screaming grief of a single tragedy; it was an accelerated, hollowed-out exhaustion.</p>
<p>The scale of what needed to be processed was simply too vast for the human psyche to absorb.</p>
<p>The deaths from Theo’s temporal distortion field weren’t the overwhelming casualty numbers produced during the Tremor engagement; those had been screams of war, bone-snapping violence, and the wet crunch of soldiers falling in battle.</p>
<p>Those had been military losses, honorable in their brutality.</p>
<p>But this? This was different.</p>
<p>Theo’s field had been an indiscriminate reaper, a silent wave that had torn through civilians without distinction, turning playgrounds into morgues and market squares into graveyards in the span of a single heartbeat.</p>
<p>The funeral was held in the academy’s northern garden. Rex’s boots crunched on the gravel as he approached, the sound unnaturally loud in the oppressive silence.</p>
<p>His stomach twisted with a flicker of recognition. This was the same garden that had received the bodies of the Tremor engagement only a month ago.</p>
<p>A month.</p>
<p>The earth hadn’t even finished absorbing the blood of the soldiers before it was asked to drink again.</p>
<p>The gathering of mourners felt brittle, like glass ready to shatter at the slightest sound. There was no comfort here, no shared strength.</p>
<p>Instead, there was a sense of profound depletion. Repetition hadn’t made the grief familiar; it had made the people thinner.</p>
<p>Their capacity to process loss had been worn down to a raw nerve, leaving them not stronger but more fragile.</p>
<p>Rex stopped at the ceremony’s edge, melting into the shadows of the ancient trees. He didn’t join the weeping, and he didn’t offer empty platitudes.</p>
<p>Instead, he stood there with the flat, terrifyingly comprehensive awareness of a predator observing a herd. He wasn’t just seeing people; he was reading them.</p>
<p>He saw the way shoulders slumped, the way eyes refused to meet, the way hands trembled. He was reading the city’s soul through its agony.</p>
<p>Then, his gaze locked onto Aurelia.</p>
<p>She stood apart from the others. She wasn’t crying, not in the way the others were.</p>
<p>Her stillness was terrifying. It was the stillness of someone holding back a dam that was seconds away from bursting.</p>
<p>Rex could see the layers of her grief, each one more complex than the last, stacked like cordwood.</p>
<p>First, there was the public grief: the deaths in the streets, the innocent lives snuffed out by Theo’s uncontrolled power.</p>
<p>Then there was the institutional grief: the deaths in the academic outer hall, a direct consequence of her own failure as a guardian. She had raised a child who had been handed a divine designation, a weapon of cosmic proportions, and that child had wielded it with the clumsy, devastating hands of an untrained mortal.</p>
<p>But beneath those layers lay something far more intimate and corrosive. Rex saw the wound that no one else could touch.</p>
<p>The city wasn’t mourning the child; they were mourning the damage the child had inflicted. They saw a monster, not a boy.</p>
<p>And Aurelia? She was mourning a son whose last moments weren’t filled with love or understanding but with the harsh, screaming chaos of the street.</p>
<p>Their final conversation hadn’t been a tender goodbye; it had been a desperate argument while Rex stood mere feet away, watching the world unravel.</p>
<p>Every time she closed her eyes, she would hear it. The last thing she ever said to him was shouted over the roar of collapsing reality.</p>
<p>Rex felt a strange, cold pressure in his own chest. He knew what it was to lose control.</p>
<p>He knew what it was to stand in the center of a catastrophe and realize that you were both the victim and the architect.</p>
