<p>The procession began to move, a slow, inexorable tide of iron and blood flowing back through the shimmering tear in reality.</p>
<p>The Underlayer’s army did not march with the frenetic energy of invaders anymore; they moved with the deliberate, heavy confidence of conquerors claiming their inheritance.</p>
<p>They weren’t just passing through a portal; they were stepping into a world they had already broken.</p>
<p>At the vanguard of this grim parade walked Xerollion. His silhouette was carved against the swirling energies of the dimensional gateway, a figure of absolute dominance.</p>
<p>Flanking him were Cassandra and Gorvasha, their weapons still stained with the viscera of their enemies, their breathing still ragged from the adrenaline of slaughter.</p>
<p>Just behind them trailed Mordecai, his usual cocky demeanor stripped away, replaced by a visible, nervous pride that made his hands twitch at his sides. He looked less like a soldier and more like a witness to something divine.</p>
<p>Further back in the column, the contrast was sickening. The surrendered remnants of Aethelgard’s army marched in a broken file, their heads bowed as if the very air of the corridor had become too heavy to bear.</p>
<p>They moved like ghosts, their boots dragging across the stone with wet, hollow sounds. They were flanked by Underlayer soldiers who no longer bothered to brandish weapons or shout threats; there was no need for force when the psychological annihilation was already complete.</p>
<p>The demons simply watched them with hungry, amused eyes, predators watching prey that had already accepted its fate.</p>
<p>The passage through the Key, the golden, reality-warping gateway, felt fundamentally wrong this time.</p>
<p>On the descent, the gateway had hummed with a specific, electric charge—the righteous fury of soldiers marching toward what they believed was a holy crusade.</p>
<p>It had been an energy born of certainty, of belief that their cause was woven into the fabric of destiny. But on the return journey, that same golden light washed over bodies that had been hollowed out, drained of everything but the raw instinct to persist.</p>
<p>Both demon and human passed through the light in a silence that lacked any true triumph.</p>
<p>By the time they reached the threshold of the other side, there was only a flat, soul-crushing exhaustion, the kind that comes when you realize that surviving a massacre doesn’t actually make you feel alive.</p>
<p>Cassandra walked half a step behind Xerollion’s right shoulder. Her transformation had receded, leaving her skin pale and her breathing shallow, but her eyes remained sharp, scanning the periphery with predator instincts.</p>
<p>Dried blood crusted her forearms like dark armor, the copper tang of it clinging to her clothes.</p>
<p>She leaned closer to him, her voice a low murmur that barely disturbed the air.</p>
<p>"The city will be harder to hold than the corridor was," she said, her tone clinical despite the tremor in her hands. "A corridor has one geometry."</p>
<p>"You can flank it, you can choke it, you can control the flow."</p>
<p>"But a city? A city has thousands of geometries."</p>
<p>"Thousands of alleys, thousands of rooftops, and thousands of terrified people who know every single crack in the pavement."</p>
<p>She glanced back at the broken Aethelgardian line, then back to the path ahead. "They won’t fight like soldiers there."</p>
<p>"They’ll fight like cornered rats... They’ll bleed us from the shadows."</p>
<p>Xerollion didn’t turn his head. His gaze remained fixed on the horizon of the world they were about to re-enter.</p>
<p>"I’m aware," he answered.</p>
<p>His voice was calm, terrifyingly calm, devoid of the heat of battle but carrying the weight of absolute conviction. "That’s precisely why the first thing they see needs to be unambiguous."</p>
<p>The procession spilled out of the dimensional rift and into Aethelgard’s great central plaza, but the city they entered was not the one they had left.</p>
<p>Days ago, this space had been a cauldron of righteous fury, a place where thousands of soldiers had gathered, their armor gleaming under a hopeful sun, their voices raised in hymns of defiance before marching toward the Underlayer.</p>
<p>Now, the plaza was a tomb of living flesh.</p>
<p>News of the massacre at the corridor had outpaced the army. It had spread like arterial spray across a white sheet fast, unstoppable, and devastating.</p>
<p>The civilians who had flooded the square were not there for celebration; they were there because there was nowhere else to run.</p>
<p>The rumor of Apollo’s fall had reached them like a cold wind, spreading through the streets, finding every crack in the city’s psyche like water finding every flaw in a crumbling dam.</p>
<p>A suffocating, unnatural silence descended. It wasn’t just the absence of noise; it was the presence of something predatory.</p>
<p>The moment the masked figure who had shattered the Apostle of Life stepped into the heart of the city, the very air seemed to thicken with dread.</p>
<p>Near the front of the terrified mass, an elderly woman, the baker who had fed the neighborhood for thirty years, stood frozen. Her knuckles were white as she clutched her grandson’s small hand.</p>
<p>Her lips moved in a rapid, silent flutter, a desperate, reflexive prayer to any god that might still be listening. Her eyes were glassy, fixed on the approaching nightmare.</p>
<p>Beside her, a young man, perhaps twenty, stared upward. His expression was a grotesque parody of human emotion: his pupils were blown wide with terror, yet he couldn’t look away.</p>
<p>He was witnessing the fundamental reorganization of reality. He was watching history die and something monstrous being born in its place.</p>
<p>In the periphery, children began to wail. They didn’t understand the tactical implications of a fallen Apostle or the collapse of a military front, but they understood the sudden, bone-deep terror of their parents.</p>
<p>They understood that the adults around them had gone suddenly, unnervingly still, as if waiting for the sky to fall.</p>
<p>Xerollion moved toward the center of the plaza with the deliberate gait of a predator walking through its own territory.</p>
<p>He climbed the wooden platform, the same platform where Apollo Brightsoul had stood mere days ago, his voice booming with promises of divine protection and inevitable victory.</p>
<p>The irony was bitter enough to taste: the same stage that had hosted the prophecy of salvation now served as the throne for its destroyer.</p>
<p>He stood at the edge of the platform, surveying the broken remains of Aethelgard. He saw the shattered soldiers, their eyes hollowed out by trauma; he saw the weeping civilians; he saw the city that thought it was eternal now realizing it was fragile.</p>
<p>He let the silence stretch. He let it become uncomfortable, letting the terror ripen until it was almost unbearable.</p>
<p>Then, he spoke.</p>
<p>"People of Aethelgard."</p>
<p>His voice didn’t just travel through the air; it resonated through the very bones of those present.</p>
<p>Using the Earthen Divine Authority that ran like veins beneath the city’s foundation, he amplified his words until they seemed to rise directly out of the cobblestones themselves.</p>
<p>Each syllable carried the weight of a hammer blow.</p>
<p>"As of today, your city stands under the Underlayer’s authority."</p>
<p>A collective gasp, thin and fragile as breaking glass, rose from the crowd.</p>
<p>"Aethelgard no longer exists as a separate entity opposed to us," Xerollion continued, his gaze cold and unyielding. "Your old gods have failed you."</p>
<p>"Your old laws have crumbled."</p>
<p>"You are no longer citizens of a dying kingdom."</p>
<p>"You are now part of something considerably larger than yourselves."</p>
