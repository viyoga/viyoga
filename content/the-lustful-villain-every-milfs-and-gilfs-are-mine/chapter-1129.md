<p>Across the sprawling, torch-lit grounds of the Grand Academy, Rex navigated the silent corridors until he reached a small, private study room.</p>
<p>This space was typically reserved for the dense, abstract debates of advanced magical theory, but tonight, the air inside was charged with something far more visceral: desperation wrapped in devotion.</p>
<p>Four familiar faces awaited him, gathered around a low table with the particular, unguarded closeness of women who had survived enough trauma together to let formality dissolve into raw honesty.</p>
<p>The moment the door clicked shut behind him, Talyra was moving. She didn’t hesitate, crossing the room in three strides to throw her arms around his neck.</p>
<p>Her embrace was fierce, almost bruising, the kind of unrestrained affection she ruthlessly armored against in public. Her face pressed against his shoulder, her voice muffled but vibrating with emotion.</p>
<p>"We’re going to fight for you down there, Rexy... Do you hear me?"</p>
<p>"Whatever hellscape opens up beneath us, whatever Underlayer abominations they unleash, we aren’t letting them touch you... We’re your shield."</p>
<p>Rex held her, his arms encircling her waist as he allowed himself a moment of private, bitter irony. He felt the sincerity in her heartbeat, the genuine terror for his safety, and he couldn’t bring himself to correct her.</p>
<p>The irony lay in the brutal reality of their coming engagement: she thought she was protecting him, but in the meat grinder that awaited them, Rex would likely be the one carving a path through corpses to keep them breathing.</p>
<p>The protection would be a blood-soaked reversal of her words.</p>
<p>"I know you will," he said quietly, his voice steady despite the storm brewing in his chest.</p>
<p>It was true enough, even if the mechanics of their survival would be written in violence rather than guardianship.</p>
<p>As Talyra reluctantly released him, Aisella stepped forward. Her approach was measured, her movements carrying the serene, unflappable grace that made her such a formidable healer.</p>
<p>She took his hand in both of hers, her palms warm and steady, the hands that had stitched skin and knit bone back together countless times.</p>
<p>"My abilities are fully prepared, Rex," she said, her eyes locking onto his with unnerving clarity. "I have calibrated my reserves for maximum output."</p>
<p>"Whatever wounds you take, however deep, however catastrophic, I will be there to close them..."</p>
<p>"Don’t you dare hold back on my account... Let the blood flow if it means winning."</p>
<p>"I appreciate that, Aisella," Rex replied. He meant it, but the reassurance struck him as almost quaint. He didn’t fear death; he feared the inefficiency of dying at the wrong moment, leaving them exposed.</p>
<p>Nerith leaned back against the edge of the heavy oak study table, her arms crossed over her chest. A faint, almost defiant smile played at the corner of her mouth, though the tension threading through the room was visible even in her relaxed posture.</p>
<p>"The forest always favors those who fight for something worth protecting," she said, her voice carrying that familiar, earthy cadence that sounded like wind through ancient leaves. "Whatever’s waiting for us in that darkness, nature has a way of aligning itself with the righteous."</p>
<p>Rex watched her deliver the line with complete, unshakeable conviction.</p>
<p>’Righteous,’ he thought grimly.</p>
<p>A flash of dark amusement crossed his mind. If she could see the true nature of what lay beneath the twisted, corrupted flesh and the mindless hunger of the Underlayer, she would realize that ’righteousness’ was a luxury they couldn’t afford down there.</p>
<p>They wouldn’t be fighting for justice; they would be fighting for survival.</p>
<p>Mireya, who had remained quieter than the others, waited for the momentary reprieve before speaking. Her voice was low, steady, and carried the weight of someone who understood the hidden costs of power.</p>
<p>"I wanted to thank you again, Rex... Properly..."</p>
<p>"Before we leave this place behind." Her gaze was intense. "For what you did during the Legion incident in the canyon."</p>
<p>"I know the price you paid to keep that secret to keep our names clean while you carried the stain yourself... I haven’t forgotten."</p>
<p>The memory of the canyon flashed through Rex’s mind: the copper tang of blood in the air, the screams of dying men, and the cold, calculated choices he’d made to ensure their survival. The weight of those secrets was a permanent part of him now, a hidden scar beneath his skin.</p>
<p>"You don’t need to thank me for protecting what’s mine," Rex said, his voice dropping to a private murmur.</p>
<p>He reached out, his knuckles brushing gently along her jawline. It was a small gesture, almost tender, but it carried the unspoken promise of a predator who would tear apart anyone who threatened his pack.</p>
<p>Underneath the tenderness, Mireya saw the steel, and her expression softened with a mixture of gratitude and grim acceptance.</p>
<p>Rex lingered in the charged silence of the study for several more minutes, offering each woman a small, deliberate measure of personal attention—a final anchor before the storm.</p>
<p>His palm rested briefly against Talyra’s cheek, feeling the heat of her skin; his fingers traced the delicate pulse point along Aisella’s wrist; and he exchanged a long, weighted look with Nerith that carried more unspoken promise than any declaration could convey.</p>
<p>Finally, he straightened, his gaze sweeping across all four of them, his voice dropping into a tone of absolute authority.</p>
<p>"Once we’re down there, remember the arrangement we’ve maintained," he said, the words cutting through the emotional haze like a blade. "To the rest of the world, to the soldiers, to the enemy, you’re still Apollo’s harem..."</p>
<p>"You are loyal to his banner first... Nothing about the coming fight changes that appearance."</p>
<p>"Whatever blood we spill, whatever hell we walk through, you maintain the deception... No exceptions."</p>
<p>The four women nodded in unison. The practiced ease with which they donned their masks of deception was unsettling; they were veterans of political warfare as much as magical combat.</p>
<p>Rex left the study room a few minutes later, feeling the quiet, cold satisfaction of a man whose foundations remained unshakably stable even as the entire world prepared to fracture around them.</p>
<p>His next stop took him to the Nightwing household’s training yard, where the air smelled of oiled steel and sweat. He found Iris and Aurelia already clad in full combat gear.</p>
<p>Even through the reinforced plates of their armor, the subtle, undeniable curves of their pregnancies were visible physical reminders of the life they carried. Yet, they moved through their warm-up drills with the lethal, disciplined focus of soldiers who refused to let their bodies betray them.</p>
<p>Rex crossed the yard, his boots crunching on the gravel, his expression hardening into the granite like firmness he reserved for conversations that would inevitably draw blood.</p>
<p>"You’re both staying behind," he said.</p>
<p>No preamble. No negotiation. Just a flat statement of fact.</p>
<p>Iris snapped upright immediately. Her assassin’s posture that coiled, deadly stillness returned in an instant, making her seem sharper, more dangerous than she had been a moment before.</p>
<p>"I am not staying behind while my family marches into a slaughterhouse," she said, her voice cutting through the morning air like a whip. "Veylor is dead..."</p>
<p>"I’ve had my closure; I’ve made my peace with the corpse, but that doesn’t mean I intend to sit at home embroidering while the bastards responsible for tearing him from me face judgment."</p>
<p>"I’m going."</p>
<p>"You’re pregnant, Iris," Rex said flatly, his eyes narrowing. "The strain alone could"</p>
<p>"I am well aware of my own condition," Iris shot back, stepping into his space until they were inches apart, her eyes blazing with a feral intensity. "Better than you are, considering I’m the one carrying the child."</p>
<p>"My body has endured worse than a few magical aftershocks..."</p>
<p>"Don’t you dare try to infantilize me."</p>
<p>Aurelia stepped between them before the confrontation could escalate into something physical. Her tone was firm, carrying the measured authority of a commander rather than Iris’s raw, wounded fury.</p>
<p>"Rex, listen to reason... I am the primary combat instructor for this household’s elite forces."</p>
<p>"I cannot... I will not allow them to march into the largest engagement this city has seen in generations without their commander at their side."</p>
<p>She took a step forward, her armored hand resting on the hilt of her sword. "This isn’t about pride or recklessness."</p>
<p>"This household’s reputation is still hemorrhaging from what Theo did to us."</p>
<p>"A formal apology written on parchment means nothing."</p>
<p>"Our honor will only be restored through genuine, visible contribution when the steel actually meets the bone."</p>
<p>"My people need to see their instructor leading from the front... They need to see that the Nightwing bloodline doesn’t break when the pressure rises."</p>
<p>Rex stared at them both, the silence between them stretching taut as a bowstring. He could see the defiance in their eyes, the same defiance that had made them indispensable to him in the first place, but he also saw the terrifying vulnerability of what they risked.</p>
<p>To refuse them was to break their spirit; to allow them was to risk losing everything he had built.</p>
