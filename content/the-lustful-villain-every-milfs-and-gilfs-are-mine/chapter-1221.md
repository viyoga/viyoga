<p>Talyra appeared like a ghost emerging from the smoke. She did not rush forward with the desperate abandon of Lily or the focused intensity of Diana; instead, she moved with a measured, almost painful restraint.</p>
<p>Her face was pale, her eyes—both grieving soldiers and curious survivors—darting across the crowd until they locked onto Rex.</p>
<p>She stood several paces away from the main circle, aware of the hundreds of eyes both grieving soldiers and curious survivors that were now fixed on the group surrounding their fallen hero.</p>
<p>Though she maintained a distance that suggested propriety, the panic was written in every line of her body.</p>
<p>Her knuckles were white where her hands remained clasped tightly in front of her, a physical manifestation of the effort she was exerting to not scream his name or throw herself into his arms.</p>
<p>The cost of that restraint was visible in the tightness of her jaw and the way her breath came in shallow, controlled sips.</p>
<p>"Are you... truly alright?" The question was barely a whisper, pitched so low it scarcely carried past the barrier of Rex’s own shoulders.</p>
<p>Rex turned his head incrementally, meeting her gaze. His eyes were hollow, playing the part of the haunted survivor to perfection.</p>
<p>"I’m fine," he murmured back, his voice a rough thread of sound that only she could catch. "Later... I promise."</p>
<p>Talyra gave a single, sharp nod, a soldier’s acknowledgment, but her eyes lingered on him for three seconds too long before she forced herself to retreat into the shadows of the crowd.</p>
<p>Rex watched her go, noting the minute relaxation in her shoulders. She had received her confirmation, but the true relief would not come until they were behind closed doors.</p>
<p>"I lost control of the construct," Rex continued, raising his voice just enough to draw the attention of Diana and Lily back to his words.</p>
<p>He needed to close the loop on the impossible mathematics of his disappearance. "The construct I had standing in for me during the initial clash... it shattered."</p>
<p>"Xerollion destroyed it right in front of me, tearing through its magical matrix like it was paper."</p>
<p>"I couldn’t stabilize it... There was nothing I could do but watch it die."</p>
<p>It was a perfect lie. It explained why the ’hero’ they thought they saw fighting had vanished and why he was now standing here, bloodied and defeated, instead of triumphant.</p>
<p>The circle tightened further as Aisella stepped forward.</p>
<p>The air around her immediately changed. A soft, soothing emerald glow emanated from her palms as she began her work.</p>
<p>The magical light swept across Rex’s skin like warm water, seeking injuries, knitting together torn flesh and soothing bruised muscle. Her expression was a mask of calm professionalism, but her eyes—those deep, perceptive eyes—betrayed a profound, aching concern.</p>
<p>"Which parts hurt the most, Rex?" she asked, her voice gentle but carrying an urgent undercurrent. "Don’t try to be brave now."</p>
<p>"Tell me exactly where the pain is deepest."</p>
<p>She worked with a practiced, almost frantic efficiency. Her hands moved from his shoulder to his ribs, her fingers dancing across his skin as she channeled her energy.</p>
<p>Rex could tell she was using the ritual as an anchor; she needed the methodical rhythm of healing to prevent her own mind from spiraling into the horror of the day’s revelations.</p>
<p>"You need to let me examine these ribs properly," Aisella said, her brow furrowing as she pressed her glowing palms against his side.</p>
<p>Her fingers lingered on a dark, mottled bruise he had deliberately failed to heal earlier. "This discoloration is spreading... It looks significantly worse than what you’re admitting to."</p>
<p>Rex felt the warm tingle of her magic working on the deception, but he kept his features grim, forcing a wince that felt genuine.</p>
<p>"It looks worse than it is, Aisella," Rex lied, letting out a pained grunt as he allowed her to continue her examination. "Just... fix what you can."</p>
<p>He allowed her to fuss over him, to treat him as the broken man he needed to be. Every touch of her healing magic was a reminder of the performance: he was allowing them to heal a body that was already whole, all to mend the shattered hope of a city that desperately needed someone to blame for their nightmare.</p>
<p>The circle of grief and deception around Rex continued to tighten as more of the women he had claimed as his own drew closer, drawn by the gravity of his supposed defeat.</p>
<p>Nerith stood at the periphery of the gathering, a solitary figure carved from sorrow. Unlike the others, she didn’t seek physical contact.</p>
<p>Tears tracked silent, silver paths down her pale cheeks as she stared at the ground, unable to lift her gaze.</p>
<p>Her mind was a maelstrom of cognitive dissonance; she remembered her own words and the promises she had made about the balance of nature and about how the universe itself would bend to protect those who fought for righteousness.</p>
<p>Those words now tasted like ash in her mouth, a cruel, mocking joke played by a universe that had clearly abandoned them.</p>
<p>Beside her, Mireya stood like an anchor. She didn’t speak, but her grip on Nerith’s hand was bruisingly tight, her knuckles white.</p>
<p>They were two broken pillars leaning against each other, trying to find some semblance of strength in their shared terror.</p>
<p>Nerith couldn’t bring herself to look at Rex; to look at him was to acknowledge his survival, and to acknowledge his survival was to force herself to confront the reality of what he had left behind in that blood-slicked corridor.</p>
<p>Then came Elizabeth.</p>
<p>She approached not with the raw panic of Lily or the wounded fury of Diana, but with a cold, calculating composure that set her apart.</p>
<p>Her stride was measured, her spine straight, but her eyes sharp, intelligent, and terrifyingly perceptive carried a question that cut through the emotional noise of the medical tent.</p>
<p>She was the only one who truly understood the architecture of the deception unfolding before them.</p>
<p>She knew the monster that had walked the plaza, and she knew the man standing before them was its architect.</p>
<p>Their eyes met for a heartbeat, a long, loaded silence that screamed louder than any shout. In that glance, the performance paused.</p>
<p>A wordless exchange passed between them: a recognition of the lie, a shared burden of the secret, and a mutual understanding of the cost.</p>
<p>Then, Elizabeth stepped into the role.</p>
<p>"How could he possibly be that strong?" Elizabeth asked.</p>
<p>Her voice was pitched with a perfect blend of shock and scholarly confusion, carrying just far enough to be overheard by the nearest soldiers. "We planned every variable."</p>
<p>"We calculated every contingency... We prepared for a conqueror, but this... this was something else entirely."</p>
<p>She turned her gaze to Rex, her expression one of devastated bewilderment. "We planned everything so carefully."</p>
<p>Rex met her gaze, his expression a hollow mask of defeat.</p>
<p>"I don’t know," he answered, his voice rough with forced exhaustion. "But he’s far beyond anything we could have anticipated."</p>
<p>The lie was seamless, but Elizabeth pressed harder, knowing exactly which thread to pull to keep the audience engaged.</p>
<p>"The Key," Elizabeth said, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper that drew the nearby survivors closer. "If something compromised it or if something weakened the barrier from the inside, I need to know now."</p>
<p>"If there’s a flaw in the work I did, if my enchantments failed to hold against his approach, people need to understand that before anyone considers going back down there."</p>
<p>She was offering them a scapegoat: the failure of magic, the failure of the Key. It was a plausible explanation for their defeat, one that allowed them to maintain their dignity while accepting their loss.</p>
<p>"There was no flaw in your work, Elizabeth," Rex said.</p>
<p>For a fraction of a second, the performance slipped. The words carried a weight that wasn’t rehearsed; a genuine flash of sincerity that bled through the layers of manipulation.</p>
<p>His voice held a grim conviction that made the surrounding soldiers nod in solemn agreement.</p>
<p>"Whatever happened today," he continued, his jaw tightening as he forced himself back into the script, "isn’t something you could have built a defense against."</p>
<p>"You can’t build a wall against an inevitability... I need you to understand that."</p>
<p>"This wasn’t a failure of preparation; it was a failure of reality itself."</p>
