<p>"Kregg was eliminated," Celestina stated, though the sheer impossibility of the event seemed to vibrate in her voice.</p>
<p>"By a single reincarnator operating in the canyon expedition team," Zane said, the data rushing out of him with a frantic, desperate energy. "I received Kregg’s final communication before the channel went dark."</p>
<p>"He identified the operative as a surface reincarnator associated with the Starlight family structure. The Lustful Villain’s surface identity."</p>
<p>The channel went dead silent. It was a silence of pure, unadulterated shock.</p>
<p>"The same person," Celestina whispered, the realization hitting her with the force of a physical blow.</p>
<p>"The canyon expedition’s composition included both the Lustful Villain’s civilian identity and the Lustful Villain in his masked form," Zane said, the pieces of the puzzle slamming together with violent clarity. "They are the same person, but they operate under two separate identities."</p>
<p>"Kregg’s elimination was the work of the surface identity..."</p>
<p>"While the Underlayer’s reconstruction was happening... that was the work of the masked identity."</p>
<p>"One reincarnator," Celestina said, her voice trembling with a rare, dark fascination.</p>
<p>"One reincarnator with two operational identities, a divine designation absorbed from a patronless apostle, and a capability profile that includes everything I reported to you tonight," Zane said, his voice rising with the sheer scale of the anomaly.</p>
<p>"That changes the priority assessment," Celestina said, her voice snapping back into a terrifying, cold focus. "For Kregg, for the canyon engagement, for the Underlayer’s reconstruction."</p>
<p>"It isn’t a group. It isn’t a faction. It is one person."</p>
<p>"One person," Zane echoed.</p>
<p>"Then he is the primary target of the Aethelgard operation," Celestina declared.</p>
<p>The decision arrived in that flat, terrifying register of a goddess pronouncing a sentence. "Not the network..."</p>
<p>"Not the Apostles. Not the Underlayer. Him. Specifically."</p>
<p>"He is not based in Aethelgard," Zane cautioned, trying to map the target’s movements. "His surface identity is an Academy student operating out of the Starlight household."</p>
<p>"He moves between the surface and the underlayer through geological access points."</p>
<p>"The island is where he returns," she countered, her charisma turning predatory. "It is where his surface identity is anchored."</p>
<p>"His maximum bond network is there, and his operational cover is there..."</p>
<p>"The Academy is also there. He has woven himself into the very fabric of that place."</p>
<p>"If the island falls," Zane said, the horror of the plan finally crystallizing, "everything he has built on the surface falls with it."</p>
<p>"Exactly," she said. "The underlayer without its surface operation is just a city underground, contained, isolated, and a prisoner of the earth with no political or intelligence access to the world above."</p>
<p>"And the man who built it? He loses the platform that makes his surface existence viable."</p>
<p>"He survives the island," Zane argued, a final attempt at logic. "His physical capability is too high."</p>
<p>"He will likely survive a geological event of that scale."</p>
<p>"I am not trying to kill him with the island, Zane... Don’t be pedestrian," Celestina said, her voice dripping with a cold, intellectual arrogance. "I am trying to strip away everything he has built on the surface and force him to choose."</p>
<p>"A person managing two operational theaters cannot respond effectively to both simultaneously when both require immediate, total devotion. I am going to break his focus."</p>
<p>"You are dividing his attention," Zane realized, a chill running down his spine.</p>
<p>"I am giving him two catastrophes at once," she corrected. "The Aethelgard operation and the response generated by the canyon engagement are both his responsibilities."</p>
<p>"Let him manage both and let him discover the specific, agonizing limitations of being one man with two identities when the world presents two simultaneous crises of sufficient scale."</p>
<p>"Let him struggle to breathe while we tighten the noose."</p>
<p>"And while he is managing both," Zane said, his eyes narrowing as he saw the endgame, "we identify the vulnerability."</p>
<p>"And we strike," Celestina finished.</p>
<p>There was a pause that felt fundamentally different from the others. It wasn’t the hesitation of a strategist weighing variables or a leader searching for the right words; it was the heavy, suffocating silence of a woman sitting inside a question she had already answered, deciding whether the truth required the mercy of an explanation.</p>
<p>"The reincarnators in the Apostle network chose their affiliation," Celestina said, her voice cutting through the silence like a blade.</p>
<p>There was no pity in her tone, only a terrifying, clinical detachment. "They chose to become instruments of the network’s agenda rather than to exist in this world as individuals."</p>
<p>"That choice carries a price... I do not control the consequences they encounter when the network commits them to an engagement they are fundamentally incapable of winning."</p>
<p>"That isn’t the same as being comfortable with the slaughter," Zane countered, his voice tight with the friction of his own morality rubbing against her pragmatism.</p>
<p>"No," she said, and for a fleeting second, a shadow of something human, perhaps a flicker of disdain for inefficiency, passed through her voice. "It is not..."</p>
<p>"The reincarnators who die in that canyon, because the network sent them into a meat grinder prepared by a geological designation at full output, will have died because their leaders made a catastrophic operational error."</p>
<p>"I find that error objectionable... But to prevent it, we would have to intervene in the network’s decision-making process, and that is a luxury we do not currently possess."</p>
<p>"And once the blood is spilled?" Zane asked. "After the canyon?"</p>
<p>"After the canyon, the network’s decision-making capacity is gutted," she said, her voice regaining its predatory momentum. "Their ability to make an error of that magnitude again is crippled."</p>
<p>"The survivors will be the ones left to hold the line."</p>
<p>"So the people who die in the canyon are the shield for the people who survive it," Zane whispered, the grim math of the war settling in his gut.</p>
<p>Celestina was quiet, the silence stretching until it felt like a physical weight.</p>
<p>"That is one way to describe the outcome," she said finally, her voice returning to the iron cold register of command. "Go... Find Ignivara."</p>
<p>"The timing window is real, Zane, and it will not expand to accommodate your sentimentality."</p>
<p>"Understood," Zane said.</p>
<p>"Zane," she called out, just as he was about to sever the connection.</p>
<p>He hesitated. "Yes?"</p>
<p>"The man who defeated Kregg," she said, her voice dropping into a warning, a low vibration of pure menace. "Do not make the mistake of underestimating him simply because of his surface identity."</p>
<p>"The Academy student cover is a masterpiece of deception; it is specifically designed to invite complacency."</p>
<p>"Kregg underestimated him, and the canyon team underestimated him as well."</p>
<p>"The entire underlayer contact network lived under the illusion of his insignificance for fourteen months."</p>
<p>"I watched him fight last night," Zane said, his eyes hardening. "I do not underestimate him."</p>
<p>"Good," she said, and there was a trace of grim satisfaction in her tone. "And do not let your history with Ignivara breed overconfidence in this operation."</p>
<p>"She is brilliant because her analysis is flawless and the plan is viable."</p>
<p>"But in our world, Zane, viable is a far cry from guaranteed."</p>
<p>"I know the difference," he said.</p>
<p>"I know you do," she replied, her voice softening just enough to be dangerous. "That is precisely why you are the one on the surface and not someone else."</p>
<p>The channel snapped shut.</p>
<p>Zane sat in the sudden, ringing silence, clutching the communication crystal in both hands. The fading warmth of the connection bled away, replaced by the biting chill of the morning air.</p>
<p>He looked toward the horizon, where the silhouette of Aethelgard hung suspended against the pale, waking sky, a divine monument unaware that its foundation was already being measured for its grave.</p>
<p>His mind drifted to Ignivara.</p>
<p>Four months.</p>
<p>Four months since he had last seen her. She had left the Underlayer before the blackout, making her exit with the same calculated precision she applied to everything else.</p>
<p>She had seen the storm coming before anyone else, and she had moved before the winds forced her hand. That was the essence of her; she didn’t react to the window; she moved ahead of it.</p>
<p>He didn’t know if she had thought of him. He didn’t know if her departure had been a tactical necessity or a personal one or if she had weighed their connection in the same cold calculus Celestina used and decided it was a variable worth sacrificing.</p>
<p>He didn’t know if she had spent the last four months in a silence of her own making or if she was simply waiting for the world to break so she could begin the reconstruction.</p>
<p>He didn’t know any of it. But he would find out in four hours.</p>
<p>He tucked the crystal into his inner pocket, rose from his crouch at the tree line, and turned his gaze toward the eastern coastal approach. The morning light was strengthening over the Convergence Waters, turning the abyss below into a shimmering, deceptive sheet of gold.</p>
<p>Above it all, Aethelgard sat in its ancient, arrogant splendor. Its towers caught the first rays of the sun, glowing with the pride of eight hundred years of stability, utterly oblivious to the fact that the very principle of its existence was about to be contradicted.</p>
<p>Zane began to walk.</p>
