<p>"Kaelen... Mara... Silas..." The Avatar allowed each name to hang in the air like a physical weight, forcing the soldiers to confront the emptiness each loss left behind. "Each one a brother... Each one a sister..."</p>
<p>"Each one a piece of the foundation we stand upon." The pauses were surgical, allowing the grief to breathe, to sting, to transform from raw pain into something sharper: resolve.</p>
<p>"I know what some of you whisper in the dark when the torches burn low," the Avatar continued, its gaze dropping slightly, as if sharing a private confidence with the masses. "You wonder if we are enough..."</p>
<p>"You wonder if a single city can truly hold back the tide of something so ancient, so hungry."</p>
<p>The Avatar looked up, its eyes Rex’s eyes scanning the formations with a calculated warmth that felt almost achingly genuine. "I know many of you have wondered why Helena Von Starlight isn’t standing here with us today."</p>
<p>A murmur of recognition swept through the crowd. "She remains on her mission with Leonidas, far from our walls."</p>
<p>"Every one of us knows she would be at the front if she could... But let me tell you this: her absence is not a crack in our armor."</p>
<p>"It is our greatest strength."</p>
<p>It stepped closer to the edge of the platform, its presence seeming to expand. "Aethelgard has never been defined by a single hero."</p>
<p>"We have never relied on one person to carry the sky on their shoulders."</p>
<p>"We stand here today with Helena, with Leonidas, with every legend you can name because we have proven that the city’s true strength lives in the person standing to your left and the person standing to your right."</p>
<p>"We stand because you choose to stand."</p>
<p>A ripple of fierce, prideful emotion stirred the ranks. The Avatar seized the moment, its voice building with the inexorable momentum of a gathering storm.</p>
<p>"Our chances are not merely hopeful; they are absolute."</p>
<p>"We carry a key forged with the genius of our finest scholars, a weapon of precision designed to pierce the heart of darkness."</p>
<p>"We carry Apollo, whose very life force will light our path through the deepest abyss."</p>
<p>"We carry Valentina’s ironclad promise that not a single innocent soul will pay the price for this crusade."</p>
<p>The Avatar’s face hardened, the mask of humility slipping to reveal something far more primal: the face of a predator ready to feast. "And most importantly, we carry you..."</p>
<p>"The bravest assembly of warriors this world has ever seen."</p>
<p>"The Underlayer has taken our homes... They have taken our children."</p>
<p>"They have taken our dead." Its voice dropped to a low, dangerous growl that vibrated in the chests of every soldier present. "Today, we take back everything they stole."</p>
<p>"Today, we hunt."</p>
<p>The field didn’t just erupt; it detonated.</p>
<p>Thousands of swords, spears, and halberds rose in a single, unified salute that blotted out the morning sun. The roar that followed was a physical force, a tsunami of sound that rolled across the plains and slammed against the ancient stone walls of Aethelgard like a battering ram.</p>
<p>On the platform, Valentina, Apollo, and the Avatar exchanged a final, silent look—a shared understanding of the deception, the danger, and the inevitable carnage to come.</p>
<p>The performance was complete.</p>
<p>The trap was set.</p>
<p>"March!" the command finally tore through the air.</p>
<p>The disciplined cacophony began as the formations shifted. The massive army began its organized, inexorable advance toward the underground gateway where the Key awaited.</p>
<p>Thousands of boots struck the earth in a rhythmic, deadly cadence as disciplined columns of steel moved toward the mouth of the underworld, marching with grim purpose and righteous fury directly into the jaws of a slaughter they believed would be their salvation.</p>
<p>Far beneath the floating majesty of Aethelgard, the world was stripped of light and replaced by something far more sinister.</p>
<p>The northern corridor stretched like a diseased throat through solid rock, a narrow, claustrophobic passage that felt less like a tunnel and more like the digestive tract of some ancient, subterranean god.</p>
<p>Veins of natural crystal threaded through the stone walls, pulsing with a sickly, arrhythmic glow, a dim, shifting luminescence that cast elongated, dancing shadows that seemed to writhe independently of their sources.</p>
<p>This was not merely a passage; it was a kill box.</p>
<p>Thousands of Underlayer soldiers stood in absolute, terrifying silence. They were arranged in meticulous, overlapping layers of defensive formations, each tier positioned with surgical precision according to Gorvasha’s complex geological calculations.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a random gathering of warriors; it was a trap designed with the patience of a spider. The air here was thick, tasting of mineral dust, cold stone, and the metallic tang of sharpened steel.</p>
<p>Every man and woman present knew they were the teeth of a trap that had been years in the making, waiting for the moment the surface world would finally expose its throat.</p>
<p>Cassandra stood at the precipice of this assembled storm. Having just concluded the final tactical briefing, her voice still seemed to echo off the crystal-veined walls.</p>
<p>The authority she wielded was not the performative bravado of a general, but something far more primal: the residual, bone-deep command of a former Demon Queen. When she spoke, the soldiers didn’t just listen; they obeyed as if her words were physical laws.</p>
<p>She had walked through every gruesome detail one last time, her mind a cold map of impending slaughter. She knew the exact millisecond the rear guard would seal the corridor’s exit, cutting off any hope of retreat.</p>
<p>She had calculated the precise delay required to lure Aethelgard’s initial wave deep enough into the throat of the corridor before the true engagement began long enough for the trap to snap shut with bone-crushing force.</p>
<p>The rotation schedules were flawless; no gap would form, no weakness would emerge, no matter how many bodies piled up in the dark.</p>
<p>’Let them come,’ she thought, her inner voice as cold as the stone beneath her boots. ’Let them march with their righteous fury and their hollow promises.’</p>
<p>’They think they come to excise a cancer, but they are merely walking into the furnace.’</p>
<p>A sudden distortion in the air near her shoulder shattered the stillness. The light didn’t just bend; it tore.</p>
<p>A high-tier teleportation shimmer coalesced into the imposing, masked silhouette of Lord Xerollion. The effect on the assembled forces was instantaneous and terrifying.</p>
<p>Thousands of warriors moved as one, boots striking the stone in a synchronized, formal salute that reverberated through the corridor like a single, thunderous heartbeat.</p>
<p>Cassandra turned to face him, her spine straightening with instinctive deference, though her eyes remained sharp with tactical satisfaction.</p>
<p>"Everything is in position," she reported, her voice steady, devoid of hesitation. "The formations are set like iron teeth."</p>
<p>"The rear guard has been briefed on the exact timing of the seal; there will be no escape once the first blade is drawn."</p>
<p>"Every commander down the line understands their role in this symphony of death; they know precisely when to hold, when to push, and when to grind them into the dust."</p>
<p>She paused, her gaze sweeping over the endless rows of weapons glinting in the sickly crystal light. "We have calculated every variable, Lord Xerollion."</p>
<p>"The trap is sprung; we simply await your hand to release the mechanism."</p>
<p>Rex gave a single, curt nod. Even beneath the inscrutable stillness of his mask, there was an unmistakable aura of predatory satisfaction.</p>
<p>He began to speak, his voice dropping into a low murmur as he offered a series of surgical refinements to the plan: minute adjustments to timing, a three-second delay in the third tier’s advance, and a slight shift in the spear wall angle to better funnel the Aethelgard cavalry into the kill zone.</p>
<p>These were not mere suggestions; they were the final touches on a masterpiece of slaughter, informed by the intimate intelligence he had absorbed while wearing the Avatar’s face at the surface gathering.</p>
<p>His gaze drifted down the endless corridor, finding Gorvasha standing several hundred yards back among the secondary formations. Their eyes met in a silent exchange.</p>
<p>Gorvasha offered a single, imperceptible nod, a cold, mathematical confirmation that the geological calculations remained flawless. The stone would hold; the corridor would not collapse prematurely; the ambush would be as precise as a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>As the assembled commanders dispersed to their respective positions, their armored boots echoing like hammer blows against the stone, a fragile window of stillness opened before the inevitable storm. Rex did not use this time for prayer or meditation.</p>
<p>Instead, he moved through the ranks with the fluid, deliberate grace of a panther, seeking out the women who occupied the most dangerous and vital positions within his war machine.</p>
<p>He found Cassandra first. She remained at the absolute vanguard, a statue of dark resolve amidst a sea of steel.</p>
<p>Rex approached her without ceremony, stopping close enough to feel the heat radiating from her body. He reached out, his gauntleted fingers surprisingly gentle as he hooked two fingers beneath her chin, tilting her face upward to study her features in the sickly crystal light.</p>
