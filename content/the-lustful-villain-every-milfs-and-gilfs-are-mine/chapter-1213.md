<p>Xerollion lowered his hand.</p>
<p>It was not a sudden movement. There was no dramatic flourish, no theatrical dismissal.</p>
<p>Instead, it was a slow, agonizing descent, each inch of movement measured with terrifying deliberation. The violet-black energy that had been coiled like a starving predator at his fingertips began to withdraw.</p>
<p>It didn’t simply vanish; it imploded, shrinking inward on itself in concentric circles of dying light, pulsing weaker and weaker like the final, desperate throbs of a failing heart.</p>
<p>Flicker... dim... flicker...</p>
<p>SHHHHHHHH...</p>
<p>The energy died with a sound like a dying breath, leaving behind only the acrid, metallic tang of ionized air and the faint smell of burnt magic hanging in the stagnant atmosphere.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was not empty; it was heavy, physical, and suffocating. It pressed down on the shoulders of every soldier, every mage, and every broken soul in the corridor.</p>
<p>The wreckage of fallen pillars and cracked stone seemed to stand as silent witnesses to this impossible reprieve.</p>
<p>Every eye, those of the victorious conquerors and the defeated defenders, was locked onto that single hand as it finally came to rest at his side.</p>
<p>Nobody moved.</p>
<p>The very concept of motion seemed forbidden, as though any sudden gesture would shatter this fragile, terrifying moment and send the world tumbling back into the abyss of slaughter.</p>
<p>Even the dust motes drifting down from the shattered ceiling seemed to hesitate midair, suspended in a state of unnatural stillness. A soldier somewhere in the Aethelgard ranks let out an audible gasp.</p>
<p>HIIIIKK!</p>
<p>But the sound was instantly swallowed by the enormity of the quiet, instantly swallowing the sound, rendering it insignificant in light of the cosmic weight of what had just transpired.</p>
<p>The construct beneath him remained motionless, a twisted corpse of metal and synthetic flesh. A single drop of black fluid leaked from its cracked ocular sensor.</p>
<p>PLIP.</p>
<p>The sound echoed like a hammer blow against the stone.</p>
<p>Xerollion turned his head slightly, his gaze raking over the kneeling Valentina. He saw the way her shoulders shook, the way her fingers dug into the rubble, the way she clung to the hope she had just traded her entire civilization to preserve.</p>
<p>"Surrender."</p>
<p>The word rolled off his tongue with glacial precision. He spoke it slowly, savoring each syllable as if he were tasting something exquisite.</p>
<p>He let the word hang in the space between them, allowing it to sink into the minds of every person present, letting them feel its weight, its humiliation, and its finality.</p>
<p>"Surrender," he repeated, his voice carrying a note of cold, razor-sharp satisfaction. "It is a delicious sound, isn’t it?"</p>
<p>"The sound of inevitability."</p>
<p>He took one step toward Valentina, his boot grinding a piece of broken ceramic underfoot.</p>
<p>CRUNCH.</p>
<p>The sound was deafening in the stillness. He loomed over her, his shadow stretching long and jagged across her tear-stained face.</p>
<p>"A heavy word," he continued, his tone almost conversational, almost gentle in its cruelty. "Especially for someone who arrived here today with such fire in her eyes."</p>
<p>"Someone who walked into this slaughterhouse believing she was bringing justice."</p>
<p>"Believing she could stand before me and balance the scales with righteous fury."</p>
<p>A cold smirk touched his lips beneath the mask.</p>
<p>"But justice is a luxury for those who still have something left to lose, Valentina."</p>
<p>"You have learned today that there is no justice; there is only power, and there is only the will to wield it."</p>
<p>"You didn’t bring justice here... You brought me exactly what I came for."</p>
<p>Xerollion took a single step back, withdrawing from the shattered corpse of Rex Rexilion. He left the construct where it lay, a twisted monument of broken metal and dying circuitry.</p>
<p>Beneath the synthetic skin, the energy matrix continued to malfunction, emitting weak, irregular pulses of light that cast sickly shadows across the rubble.</p>
<p>Flicker... pulse... flicker.</p>
<p>Each pulse seemed to synchronize with the dying hopes of the onlookers.</p>
<p>Turning away from the wreckage, he shifted his entire presence toward Valentina. The full weight of his attention fell upon her like a physical burden.</p>
<p>She remained on her knees, her body language stripped of all dignity; she was simply a broken woman, her shoulders shaking with a profound, soul-deep exhaustion that no amount of sleep could ever cure.</p>
<p>The tears streaming down her face weren’t just salt and water; they were the accumulated grief of a lifetime of leadership crumbling in real time.</p>
<p>"Very well," Xerollion said, his voice cutting through the static of the room with the clarity of a blade. "I accept Aethelgard’s surrender."</p>
<p>He allowed that statement to sink in, watching the way the light died in Valentina’s eyes.</p>
<p>"Now, prove the sincerity of your words," he commanded, his tone shifting from amused to coldly transactional. "Order every soldier you have left to lay down their weapons."</p>
<p>"Every blade, every staff, every enchanted crossbow."</p>
<p>"Do it now, or I will consider this surrender a deception, and I will begin the execution of your people where they stand."</p>
<p>The threat hung in the air like a guillotine blade poised to drop.</p>
<p>VROOOOOOOM.</p>
<p>The ambient magical pressure around Xerollion spiked briefly as a warning, a reminder that his patience was as finite as their lives.</p>
<p>Valentina swallowed hard, the motion visibly painful. She forced herself to rise, a process that looked more like a slow resurrection than a simple movement.</p>
<p>Her legs buckled twice before she managed to plant her palm against the jagged edge of a broken pillar.</p>
<p>GRIND.</p>
<p>Her nails scraped against the stone as she used it to steady herself, her body swaying dangerously. For several agonizing seconds, she simply stood there, head bowed, gasping for air that refused to fill her lungs properly.</p>
<p>’I have failed them,’ she thought, the realization a cold blade twisting in her gut. ’I have traded our freedom for scraps of mercy.’</p>
<p>’What kind of headmaster am I? What kind of protector?’</p>
<p>The silence of the corridor was absolute, broken only by the distant, wet coughing of a wounded soldier.</p>
<p>HACK... COUGH.</p>
<p>The tension was so brittle that it felt as though a single whispered word could shatter the entire fortress.</p>
<p>Finally, Valentina lifted her head. Her face was a mask of blood, grime, and tears, but her eyes found the horizon of her own army.</p>
<p>She turned to face the ranks—the broken, the terrified, the dying. For one heartbeat, her throat seized.</p>
<p>Her vocal cords felt like they had been replaced by rusted wire. No sound came out.</p>
<p>She stood there, paralyzed by the enormity of what she was about to ask them to do.</p>
<p>Then, she drew a ragged, shuddering breath that sounded like tearing silk.</p>
<p>HAAAAA...</p>
<p>"Lower your weapons," she called out.</p>
<p>Her voice was hoarse, stripped of its usual melodic authority, but it possessed a raw, haunting desperation that cut through the sobbing and the shocked murmurs that had settled over the ranks like toxic smoke.</p>
<p>"Lower them!" she screamed louder this time, the command cracking with emotion. "All of you!"</p>
<p>"Drop your steel! Drop your magic!"</p>
<p>The command rippled through the Aethelgard lines. One by one, the sounds of metal hitting stone began to echo through the hall.</p>
<p>CLANG. THUD. CLATTER.</p>
<p>The sound of surrender was louder than any battle cry.</p>
<p>"It’s over," she whispered, though in the unnatural quiet of the corridor, the words carried to every corner. "It’s really..."</p>
<p>"...over."</p>
