<p>Rex stepped into the heart of the commercial district, and the world recoiled.</p>
<p>It wasn't a coordinated movement; it was a visceral, instinctive retreat. People didn't move out of his way out of politeness or respect; they moved because their primal nervous systems had screamed a warning before their conscious minds could even process what they were seeing.</p>
<p>A woman, mid-stride with a basket of market goods, pressed herself so hard against a wooden stall that the timber groaned. A merchant, caught in a heated argument with a supplier, went dead silent, the words dying in his throat as his eyes locked onto the monolithic figure approaching.</p>
<p>Two academy students, laughing as they headed toward the east entrance, suddenly veered in the opposite direction, their faces pale, driven by an inexplicable urge to put distance between themselves and the stranger.</p>
<p>Rex ignored them all.</p>
<p>He didn't walk with the performative arrogance of a man trying to look important. He walked with the terrifying, singular purpose of a force of nature.</p>
<p>To him, the crowd was not a social collective; they were merely navigational variables, obstacles to be bypassed with the same indifference a river shows to the pebbles in its path.</p>
<p>As he reached the edge of the central plaza, the island's substrate surged beneath his feet, connecting with the dense, artificial gradient of the town's heart. He looked up at the surrounding buildings, his mind flashing back to the speeches he had delivered in the Underlayer and from the Spire.</p>
<p>He thought about the weight of those words, the way they had reshaped destinies in the dark. But he would not offer them the courtesy of a speech here.</p>
<p>He would let the earth speak for him.</p>
<p>With a sudden, violent motion, he drove his right fist into the stone surface of the plaza.</p>
<p>The impact was a masterpiece of calibrated destruction. It wasn't the world-shaking cataclysm that had buckled the Underlayer's dome, nor the seismic fury of the Gorvasha calibration.</p>
<p>It was something more precise, more surgical. It was the exact amount of force required to shatter the peace without collapsing the homes of the people living within them.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound was like a thunderclap trapped underground. A massive fissure erupted from the impact point, tearing through the plaza in a perfect radial pattern.</p>
<p>The crack raced outward, following the natural fault lines of the island's geological substrate, snaking through the streets and into the very foundations of the district.</p>
<p>The buildings didn't fall, but they groaned in protest. The walls settled with a sickening, grinding sound; the foundations shivered as the stone reasserted its allegiance to the force that had just surged through it.</p>
<p>The message was unmistakable: the island was no longer a passive stage. It was inhabited by something new.</p>
<p>The silence that ensued was short yet filled with an ominous tension.</p>
<p>People began to spill out of doorways, eyes wide with terror. City guards scrambled from their posts, weapons drawn.</p>
<p>The Academy's outer gates swung wide, and a contingent of Academy-affiliated fighters emerged, a disciplined response force that had clearly been on high alert since the tremors of the night before had signaled the purge.</p>
<p>Rex stood motionless in the center of the fractured plaza, a god of stone amidst the chaos. He let them orient and he let the panic settle into a tense, vibrating readiness.</p>
<p>He watched them with the same flat, unblinking attention he had used on the Underlayer's coalition, the gaze of a man whose verdict was already rendered, simply waiting for the subjects to realize they had been judged.</p>
<p>The fighters moved with practiced efficiency: establishing a perimeter, assessing the threat, and determining the engagement protocol. They were professionals, trained to face monsters and magic.</p>
<p>Rex watched them work.</p>
<p>Through the golem relay, his mind was still a storm of data. Eleven simultaneous perspectives from across the island fed him a constant stream of intelligence, the consolidation sweep running in perfect parallel with the tension in the plaza. He was a conductor, and the island was his orchestra.</p>
<p>He raised his right hand.</p>
<p>The gravity manipulation skill, which had been integrating into his very essence since the morning, was ready for its first true test. This wasn't a training exercise; it was an operational deployment.</p>
<p>He designated the response force.</p>
<p>In a span of two seconds, the local gravity within the fighters' perimeter surged from standard to three times its normal weight. It was a clean, brutal application of physics.</p>
<p>The effect was instantaneous. The fighters didn't crash to the ground, but they were slammed into a sudden, crushing lethargy.</p>
<p>The transition from upright combat readiness to heavy, labored movement was dramatic. It was the sensation of suddenly trying to fight while submerged in deep, viscous water; every muscle fiber strained, every breath a conquest, every movement an exhausting struggle against an invisible, downward weight.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the center of the devastation, watching them struggle against the very air, his eyes patient and cold, conducting his assessment of the souls who dared to stand in his way.</p>
<p>They were formidable. Rex watched them through the lens of a cold, analytical god.</p>
<p>The Academy's fighters weren't mere militia; they possessed the hardened grit of professionals, the kind of determination that only comes from years of brutal training. A few of them, the elite among the elite, were actually defying the theoretical limits of the gravity field, their muscles corded and trembling as they pushed back against the crushing weight better than any baseline should allow.</p>
<p>He didn't just see them; he cataloged them. He mentally tagged the outliers, filing them away into a mental ledger of assets that would need to be accounted for during the post-conflict consolidation.</p>
<p>Then, he began to walk through them.</p>
<p>At three times standard gravity, their reaction speeds were butchered, throttled down to a sluggish forty percent of their true potential. The response force could only watch him pass, a collective of frozen statues.</p>
<p>They were exerting maximum effort, their faces contorted with the strain of existence, yet they were producing almost no commensurate movement. They were spectators in their own battlefield, trapped in a slow-motion nightmare as the masked titan drifted through their ranks.</p>
<p>The golem relay pulsed in his mind, a frantic rhythm of data. Three of the island's flagged reincarnators weren't fleeing the epicenter of the tremor; they were charging toward it.</p>
<p>They had felt the earth scream, and they were coming to see who had dared to do it.</p>
<p>Rex didn't flinch, and he let them come. The Apostle network would react to this geological tantrum in due time, but he was the master of this moment.</p>
<p>He had all the time in the world.</p>
<p>The three reincarnators emerged from the side streets of the commercial district like predators sensing a shift in the wind.</p>
<p>The first was a brute, a combat type whose very energy signature screamed strength amplification. He was a hero system archetype designed for one thing: the violent, direct annihilation of an opponent.</p>
<p>The second was a caster, her presence marked by the clean, whistling resonance of a wind affinity. She moved with the poise of someone who had spent a lifetime refining a single, lethal elemental focus.</p>
<p>The third, however, was the one that made Rex sharpen his focus. A detection type.</p>
<p>She was the most dangerous of the three because her system-granted perception meant her "read" on him wouldn't be a guess; it would be a revelation.</p>
<p>He locked his gaze on her.</p>
<p>She appeared to be in her late twenties, dressed in the practical, high-quality attire of an academy student. Her expression was one of intense, terrifying concentration; he could see the gears of her mind turning as she integrated a massive influx of sensory data in real time.</p>
<p>She stopped ten meters from him, the distance a calculated buffer between the known and the impossible.</p>
<p>"You're not from the surface," she stated.</p>
<p>It wasn't a question; it was a fact her system had just handed her.</p>
<p>"No," Rex replied, his voice resonating with the weight of the earth itself.</p>
<p>"The energy signature is geological tier," she continued, her eyes darting with mechanical precision. "Divine designation... absorbed, rather than granted."</p>
<p>She wasn't just looking at him; she was scanning him. Her eyes traced the jagged, divine texture of his gauntlets, moved up to the impenetrable surface of his mask, dropped to the massive fissure splitting the plaza, and snapped back to his hands.</p>
<p>It was a high-speed, holistic scan, a detection type building a complete tactical profile in a heartbeat.</p>
<p>"The Earthen Apostle designation," she whispered, her voice trembling slightly with the weight of her discovery. "The one from the island assessment... the reports said it was lost to an undead attack."</p>
<p>Rex met her gaze, his eyes unblinking behind the mask. "Indeed... but..."</p>
<p>"It was staged," he said, the truth dropping between them like a heavy stone.</p>
<p>She didn't gasp or recoil. She processed the lie with the clinical, contained efficiency of a professional, her mind weighing the implications of a grand deception.</p>
<p>"What do you want?" she demanded, her voice hardening.</p>
<p>Rex shifted his gaze, encompassing all three of them: the brute, the wind weaver, and the seer.</p>
<p>"Nothing from you specifically," he said, his tone dismissive yet profound. "You are here because the event in this plaza demanded a response, and you provided it."</p>
<p>"Your response is noted."</p>
<p>He spared a glance at the perimeter, where the Academy fighters were still fighting the invisible ocean of gravity, their bodies caught in the agonizing slow motion of the field.</p>
<p>"Release," he murmured to himself.</p>
<p>Instantly, the crushing weight vanished. The gravity normalized, leaving the fighters stumbling, gasping for air as they suddenly regained the freedom of their own limbs.</p>
