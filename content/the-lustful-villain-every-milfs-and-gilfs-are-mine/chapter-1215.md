<p>While the chaos of Mordecai’s hysteria roared around them like a storm, the other commanders of the Underlayer experienced the victory through their own fractured lenses of trauma, relief, and hard-won respect.</p>
<p>Pavellia remained an island of unnatural stillness amidst the sea of demonic revelry. Her posture was perfect, her spine a straight line of absolute discipline that refused to bend even to the gravity of the moment.</p>
<p>To any observer, she was merely observing, the consummate professional assessing the aftermath. But beneath the surface, something was fraying.</p>
<p>Her quill trembled just once, a microscopic shudder that betrayed the adrenaline still coursing through her veins.</p>
<p>Scritch. Scritch.</p>
<p>The sound of her pen against parchment was faint, but in her own mind, it sounded like bone grinding against stone. Her report was a clinical dissection of slaughter: casualty numbers, magical expenditure, and resource depletion.</p>
<p>Her office had prepared projections of near total annihilation, but the reality as brutal as it had been was survivable. Beneath the columns of cold data and official terminology, her fingers hesitated.</p>
<p>For one unguarded second, she allowed herself a single line of text that no auditor would ever approve:</p>
<p>’A good day.’</p>
<p>The words felt foreign on the page, almost sinful. She stared at them for a heartbeat longer than protocol allowed before snapping the folder shut with a decisive THUD.</p>
<p>She took a breath, smoothing her expression back into its impenetrable mask before turning her gaze toward the celebrating masses.</p>
<p>A few yards away, the atmosphere was starkly different. Viscaria Amorphis was not a creature of protocol; she was a creature of raw, visceral emotion.</p>
<p>She threw her arms around several of her slime soldiers, her laughter mixing with the wet, squelching sounds of their gelatinous bodies as they embraced.</p>
<p>SQUELCH. SPLAT.</p>
<p>"We lived!" she cried out, her voice cracking with a relief so profound it felt like a physical wound opening. "By the stars, we actually lived!"</p>
<p>As the laughter subsided into grateful sobs, her hand drifted instinctively to the gentle curve of her belly. Her fingers pressed against the fabric of her armor with a protective ferocity.</p>
<p>All through the screaming, all through the smell of burning flesh and ionized magic, she had maintained a fragile mental barrier, refusing to acknowledge the terror of what could have happened.</p>
<p>Now that the threat had passed, that barrier shattered. The fear she had kept at arm’s length rushed back in not as a cold dread, but as an overwhelming, disbelieving flood of joy that left her knees weak.</p>
<p>Her child was safe. The future was still intact.</p>
<p>Near the front of the victorious line, two figures stood apart from the mindless cheering: Cassandra and Gorvasha. They didn’t join the shouting.</p>
<p>Instead, they shared a long, weighted silence, their eyes meeting in a way that only two predators who had survived a killing field could understand.</p>
<p>Then, the silence broke with Cassandra’s laughter sharp, genuine, and startling.</p>
<p>SLAP.</p>
<p>Cassandra delivered a thunderous clap to Gorvasha’s shoulder, the impact echoing like a hammer against an anvil.</p>
<p>CLANG.</p>
<p>Any lesser soldier would have been sent sprawling into the rubble, but the orc didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>"I’ll admit it, you green bastard," Cassandra said, a wide, hungry grin splitting her face despite the dried blood that crusted her forearms like crimson paint. "Your corridor held exactly the way you said it would."</p>
<p>"I was half convinced you’d overestimated your own terrain."</p>
<p>Gorvasha let out a deep, rumbling sound from her chest—a laugh that felt like tectonic plates shifting. She thumped a closed fist against her own breastplate with enough force to send a shockwave through her ribs.</p>
<p>THUMP.</p>
<p>"I never overestimate terrain I’ve walked myself," Gorvasha answered, her voice gravelly and thick with pride.</p>
<p>Her eyes burned with the satisfaction of a craftsman who had seen their work tested in fire. "Underestimate the enemy... Never the ground."</p>
<p>The roar of the army had already reached a fever pitch, but then Gorvasha threw her head back. Her throat swelled as she unleashed a single name, a rough, guttural sound that tore from her lungs like molten metal.</p>
<p>"XEROLLION!"</p>
<p>The name acted as a spark in a powder keg. Within seconds, the rest of the army caught the rhythm, their voices multiplying by the dozens, then hundreds, then thousands.</p>
<p>The chant became a physical force, a wall of sound that pressed against the cracked stone pillars and rolled back in waves of pure, unadulterated power.</p>
<p>"XEROLLION! XEROLLION! XEROLLION! "</p>
<p>The vibrations were so intense they seemed to rattle the teeth of every soldier present. It wasn’t just noise; it was a declaration of existence.</p>
<p>For centuries, they had been the footnotes of history, the monsters in the dark, the expendable pawns of a cruel hierarchy.</p>
<p>Now, their voices claimed the space.</p>
<p>From the shadows near the corridor’s edge, Lilith emerged.</p>
<p>She didn’t walk; she sprinted, weaving through the chaos of embracing soldiers with a wide, delighted grin that lit up her face.</p>
<p>She threw herself into the chanting crowd, her laughter mingling with the thunderous roars. Her eyes, sharp and bright, swept across the battlefield until they locked onto Xerollion.</p>
<p>Across the distance, framed by the wreckage of broken constructs and smoking rubble, their gazes met. For that suspended moment, the noise seemed to fade into a distant hum.</p>
<p>She offered him a small, private nod, none of the theatrical flirtation she usually employed, none of the performative affection she wore like armor. It was a gesture of raw, uncomplicated admiration.</p>
<p>’You did it,’ the nod said. ’You actually did it.’</p>
<p>Then, as quickly as the connection formed, she was swept away by a group of cheering soldiers, pulled back into the intoxicating madness of the moment.</p>
<p>But deeper in the ranks, where the adrenaline began to ebb and the reality of what had transpired started to seep through the cracks of shock, the celebration took on a different quality.</p>
<p>These were the soldiers who had spent their entire lives under the crushing shadow of a surface world that viewed them as vermin. They were the hunted, the dismissed, the creatures who lived in the margins.</p>
<p>Several of them simply stood frozen, their weapons lowered to the blood-slicked stone, absorbing the moment with a disbelief that bordered on terror.</p>
<p>They had marched down this corridor half convinced they were walking into a mass grave. They had prepared to die.</p>
<p>Instead, they had witnessed an inversion of destiny.</p>
<p>In two centuries of demonic history, they had never known a victory this absolute. The scale of the shift from prey to conqueror was so vast that it felt like the very foundation of their reality had cracked.</p>
<p>Near the rear of the formation, a young Shadeveil soldier stumbled slightly, her legs feeling like water. She was barely old enough to have seen blood spilled in anger, let alone the apocalyptic slaughter she had witnessed today.</p>
<p>Her face was wet, tears carving pale tracks through the soot and grime. She turned to the veteran standing beside her, a scarred man who had seen empires rise and fall with nothing but grim resignation.</p>
<p>"We won," she whispered.</p>
<p>The words sounded fragile, as if they might shatter if she spoke them too loudly. "We... we actually won."</p>
<p>Her voice caught on the second syllable, breaking into something resembling a sob.</p>
<p>The veteran didn’t look at her at first. He kept his eyes fixed on the horizon of the corridor, his jaw working silently.</p>
<p>When he finally turned to her, his own eyes were bright, glassy with unshed tears that he made no effort to hide. The stoic mask he had worn for decades had finally crumbled.</p>
<p>"We did," he answered, his voice thick and unsteady.</p>
<p>For once in his life, he didn’t offer a lesson in tactical caution or a reminder of the costs of war. He didn’t correct her disbelief.</p>
<p>He simply stood there in the wreckage of the old world, sharing her wonder. "We fucking did."</p>
