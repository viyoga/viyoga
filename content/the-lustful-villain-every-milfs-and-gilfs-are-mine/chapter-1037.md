<p>Twenty-nine years. Twenty-nine years of striving, of building, of believing in a system that was secretly being dismantled beneath his feet.</p>
<p>Twenty-nine years of climbing toward a summit only to find he was standing on the edge of an abyss.</p>
<p>But here, in this impossible place, the God of Time was offering him something that twenty-nine years of mortal effort had never produced.</p>
<p>He was offering him a variable. He was offering him the specific, mathematical possibility that the gap between them was not infinite and that the impossible could be made inevitable if the method was finally right.</p>
<p>Theo stood in the center of Kaeleth’s domain, surrounded by the swirling, hypnotic patterns of amber gold symbols that pulsed like the heartbeat of the universe itself.</p>
<p>He felt something stirring beneath his ribs. It wasn’t the desperate, clawing hope that had sustained him in his weaker moments.</p>
<p>Hope was fragile; hope could be disappointed. Hope could be broken.</p>
<p>This was different.</p>
<p>It was a profound, terrifying emptiness. It was the absolute ’nothing’ that comes when a man finally releases everything he was holding onto: his pride, his grief, his past, and his very identity.</p>
<p>He had let go of the rope, expecting to plummet into madness, only to find that the release was not a collapse.</p>
<p>The release was not a fall. It was a clearing.</p>
<p>He realized then that emptiness was not the enemy. Emptiness was not the worst thing that could happen to a man.</p>
<p>Emptiness was a canvas. Emptiness was a foundation.</p>
<p>From this void, from this absolute nothingness, he would build something that would tear Rex Rexilion apart.</p>
<p>"Yes," Theo said, his voice steady despite the storm raging in his soul. "I will do it."</p>
<p>There was no triumphant smile from Kaeleth. The God of Time did not perform satisfaction; he was too ancient, too woven into the fundamental mechanics of existence for such mortal displays.</p>
<p>He was beyond emotion; he was beyond approval. He was simply a function of reality responding to a new input.</p>
<p>Kaeleth extended his hand, or rather, he performed the precise gesture that communicated ’hand’ within the complex, nonlinear language of the temporal register.</p>
<p>Between them, the amber gold light began to churn and spiral, condensing with terrifying intensity. It became something that defied human optics, neither fully visible nor completely invisible.</p>
<p>It existed in that liminal state of something that was undeniably real but had not yet been granted the physical form required by the three-dimensional world. It was raw potentiality made manifest.</p>
<p>"The designation of the Temporal Domain," Kaeleth began.</p>
<p>His voice shifted. It was no longer the voice of a being speaking to a man; it was the voice of a being speaking to the very framework of reality.</p>
<p>He was addressing the divine operational code, the cosmic laws that governed all agreements.</p>
<p>He was stating a fact to the universe itself, a declaration that he was not violating any law because Theo had given his consent, and in the realm of gods, consent was the only operative condition required to rewrite fate.</p>
<p>"This power is given to Theo Morr Nightwing, who receives it freely and holds it for the purpose and duration defined by his will and the domain’s agreement."</p>
<p>The condensed amber gold mass moved. It drifted toward Theo like a predator seeking its mark.</p>
<p>When it touched his chest, there was no impact, no sound of flesh meeting force. It did not pierce the skin; it entered.</p>
<p>It was a nonviolent penetration that bypassed the biological entirely, sliding past muscle, bone, and blood to reach the layer beneath the fundamental register where the soul anchored itself to reality.</p>
<p>Theo felt it arrive.</p>
<p>It wasn’t pain. Pain was a biological signal, a warning from the nerves.</p>
<p>This was something far more profound and an overwhelming, terrifying quality of something becoming true that had never been true before. It was like being plunged into a room of absolute darkness and then having every light source ignited simultaneously.</p>
<p>The flash didn’t just illuminate the room; it illuminated the very concept of perception itself.</p>
<p>Time.</p>
<p>He could see it.</p>
<p>The word ’time’ had been a hollow metaphor for twenty nine years. It had been a concept, an abstract property of physics, a ticking clock, and a fading memory.</p>
<p>Now, the metaphor shattered. The illusion died.</p>
<p>He saw it as it truly was: a dimensional property of the universe, running in a continuous, inexorable flow. He saw it not as a sequence of events, but as the fundamental medium in which all things existed.</p>
<p>He saw the way movement was not merely a change in position but a ripple through the temporal fabric itself. The movement was the substance; the destination was merely an artifact of perception.</p>
<p>From where he stood in the amber void, he could see the entire timeline of Aethelgard laid bare. It was a vast, interconnected tapestry of cause and effect, stretching from the primordial dawn to the inevitable heat death of the universe.</p>
<p>And there, coiled like a parasite upon that tapestry, was Rex Rexilion’s temporal signature. It was unmistakable now—a jagged, cancerous wound in the flow of time, consuming causality, bending the threads of fate to suit its own monstrous expansion.</p>
<p>For the first time in all of those years of desperate, failing effort, Theo saw the truth. He saw the gap. He saw the distance between his own fragile existence and Rex’s overwhelming power.</p>
<p>But the horror of the previous moment was replaced by something sharper, something colder, something far more dangerous.</p>
<p>He observed the specific property of the designation he had just received. The gap was no longer a permanent feature of his reality.</p>
<p>The gap was no longer an unbridgeable chasm.</p>
<p>The gap was now simply a problem. And he finally had the tools to solve it.</p>
<p>Then, the sound broke through the sacred silence of the void.</p>
<p>It started as a low rumble in Theo’s chest, a sound that felt foreign to his own throat. Then it erupted into a laugh.</p>
<p>He laughed.</p>
<p>It wasn’t the laugh of a man who had found humor in the absurd, nor was it the laugh of someone who had discovered amusement in his fate.</p>
<p>It was something far more primal and terrifying. He had not laughed in six weeks, not since the last shred of his old life had been torn away by Rex’s shadow.</p>
<p>This laugh was the sound of structural failure; it was the sound of a man who had been compressed under the crushing weight of twenty-nine years of inadequacy until his very soul had crystallized into something harder, sharper, and infinitely more dangerous.</p>
<p>It was the release of pressure that precedes a catastrophic explosion.</p>
<p>Kaeleth did not interrupt. The God of Time simply let it run, watching with detached fascination as the last vestiges of Theo Morr Nightwing the man who struggled, who hoped, who grieved were burned away in that hysterical release.</p>
<p>When the laughter finally died away, it left behind a silence that was heavier than before. Theo stood panting, his breath coming in ragged, visible gasps that seemed to disturb the amber gold light of the domain.</p>
<p>His hands were clenched into fists so tight that his knuckles bled where his nails tore through the skin, but he didn’t seem to feel it. His face was transformed.</p>
<p>The exhaustion and the hollow grief were gone, replaced by something alien. He looked like someone who had walked through the fire and discovered that he was not made of flesh but of something that could burn.</p>
<p>He had arrived at the far side of the abyss. And he had found that the far side was not better.</p>
<p>It was simply different.</p>
<p>"I will go back," Theo said.</p>
<p>His voice was no longer that of a victim. It was quiet, carrying the terrifying stillness of a predator that has finally spotted its prey through the treeline.</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth answered.</p>
<p>The God’s acknowledgment was the only sound in the vastness.</p>
<p>"I will find Rex Rexilion," Theo continued.</p>
<p>Each word felt like a nail being driven into the coffin of the old world. "I will hunt him through every second, every minute, and every fractured moment of his existence."</p>
<p>"I will find him where he thinks he is safe, and I will drag him into the present."</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>"And I will kill him."</p>
<p>There was no anger in the statement. There was no vengeful heat.</p>
<p>The words were flat, absolute, and terrifyingly certain, like a law of physics. It was not a desire he held; it was not a wish or a hope.</p>
<p>It was a decision that had already been written into the fabric of reality. He wasn’t promising to try; he was stating what would happen.</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth said, the word echoing like a tolling bell.</p>
<p>Theo’s eyes, now reflecting the shifting amber currents of the domain, locked onto the gods. One final question remained, the one that had haunted his every waking thought since the cliff’s edge.</p>
<p>"And after?" Theo asked, the question carrying the weight of all the years he had lost, all the people he had failed, and all the emptiness he now carried as armor. "When the disruption is corrected."</p>
<p>"When the instrument has finished its work... What remains for me?"</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s form seemed to expand, filling the void with a presence that was both ancient and new.</p>
<p>"After," Kaeleth said, his voice carrying the promise of an infinite horizon, "is yours."</p>
