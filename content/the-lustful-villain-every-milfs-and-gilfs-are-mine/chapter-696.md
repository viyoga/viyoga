<p>Ren Askar had not moved an inch. He stood in a state of profound, unnatural stillness, the kind of rigid composure possessed only by those who are undergoing a violent cognitive restructuring.</p>
<p>He was holding his physical form in a state of total stasis, diverting every ounce of metabolic and mental energy away from his muscles and into the frantic, terrifying processing of the data Rex had just unleashed.</p>
<p>"The woman who founded the Legion," Ren said, his voice sounding hollow, as if it were coming from a vast distance. "Celestina Von Starlight."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex replied.</p>
<p>"She isn't just a shadow, is she?" Ren's eyes were fixed on nothingness. "She is moving against Aethelgard."</p>
<p>"She is building toward it," Rex corrected, his tone sharpening. "The canyon engagement isn't just a distraction; it is her window of opportunity."</p>
<p>"The moment the shield is lowered, she strikes."</p>
<p>Ren's gaze dropped to the shattered flagstones of the practice ground, the physical evidence of a sudden, violent emergence.</p>
<p>"The Academy archives," he whispered, the realization crystallizing. "The records of the 'unexplained' disappearances."</p>
<p>"Fourteen documented incidents in the past eighteen years within Aethelgard's regional sector," Rex provided, the numbers hitting like hammer blows. "The incidents cluster around periods of intense Apostle network activity."</p>
<p>"High activity periods attract the Legion's eyes because they create concentrations of reincarnators in predictable, vulnerable locations."</p>
<p>Ren's jaw tightened. "The canyon expedition... it will be the highest activity period the network has seen in years."</p>
<p>"Precisely," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Which means the Legion hasn't just been watching," Ren said, his voice rising with a sudden, sharp urgency. "They've been studying the buildup."</p>
<p>"They have a complete tactical picture of the expedition's composition before the first soldier even deploys."</p>
<p>"Celestina's network has been embedded on this island for at least two years, Ren," Rex said, his voice a grim confirmation. "They don't just have a picture, but they have a blueprint."</p>
<p>A heavy, suffocating silence descended upon the practice ground. Ren was silent for a long moment, the weight of the impending massacre pressing down on his shoulders.</p>
<p>"The archive," Ren said, the word a vow.</p>
<p>"The archive," Rex echoed.</p>
<p>With a sudden, decisive motion, Ren broke his combat stance. It was a profound physical transition, the movement of a man who had realized that the battle he had prepared for this morning was a mere skirmish compared to the war that was actually coming.</p>
<p>He looked at the golem one last time, his eyes searching the stone for a truth he wasn't yet ready to fully grasp.</p>
<p>"The geological anomaly that affected this island this morning," Ren said, his voice trembling slightly. "The energy signature... it's the same thing that caused the upheaval in the Underlayer last night."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And it is on the surface now," Ren realized, a chill running down his spine. "It's already here."</p>
<p>"It arrived this morning," Rex confirmed.</p>
<p>Ren turned his gaze toward the massive silhouette of the Academy, his eyes tracking the upper floors where the indicators for the restricted archives were visible from the ground.</p>
<p>"If I pull those records," Ren said, his voice hardening into a blade of intent, "and if the truth matches your account... if the pieces of this nightmare actually fit together... then when I meet you at the market square, you had better be ready to tell me exactly what the Underlayer's governance wants from someone in my position."</p>
<p>"They want a clear-eyed person in the right location at the right time," Rex said. "The canyon engagement is going to require someone on the surface who understands the gravity of both sides."</p>
<p>"A hero who has made an informed decision is worth ten who simply follow the network's call."</p>
<p>"You aren't describing a soldier," Ren said, his eyes flashing. "You're describing an intelligence asset."</p>
<p>"I am describing the role that emerges from your designation and the truth you are about to uncover," Rex countered. "Whether you accept it depends on what the archives tell you and what you decide that truth is worth."</p>
<p>Ren Askar turned toward the Academy entrance, his stride long and purposeful.</p>
<p>"I will be at the square," he said, a final command.</p>
<p>He disappeared into the building, leaving the silence of the practice ground behind.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>In the digital ether, Rex released the fifth golem's primary focus, settling his consciousness back into the vast, distributed relay network. He felt the world through eleven simultaneous perspectives, a kaleidoscopic sweep of the island's morning life.</p>
<p>The sixth golem had successfully located its contact. The seventh was weaving through the residential substrate.</p>
<p>The eighth had surfaced at the outer settlement's intersection, waiting for a reincarnator whose signature suggested they were still hiding behind closed doors. But Rex's primary attention was anchored to the ninth.</p>
<p>The ninth golem was surging toward the most significant energy signature in the residential district, the one that Veran Caulstow's hunter designation had flagged as the second most notable read on the entire island. It was a signature Rex had identified during the geological mapping as the unmistakable architecture of a detection-type system running at full, passive output.</p>
<p>Detection type systems were the most dangerous kind of intelligence. They were the ones that knew he was already here.</p>
<p>They had known since before the sun had even breached the horizon. And the ninth golem broke the surface, and the hunt truly began.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Rex stood at the island's jagged perimeter, his consciousness stretched thin across the horizon. Through the eleven simultaneous perspectives granted by the Earthen Authority's relay architecture, he was a god of fragmented vision.</p>
<p>He watched every interaction, every small movement, and every gathering point with focused but divided attention while the island's geological map pulsed below him, providing a steady flow of data on movement patterns, energy signatures, and the land's heartbeat.</p>
<p>The consolidation was working. It was proceeding with a terrifying, mathematical precision.</p>
<p>Eleven golems. Twenty-three reincarnators.</p>
<p>The variables were shifting, the outcomes dictated by the flagging status of each soul. The unflagged ones moved toward the central market square with a singular, chilling efficiency, the movement of people who had heard the message and understood that their world had just changed.</p>
<p>The flagged ones, however, were receiving a different kind of message.</p>
<p>Rex watched through a distant lens as two flagged engagements reached their conclusion. It was a brutal, lopsided display of power: geological force meeting high-tier combat systems.</p>
<p>The result was predictable.</p>
<p>When a combat system meets a force that possesses the home field advantage of operating through the very material it is made of, the struggle is brief. The island itself was not just the battlefield; it was the weapon.</p>
<p>He began to walk toward the main settlement.</p>
<p>The Earthen Authority's passive awareness flowed with him like a second skin. The geological substrate gave him a constant flow of information about the island's strength, the changing population, and the unique signs of the reincarnators he encountered.</p>
<p>As he reached the edge of the commercial district, the island's substrate connected with the dense, artificial gradient of the central plaza. Rex paused, his gaze sweeping over the surrounding architecture.</p>
<p>He had worn the mask of the Lustful Villain for so long that the partial integration had become a seamless part of his existence. He no longer consciously registered the weight of it; instead, he registered the effect.</p>
<p>The mask had fundamentally rewritten the way the world perceived him. The Lustful Villain was not Rex Rexilion.</p>
<p>The surface of the mask reflected the image of the student: a reliable, disciplined academy scholar who had shaken Elliot's hand, sat in Elizabeth's study, and shared quiet breakfasts at the Starlight household.</p>
<p>But the man standing at the edge of the commercial district this morning was a stranger to that life.</p>
<p>The mask's surface possessed the heavy, suffocating quality of compressed divine mineral material. It had fused with the skin beneath, its edges not the clean, artificial lines of a garment but a gradual, terrifying transition of flesh becoming stone. His gauntlets were fully exposed, the Earthen Authority's raw, stone-mineral texture bleeding from his forearms down over his hands.</p>
<p>Between the masked visage and the geological limbs, he did not look like a man who had arrived from the sky or the sea. He looked like something that had clawed its way out of the primordial dark.</p>
<p>Lord Xerollion, as the Underlayer had christened him, looked every bit the part of a subterranean god walking upon the surface.</p>
<p>"Time to have some fun in the Aethelgard after so long..."</p>
