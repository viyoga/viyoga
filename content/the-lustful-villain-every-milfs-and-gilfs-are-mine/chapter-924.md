<p>It happened again over the following days, not as a sudden storm but as a slow, insidious seepage she couldn’t quite stem. Small observations that she found herself cataloguing with more precision than she cared to admit, each one a tiny prick of irritation beneath her skin.</p>
<p>She noticed the junior maids first. Two of them, girls who usually moved through the household like frightened mice, had developed an increasingly elaborate repertoire of excuses to visit the training hall during Rex’s sessions.</p>
<p>They brought refreshments that didn’t need chilled water when the servants had already delivered fresh casks and honeyed pastries that seemed suspiciously out of season. They would linger just a heartbeat too long, their eyes darting toward the sweat-slicked muscle of his back as he drove a practice blade through a training dummy with bone-crushing force.</p>
<p>They wanted to watch the way his shirt clung to his shoulders, the way his breathing came in heavy, controlled grunts that sounded dangerously primal.</p>
<p>Then there was the visiting cousin, a woman several years older than Diana, who had come to observe the morning’s session out of idle curiosity. Iris had watched from the shadows of the colonnade as the woman approached Elaris afterward.</p>
<p>The cousin’s interest had been poorly disguised, her voice carrying that particular honeyed lilt that women used when they were sizing up prey. She had asked Elaris, with obvious fascination, whether Rex Rexilion was formally attached to anyone or if he was simply... informally busy.</p>
<p>Iris had been standing close enough to catch every syllable. She watched Elaris deliver her answer with that usual, delighted diplomacy, a masterful performance of deflection that managed to acknowledge the question without actually clarifying anything.</p>
<p>It was a perfect non-answer, leaving the door wide open for any ambitious woman to try her luck.</p>
<p>And Iris had found herself unexpectedly, violently annoyed by the entire exchange.</p>
<p>The irritation burned hot and sharp in her chest, a foreign sensation that had no business existing. She reminded herself repeatedly of the facts: she and Rex had no arrangement.</p>
<p>There were no vows, no claims, and no soft words whispered in the dark that implied ownership. Their connection was forged in blood, steel, and the shared trauma of what had been lost.</p>
<p>He was a man who promised her violence and a conversation with a ghost, nothing more.</p>
<p>’Why does it matter?’ she asked herself, her jaw tightening as she cleaned her blade in the privacy of her quarters. ’He belongs to no one...’</p>
<p>’He is a storm that passes through places, leaving nothing but wreckage behind...’</p>
<p>’Why does it feel like someone is trying to claim territory that isn’t mine?’</p>
<p>But the logic didn’t soothe the sting. The annoyance was a physical thing, a tightness in her throat that made it hard to swallow.</p>
<p>She sat with that feeling for a long time, turning it over in her mind the way she turned over an unfamiliar blade, testing its weight, probing its balance, searching for a flaw that would allow her to discard it. She examined the edge of the emotion, wondering if it was anger or something more vulnerable and therefore more dangerous.</p>
<p>She did not reach a conclusion. She was not the type to surrender to sentimentality without a fight.</p>
<p>Instead, she simply acknowledged it with the flat, brutal honesty she applied to every other aspect of her existence. The feeling was there.</p>
<p>It was new, it was unwelcome, and it was undeniable. And most damning of all, she realized with a grim certainty that this feeling arrived most reliably whenever Rex was surrounded by women who had found some fresh, thin excuse, some pathetic excuse involving training or thirst, to stand within his orbit.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The household’s main sitting room, on the third afternoon after the training hall’s opening, had ceased to be a place of leisure. The soft cushions and delicate porcelain had been abandoned, replaced by the grim, cluttered atmosphere of a war room.</p>
<p>The air was thick with the smell of old parchment, spilled ink, and the charged, electric tension of minds working at their absolute limits.</p>
<p>Elaris had commandeered the largest oak table, clearing away everything that didn’t serve a purpose.</p>
<p>Three separate sets of documents now dominated the space: the Nightwing archive’s fragmentary references to the key to the underlayer’s construction methodology, the charred and bloodstained partial fragments Elizabeth had recovered from the plaza’s rubble and a sprawling set of cross-referenced notes. Elizabeth had been compiling like a surgeon preparing for an operation.</p>
<p>These notes correlated what remained of the Key against the Academy’s ancient, dusty records on divine artifact reconstruction.</p>
<p>Elizabeth sat across from Elaris, her usual composed precision honed into something sharper, more dangerous. Her sleeves were pushed up, revealing forearms smudged with ink and dust, her eyes bright with the feverish intensity she brought to a problem that threatened to unravel the world.</p>
<p>Beside her, Lily sat like a silent sentinel, surrounded by a mountain of the Nightwing archive’s oldest, most fragile volumes. Lily read with the specific, careful focus she had adopted since Rex had mentioned, almost as an afterthought, that understanding the Key’s origins would be vital.</p>
<p>She wasn’t just reading; she was hunting for a needle in a haystack of dead languages.</p>
<p>"This passage right here," Elizabeth said, her voice hushed but carrying an edge of excitement. She tapped a fingertip against a line on one of the fragments, the paper crinkling under her touch. "It references a resonance frequency, a specific harmonic signature that doesn’t match anything in the Academy’s standard cataloguing system for divine artifacts."</p>
<p>"The Academy’s system is clean, orderly, and utterly wrong. This frequency is... wrong. It’s jagged."</p>
<p>’It’s not just a number,’ Elizabeth thought, her mind racing through layers of theoretical physics and ancient magic. ’It’s a heartbeat... A heartbeat from somewhere that shouldn’t exist.’</p>
<p>"It’s not wrong, Elizabeth," Elaris countered, sliding one of the ancient, leather-bound volumes across the table with practiced grace. "It’s simply older."</p>
<p>"The household maintained its own private classification system for two centuries before the academy came along with their standardized, sanitized versions."</p>
<p>"If that fragment predates the Great Standardization, it won’t speak their language... It will speak ours."</p>
<p>Lily’s hands moved with surprising speed through the pages Elaris indicated. She possessed a quiet, relentless focus, the kind of focus she had unconsciously mirrored from Rex.</p>
<p>She wasn’t interested in the poetry of the texts; she was looking for the mechanics, the hidden bones beneath the skin of the words.</p>
<p>"Found it," Lily said after several minutes of silent searching.</p>
<p>Her voice broke the concentration of the room like a blade cutting through silk. She slid the volume back toward Elizabeth.</p>
<p>"Here. Look at the section on harmonic harmonics."</p>
<p>"This entry describes a frequency class they called ’deep resonance.’ It’s specifically reserved for artifacts designed to interface with dimensional layers existing below the primary physical plane, rather than simply manipulating what’s within it."</p>
<p>Elizabeth froze. She read the passage twice, then three times, her lips moving silently as she translated the archaic terminology. Then, a slow smile spread across her face, the hungry, satisfied smile of a scholar who had just tasted blood.</p>
<p>"That is exactly it," Elizabeth whispered, her eyes blazing. "The Key wasn’t just a spatial artifact designed to open doors between places."</p>
<p>"It was constructed to interface with something deeper."</p>
<p>"Something beneath the underlayer itself... It wasn’t built to travel through dimensions; it was built to pierce them."</p>
<p>She leaned back, her mind already constructing new, more terrifying theories. "That reframes everything."</p>
<p>"If the Key can reach below the Underlayer, then what we thought was the bottom of existence is merely the ceiling of something else entirely."</p>
<p>"We’ve been looking at the map upside down."</p>
<p>Elaris watched them both, a spark of genuine pride lighting her features. "That is a significant find, Elizabeth."</p>
<p>"Possibly the most significant we’ve uncovered since the attack." She looked between the two women, the scholar and the girl who had learned to hunt for truth as efficiently as Rex hunted for enemies. "You two make an excellent research pair."</p>
<p>"One provides the vision; the other provides the precision."</p>
