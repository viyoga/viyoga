<p>The first day’s travel retraced the ridge path in reverse, Drevash receding in the morning light as the carriages picked their way back toward the main road. Rex spent it watching the terrain and thinking.</p>
<p>The document from Kregg was memorized.</p>
<p>The relay structure was mapped in his head.</p>
<p>The name "Celestina Von Starlight" sat where it had been sitting since the canyon, waiting for the right context.</p>
<p>Aurelia was in his carriage for part of the first day and said, in the loose stretch before the ridge descent, "You remind me of someone."</p>
<p>"The version of you they remind me of made worse choices with what he had."</p>
<p>"Who?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"My son," Aurelia said. "The similarities represent the best aspects of what he could have become."</p>
<p>She paused. "I don’t say that as a compliment. I say it as context."</p>
<p>Rex held her gaze for a moment. "I’ll take it as context," he said.</p>
<p>"That’s the right way to take it," she said.</p>
<p>’But still... your son is going to have it harder after I get back to Aethelgard, hahaha!’</p>
<p>The second day brought them off the ridge and onto the broad coastal road, and in the far distance the floating island of Aethelgard sat above the Convergence Waters with the afternoon light catching its underside.</p>
<p>The third day was arrival.</p>
<p>Rex spotted Lily before he had fully descended the carriage steps. She rushed across the courtyard with the urgency of someone who had been eagerly anticipating something and was now done waiting.</p>
<p>BAM!</p>
<p>The impact of her embrace hit him hard, as if she had been tracking the three-day gap with the intensity of a strong bond and was now confirming her conclusion. Rex caught her weight just in time to prevent either of them from stumbling.</p>
<p>"You’re here, you’re finally home safely...!" she said into his shoulder.</p>
<p>"Generally," Rex said.</p>
<p>Diana stood a few steps behind, embodying her characteristic restrained relief for matters she felt deeply about. Not overtly expressive, yet undeniably present, her emotions were clear to anyone familiar with her subtle cues.</p>
<p>Her gaze quickly scanned the situation, conducting a thorough assessment before settling into the warm steadiness she mostly kept internal.</p>
<p>"How bad was it," Diana said. "the expedition?"</p>
<p>"Eh, manageable," Rex said.</p>
<p>"That’s not the same as fine," Diana said.</p>
<p>"It’s better than fine," Rex said. "Fine means nothing happened."</p>
<p>Diana looked at him for a moment and then accepted this with the slight nod of someone who has learned to work with the information she’s given rather than pushing for more in a public courtyard.</p>
<p>Lily had stepped back enough to look at him properly.</p>
<p>"That ice princess was watching you the entire last leg of the carriage ride," she said. "It felt strange. Not hostile—just... watching."</p>
<p>"She’s processing," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Processing what?"</p>
<p>"The expedition," Rex said.</p>
<p>Lily looked at him with the expression she used when she had learned to distinguish between Rex’s complete statements and his technically accurate ones. She let it go.</p>
<p>"Come find me later," she said.</p>
<p>"I will," Rex said, and kissed her forehead, and she accepted this with the small sound of someone filing information they intend to return to at a more private moment.</p>
<p>Elizabeth crossed the courtyard toward him about ten minutes after the carriages were unloaded, moving with the composed, deliberate pace of someone who has been waiting for the right moment and has identified it.</p>
<p>"When you have a moment," she said, using the academic way of saying ’now.’</p>
<p>"The garden on the east side is empty at this hour."</p>
<p>"Ten minutes," Rex said.</p>
<p>She turned and walked toward it.</p>
<p>"What does she want?" Lily said, quietly.</p>
<p>"Expedition debrief," Rex said.</p>
<p>Lily looked at him. She let it go again.</p>
<p>The garden on the east side of the building was a long, narrow space between two walls where the formal arrangement had long since given way to something more self-directed.</p>
<p>The late afternoon light came in over the west wall and turned the space amber. Elizabeth was standing near the far end when Rex arrived, her back partially to him, and she turned when she heard his footsteps.</p>
<p>"I need to be direct with you," she said, her tone reflecting the hours she had spent preparing for this moment.</p>
<p>"Then be direct," Rex replied.</p>
<p>"The Key," she said. "I want to know what actually happened."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her steadily. "What would change in your position if I told you the destruction was deliberate?"</p>
<p>Elizabeth was quiet. "That depends on the reason," she said.</p>
<p>"The Legion had it," Rex said. "Their relay network was already triggered by Kregg’s compromise."</p>
<p>’The Balance Keeper’s monitoring infrastructure would have tracked the Key in transit before we reached Drevash."</p>
<p>"The Apostle network would have had it in analysis for six months and then attempted an Underlayer approach under conditions that the second-stratum situation makes genuinely dangerous." He paused. "Four fragments in your jacket pocket is the safest version of the Key that currently exists."</p>
<p>"That’s a unilateral decision," she said.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Made without consulting me or the network or Valentina, who funded and approved this expedition."</p>
<p>"Another yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth pressed her lips together briefly.</p>
<p>"I have to stand in front of my mother," she said, and the professional composure in her voice had a specific stress line in it that Rex had been listening for. "She is going to examine everything."</p>
<p>"She is going to pull on every thread until she has the complete picture. And if I walk in there with a story that has gaps she can find, she will find them."</p>
<p>"I can give you material that doesn’t have gaps," Rex said. "The relay structure, the document from Kregg, the ring, the intelligence on the Balance Keeper’s monitoring infrastructure."</p>
<p>"The analysis holds. The conclusion that the Key couldn’t have been safely returned is supportable."</p>
<p>"You’re offering to help me build an account," she said.</p>
<p>"I’m offering to share accurate information," Rex said. "So the account you present to Lady Valentina has no threads that fail examination."</p>
<p>"And in exchange," she said.</p>
<p>"The question of how the Key was destroyed stays between us," Rex said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked at the garden plants for a long time. The amber light deepened toward evening.</p>
<p>"Alexander," she said.</p>
<p>"He thinks he stumbled," Rex said. "Genuinely."</p>
<p>"If I tell Lady Valentina what I suspect, she looks at him and sees the person who dropped the most critical artifact of the expedition and can’t account for why." She closed her eyes briefly. "That follows him for years."</p>
<p>Rex said nothing.</p>
<p>"You built this carefully," she said. "Every element of it is in the right position."</p>
<p>"The elements are what they are," Rex said. "I’m giving you the accurate version of them."</p>
<p>"The accurate version that also happens to require me to keep a secret from my mother and protect you from accountability for a decision you made without asking anyone." She looked at him with the evaluating directness she used for things she had fully assessed and was stating plainly. "I know what this is, Rex."</p>
<p>"I know you know," Rex said.</p>
<p>She was quiet for another long moment.</p>
<p>"I need tonight to think," she said.</p>
<p>"Take the time you need," Rex said.</p>
<p>She looked at him once more, with the expression of someone who has seen clearly and is deciding whether clarity is going to change their decision. Then she turned and walked back toward the building.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the garden alone for a moment.</p>
<p>He thought about the name Celestina Von Starlight, seven days south, and a family tree that had just become the most significant strategic variable he was currently managing.</p>
<p>He thought about Valentina, who was the most powerful mage in Aethelgard and who had granted him Honor Student status personally and who looked like a woman in her late twenties.</p>
<p>Then he went back inside.</p>
<p>’I think I’m starting to see a good idea... but I’ll need to go to the Underlayer again after this.’</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The city’s midnight had the specific quiet that a settlement achieves only in the hours between the last movement and the first, when the ambient noise of thousands of people drops to the underlying hum of buildings and water and wind.</p>
<p>Rex stood outside the inn’s side entrance, where the garden wall met the building’s eastern face, and the Convergence Waters spread out in the dark beyond the rooftops. The night air had the mineral sharpness of the harbor, cold and carrying the faint sound of the water far below.</p>
<p>He heard her footsteps before she came around the garden wall’s corner.</p>
<p>Elizabeth emerged with the air of someone who has spent the evening making a decision, arriving at it, and now carrying the weight of that choice without wanting it to show. She wore a coat, her hair cascading down, and her expression held the distinct composure of someone who has set something down within themselves and is now deliberating what to carry forward.</p>
<p>She stopped a meter from him.</p>
<p>"I want to be clear about what this is," she said.</p>
<p>"Then be clear," Rex said.</p>
<p>"This is a transaction," she stated, her voice unwavering. "The account that protects Alexander and gives me something coherent to take to Lady Valentina in exchange for tonight."</p>
<p>She held his gaze. "It doesn’t change what I know about the canyon, and it doesn’t change what I know about how the Key broke."</p>
<p>"I understand," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And you understand that I’m here because of what Alexander needs," she said. "Not because I’m unclear on what you are."</p>
<p>"I understand that too," Rex said.</p>
<p>She held his gaze for a moment longer, the composure in place and fixed. "Then that’s everything I needed to say before."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her with the patient, level attention he brought to things that mattered.</p>
<p>"You’re free to do anything you want with me," she said, "as long as the secret stays between us."</p>
<p>"Anything, huh...?"</p>
<p>"I know that you’ve done a lot for the expedition, especially getting that key, which was the hardest job while also handling those Legion..." Elizabeth sighed. "And all of that was ruined because of him."</p>
<p>"This is a punishment for my stubborn fiance, and after that... I do hope you could help me with this problem after you’re having your way with me."</p>
<p>"Yes..." Elizabeth pressed both her breasts with her arms. "Anything you want..."</p>
<p>’She seems chill about this...’</p>
<p>"Oh, of course, I will," Rex smirked. "In the end... it’s all your fiance’s fault."</p>
<p>The night air flowed between them, infused with the mineral sharpness of the water, while the midnight city enveloped the moment in a way that suggested a transformation was imminent, one that would alter everything it had been.</p>
<p>He reached past her and pushed open the side entrance door.</p>
<p>"Let’s go to my room."</p>
