<p>Lustia let out a laugh, a short, unsteady sound that cracked like shattering crystal. It was a sound completely devoid of divine composure, almost human in its fragility.</p>
<p>She reached up, wiping at the corner of her eyes with the back of her hand in a raw, unguarded gesture that belonged to someone far younger than the millennia etched into the stones of her domain.</p>
<p>"Look at what you have done," she said, shaking her head as she tried to reassemble her shattered dignity. "I brought you here to warn you, to tell you that a god attempted to erase the very foundation of your existence, and somehow, this is the part of the night that undoes me."</p>
<p>Rex watched her, his expression unreadable, though the intensity in his gaze never wavered.</p>
<p>"I did not plan it that way," he said simply.</p>
<p>"I know," she whispered, a bitter smile touching her lips. "That is exactly why it worked."</p>
<p>The moment of vulnerability passed, sealed away by necessity. Lustia straightened her spine, the goddess reasserting control over the woman.</p>
<p>Then, she began to speak.</p>
<p>She poured out everything she knew about Kaeleth’s domain. She spoke of temporal eddies that could shred a soul into infinite screaming fragments; of defensive architectures woven from crystallized moments of frozen time; and of the way the domain’s gravity didn’t pull at the body, but at the causality of one’s own past.</p>
<p>Rex listened with a flat, systematic attention, the kind of cold, predatory focus he reserved for intelligence briefings where every syllable could mean the difference between a successful kill and a catastrophic failure.</p>
<p>By the time she finished, the incomplete picture was burned into his mind. He saw the map not as a series of locations but as a series of vulnerabilities.</p>
<p>He saw the gaps in her knowledge, the dark spaces where divine sight failed, and he marked them as places where he would have to wade through blood to find answers.</p>
<p>’The temporal mechanics...’ Rex thought, his mind already dissecting her words. ’If the domain operates on a feedback loop of cause and effect, then any intrusion that doesn’t match the established timeline will trigger a cascade failure.’</p>
<p>’I won’t just be fighting guards; I’ll be fighting the very concept of... now.’</p>
<p>’Every step forward will be a war against the universe trying to correct my presence.’</p>
<p>The ambient light of the domain began to shift, the warm amber hues deepening into a bruised purple, signaling that the session was drawing to its inevitable conclusion.</p>
<p>"One more thing," Rex said, his voice cutting through the settling silence.</p>
<p>Lustia turned back to him, her eyes wary.</p>
<p>"You said the reset would have undone everything I built since the working began," Rex said, his mind already calculating the stakes. "The Underlayer’s reconstruction..."</p>
<p>"The blood spilled in Aethelgard and the intelligence extracted from Ignivara..."</p>
<p>"All of it erased."</p>
<p>"Yes," Lustia confirmed, her voice dropping to a solemn register. "Wiped clean as if the last months were never written in the book of fate."</p>
<p>"What is the specific anchor point?" Rex asked, leaning forward, his pupils contracting. "The exact moment from which the reset would have triggered?"</p>
<p>Lustia paused, her lips pressing into a thin line as she searched through layers of cosmic memory.</p>
<p>"The working’s anchor point," she said finally. "The moment the chain became unbreakable."</p>
<p>"The moment Celestina contracted with Kaeleth."</p>
<p>Rex’s jaw tightened. "Which was..."</p>
<p>"Approximately," Lustia said, choosing her words with deadly precision, "six months before the current date as experienced by the inhabitants of Aethelgard."</p>
<p>Rex closed his eyes for a second, the implications crashing over him like a tidal wave. Six months.</p>
<p>Half a year of slaughter, of strategic positioning, of building his empire of bone and steel—all of it hung on a thread that a single divine whim could have severed.</p>
<p>’Six months,’ he thought bitterly. ’Six months of playing chess with destiny, only to realize the board was rigged from the start.’</p>
<p>’Kaeleth didn’t just try to kill me; he tried to make it so I never existed to challenge him.’</p>
<p>’He tried to delete my struggle.’</p>
<p>"But keep in mind... the timeline is currently fucked, so no one... even we didn’t know the real time, so just to make sure, you need to fix it by teaching Kaeleth a lesson."</p>
<p>A cold, murderous resolve crystallized in Rex’s chest. His grip tightened on the armrest of his chair, the wood creaking ominously under the pressure of his strength.</p>
<p>’Let him try to reset the clock again,’ Rex vowed internally, his thoughts turning as sharp as a guillotine blade. ’Next time I step into his domain, I’m not going to play by his rules...’</p>
<p>’I’m going to break the clock, smash the gears, and drag him into whatever fractured reality he thinks he can control.’</p>
<p>’I’ll make him feel every second he tried to steal from me.’</p>
<p>Rex fell into a silence that was not empty but loaded a pressurized stillness that seemed to warp the space between them. His mind was a furnace, burning through data, cross-referencing variables with the ruthless efficiency of a predator calculating the trajectory of a kill.</p>
<p>"The six months everyone keeps referencing," he said finally, his voice carrying the flat, dangerous edge of a blade being drawn from its sheath.</p>
<p>"Yes," Lustia replied, her voice steadying but still carrying the faintest tremor of the vulnerability she had just shown.</p>
<p>"My subjective timeline is significantly shorter," Rex stated. It wasn’t a question; it was a diagnosis of a fundamental flaw in reality.</p>
<p>Lustia nodded slowly, her divine eyes reflecting the complex geometries of the temporal wound she was describing. "Because the temporal working’s influence was not uniform across the island, Rex."</p>
<p>"Time did not flow like a river there; it moved like a storm, swirling around obstacles."</p>
<p>"The working affected people based on their connection to the island’s foundational substrate..."</p>
<p>"And you..." she paused, choosing her words as if walking through a minefield, "...you are connected to that substrate at a divine level through the Earthen Authority."</p>
<p>"That connection created a different relationship to the work’s influence than the standard biological connection of a mortal."</p>
<p>"I experienced the actual time," Rex said, his jaw tightening. "The raw, unvarnished seconds as they truly passed."</p>
<p>"You experienced something closer to it," she corrected gently. "The discrepancy between your timeline and the island’s is precisely what produced the inconsistencies you noticed."</p>
<p>"You were walking through the real world while everyone else was living inside a temporal bubble."</p>
<p>Rex’s mind began to sprint. He replayed every conversation, every piece of paper, every strategic move he had made.</p>
<p>He saw Valentina’s resource authorizations flashing before his eyes. He saw the research timelines laid out in meticulous detail.</p>
<p>He saw the documented history, the very thing that had provided his bulletproof alibi.</p>
<p>"The documented history," Rex said, the realization clicking into place like the hammer of a gun. "The archive entries."</p>
<p>"Elizabeth’s authorization codes... These weren’t just records; they were created within the working’s influenced timeline."</p>
<p>"Yes," Lustia said grimly. "Which means they reflect the island’s experienced time, not the actual time."</p>
<p>"They are consistent within the working’s framework," Rex mused, a cold smile tugging at the corner of his mouth, a smile that promised violence to anyone who dared challenge his logic. "Whether that framework was a legitimate manipulation of reality or a divine deception, the documents exist."</p>
<p>"They are internally consistent and they hold up under any scrutiny."</p>
<p>"Yes," she agreed.</p>
<p>"Which means the alibi works regardless of whether the working’s existence is proven or denied," Rex concluded.</p>
<p>The trap he had built was stronger than he had imagined. Even if Kaeleth revealed the truth of the temporal manipulation, the paper trail would still point to the same conclusion: Rex was exactly where he claimed to be.</p>
<p>"In the sense that the documents exist, are verifiable, and align with the perceived reality of every witness... yes," Lustia said, almost impressed by the ruthless pragmatism of his mind.</p>
<p>Rex looked at her one last time before the domain’s light completed its shift. The amber glow was fading into a deep, obsidian dusk.</p>
<p>The look he gave her wasn’t the transactional glance of an apostle speaking to his goddess. It was something heavier.</p>
<p>An acknowledgment of a shared secret, of a vulnerability exposed, and of a debt that might never be fully repaid.</p>
<p>"Thank you," Rex said, his voice dropping to a sincere, gravelly register. "For the whole truth."</p>
<p>"I know it cost you in more ways than one to give it to me in that order."</p>
<p>Lustia drew a breath, finally reassembling the fragments of her regal persona. Her voice regained its melodic strength, though the ghost of her earlier emotion still lingered in her eyes.</p>
<p>"You are welcome. But Rex... come back and say things like that more often."</p>
<p>’If you continue, I may start to actually enjoy these visits instead of merely orchestrating them."</p>
<p>"I will keep that in mind," Rex said.</p>
<p>As the divine realm began to dissolve around him, Rex allowed himself a single, quiet exhale, the sound of a man who had just survived a psychological ambush and emerged with a weapon in hand.</p>
<p>’The pieces are set,’ he thought as the darkness rushed in. ’ I have my alibi, I have my target, and I have the truth...’</p>
<p>’Now, all that’s left is to break them...’</p>
<p>His consciousness plunged downward, tearing through layers of astral energy until the familiar, crushing weight of physical reality returned.</p>
<p>He returned to his body.</p>
