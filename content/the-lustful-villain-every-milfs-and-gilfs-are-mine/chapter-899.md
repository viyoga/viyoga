<p>They crashed to the ground together, a tangle of limbs, blood, and labored breathing. They rolled across the blood-slicked floor, each trying to position themselves for a killing blow.</p>
<p>Rex managed to pin her wrist against the hardwood, his forearm pressing down on her throat with enough pressure to bruise, while Iris had her fingers hooked like talons around the tendons of his inner elbow.</p>
<p>The hall was silent save for their ragged, animalistic panting. Blood from both of them smeared the floor where they struggled, creating a grotesque tableau of crimson streaks.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at her, his vision swimming with adrenaline and pain. Iris looked up at him, her face a mask of blood and sweat, her eyes burning with a terrifying, beautiful ferocity.</p>
<p>"You’re not... done," she whispered, the words bubbling through the blood in her mouth.</p>
<p>The violence in the center of the room died not because the combatants chose to stop but because the very atmosphere of the hall shifted. The air grew heavy, charged with a different kind of pressure, not the chaotic, adrenaline-fueled pressure of a fight, but the crushing, absolute weight of sovereignty.</p>
<p>Aurelia stepped onto the training floor.</p>
<p>Her movement was precise, each footfall carrying the undeniable authority of the woman who owned the ground beneath them. She moved like a queen entering a battlefield she had already won.</p>
<p>The twelve fighters, their bodies bruised and bloodied from Rex’s earlier rampage, scrambled to find some semblance of dignity, straightening their backs despite their injuries.</p>
<p>Diana, standing at the hall’s edge, went deathly still, her breathing catching in her throat. Elaris opened her mouth as if to protest or interject, but the look in Aurelia’s eyes froze the words before they could form.</p>
<p>Aurelia didn’t look at the wounded; she looked at the survivors. Her gaze swept over the assembled family, the guards, and the broken bodies on the floor.</p>
<p>"Rex Rexilion has defeated twelve of this household’s elite unit without invoking a single system ability," she began, her voice carrying to every corner of the vaulted ceiling like a tolling bell. "He engaged Iris, an assassin who survived on instinct alone, and neutralized her in less than three minutes of sustained combat."</p>
<p>She paused, letting the implications sink in. The silence was deafening.</p>
<p>"What you have witnessed in this hour is not the performance of a man who succeeded through fortune or exceptional circumstances," she continued, her eyes locking onto Rex’s with unsettling intensity. "What you have seen is a predator whose capabilities exceed nearly everything this household has ever produced."</p>
<p>A cold shiver seemed to ripple through the remaining guards. The Nightwing household was built on a foundation of superiority, a legacy forged in blood and maintained through generations of absolute dominance.</p>
<p>"The Nightwing household has maintained its position in Aethelgard for four generations through one singular commitment," Aurelia said, her voice hardening. "We produce fighters who are objectively better than those who seek to replace us."</p>
<p>"My husband is currently on an extended mission, and during his absence, this household’s military capacity has been managed through the existing unit structure."</p>
<p>"But the unit structure has proven insufficient."</p>
<p>She turned fully toward Rex, her silhouette sharp against the torchlight.</p>
<p>"I am offering you the position of acting combat director for this household’s training program," she stated. "This authority is temporary, valid only until my husband’s return."</p>
<p>"But the mandate is absolute: you will improve this unit... You will raise them to a standard that matches our name."</p>
<p>The twelve fighters exchanged glances, some filled with resentment, others with a dawning, terrified respect. The idea of a stranger, a man who had just dismantled them like marionettes, commanding their training was a bitter pill to swallow.</p>
<p>Aurelia’s lips curled into something that wasn’t quite a smile but lacked any warmth.</p>
<p>"However," she added, her voice dropping to a dangerous, intimate register. "Power must be validated and authority must be earned through more than just words."</p>
<p>She stepped closer to Rex, crossing the invisible line that separated the instructor from the student. The tension in the room spiked to a breaking point.</p>
<p>"Before we discuss contracts and mandates, I have one question, Rex Rexilion." Her eyes searched his, challenging every fiber of his being. "Strip away the systems. Strip away the enhancements."</p>
<p>"If we were to stand here, right now, in a fair fight—no abilities, no magic, no tricks, just two bodies, two sets of hands and feet in a raw, primal brawl... could you beat me?"</p>
<p>The question hung in the air like a guillotine blade. She wasn’t asking for a demonstration; she was offering a test of fundamental truth.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He didn’t even blink.</p>
<p>He stood amidst the bloodstains and the broken bodies of the elite unit, his chest rising and falling in a slow, controlled rhythm that betrayed none of the carnage he had just endured.</p>
<p>"You want to talk about validation?" Rex asked, his voice carrying a gravelly resonance that seemed to vibrate in the very bones of those listening. "You want to know if I can back up the claim of authority?"</p>
<p>A slow, dangerous smile spread across his face. "I will accept your position, Aurelia."</p>
<p>"I will take your mandate. But I won’t take it as some hired hand who simply follows orders..."</p>
<p>"I will take it because I believe in the necessity of what must be done."</p>
<p>He turned his head to survey the room, the resentful fighters, the stunned guards, and the silent family members. "If you want me to improve this household, then you must understand something fundamental about strength."</p>
<p>"Strength isn’t something you inherit through a family name."</p>
<p>"It isn’t something you preserve in a textbook or protect behind a system ability."</p>
<p>"Strength is a living thing. It is something that must be hunted, something that must be bled for, something that must be taken from the world through sheer, unrelenting will."</p>
<p>His eyes burned with a fervor that made several of the guards step back. "I will fight every single person in this room if I have to."</p>
<p>"I will break every bone, bruise every muscle, and shatter every assumption until only the truth remains: either you are strong enough to survive, or you are not."</p>
<p>"If you want me to lead them, I will not teach them how to follow... I will teach them how to dominate."</p>
<p>He turned his attention back to Aurelia, the intensity of his gaze locking onto hers like a physical weight. "Power isn’t a gift, Aurelia. It’s a conquest."</p>
<p>Aurelia watched him, her expression unreadable. She had spent her life surrounded by nobles who spoke of power in whispers and politics, men who wore their authority like expensive silk.</p>
<p>But this man, this man, spoke of power like a butcher speaks of meat, raw and undeniable. For the first time in years, something unfamiliar stirred beneath her composure.</p>
<p>Something primal. Something that recognized its equal.</p>
<p>A sound escaped her throat, a soft chuckle that quickly escalated into a rich, genuine laugh that echoed off the vaulted stone ceiling. It was a sound of pure, exhilarated recognition.</p>
<p>"You speak like a man who has already burned down the world to build something better," she said, her laughter dying down into a hungry grin.</p>
<p>She began to move, but not toward him. She circled, her movements fluid and deceptively casual, as she shed the mantle of the noble matriarch.</p>
<p>"Very well, Rex Rexilion. Let us test the foundation of your philosophy."</p>
<p>She dropped into a fighting stance. It wasn’t the polished, practiced stance of a duelist; it was something older, something more dangerous.</p>
<p>Her center of gravity was low, her hands open and relaxed, fingertips twitching like a cat preparing to spring. The air around her seemed to tighten.</p>
<p>Rex mirrored her, stepping into his own stance, compact, coiled, and utterly lethal. The muscles in his shoulders bunched, his eyes narrowing as he entered the hyperfocused state of the predator.</p>
<p>"One warning, Aurelia," Rex said, his voice dropping to a deadly murmur. "If I win, if I prove that my philosophy holds true... do not regret it when everything in this household starts changing."</p>
<p>"Do not regret it when I tear down the old ways to make room for the new. Because once I start, I won’t stop until the transformation is complete."</p>
<p>Aurelia’s eyes flashed with defiance, a challenge that burned brighter than any torch in the hall. "Regret? You mistake me for one of these children, Rex."</p>
<p>"I didn’t survive four generations of Nightwing politics by fearing change." She beckoned him forward with a flick of her wrist, her stance shifting to something even more aggressive. "Come then."</p>
<p>"Show me this ’conquest’ you speak of..."</p>
<p>"Show me if you are truly the storm you claim to be or if you’re just another gust of wind passing through my halls."</p>
