<p>Elliot let out a laugh, not a manic one, but something raw and almost desperate, filled with the intoxicating relief of a man who had finally found his purpose.</p>
<p>For months, he had carried the gnawing guilt of a coward who had chased personal mastery while his city bled; now, standing in the center of a screaming hellscape with Apollo at his back, that guilt was incinerated.</p>
<p>"This is it!" he roared, his voice cracking with adrenaline. "This is what we trained for!"</p>
<p>His blade erupted in a blinding corona of Heroic Strike energy as he plunged forward, the fourth defensive line buckling like rotted wood under the combined pressure of his fury and Apollo’s relentless advance.</p>
<p>While the center of the corridor became a churning vortex of golden light and demonic screams, Evelyn Astralight moved through the chaos like a ghost woven from starlight.</p>
<p>For two years, Evelyn had played a dangerous game of deception. She had moved through the world with deliberate restraint, revealing barely forty percent of her true capabilities, a habit born from the cold, calculating instinct of a reincarnator who knew that to reveal one’s full hand was to invite certain death.</p>
<p>But today, the pretense shattered. Today, she stopped pretending to be mortal.</p>
<p>She moved with a speed that defied physics, crossing thirty meters of packed, blood-slicked corridor in less than a heartbeat. To the soldiers around her, she was nothing more than a blur of silver and white.</p>
<p>To the demons in her path, she was an inevitable catastrophe. She didn’t swing wildly; she struck with the surgical precision of a god.</p>
<p>Her blade found the soft gaps between armor plates, severing tendons at the kneecaps, punching through nerve clusters in necks, and splitting skulls with terrifying accuracy.</p>
<p>Behind her, a chain reaction of collapses followed like the wake of a predator: twelve demons crumpled simultaneously, their legs severed or their spines shattered before their brains could even register an attack.</p>
<p>Twenty more followed in rapid succession, their bodies dropping in a grotesque domino effect as their commanders screamed futile orders that died in their throats.</p>
<p>’Faster, sharper, don’t breathe, don’t think,’ Evelyn commanded herself, her inner monologue a cold stream of tactical calculations amidst the sensory overload. ’The blood is slick. Watch the footing. Three demons on the left flank approaching strike, move, repeat.’</p>
<p>Yet, despite the lethal rhythm of her dance, something kept pulling at her attention.</p>
<p>Something wrong.</p>
<p>Something that made the hair on her arms stand up even as she carved through flesh.</p>
<p>Her eyes kept darting toward the dead center of the Underlayer formation.</p>
<p>The masked figure Xerollion had not moved.</p>
<p>In the midst of a massacre where every inch of ground was bought with screams and arterial spray, he remained an impossible island of stillness.</p>
<p>He stood at the heart of the demonic storm, unmoved by the vibrations of earth-shaking magic or the wet thud of corpses hitting the stone. He wasn’t commanding; he wasn’t shouting orders to rally his troops.</p>
<p>He was simply... present.</p>
<p>’Why isn’t he engaging?’ Evelyn wondered, the thought slicing through her focus. ’Is he arrogant? Or is he waiting?’</p>
<p>It wasn’t the stillness of a general watching his chess pieces move. It was something far more sinister than the stillness of an architect watching a clockwork mechanism unfold exactly as designed.</p>
<p>He wasn’t watching a battle; he was watching a process. He was watching an inevitability.</p>
<p>And beneath that tactical unease lay something more personal, something that gnawed at the back of her mind like a splinter she couldn’t reach.</p>
<p>There was a cadence to his presence, a specific, unhurried weight to the way he held himself that felt achingly familiar. Every time she caught a glimpse of that obsidian armor through the strobing magical lights, her heart gave a traitorous flutter of recognition.</p>
<p>That voice, the way it had cut through the paralysis, resonated with a memory she couldn’t quite grasp, a ghost of a feeling she had been trying to pin down since the moment he first spoke.</p>
<p>’Not now,’ she snarled internally, forcing the recognition down into the darkest corner of her mind. ’You can’t afford to hesitate.’</p>
<p>’If you lose focus now, you’re just another corpse in this tunnel.’</p>
<p>With a snarl of frustration, she pivoted, her blade singing as it severed the arm of an approaching demon, burying her focus back into the brutal necessity of survival.</p>
<p>There would be time to solve the mystery of the mask later, assuming there was any ’later’ left for her to see.</p>
<p>Valentina did not fight like a soldier; she fought like an architect rearranging the fundamental laws of physics. While the center of the corridor was a chaotic meat grinder of steel and sinew, she held the rear third with a terrifying, methodical dominance that reminded every living soul present why she had occupied the highest seat at the Grand Academy for decades.</p>
<p>Her magic was not explosive; it was structural. With precise, sweeping gestures of her hands, she wove interlocking barrier matrices across the span of the corridor—translucent geometric webs that didn’t just block incoming spellfire but consumed it.</p>
<p>When a demon sorcerer unleashed a torrent of sickly green necrotic energy, Valentina didn’t flinch. Her barrier caught the spell and absorbed its momentum with a resonant hummm, and then she redirected the overflow.</p>
<p>With a sharp flick of her wrist, she vented the redirected energy into the stone floor, causing the ground beneath a flanking demon squad to detonate in a spray of shrapnel and pulverized rock.</p>
<p>’Layer one: containment.’</p>
<p>’Layer two: redirection.’</p>
<p>’Layer three: annihilation,’ she cataloged silently, her mind a flawless machine.</p>
<p>Her breathing remained eerily steady, a slow, controlled rhythm that stood in stark contrast to the ragged gasps of the dying around her.</p>
<p>Her soldiers watched her back like she was a lighthouse in a hurricane; as long as Valentina remained calm, the slaughter felt like something that could be won.</p>
<p>Behind her, Morwenna Nightwing commanded the reserves with the lethal economy of a woman who had spent thirty years teaching others how to die properly. There was no wasted motion in her combat stance.</p>
<p>Every parry she executed was not merely defensive; it was a setup. When a demon blade whistled toward her throat, she didn’t just deflect it; she caught the momentum on her vambrace, twisted her hips, and drove her elbow into the creature’s windpipe with a wet crunch that echoed off the ceiling.</p>
<p>"Close the gap on the left!"</p>
<p>"Third rank, pivot three degrees... NOW!" Morwenna barked, her voice cutting through the cacophony without ever breaking into panic. "Don’t let them exploit the stagger!"</p>
<p>"Hold the line or lose your heads!"</p>
<p>Her Nightwing elites moved like extensions of her own body. They didn’t need complex orders; they read the shift in her shoulders and the tilt of her blade and adjusted their formation instinctively.</p>
<p>She moved through the blood-slicked passage like a reaper, pulling soldiers back from collapsing positions just seconds before they would have been overwhelmed, her blade a silver streak that left severed limbs and arterial sprays in its wake.</p>
<p>Yet, beneath the iron discipline of her combat stance, Morwenna’s mind was betraying her.</p>
<p>In the brief, microscopic pauses between strikes, the split second after a demon’s skull split under her boot, her thoughts fled from the gore to something she had buried deeper than any wound.</p>
<p>A memory.</p>
<p>A sun-drenched morning in her own household, the smell of lavender and expensive tea, and a secret she had never whispered to another living soul. The memory burned brighter than the magical fires consuming the tunnel.</p>
<p>’Focus, damn you,’ she hissed at herself, her inner voice dripping with self-loathing as she parried a spiked mace. ’The Nightwing name isn’t built on secrets.’</p>
<p>’It’s built on blood and steel.’</p>
<p>She knew the stakes. This wasn’t just about survival; it was about legacy.</p>
<p>Every killing blow she landed, every flawless maneuver she executed, was a testament carved in flesh. If she faltered here, if she showed even a crack in her legendary composure, the Nightwing household would be reduced to footnotes in history.</p>
<p>She wasn’t just fighting for Aethelgard; she was fighting to ensure that when the survivors spoke of this day, they spoke of the Nightwing contribution with awe, not pity.</p>
<p>The tension in the air began to shift. The clash of steel was being overtaken by something more primal: the grinding of magical energies.</p>
<p>The air grew heavy and ionized, tasting of copper and burnt ozone. The magical pressure was building to a breaking point, the very stones of the corridor beginning to vibrate as the true power of the two armies prepared to collide in a final, devastating magical crescendo.</p>
<p>Twenty meters above the carnage, perched on a jagged shelf of granite that jutted from the eastern wall like a broken fang, Talyra Skydancer became an extension of her bowstring.</p>
<p>She was no longer a woman; she was a machine of lethal geometry.</p>
<p>Thwip crack!</p>
<p>Her first arrow punched through the eye socket of a demon berserker just as it raised a spiked mace over a fallen Aethelgard sergeant.</p>
<p>Before the corpse had even hit the blood-slicked stone, Talyra had already drawn again. Her fingers moved with a hypnotic, terrifying economy of motion: nock, draw, anchor, release.</p>
<p>Each arrow found the impossible gaps: the seam beneath an armpit, the slit in a visor, and the soft flesh behind a knee. Each shot was punctuated by the wet thunk of steel meeting meat.</p>
<p>’Two targets down... Three more closing on the left flank...’</p>
<p>’Shift angle...’</p>
