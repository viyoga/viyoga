<p>Rex didn’t flinch when the stone comet shattered against Kaeleth’s barrier. In fact, he had counted every microsecond of the deflection.</p>
<p>His Divine Perception wasn’t just watching the attack; it was dissecting the barrier’s response. He felt the way the divine light rippled, the specific harmonic frequency at which Kaeleth’s defensive architecture stabilized itself.</p>
<p>He now knew exactly where the god’s physical invulnerability ended and his temporal authority began.</p>
<p>’He’s relying too much on the rules of time,’ Rex thought, a dark, hungry smile spreading across his blood-slicked lips. ’But he forgot about the rules of weight.’</p>
<p>"Let’s see how you deflect something that doesn’t care about time," Rex growled.</p>
<p>He unleashed Gravity Manipulation.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a magical spell or a system-based energy pulse; it was a fundamental manipulation of the physical medium. In his previous engagement in the Underlayer, Rex had confirmed that gravity was a blunt instrument that bypassed the elegant defenses of temporal manipulators.</p>
<p>Kaeleth could slow a spell, he could fracture a timeline, but he couldn’t stop the very fabric of space from wanting to collapse inward.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s eyes widened. For the first time, the god’s composure didn’t just slip; it evaporated.</p>
<p>He felt the sudden, impossible wrongness of his own domain. The air grew thick, not with magic, but with sheer, crushing mass.</p>
<p>The stone floor beneath him didn’t just crack; it imploded, the atoms themselves screaming as they were forced toward a single, crushing epicenter: Kaeleth’s own body.</p>
<p>"What... what is this?" Kaeleth gasped, his voice strained as the divine pressure began to pin his limbs to his sides. "This isn’t temporal magic! This is—"</p>
<p>"This is physics, you arrogant bastard," Rex interrupted.</p>
<p>The compression wasn’t enough to crush Kaeleth’s divine form; his body was too ancient, too structurally reinforced by millennia of power, but it was constraining.</p>
<p>It turned the god’s own sanctum into a vice, trapping him in a localized well of immense pressure that made every movement an agonizing struggle against tons of invisible force.</p>
<p>Rex seized the opening. He moved like liquid shadow through the crushing field, his Martial Arts Mastery activating with lethal efficiency.</p>
<p>He wasn’t just fighting a god; he was fighting a target that was being forced into a predictable, compressed position.</p>
<p>Rex became a whirlwind of bone-breaking violence.</p>
<p>Crack.</p>
<p>The first strike, a palm heel to the sternum, didn’t just hit; it transmitted a shockwave designed to bypass divine armor, rattling Kaeleth’s very essence.</p>
<p>Thud.</p>
<p>The second strike, a brutal elbow to the ribs, sent a cascade of golden ichor spraying from the god’s side as the divine structure buckled.</p>
<p>Snap.</p>
<p>The third strike, a spinning kick to the temple, was timed to the exact millisecond Kaeleth tried to reassert his temporal control, disrupting his focus.</p>
<p>Then came the fourth hit.</p>
<p>Rex channeled everything. He didn’t just use strength; he used the Gravity Manipulation itself, carrying the crushing force of the gravitational well through his fist.</p>
<p>He calibrated the frequency of the strike to match the resonant frequency of Kaeleth’s own domain structure, the very ’song’ that kept the god’s power coherent.</p>
<p>The punch landed with the sound of a world breaking.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a tactical grunt of pain. It wasn’t a calculated roar of defiance.</p>
<p>Kaeleth screamed.</p>
<p>It was a raw, primal, involuntary shriek of pure agony. It was the sound of something eternal being violated by something mortal.</p>
<p>The strike didn’t just hurt; it shattered his divine coherence. For a moment, the god’s very identity flickered, his form destabilizing as the gravitational frequency tore through his spiritual foundation.</p>
<p>The golden light that defined him turned sickly and mottled, bleeding out of him in ragged spurts.</p>
<p>The domain itself reacted to its master’s torment. The sky above the sanctum convulsed, the stars streaking and distorting as the fundamental laws of the space began to unravel in sympathy with Kaeleth’s pain.</p>
<p>The domain shook with the fury of a dying god.</p>
<p>The tremor wasn’t metaphorical. It was a physical violation of reality. When Rex’s fist connected with Kaeleth’s divine coherence, the shockwave didn’t just ripple through the air; it tore through the very medium of space itself.</p>
<p>The amber gold symbols etched into the sanctum’s walls shrieked as they vibrated at a frequency that threatened to liquify stone. Fragments of previous spells and shards of frozen time and petrified earth were caught in the blast, spinning outward like shrapnel in a detonation.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t get swept away. Gritting his teeth, he slammed his power into the Earthen Authority, anchoring his boots into the floor as if he were part of the mountain itself.</p>
<p>His muscles coiled, veins standing out like corded steel beneath his tan skin, as he braced against the feedback.</p>
<p>The shockwave roared past him, a wall of displaced existence that left his ears ringing and his vision swimming with afterimages.</p>
<p>Silence fell.</p>
<p>A heavy, suffocating silence that tasted of copper and burnt divinity.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s form flickered like a dying hologram before stabilizing. But he was changed.</p>
<p>The regal, overwhelming radiance that had defined his presence at the start of the fight had dimmed. The amber gold symbols swirling around the domain were moving slower now; their rhythmic pulse labored and became arrhythmic.</p>
<p>Through Divine Perception, Rex saw the truth in raw numbers: Kaeleth’s available expression was plummeting. He was bleeding energy, not blood, but the very essence of his authority.</p>
<p>The god stood there, swaying slightly, blood golden, luminous, and thick as syrup dripping from his split lips onto the cracked marble floor.</p>
<p>He looked at Rex, not with the rage of a warrior, but with the cold, terrifying intensity of a mathematician facing a broken equation.</p>
<p>He was calculating.</p>
<p>Rex watched him, his own breathing controlled despite the adrenaline hammering in his chest.</p>
<p>’Look at you,’ Rex thought bitterly. ’Two thousand years of absolute certainty, and now you’re reduced to guessing.’</p>
<p>"The Forbidden God designation," Kaeleth said finally.</p>
<p>His voice was hoarse, stripped of its melodic divinity, carrying only the weight of centuries. "Its amplification effect on your combat parameters... it’s not a static buff."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t move. "Keep talking."</p>
<p>"You entered this domain at the suppressed baseline of Presence Manipulationpresence manipulation," Kaeleth continued, his eyes tracking Rex’s every micro movement with desperate focus. "Then, after the opening exchange, you deployed the Lustful Villain form’s amplification."</p>
<p>"You’re not just stronger, Rex Rexilion... You’re evolving in real time."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex answered simply, the word a taunt.</p>
<p>"The amplification is proportional to the accumulated desire bonds," Kaeleth muttered, more to himself than to Rex. "Every soul you’ve touched, every conquest in Aethelgard... they are feeding the furnace."</p>
<p>"The current level of your power is exponentially higher than the designation’s initial grant."</p>
<p>"Yes."</p>
<p>"And the Adaptation," Kaeleth’s voice rose with a frantic edge. "The analysis windows are shrinking."</p>
<p>"The time it takes for you to parse my abilities is dropping every second..."</p>
<p>"I’m not fighting a man who learns; I’m fighting a system that consumes my own power to accelerate its own comprehension."</p>
<p>Rex’s gaze hardened. "You’re trying to solve me."</p>
<p>"I am trying to understand the mathematics of your existence," Kaeleth corrected, his hands trembling slightly as he began to weave a new pattern in the air. "Every equation has a solution... Every anomaly has a limit."</p>
<p>"You’re looking for an angle," Rex said, stepping forward, each footfall echoing like a death knell in the emptying sanctum. "A weakness in the math."</p>
<p>"There is always an angle," Kaeleth snapped.</p>
<p>His voice regained a flash of that ancient, unshakable confidence—the arrogance of a being who had never known an unsolvable problem. "There is always a variable I can control."</p>
<p>Rex stopped ten feet from him. The air between them hummed with lethal tension, the smell of blood and burnt ozone thick enough to choke on.</p>
<p>"There’s your mistake," Rex said, his voice dropping to a predator’s growl. "There is an angle for every problem but only for the person who has the capability to implement it."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes glowed with a cruel, knowing light. "The angle you’re hunting requires you to be faster than my Adaptation’s analysis speed."</p>
<p>"But the problem is, Kaeleth... the more you fight, the faster I get."</p>
<p>"Every time you strike, every time you try to adapt, you’re giving me the real-time data I need to close the loop."</p>
<p>"You’re not solving the equation."</p>
<p>Rex’s lips curled into a savage grin as he prepared to close the distance for the kill.</p>
<p>"You’re writing the answer for me."</p>
