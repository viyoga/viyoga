<p>Iris’s eyes were piercing, seeing past the physical imposingness to the machinery beneath. "You have not stopped since I have known you."</p>
<p>"Even yesterday, standing in front of a coffin, you were doing something with your hands the entire time."</p>
<p>"Small, constant movements. Like you were working through a problem, solving a puzzle, even while you were grieving with me."</p>
<p>Rex turned his head to study Iris, a flicker of genuine surprise crossing his features. Most people saw the surface, the calculated composure, and the unshakable focus, but few had the acuity to notice the restless machinery of his mind working beneath the skin.</p>
<p>"I was," he admitted, his voice dropping to a gravelly register. "And I won’t pretend otherwise."</p>
<p>"That doesn’t mean the grief wasn’t real... It just means I don’t know how to hold one truth without also holding three others."</p>
<p>"My mind doesn’t know how to switch off the calculus of survival just because my heart is breaking."</p>
<p>Iris met his gaze, her eyes reflecting a shared, weary understanding.</p>
<p>"I think I understand that better than most would," she said. "My entire life has been a performance of duality."</p>
<p>"I have spent years scanning every corner of a room, calculating exits, and measuring distances between exits and weapons, all while maintaining the flawless cadence of a social conversation."</p>
<p>"It is exhausting pretending that there isn’t a predator’s calculus running parallel to every polite smile."</p>
<p>"Then stop pretending with me," Rex said, his tone absolute. "I would rather you be exactly as tired and as calculating as you truly are than perform some hollow version of yourself for my benefit."</p>
<p>Iris searched his face for a long moment. Behind those pale eyes, the assassin’s mind ran its silent, lethal assessments, weighing his sincerity against his intent.</p>
<p>Then, something in her shoulders shifted. The rigid tension bled out of her posture, a subtle, unguarded loosening that spoke of a woman who had just been granted permission to simply exist without armor.</p>
<p>Rex turned his attention back to the violence unfolding on the training floor.</p>
<p>Aurelia was a force of nature. She didn’t move like someone who had studied combat; she moved like someone for whom violence was as natural as breathing.</p>
<p>Her competence was effortless, honed by decades of blood and bone. At that moment, she was working with one of the twelve, her movements precise as she corrected the student’s stance.</p>
<p>She didn’t just point at his arm; she grabbed it, her fingers finding the exact pressure point that forced his shoulder into the proper alignment. The student gasped as the correction landed with the brutal efficiency of a lesson learned through pain, his posture snapping into something far more lethal under her guidance.</p>
<p>Rex watched her, his mind cataloging every strike, every pivot, and every ounce of raw power she commanded. Then he looked back at Iris.</p>
<p>"Aurelia," Rex said, his voice cutting through the ambient noise of the hall. "The offer she made yesterday..."</p>
<p>"Is she in here?" Rex still asked something obvious because he’s trying to see if Iris would take the bait.</p>
<p>Iris glanced toward the center of the chaos where Aurelia was already moving to her next target, a young warrior who was already sweating through his gambeson. "She is... you can actually hear her from here."</p>
<p>"Well then... Will you take me to her?" Rex asked.</p>
<p>Iris offered him a dry, almost challenging look. "You could walk across that floor yourself, Rex."</p>
<p>"You don’t need a guide."</p>
<p>"I know I don’t need you to do it," Rex said, leaning slightly toward her. "I’m asking because I would rather have you beside me when I face her and because I think you’ve been sitting at this edge of the world long enough today."</p>
<p>Iris held his stare for a second longer than necessary. In that moment, the mask slipped; the crack in her composure widened, allowing a flash of something warm, something desperately human, to shine through before she forcibly pulled the shutters closed again.</p>
<p>She stood. Her movement had the measured, deliberate quality of someone gathering courage, the assassin’s practical acceptance of a necessary risk.</p>
<p>She walked to the edge of the training floor, the stone floor echoing her footsteps, and called out Aurelia’s name.</p>
<p>’It worked...’</p>
<p>The training hall seemed to hold its breath. Aurelia paused mid-instruction, her hand still hovering near a student’s ribs. She looked up, her sharp eyes cutting through the dust and sweat of the hall to find Rex standing at the entrance.</p>
<p>Her expression went through a rapid-fire sequence: the initial tactical read, the cold confirmation of who was standing there, and finally, a flat, genuine acknowledgment. But beneath the neutrality, Rex saw it: satisfaction.</p>
<p>She looked like a predator who had set a trap and watched the prey walk straight into it without hesitation. She looked like someone who had made a challenge expecting a delay, only to find a man who didn’t believe in wasting time.</p>
<p>"Break," Aurelia commanded her students, her voice carrying the authority of a general. "Five minutes... recover."</p>
<p>The elite unit dispersed, their breathing heavy, some wiping blood from split lips or grimacing as they nursed bruised ribs. Aurelia turned and strode across the training floor, her presence parting the students like water around a prow.</p>
<p>She stopped inches from Rex, the heat of her recent exertion rolling off her in waves. Her eyes were fierce, unblinking, and entirely focused.</p>
<p>He met her gaze, neither flinching nor faltering.</p>
<p>"You came early," Aurelia said.</p>
<p>Her voice wasn’t accusatory; it was simply stating a fact, delivered with the same flat neutrality she used when calling out a student’s mistake. But beneath the surface, there was a challenge, a test of his nerve.</p>
<p>"The offer was genuine," Rex replied, stepping further into the space where the air tasted of copper and sweat. "And I treat genuine offers with the appropriate urgency."</p>
<p>Aurelia’s eyes narrowed slightly, scanning him. "Most people who receive an offer from me treat it like a social formality."</p>
<p>"Something said in a moment of gratitude, a polite fiction they assume neither of us will follow up on."</p>
<p>"Yet here you are, standing in my training hall before the household has even finished breakfast."</p>
<p>"I do not make offers I do not intend to keep," Rex said, his voice steady as stone. "And I assumed the same courtesy applied in reverse."</p>
<p>"In my experience, when someone like you speaks with that kind of conviction, it isn’t a courtesy; it’s a contract."</p>
<p>Aurelia fell silent. She studied him with the predatory assessment of a seasoned commander who had just realized her opponent wasn’t playing by the expected rules.</p>
<p>She was weighing whether he was a man of his word or merely a man of dangerous assumptions.</p>
<p>"That is a very specific way of saying you expected me to mean it," she said, her tone sharpening.</p>
<p>"You bowed to me in the ruins of a broken street, in front of your own household’s gate," Rex countered.</p>
<p>The memory of that moment flashed between them: the dust, the blood, the humiliation of a matriarch lowering herself before a stranger. "People do not perform that kind of gesture lightly."</p>
<p>"Either it costs them something to say, or it doesn’t happen at all."</p>
<p>"Yours cost you something. I saw the calculation in your eyes even as you lowered your head."</p>
<p>"So no, I did not think you were speaking out of a moment’s gratitude that would evaporate with the morning dew."</p>
<p>A muscle jumped in Aurelia’s jaw. For a heartbeat, the training hall seemed to grow colder.</p>
<p>Her expression shifted not into anger, but into something far more dangerous: recognition. It was the specific settling of a warrior who has just realized she is being seen, truly seen, by someone who has not yet decided whether to trust her or kill her.</p>
<p>"You watch people closely," she observed, her voice dropping to a dangerous murmur.</p>
<p>"It is one of the few things I am reliably good at," Rex said. "I have found it useful in more situations than combat, if I am honest, which is not a confession I make to many people."</p>
<p>"Then why say it to me?" Aurelia challenged, stepping closer until she was well within his personal space, her presence overwhelming and physical. "Why strip away the pretense now?"</p>
<p>"Because you asked a direct question, and I would rather answer it than spend my energy managing you the way I manage most rooms," Rex said without blinking. "You do not strike me as someone who responds well to being managed, Aurelia."</p>
<p>"Iris told me something similar this morning, in her own way. And I believed her."</p>
<p>Aurelia’s gaze flickered momentarily toward Iris, who stood a few paces back. Iris’s face was a battlefield of conflicting emotions; she was trying desperately to maintain her assassin’s neutrality, but the faint tension in her jaw betrayed her.</p>
<p>A silent communication passed between the two women, a thread of shared understanding, warm and slightly amused, passing between them without a single word spoken.</p>
<p>The tension in the air didn’t dissipate; it simply transformed. The verbal sparring was over. The real negotiation was about to begin.</p>
<p>Aurelia turned her attention fully back to Rex. The commander had receded, replaced by the teacher of violence.</p>
<p>"The training floor is open," she said. It wasn’t an invitation; it was a declaration of terms.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t hesitate. He stepped past her, crossing the threshold onto the bloodstained stone of the training floor.</p>
<p>The hunt had begun.</p>
