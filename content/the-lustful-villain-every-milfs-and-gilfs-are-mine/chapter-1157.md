<p>Beside Valentina, Morwenna Nightwing, a woman whose reputation was built on a foundation of unshakable composure and icy defiance, had collapsed.</p>
<p>She had lowered herself onto one knee with the mechanical inevitability of a marionette whose strings had been yanked by an unseen hand. When her conscious mind finally caught up to her body’s treasonous movement, she didn’t fight it.</p>
<p>She didn’t reach for her sword or attempt to reclaim her dignity. She simply remained there, frozen, because her instincts had made a far more accurate assessment of the situation than her pride ever could.</p>
<p>Her body knew what her mind refused to acknowledge: they were not standing in the presence of a guest; they were standing in the presence of the Host.</p>
<p>’This isn’t power,’ Morwenna thought, her knuckles white where they pressed against the stone. ’This is... inevitability.’</p>
<p>Twenty meters away, hidden behind the unreadable porcelain surface of his mask, the real Rex was experiencing something far more terrifying than awe. He was experiencing the complete and total failure of his own god-tier perception.</p>
<p>Since the first blow had been struck in the duel, Rex’s Divine Perception had been running at full capacity, a continuous stream of data flooding his consciousness.</p>
<p>It had been handling Apollo’s designation with the practiced ease of a master surgeon parsing the intricate lattice of the Life affinity, mapping the flowing channels of golden energy, and cataloguing the exact source of Apollo’s authority.</p>
<p>To Rex, Apollo had been a problem to be solved, a finite equation.</p>
<p>Then the light descended.</p>
<p>The instrument of his mind did not report a larger number. It did not report a spike in power.</p>
<p>It reported something that shouldn’t exist: an absence of edges.</p>
<p>’What... what am I looking at?’ Rex’s inner voice was a scream muffled by layers of analytical detachment.</p>
<p>He tried to find the boundary of the presence, the perimeter where the divine ended and the mortal began, and he found nothing.</p>
<p>He tried to map its structure, to dissect it into components he could understand, but every attempt returned the same impossible sensation: it was like trying to measure the volume of a raging ocean by dipping a single fingertip into the water.</p>
<p>He remembered Kaeleth. Kaeleth had been enormous, yes.</p>
<p>Kaeleth had been a force of nature that commanded terror. But Kaeleth had been finite.</p>
<p>Kaeleth had possessed a domain, a throne, and an anchor structure, a metaphysical tether that could be found, grasped, and shattered. Rex had located those vulnerabilities within minutes of entering Kaeleth’s domain and had systematically torn them apart.</p>
<p>This thing? This had no shape.</p>
<p>This had no vulnerability.</p>
<p>This was not a being standing somewhere else and extending its attention into this corridor. This was not a projection or an avatar.</p>
<p>This was simply present. It was in the stone beneath his boots; it was in the air filling his lungs; it was woven into the very atoms of the blood coursing through his veins.</p>
<p>The column of light pouring from the ceiling was not the entity itself; it was merely the fraction of itself that had chosen to become visible, a pinprick of light that was so obviously a mere fragment that Rex’s mind physically refused to construct an estimate of what the whole might look like.</p>
<p>A cold, visceral realization settled in his gut, more brutal than any blade.</p>
<p>The Goddess of Life had not descended to join the fight. She had simply pressed one finger against the fabric of the world, and that single finger alone was more massive, more terrifying, and more absolute than everything Rex Rexilion had ever fought combined.</p>
<p>Behind the mask, his face remained a perfect, terrifying statue of calm. But beneath the porcelain, his pulse betrayed him.</p>
<p>His heart rate rose by exactly eleven beats per minute, the only outward sign of a man realizing he was standing in the shadow of something that could erase him without even noticing it was there.</p>
<p>Rex kept his posture exactly as it had been: rigid, unyielding, a statue carved from shadow and defiance. To every terrified soldier in the Aethelgard ranks and every snarling demon in the Underlayer, he appeared as a masked figure standing in perfect, unnatural stillness.</p>
<p>He looked like a man who had seen the face of God and found it insufficiently impressive. That impression that mask of absolute, unflinching control was worth maintaining at almost any cost.</p>
<p>If he flinched now, if he showed even a tremor of recognition for the power pouring through the ceiling, the psychological collapse of both armies would be instantaneous.</p>
<p>But beneath the porcelain stillness, Rex’s mind had descended into a state of hyperaccelerated calculation. His thoughts were no longer human; they were cold, mechanical, and running through millions of variables with the speed of a supercomputer under stress.</p>
<p>’This is a catastrophic anomaly,’ he thought, the words echoing in the sterile space of his mind. This is no longer a duel against an Apostle. ’This is a fight adjacent to the upper framework of existence itself.’</p>
<p>’Ten thousand witnesses.’</p>
<p>’No retreat possible.’</p>
<p>’My surface identity is forty meters deep in the enemy’s killing zone.’</p>
<p>’If I move, I expose the lie...’</p>
<p>’If I stay, I am cornered by a deity...’</p>
<p>Lustia’s warning crawled up his spine like frost, each word carrying an uncomfortable, razor-sharp clarity: ’Don’t let your name get loud in the wrong circles.’</p>
<p>’Fuck,’ Rex thought grimly. ’My name hasn’t just gotten loud.’</p>
<p>’I just set off every alarm bell in the universe.’</p>
<p>Twenty meters away, Apollo Brightsoul began to rise. His movement was not the clumsy struggle of a wounded man but something fluid and terrifyingly graceful.</p>
<p>He ascended into the column of light with his eyes closed, his face upturned as if drinking from a fountain of liquid stars. When his eyelids finally fluttered open, the sound that escaped his throat was not a gasp but a low hum of harmonic resonance.</p>
<p>His eyes had changed.</p>
<p>The familiar gold of his affinity had been consumed.</p>
<p>Now, they burned with a light that had transcended color to a white-hot, pulsing brilliance that seemed to contain its own internal weather patterns.</p>
<p>A secondary register of light, whiter and more ancient than anything mortal could conceive, pulsed beneath the gold like the heartbeat of a star.</p>
<p>The aura surrounding his body had split: there was still the familiar, comforting warmth of his own power, but it was now encased in a second, purer sheath. This new layer clung to his flesh like divine mercury, rippling and snapping with every micro movement of his muscles.</p>
<p>CRACK THOOM.</p>
<p>The sound of his power stabilizing was like a thunderclap contained within a vacuum.</p>
<p>Apollo rolled his shoulders once. The sound of his joints popping was audible even over the divine hum, each click and pop echoing with supernatural clarity.</p>
<p>He felt the power coursing through him, not as an external gift, but as an intrinsic part of his biology. His muscles felt denser, his reflexes sharper than the speed of thought, his very soul feeling as though it had been forged anew in a furnace of pure concept.</p>
<p>Everything worked. Everything worked better than it had ever worked.</p>
<p>The pain that had moments ago been a screaming agony in his ribs and skull had been replaced by a terrifying, exhilarating emptiness.</p>
<p>"You won’t touch me again," Apollo said.</p>
<p>His voice did not merely carry across the distance; it struck. It came out doubled, his own heroic tenor layered beneath a deeper, resonant divine timbre that made the stone walls of the corridor vibrate in sympathetic agony.</p>
<p>The words landed physically against the soldiers’ armor, a shockwave of vocal authority that made several men stagger.</p>
<p>"Not with what I’ve just been given."</p>
