<p>Cassandra looked at him, apparently not expecting agreement.</p>
<p>"The answer to it," Rex said, "is that the review moves fast."</p>
<p>"Not thorough at the expense of speed, but fast enough that the uncertainty window is narrow. Pavellia runs the monitoring side."</p>
<p>"I address the eleven contacts directly and immediately, starting tonight, which removes the active intelligence leak before the review officially begins."</p>
<p>"Starting tonight," Cassandra said.</p>
<p>"The eleven know the Key is gone," Rex said. "They know the surface operation that ended the canyon engagement produced intelligence."</p>
<p>"They don’t know how much, but... they’re currently in a state of waiting to see whether the network goes quiet or sends new instructions." He paused. "Tonight.."</p>
<p>"...I give them an answer."</p>
<p>The room was quiet.</p>
<p>"Full authorization," Mordecai said finally. "My personal authorization, in front of the whole kingdom, for the restructuring as you define it."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at Cassandra. Cassandra looked at Rex with the flat, controlled frustration of someone who had not been given sufficient time to develop a counter-argument and knew it.</p>
<p>"This is a liability," she said. "What he does in the Underlayer will be attributed to us!"</p>
<p>"What the Legion has already done in the Underlayer," Rex said, "is also attributed to you."</p>
<p>"The difference is that one of those is a story you control and one is a story that gets told about you."</p>
<p>Cassandra held his gaze. "I... ngh..."</p>
<p>"I disagree," she said.</p>
<p>Rex met her eyes. The telekinesis moved in a slow, controlled arc that brought her three inches off the ground and then set her down again, a gesture that was specifically calibrated to communicate: your disagreement is noted, and this is how much weight it carries in the current structure.</p>
<p>She did not fight it. She set her feet and looked at him with the specific expression that had been building in her for months, the one that sat between anger and something else entirely.</p>
<p>"You do that again," she said quietly, "and I will make this very difficult for you."</p>
<p>"You’re welcome to try," Rex said, with the same level tone he had used on Gelion. "But I’d rather you didn’t, because you’re more useful as someone who is genuinely trying to find the holes in the plan than as someone who is fighting the plan on principle."</p>
<p>Cassandra looked at him.</p>
<p>"You found one," Rex said. "The transition window is a real vulnerability."</p>
<p>"That’s a useful contribution." He held her gaze with the steady attention of someone who means what they are saying. "Keep doing that..."</p>
<p>"I can work with disagreement that comes from actually thinking about the problem."</p>
<p>The expression on Cassandra’s face changed rapidly, ultimately settling into an expression that was neither fully satisfied nor completely appeased, but rather somewhere in between the two.</p>
<p>She said nothing, which was its own kind of acceptance.</p>
<p>"Pavellia," Rex said.</p>
<p>She stepped forward. She had been waiting with the specific patience of someone who had assessed the room’s dynamics early and had concluded that the most useful thing she could do was wait for the moment when her specific skills were needed.</p>
<p>"The whole kingdom-wide address system," Rex said.</p>
<p>"You’re talking about the amplification sphere," she said. "It’s in the primary spire..."</p>
<p>"I can have it here in twenty minutes, and I can assist you further with my project while it works its magic."</p>
<p>"Oh...? About your projection working," Rex said. "You can cast it at range?"</p>
<p>"I can project a visible image to any point in the Underlayer," she said. "Any point with a line of sight to the sky, which in this kingdom is approximately eighty percent of the population."</p>
<p>"The other twenty percent?" Rex said.</p>
<p>"Acoustic range from secondary amplification points," she said. "Sound reaches them even if the image doesn’t."</p>
<p>"The address has full coverage, but the visual coverage is only partial."</p>
<p>"That’s sufficient," Rex said. "Twenty minutes starting now."</p>
<p>Pavellia inclined her head once and immediately turned to leave.</p>
<p>"Wait," Cassandra said sharply.</p>
<p>Pavellia stopped. The room’s attention shifted toward the two women instantly.</p>
<p>Cassandra stepped forward slightly, arms folded.</p>
<p>"This is precisely the kind of escalation I was warning about."</p>
<p>Pavellia regarded her calmly.</p>
<p>"The address system is not escalation," she said. "It’s stabilization."</p>
<p>"Public stabilization through fear," Cassandra replied.</p>
<p>"Through clarity," Pavellia corrected. "The Underlayer currently exists in an information vacuum."</p>
<p>"Rumors are already spreading faster than official instruction."</p>
<p>She glanced toward Rex briefly before continuing. "If he doesn’t define the narrative tonight, someone else will define it for him by tomorrow morning."</p>
<p>Cassandra’s jaw tightened slightly. "You’re assuming the population accepts him."</p>
<p>"I’m assuming the population accepts power," Pavellia said evenly. "Those are not the same thing."</p>
<p>And then silence came.</p>
<p>Pavellia continued with the same composed tone.</p>
<p>"The kingdom already knows the Legion lost operational control of the canyon situation."</p>
<p>"They know the Key disappeared."</p>
<p>"They know Gelion has been under containment."</p>
<p>"And they know Mordecai has not publicly addressed any of it."</p>
<p>Mordecai visibly sank a little deeper into his chair. Pavellia did not acknowledge it.</p>
<p>"At this stage," she said, "silence is no longer interpreted as caution."</p>
<p>"It’s interpreted as weakness."</p>
<p>Cassandra looked irritated because the logic itself was difficult to attack. "That still doesn’t justify handing the entire narrative structure over to him."</p>
<p>"It doesn’t need to justify it," Pavellia replied. "It only needs to work."</p>
<p>Cassandra exhaled sharply through her nose. "You are disturbingly comfortable with authoritarian solutions."</p>
<p>"And you’re disturbingly attached to procedural delay during active instability."</p>
<p>The room became quiet again. Cassandra stepped closer now.</p>
<p>"And what happens if this situation turns the entire kingdom into a cult of personality around him?"</p>
<p>Pavellia answered instantly. "Then, at minimum, the kingdom survives long enough for that to become a problem."</p>
<p>Cassandra opened her mouth again. Rex moved his fingers slightly.</p>
<p>The telekinesis hit instantly. Cassandra’s body slammed sideways into the stone floor hard enough for the impact to echo through the chamber.</p>
<p>The air left her lungs violently. "GAAGHH!"</p>
<p>Several people flinched.</p>
<p>Rex looked at her without emotion. "Shut the fuck up."</p>
<p>Cassandra slowly pushed herself upward on one elbow, breathing hard more from anger than injury. Rex continued speaking in the same calm tone.</p>
<p>"You already made your objection."</p>
<p>"I heard it."</p>
<p>"You contributed useful concerns."</p>
<p>"Now stop repeating yourself like the meeting is going to magically become democratic if you complain long enough."</p>
<p>The room stayed perfectly still. Rex’s eyes remained on Cassandra.</p>
<p>"You wanted someone capable of holding the Underlayer together after the Legion fucked itself into instability."</p>
<p>"You found one."</p>
<p>"So either help solve the problem..."</p>
<p>"...or get out of the way while the real deal works."</p>
<p>"You know what your problem is?" Rex said calmly while looking at Mordecai.</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at him carefully. "Several, probably."</p>
<p>"You keep trying to be liked."</p>
<p>The answer landed instantly. Mordecai’s silence confirmed it.</p>
<p>Rex continued walking slowly toward the chair. "You built systems meant to preserve stability while avoiding fear."</p>
<p>"You negotiated when you should’ve crushed resistance."</p>
<p>"You explained yourself when you should’ve given orders."</p>
<p>"You treated leadership like management."</p>
<p>His gaze swept across the room.</p>
<p>"And because of that, the entire Underlayer spent fourteen months forgetting what power is supposed to feel like."</p>
<p>The darkness around him seemed heavier now. It was not magical, but it was psychological.</p>
<p>The kind of pressure created when everyone in a room instinctively understood who possessed the ability to decide whether they lived or died.</p>
<p>Rex reached the chair. Then turned around slowly.</p>
<p>"You don’t maintain a kingdom like this through consensus."</p>
<p>"You maintain it through certainty."</p>
<p>His voice remained level. Which somehow worsened it.</p>
<p>"People obey strength long before they obey reason."</p>
<p>"And demons?" Rex gave a slight smile. "Demons obey fear before either."</p>
<p>Nobody interrupted him because nobody wanted to.</p>
<p>"The Legion made a mistake," Rex said. "You all thought secrecy was enough."</p>
<p>"You thought intelligence networks and hidden leverage and controlled information would stabilize the Underlayer."</p>
<p>"It didn’t."</p>
<p>His eyes hardened. "It made everyone comfortable."</p>
<p>The pressure in the room intensified subtly. "And comfort produces weakness."</p>
<p>Rex rested one hand against the arm of the chair. "I am not going to repeat that mistake."</p>
<p>Mordecai looked at him carefully now. Not defensively nor even resentfully anymore, but just watching closely.</p>
<p>Rex’s gaze shifted across every person present one by one.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer doesn’t need a bureaucrat sitting beside the throne pretending exhaustion is sacrifice."</p>
<p>Mordecai winced slightly.</p>
<p>"It needs a ruler."</p>
<p>"A real Demon Lord who doesn’t show any weakness," Rex continued. "Someone decisive enough that betrayal becomes terrifying again."</p>
<p>"Someone cruel enough that failure has consequences."</p>
<p>"Someone stable enough that two hundred thousand people can sleep at night knowing there is no uncertainty about who controls this kingdom."</p>
<p>His eyes flicked briefly toward Cassandra. "Not everyone is necessary."</p>
<p>Then toward the restrained Gelion. "Not everyone deserves mercy."</p>
<p>Then finally toward Mordecai. "And if maintaining order requires blood..."</p>
<p>Rex sat down on the chair. "...then the blood gets spilled."</p>
<p>The impact of the moment was immediate. Because unlike Mordecai—</p>
<p>Rex actually looked correct sitting there. The chair stopped resembling decorative architecture.</p>
<p>It became power. The aura rolling off him now was no longer merely intimidating.</p>
<p>It was oppressive and even ancient. The instinctive pressure of something that did not ask permission to rule.</p>
<p>Lilith visibly shivered in excitement. Pavellia lowered her head slightly without conscious thought.</p>
<p>Even Cassandra’s expression tightened despite herself. Rex rested his chin lightly against one hand and looked down at the room like judgment itself had decided to sit down.</p>
<p>"From this point onward," he said quietly, "the Underlayer belongs to me."</p>
<p>The silence afterward felt absolute.</p>
<p>Then Rex smiled slightly.</p>
<p>"And anyone unnecessary to its future..."</p>
<p>The darkness around the throne deepened.</p>
<p>"...dies."</p>
