<p>Kaeleth fell silent, the weight of Celestina’s suggestion hanging between them like a guillotine blade suspended by a fraying rope. The air in the throne room grew frigid, charged with the static of divine implications.</p>
<p>"You have been without an apostle for eighteen years," Celestina said, her voice cutting through the stillness with clinical detachment. "Your last three all died in circumstances that the temporal domain’s operational requirements produced sacrifices to the very mechanics of your power."</p>
<p>"Since then, you have been using our contract arrangement as a convenient substitute for the Apostle relationship. But a contract is a transaction; an apostle is a weapon."</p>
<p>"Apostle selection requires more than mere utility, Celestina... It requires the mortal to be suitable to be a vessel capable of containing the strain without shattering," Kaeleth warned.</p>
<p>"I know exactly who is suitable," she countered.</p>
<p>Kaeleth turned his gaze toward her, his eyes burning with a cold, ancient fire. "Given what I have just revealed to you, I need to be certain you grasp the magnitude of what you are proposing."</p>
<p>"You are suggesting we deploy a mid-tier reincarnator... a boy carrying a divine designation he cannot comprehend against something whose very essence defies my perception."</p>
<p>"If the gap in Rex’s thread means what I fear it might mean, the boy will not merely fail."</p>
<p>"He will not even register as a threat worth Rex’s full attention before he is torn apart."</p>
<p>"I understand the risk," Celestina said, and in that moment, her voice sounded less like a ruler and more like an executioner. "I am proposing it anyway. Because the alternative is stagnation."</p>
<p>"Every week we hesitate, Rex consolidates his position on that island, building a fortress of influence that becomes harder to breach with every passing sunset."</p>
<p>"You told me yourself your options are paralyzed... Mine are not unlimited, but they are not this restricted."</p>
<p>"Theo Morr Nightwing is a resource I am willing to spend on incomplete information because incomplete information is the only currency we have left."</p>
<p>"Waiting for certainty means waiting for Rex Rexilion to decide the terms of our next conversation... I prefer we set them."</p>
<p>Kaeleth stared at her, a flicker of something resembling grim respect crossing his features. "That is a colder calculation than most mortals make about a person they are sending to their death."</p>
<p>"I built an organization on cold calculations," Celestina snapped, the steel in her voice hardening. "It is the only reason we have survived twelve years against enemies who would see us burned to ash."</p>
<p>"I am not going to start softening the arithmetic now simply because the target has become more frightening than anticipated."</p>
<p>"The Nightwing boy," Kaeleth said, testing the name.</p>
<p>"Theo Morr Nightwing," Celestina corrected. "Reincarnator... Mid-tier system non-combat class."</p>
<p>"That makes him perfect: he is exactly the kind of reincarnator who can be elevated significantly through an Apostle blessing without that elevation creating an independent power base that could threaten you later."</p>
<p>"His history with Rex is documented: public assault, a forced apology under household pressure, and simmering, unspoken resentment."</p>
<p>"He hasn’t reconciled with Rex Rexilion... He is merely performing the ritual of reconciliation."</p>
<p>"Reincarnators are the variable that breaks the equation," Kaeleth muttered, his mind already spiraling through the tactical implications. "Designating one as my Apostle is--"</p>
<p>"A pragmatic decision," Celestina interrupted. "You are not endorsing reincarnators as a category."</p>
<p>"You are using one specific tool to excise the most significant threat you have encountered in two centuries."</p>
<p>"And the tool will need to be disposed of afterward," Kaeleth said flatly.</p>
<p>"I know," Celestina replied without hesitation. "That is not a problem."</p>
<p>Kaeleth closed his eyes, mentally cataloguing the lethal parameters of the blessing he would have to bestow. "The Apostle designation for the temporal domain provides specific, devastating abilities."</p>
<p>"Enhanced time perception. Limited temporal acceleration and deceleration at a local scale. And most importantly, the Temporal Strike."</p>
<p>"A capability that operates at the soul level rather than the biological level."</p>
<p>Celestina’s eyes gleamed with predatory interest. "Which Rex Rexilion’s Infinite Regeneration cannot address."</p>
<p>"Correct," Kaeleth said. "The Temporal Strike bypasses biological healing because it does not damage the flesh."</p>
<p>"It does not break bones or sever arteries; it severs the temporal anchor that binds the soul to the physical timeline."</p>
<p>"Kill the anchor," Celestina whispered, the concept tasting like copper on her tongue, "and the soul loses its connection to the body."</p>
<p>"Death that cannot be healed," Kaeleth confirmed, his voice dropping to a funereal register. "The body may remain intact, the wounds may be nonexistent, but the soul becomes a ghost in its own machine, no longer connected to the timeline in which the body exists."</p>
<p>"The body dies because the inhabitant has been erased from its present."</p>
<p>Celestina leaned forward, the tension in the room reaching a breaking point. "Will that work on him?"</p>
<p>"Given what you said about the gap in his thread?"</p>
<p>"If part of his history exists outside the categories your function was built to read, is there a chance his soul’s temporal anchor is equally alien?"</p>
<p>"What if the strike finds nothing to sever because his connection to time is not what we understand it to be?"</p>
<p>Kaeleth fell into a silence that was not merely quiet; it was a void. Even through the limited, flickering transmission of the crystal, the pause felt agonizingly elongated, as if the god himself were momentarily suspended between two heartbeats.</p>
<p>The divine frequency wavered, crackling with the static of a predator recognizing its own vulnerability.</p>
<p>"I do not know," Kaeleth finally said, the words landing like stones dropped into a deep well. "I want to answer that with absolute certainty because uncertainty is a poison to my nature."</p>
<p>"I am the keeper of threads; I do not operate in the fog..."</p>
<p>"Yet, in this single conversation, I have handed you more uncertainty than I have provided in the twelve years since our pact began."</p>
<p>He paused, his voice gaining a grim, analytical edge. "What I can tell you is this: every soul I have ever measured, mortal, divine, or otherwise, possesses a temporal anchor of some description."</p>
<p>"To exist within a timeline is to be moored to it; without an anchor, one simply drifts into the nonexistence of the void."</p>
<p>"I have no evidence that Rex’s anchor is anything other than standard. And I must also confess..." His voice dropped to a whisper that carried more menace than a scream. "Until six hours ago, I had no evidence that a working of this magnitude could be destabilized by a single severance."</p>
<p>"I believed my architecture was absolute... I was wrong."</p>
<p>"So we are not just planning an assassination," Celestina said, her eyes reflecting the amber glow like twin coals. "We are conducting an experiment."</p>
<p>"We are testing the limits of reality itself," Kaeleth agreed, the admission tasting like ash. "I cannot offer you a plan that does not involve this gamble."</p>
<p>"The alternative is inaction, and we have already established the catastrophic price of standing still while he builds his empire."</p>
<p>Celestina straightened, her fingers curling around the armrest of her throne. The motion was precise, almost mechanical.</p>
<p>"Then we deploy the variable... Give it to Theo."</p>
<p>"The boy is unprepared for the spiritual backlash alone," Kaeleth warned.</p>
<p>"He doesn’t need to be prepared; he needs to be close," Celestina countered. "Let him approach Rex through the facade of reconciliation he is already maintaining."</p>
<p>"The Nightwing household’s fragile truce with Rex Rexilion provides exactly what an apostle requires: proximity without suspicion."</p>
<p>"Theo has access to Rex’s inner circle access that Rex would never grant to anyone he recognized as a legitimate threat."</p>
<p>"He will be the knife in the dark that Rex never sees coming because he is too busy looking at the horizon."</p>
<p>"And when the strike lands?" Kaeleth asked. "When Rex Rexilion dies?"</p>
<p>"Then the Aethelgard operation continues through conventional means," Celestina said, her voice devoid of any sentimentality. "Without his system, without the protection of whatever unknown patron lurks behind that gap in his history, and without the specific capabilities that allowed him to fracture our loop architecture, the city’s reincarnator population becomes manageable again."</p>
<p>"He becomes another problem for the Legion to solve with standard brutality."</p>
<p>"And the boy?" Kaeleth pressed, his gaze searching hers for any flicker of human mercy. "The tool that delivers the blow?"</p>
<p>Celestina turned her face toward the window, looking out over her domain with the cold, unblinking stare of a statue.</p>
<p>"He is a reincarnator," she said simply. "And our organization was founded on the principle that reincarnators are anomalies to be controlled, not allies to be cherished."</p>
<p>"Once he has served his purpose and the anchor is severed and the threat is excised, he will be addressed like any other reincarnator..."</p>
<p>"He will be processed and he..."</p>
<p>"...will be contained."</p>
