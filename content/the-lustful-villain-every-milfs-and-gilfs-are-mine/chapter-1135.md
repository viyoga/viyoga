<p>"Forward! March!" Apollo’s command tore through the chamber like a battle cry, and the disciplined machine of Aethelgard began to grind into motion.</p>
<p>The formations shifted with terrifying synchronization. Apollo himself plunged through the shimmering veil of the gateway first, his cape swirling behind him as he led the primary assault force into the unknown.</p>
<p>Valentina followed with the cold precision of a predator, her division positioning themselves to secure the retreat corridor, a necessary precaution that she treated as a tactical inevitability.</p>
<p>At the rear, Morwenna’s elite Nightwing soldiers fell into formation like shadows coalescing, their dark armor swallowing what little light remained.</p>
<p>The Avatar moved through the central ranks like an avenging god, its face a mask of serene, inhuman resolve, while Elizabeth stumbled slightly as she joined the procession, her breathing ragged, the golden light in her hands fading to a dull, exhausted ember now that the impossible task of tearing open reality was complete.</p>
<p>The transition through the gateway was not a simple walk; it was a violation of the senses. For each soldier, those few seconds felt like an eternity spent drowning in liquid gold.</p>
<p>The air became impossibly dense, pressing against their lungs until they gasped for breath, their vision consumed by a kaleidoscopic distortion that made their own hands look alien. Then, with the suddenness of a blade’s strike, the light vanished.</p>
<p>They plunged into the thick, cool darkness of the Underlayer.</p>
<p>The disorientation was immediate. One moment they were bathed in divine radiance; the next, they were stumbling through a claustrophobic throat of stone.</p>
<p>The only light came from the faint, sickly pulse of natural crystals embedded in the walls, casting long, dancing shadows that seemed to writhe with malevolent intent.</p>
<p>The sound was overwhelming the thunderous, echoing rhythm of thousands of armored boots striking stone, a metallic heartbeat that reverberated off the narrow walls.</p>
<p>Apollo emerged at the head of the column, his sword drawn and glowing with a faint holy light. He scanned the northern corridor, his eyes narrowing as he took in its grim architecture.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, a flicker of tactical satisfaction crossed his face. The corridor was perfect, a flawless funnel that would channel their overwhelming numbers directly into the enemy’s heart. It was exactly what an invading force needed.</p>
<p>But as the last of his vanguard passed through the threshold, that satisfaction curdled into something cold and poisonous.</p>
<p>"Something is wrong," Apollo whispered to himself, though his words were drowned out by the clatter of marching steel. "Too quiet..."</p>
<p>"This shouldn’t be this... quiet."</p>
<p>The silence of the Underlayer wasn’t the absence of sound; it was an active, hungry thing. It pressed against their ears like water. There was no echo of enemy patrols, no distant sounds of mining, and no skittering of vermin, just the rhythmic, deafening march of Aethelgard’s boots.</p>
<p>It felt like walking into the gullet of a beast that had already swallowed and was now simply waiting for the next course to pass through its teeth.</p>
<p>’It’s a trap,’ a voice screamed in the back of Apollo’s mind, but he suppressed it. ’No, we have the numbers... We have Rex.’</p>
<p>’We have the Apostles.’</p>
<p>’We will crush whatever waits in the dark.’</p>
<p>The unease spread through the ranks like a plague. Soldiers glanced at one another, their eyes darting toward the shadows where the crystal light failed to reach.</p>
<p>The air felt wrong too—still, too heavy, as if the oxygen itself had been replaced by something thicker, something that tasted faintly of old blood and cold metal.</p>
<p>Apollo felt the hair on his neck bristle. His instincts, honed through decades of holy warfare, were screaming at him.</p>
<p>The silence wasn’t peaceful; it was the silence of a held breath. The silence of a predator mid-leap.</p>
<p>"HALT!" Apollo roared, thrusting his fist upward. "Form defensive squares!"</p>
<p>"Tighten the files! Eyes on the flanks!"</p>
<p>The column began to slow, the thunderous marching breaking into the confused scraping of armor and boots trying to find purchase on the stone.</p>
<p>But the warning was a heartbeat too late. The trap didn’t just spring; it exploded.</p>
<p>The silence didn’t break; it shattered.</p>
<p>From the impenetrable darkness at the far end of the corridor, something began to manifest. At first, it was merely the suggestion of movement, pale reflections off steel, and the faint scrape of leather on stone.</p>
<p>Then, the shapes coalesced. A handful of figures emerged, then dozens, then hundreds, then thousands.</p>
<p>They didn’t stumble from the shadows; they materialized from them in perfect, terrifying synchronization.</p>
<p>Underlayer soldiers stepped forward as one, a wall of disciplined steel and obsidian armor that seemed to absorb the dim crystal light rather than reflect it.</p>
<p>Their weapons—long spears, serrated cleavers, and heavy maces—were held at precise angles, creating a forest of deadly metal. There was no chaos here.</p>
<p>No panicked shouts. No disorganized rabble.</p>
<p>This was a machine of war, polished and oiled, waiting for the signal to grind its teeth.</p>
<p>A visible ripple of terror tore through Aethelgard’s front ranks. The confidence that had carried them through the gateway—the righteous certainty of divine conquest—evaporated like mist in a furnace.</p>
<p>These weren’t the scattered insurgents they had been promised; these were professionals who had been waiting for them.</p>
<p>"Gods above..." one young Apostle whispered, his voice cracking as he gripped his sword with white knuckles. "They knew... They were waiting for us."</p>
<p>Apollo didn’t answer. His jaw was set so tight it felt as though his teeth might crack.</p>
<p>His entire body was coiled like a spring, every nerve ending screaming warnings of imminent annihilation. His gaze swept across the opposing wall of death, searching for a weakness, a gap, a single flaw in their flawless formation.</p>
<p>But there was none. The Underlayer formation was a masterpiece of tactical positioning, a defensive structure so complete and so deliberate that it felt less like an army and more like a trap that had been set months ago.</p>
<p>Then, Apollo saw him.</p>
<p>At the dead center of the opposing mass, the crowd of soldiers seemed to part instinctively, creating a focal point of absolute stillness.</p>
<p>Flanked by the terrifyingly poised figures of Cassandra and Gorvasha, a single masked figure stood motionless. Even across the stretch of the narrow stone passage, the aura radiating from the masked commander was suffocating.</p>
<p>It wasn’t just authority; it was a cold, predatory calm that seemed to drain the heat from the air.</p>
<p>’Someone is...’ Apollo’s inner voice hissed, a cold dread settling in his gut. ’Someone orchestrated this...’</p>
<p>’Every step we took, every breath we drew since the gateway opened... it was all... his design.’</p>
<p>The two armies faced each other like two tectonic plates grinding against one another, the pressure between them building to an impossible degree. The air itself seemed to hold its breath, suspended in that horrific, agonizing moment before a storm breaks.</p>
<p>Neither side moved. Neither side blinked. Thousands of eyes locked onto thousands of others, righteous fury meeting cold, calculated annihilation.</p>
<p>The silence stretched, taut as a bowstring pulled to the point of snapping. Then, from somewhere deep within the Underlayer ranks, came the first sound: the metallic click of a shield locking into place.</p>
<p>Then another. Then the low, guttural scrape of swords being drawn from scabbards.</p>
<p>The sound rippled outward through the formations like a wave of impending doom.</p>
<p>It was the prelude. The final heartbeat before the slaughter began.</p>
<p>Both sides understood now that this was not a battle for territory or glory; it was a collision of fates that would leave nothing but blood and broken bones in its wake.</p>
