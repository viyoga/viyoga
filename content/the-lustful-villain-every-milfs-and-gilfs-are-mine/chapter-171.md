<p>The ceiling above him was just plain stone, like the kind used in institutional buildings. The light coming through the narrow window was still blue-dark, like it was before dawn.</p>
<p>This meant that Lustia’s domain worked outside of time in the practical sense of the word; even though the conversation had felt long, the world had probably only moved an hour in his absence.</p>

This looseness changed her appearance in a specific way: she seemed less composed, less deliberate, and more like a person in bed who had, after a very memorable evening, found herself in her current position.</p>

<p>She turned over and looked at him, and what crossed her face in the first second before she organized her expression was a sequence he filed carefully: recognition, warmth, and then the very specific quality of a person who had done something significant and was now, in the morning’s first clear light, looking at the person they’d done it with and finding that their feelings about it had not changed overnight into anything regrettable.</p>
<p>"Rex..." she said his name, not as a greeting but as a statement, like people do when they talk about things that have changed in their minds.</p>
<p>"Good morning, sleepyhead," Rex said. "Looks like last night made you sleep pretty well, huh?"</p>
<p>Diana put one hand over her face, which he had never seen her do before and which he found very unsettling. "What time is it?"</p>
<p>"Still early, I guess," he said. "It’s still dark outside."</p>
<p>She moved her hand and looked out the window to confirm this. Then she looked back at him with an expression that was a mix of Diana’s calm demeanor and her usual reckless behavior.</p>
<p>"Oh shit!" she said. "We have to go before anyone gets here!"</p>
<p>Rex said, "I know."</p>
<p>"Before the sun rises," she said.</p>
<p>Rex said again, "I know."</p>
<p>She was looking at him like someone who had something to say and was trying to decide if now was the right time to say it.</p>
<p>He waited because Diana was not the kind of person you rushed to conclusions about.</p>
<p>"About last night..." Diana held the blanket that covered her vulnerable body. "Well... I... uhh..."</p>
<p>"Hm?"</p>
<p>"I don’t regret it at all," she said, in the straightforward way she always spoke when she had made up her mind about something. "T-That’s it... I just want to make that clear for you."</p>
<p>"Good to hear it then," Rex said.</p>
<p>"But I don’t know exactly what it is yet," she said, which was the most honest thing she had said since he had known her, and he recognized it as the kind of honesty that cost something to produce.</p>
<p>"And I’d like to figure that out before it becomes something everyone else is also figuring out."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her. "I can work with that," he said.</p>
<p>She held his gaze for a moment and found something in his eyes that seemed satisfactory, as her expression transformed into a version of composure that was genuine rather than performative.</p>
<p>"Okay," she said.</p>
<p>She sat up, and the competent, deliberate version of Diana Von Starlight began the process of re-establishing itself in the particular sequence of someone reordering the world around them. "Let’s begin our day as we usually do."</p>

<p>And then she looked at him with the kind of look on her face that was still partly the unguarded one, the one that showed warmth.</p>
<p>"You still cheated," she said.</p>

<p>"At archery," she said, to make it clear. "You were holding back, and then you weren’t, and that’s not fair."</p>
