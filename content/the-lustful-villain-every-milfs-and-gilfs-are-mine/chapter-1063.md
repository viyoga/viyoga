<p>Rex didn’t reach for a weapon. He didn’t summon a blade of divine energy or prepare a spell that would crack the foundations of the domain.</p>
<p>Instead, his hand moved with a casual, almost insulting familiarity toward his left jacket pocket.</p>
<p>He pulled something out.</p>
<p>The throw was not the graceful arc of a telekinetic spell or the calculated trajectory of a seasoned warrior. It was something far more unsettling.</p>
<p>It was a flat, human throw, the dismissive toss of a man throwing a piece of trash across a room. The throw lacked any pretense of respect for its target.</p>
<p>The object sailed through the amber gold haze, cutting through the swirling temporal symbols like a stone through water. It landed with a wet, sickening thud at the very center of Kaeleth’s manifestation.</p>
<p>Kaeleth, the God of Time, whose consciousness spanned millennia, froze. The entire domain seemed to stutter.</p>
<p>The cascading glyphs slowed their rotation, the golden light dimming as the God’s attention locked onto the object that had violated his sanctum.</p>
<p>For one suspended moment, there was only the sound of the universe holding its breath.</p>
<p>Kaeleth looked down at what had arrived. Then, he looked up at Rex.</p>
<p>It was Theo’s head.</p>
<p>But it was not a head in any mortal sense. It was worse.</p>
<p>Through the brutal application of Soul Manipulation’s object creation function, Rex had not merely killed a boy; he had externalized the wound itself.</p>
<p>He had taken the broken part of the Temporal Designation, the raw, spiritual essence that had once linked the boy to the God’s power, and made it into a horrifying, undeniable form.</p>
<p>In the soul register’s spatial representation, the component bore the exact, twisted shape of the soul signature at the moment of investment.</p>
<p>Rex had forced that abstract concept into reality as a physical trophy: a grotesque, gore-slicked skull with eyes that still seemed to hold the fading echo of divine recognition. It was a message written in bone and stolen divinity. It was the physical manifestation of a severed connection.</p>
<p>Rex stood there, his silhouette sharp against the golden chaos. His expression was not one of triumph nor of rage.</p>
<p>It was worse. It was the flat, hollow indifference of a man who had simply completed a tedious chore.</p>
<p>He looked at the God of Time with the same detached attention one might give to a malfunctioning clock.</p>
<p>"You had," Rex said, his voice cutting through the divine silence like a scalpel through silk, "an Apostle for approximately twelve hours."</p>
<p>The words hung in the air, heavier than any spell. They were a mockery of Kaeleth’s eternal nature, a reminder that even divine appointments could be slaughtered and discarded like carrion.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes remained fixed on Kaeleth’s shifting, panicked form. There was no hesitation in his stance, no tremor in his voice.</p>
<p>He had arrived exactly where he intended to be, and he was not about to perform any grandiosity for the benefit of the audience.</p>
<p>"That is," Rex added, his voice dropping to a cold, venomous whisper that seemed to vibrate through the very symbols of the domain, "a very short tenure."</p>
<p>The moment the words left Rex’s lips, the amber gold reality of the domain didn’t just react; it screamed.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s form abandoned all pretense of ambient presence. The God of Time fully engaged.</p>
<p>The temporal substrate of the domain, the very foundation of existence in this space, shifted from a flowing river into a crushing tidal wave.</p>
<p>This was not a targeted working, not a spell aimed at a target; this was the domain-level expression of absolute temporal authority. It was the weight of two thousand years of chronological dominion asserting itself as a physical force.</p>
<p>Rex felt the impact.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a blow to his flesh; it was a blow to his continuity. The initial impact of a powerful divine action hit Rex’s body on all levels at once—physical, spiritual, and time-related.</p>
<p>For a microsecond, the God’s will forcibly collapsed and recollapsed Rex’s body, transforming it from a solid object into a series of probability waves.</p>
<p>His bones groaned under the pressure of centuries passing in an instant; his blood felt as though it were attempting to flow backward while simultaneously freezing in his veins.</p>
<p>’This is the pressure of eternity,’ Rex thought through the static of the temporal assault. ’The universe is trying to remind me that I am an intruder.’</p>
<p>Inside his mind, his full potential adaptation roared to life. It didn’t fight the pressure; it consumed it.</p>
<p>It treated the divine onslaught as raw data, a foreign language that needed to be learned, parsed, and internalized.</p>

<p>He was a god standing at the edge of an abyss, throwing everything he owned into the dark to see if it would stop the fall.</p>
