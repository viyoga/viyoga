<p>The kitchen at Silver Rest Inn smelled exactly as it always had in the fragile, gray hours before dawn: the comforting aroma of warm yeast from freshly baked bread, the sharp herbal tang of steeped tea, and that particular, comforting mustiness of ancient wooden beams that had inhaled decades of cooking smoke and hearth fire.</p>
<p>For a moment, Rex lingered in the doorway, the threshold separating the mundane peace of the inn from the apocalyptic storm he was orchestrating.</p>
<p>He let the familiarity of the space settle over his shoulders like a familiar cloak, anchoring him to the world he was about to set on fire.</p>
<p>Three days. That was all that remained before the offensive against the Underlayer would tear the sky asunder.</p>
<p>Three days that felt like an eternity of suspended animation, yet simultaneously rushed past like sand through a broken hourglass.</p>
<p>Somewhere between the cold, calculated precision of finishing the Key with Elizabeth and the bloodthirsty finality of sealing the trap with the Underlayer leadership, Rex had made a decision.</p>
<p>This particular morning this fleeting moment of stolen normalcy belonged to Mara and Marceline. Before the screaming started, before the bones began to snap, before his hands were stained beyond recognition, he needed this.</p>
<p>When he finally stepped inside, the floorboards groaning softly under his weight, he found them already seated at the long oak table.</p>
<p>They sat side by side, a formation they had adopted instinctively as their pregnancies became impossible to hide, two islands of fragile life in a sea of encroaching darkness.</p>
<p>Mara’s hand rested against the soft curve of her belly, an unconscious, protective gesture she performed almost reflexively now, as if her body knew something her mind refused to acknowledge.</p>
<p>Marceline’s fingers were laced loosely over her own middle, her posture tense. Both women looked up as the door creaked, and for a split second, Rex saw it: the flash of unguarded anxiety in their eyes before they forcibly smoothed their features into welcoming smiles.</p>
<p>Those smiles were too bright, too immediate—the kind of smiles people wear when they are terrified of what the silence might reveal.</p>
<p>"You’re here early," Mara said, her voice soft but carrying an edge of surprise as she began to rise halfway, her movements slower and more deliberate than they once were.</p>
<p>"Stay down, Mara... Please," Rex said gently, raising a hand to wave her back into her seat. "I just wanted to see you both before things get complicated."</p>
<p>He sank into the chair across from them, the wood creaking under his solid frame. Without a word, Marceline slid a steaming cup of tea toward him.</p>
<p>She didn’t ask; she simply knew. He accepted the ceramic mug, his fingers wrapping around it. It was far too hot to drink, the steam rising in lazy spirals that blurred his vision, but he didn’t care.</p>
<p>He held it simply for the heat, letting the warmth seep into his palms, trying to ground himself in the sensation of something simple and harmless.</p>
<p>’Three days,’ his inner voice whispered, dark and relentless. ’Three days until the world breaks.’</p>
<p>’Three days until the innocent become collateral, and the guilty become corpses.’</p>
<p>’Look at them so soft, so unaware.’</p>
<p>’They think the storm is coming from somewhere else... They don’t realize I am the storm...’</p>
<p>"You look tired, Rex," Marceline said quietly, her eyes searching his face with a perceptive tenderness that made him want to flinch. "More than usual..."</p>
<p>"Is something happening at the academy?"</p>
<p>Rex forced a smile, a genuine one this time, though it felt brittle. "Just a lot of moving parts, Marceline."</p>
<p>"Lots of preparations... Nothing you need to worry about."</p>
<p>He lied with the practiced ease of a god playing at being a man. He watched them sip their tea, watching the way the morning light caught the gold in Mara’s hair, and he felt a pang of something dangerously close to grief.</p>
<p>He was building a monument of corpses, and he was doing it to ensure that this fragile, quiet moment could exist at all even if he could never truly be a part of it again.</p>
<p>The silence that followed Marceline’s question wasn’t empty; it was heavy, a physical pressure that seemed to press against the walls of the kitchen.</p>
<p>Rex could read the topography of their fear with devastating clarity. It was written in the way Mara’s fingers compulsively traced the fabric over her belly, a protective reflex for the lives growing inside her.</p>
<p>It was written in the way Marceline’s gaze kept fracturing, darting toward the window as if she expected the sky to split open at any moment, as if the coming apocalypse might announce itself through the glass.</p>
<p>Garrett was somewhere in the back of the inn, likely humming some tuneless melody while polishing pewter, blissfully unaware that the world was balanced on the edge of a knife.</p>
<p>And Bryan... Bryan had been dealt with months ago in a manner so brutal, so final, that mentioning his name felt like invoking a curse.</p>
<p>That left only the three of them here, alone with the crushing reality of what the next seventy-two hours would demand.</p>
<p>"You’re going to be careful," Mara said finally.</p>
<p>Her voice didn’t waver, but it lacked the inflection of a question. It was a plea disguised as a command.</p>
<p>"Whatever this fighting is that everyone keeps whispering about... whatever storm is gathering beneath our feet, you’re going to come back."</p>
<p>"You promised me that, Rex... A long time ago."</p>
<p>"Before any of this... Before I even understood the shadow you cast."</p>
<p>Rex stared into the swirling depths of his tea, his reflection distorted by the steam. "I remember the promise, Mara."</p>
<p>"I’m not asking if you remember it," she snapped, her voice cracking with a sudden, sharp desperation. "I’m asking if you’re going to keep it..."</p>
<p>"Because promises don’t mean anything if you’re lying in a ditch somewhere while we’re left here alone."</p>
<p>Marceline reached across the table, covering Mara’s hand with her own. It was a small, steadying gesture, but it spoke volumes about how thoroughly their lives had fused together.</p>
<p>Since the pregnancies had begun, they had become a single unit, sharing a husband’s affection and a shared terror in ways neither had anticipated, but both had come to rely on like oxygen.</p>
<p>"What she means," Marceline said, her voice softer, laced with a weary tenderness, "is that the stakes have changed."</p>
<p>"We have more reasons than we ever did to need you alive..."</p>
<p>"It isn’t just about us anymore, Rex... It’s about them." She glanced pointedly at Mara’s stomach.</p>
<p>Rex felt the weight of their stares on two women who represented everything precious and everything fragile in a world he was about to shatter. He set the tea down with deliberate slowness, the ceramic clicking against the oak.</p>
<p>He reached across the table, taking each of their free hands in his. His skin felt rough and battle-hardened against their softness.</p>
<p>He let his Silver Tongue work, letting his voice slide into that hypnotic register of absolute, unshakable certainty, the kind of voice that could convince a man to walk into hell if he believed it was safe.</p>
<p>But as he looked at them, he realized something unsettling: he wasn’t entirely performing.</p>
<p>These two women were the last remaining threads connecting him to his humanity. They were the only part of his life in Aethelgard that wasn’t built on deception, bloodlust, or divine manipulation.</p>
<p>Long before the Underlayer became a weapon, before the Apostles became pawns, there was this. This is quiet. This kitchen.</p>
<p>"Listen to me," Rex said, his voice dropping to a low, resonant growl that commanded attention without raising volume.</p>
<p>His thumbs brushed lightly over Mara’s knuckles, then Marceline’s, grounding them. "I have survived things that would drive lesser men to madness."</p>
<p>"I have stood before a god who held dominion over time itself for two millennia and refused to kneel."</p>
<p>"I have walked through slaughterhouses where the air was more blood than oxygen, where bones snapped like dry kindling underfoot, and I emerged untouched."</p>
<p>His eyes locked onto theirs, burning with an intensity that was both terrifying and reassuring.</p>
<p>"Whatever happens in the next few days, whatever carnage breaks loose, I am not leaving either of you to raise these children alone."</p>
<p>"I will not let them grow up knowing their father was a ghost." He paused, letting the gravity of his words settle. "That isn’t some comforting lie I’m feeding you to quiet your nerves."</p>
<p>"It isn’t a performance... It is simply the truth."</p>
<p>"I will return, because I have no intention of letting anyone take what is mine."</p>
<p>’And I will kill anything that tries,’ he added silently, the dark promise echoing in the cathedral of his mind. ’I will paint the world crimson before I let a single hair on your heads be harmed.’</p>
<p>’I will break every spine and shatter every skull that dares to cross my path.’</p>
