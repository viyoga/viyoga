<p>The transition through the failing temporal spiral left Rex suspended in a pocket of absolute, ringing silence. For several precious seconds, he was alone with his own thoughts, a luxury he rarely afforded himself.</p>
<p>His mind flickered back to the anomaly. The figure at the far edge of the domain had not reappeared.</p>
<p>Divine Perception, still humming as a passive subroutine in his consciousness, continued its relentless scan of the surrounding space. It returned nothing: no signature, no residual energy, and no indication that anyone god or demon had been watching.</p>
<p>Rex filed the anomaly away with cold, surgical precision. It would be a problem to solve later, a thread to pull once the war concluded.</p>
<p>But right now, there was a more immediate audience waiting. Beyond the doorway of this collapsing reality lay a corridor lined with ten thousand witnesses, and they would not wait patiently for their entertainment.</p>
<p>As he prepared to reemerge, Rex considered his next move with the same ruthless practicality he applied to every tactical decision. He weighed two futures.</p>
<p>In one, he would simply execute Apollo the instant they breached the corridor—a quick, clean death that denied the spectators any theater.</p>
<p>In the other, he would drag this broken, humiliated husk before them as a living monument to the futility of resistance.</p>
<p>Both had merit. But only one of them would allow him to watch Elizabeth’s face as she witnessed the true depth of his depravity.</p>
<p>Only one would allow him to gauge, in real time, exactly how much terror a single demonstration could sow through the hearts of an entire army.</p>
<p>He had already decided. In the wreckage of this dying domain, he had chosen his path.</p>
<p>He would not make it quick.</p>
<p>Rex stepped forward, his boots crunching on bone fragments and dried blood. He loomed over Apollo’s ruined form like a monument to inevitability.</p>
<p>The Apostle’s breathing was a wet, stuttering thing, the sound of air struggling through lungs that refused to stop functioning despite their collapse.</p>
<p>Rex brought his boot down onto Apollo’s already shattered right leg. He didn’t stomp; he pressed... Slowly... Deliberately...</p>
<p>He applied pressure with the measured force of a man testing the ripeness of fruit. The sound that followed was sickeningly distinct: the wet, splintering CRUNCH of bone fragments grinding against one another as the femur gave way for the second time.</p>
<p>Because Apollo had no strength left to tense his muscles, there was no resistance to soften the impact. The injury didn’t just break; it disintegrated.</p>
<p>Apollo’s body jerked once, a reflexive spasm of agony that found no outlet. His mouth opened, but no scream emerged, only a single, ragged exhale that sounded like wind through dead leaves.</p>
<p>Rex wasn’t done. His hands reached down, gloved fingers wrapping around Apollo’s left arm.</p>
<p>He gripped the shoulder joint, feeling the heat of the infection and the blood beneath the skin. With careful, unhurried precision, Rex began to twist.</p>
<p>He moved with the patience of a craftsman, rotating the limb millimeter by millimeter, feeling the tendons stretch to their breaking point before they finally surrendered.</p>
<p>SQUELCH. GRIND. POP.</p>
<p>The shoulder joint separated from the socket with a wet, grinding sound that echoed off the walls of the crumbling chamber. The arm hung at an impossible angle, held on only by ravaged muscle and torn skin.</p>
<p>Apollo made no sound at all now. His eyes had lost their focus, staring blankly at some distant point beyond reality, his pupils blown wide as his consciousness retreated further into the dark.</p>
<p>With each new injury Rex inflicted, the man seemed to sink deeper into the cracked floor, his body collapsing further into itself, a hollowed-out shell that had nothing left to resist the inevitable.</p>
<p>There was a particular, nauseating quality to the silence that followed each new injury, a stillness that felt less like unconsciousness and more like the profound resignation of a man who had simply stopped believing that any response he could produce would alter the inevitable.</p>
<p>Apollo wasn’t just broken; he was hollowed out. The spark that had made him a threat, the divine fire that had once burned with such righteous conviction, had been systematically extinguished by Rex’s relentless cruelty.</p>
<p>Rex watched this collapse with the detached precision of an entomologist observing a beetle pinned to a board. He noted the specific way Apollo’s pupils refused to track movement, the way his breathing had shifted from labored gasps to a shallow, rattling wheeze.</p>
<p>He filed this under Psychological Attrition, a data point to be analyzed later. Even this, even the specific, visceral texture of a person’s complete spiritual surrender, held its own small, perverse measure of interest.</p>
<p>"I’ve enjoyed this quite thoroughly," Rex said, his voice cutting through the stagnant air like a scalpel.</p>
<p>He stood over the barely moving wreck of the man he had once called a friend, his expression settling into the flat, faintly bored look of a child who had finished with a toy that no longer produced an interesting sound. "But I find myself growing impatient..."</p>
<p>"The audience is waiting, Apollo... And I hate to keep them waiting for the finale."</p>
<p>Rex raised one hand toward the dying sky of the domain.</p>
<p>Violet black energy began to hemorrhage from his fingertips, drawn from the Mark of Lust Authority that had never truly left him. The energy didn’t just glow; it writhed like a living shadow.</p>
<p>Simultaneously, the fragments of his shattered mask—pieces of obsidian slick material—scattered across the blood-soaked floor since Apollo’s earlier desperate strike began to stir. They levitated in slow, unnatural spirals, caught in a localized gravitational well.</p>
<p>SHHHHH VROOOOOM</p>
<p>The fragments drifted upward, humming with malevolent intent, converging across the upper half of Rex’s face in a seamless dance of reconstruction.</p>
<p>The reformation took only seconds: the violet surface knitted itself back together, sealing away the human features Apollo had exposed with such devastating effort.</p>
<p>The mask didn’t just repair itself; it reasserted itself, restoring the terrifying visage of Lord Xerollion completely, as though the damage had never occurred.</p>
<p>Rex rolled his shoulders once beneath the renewed armor, testing the fit like a man adjusting a well-worn coat, feeling the familiar, comfortable weight of his godhood settling back into place around whatever remained of his humanity.</p>
<p>The domain itself was dying around them. The slow, violet pulse that had once sustained this pocket dimension was now reduced to a stuttering, sickly flicker.</p>
<p>Whole sections of the floor beneath them simply ceased to exist, plunging into an absolute, hungry darkness that seemed to lap at the edges of the remaining light.</p>
<p>Rex glanced at the temporal readings flickering across his inner vision. He had perhaps another sixty seconds before the structural integrity of this reality failed completely, depositing them both somewhere considerably less controlled than his own doorway.</p>
<p>In any other circumstance, that would be a tactical disadvantage. But Rex was not any other circumstance.</p>
<p>A minute was more than sufficient to deliver a killing blow.</p>
<p>"One last lesson before we depart, Apostle," Rex whispered, the mask’s voice carrying an unnatural, layered resonance. "In the end, light doesn’t defeat darkness..."</p>
<p>"It just provides the contrast necessary for darkness to be seen."</p>
