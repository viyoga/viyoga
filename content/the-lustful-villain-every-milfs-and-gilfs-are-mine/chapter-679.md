<p>Rex stood, his presence commanding the entire room, his aura expanding until he seemed to loom over them all.</p>
<p>"Then we do not wait," he declared, the command absolute. "The survey and the contact initiation will happen simultaneously."</p>
<p>"Gorvasha, you map the formation and find the leverage."</p>
<p>"I will run the geological contact through the relay points."</p>
<p>"We will hit them with a signal so loud and so intentional that they cannot possibly ignore it."</p>
<p>"The second stratum will receive an active, divine greeting from the Underlayer’s new authority."</p>
<p>He turned his piercing gaze toward Mordecai. "You will be at the contact point."</p>
<p>"When they scream back, the diplomatic response is yours. You will be the voice of the new era."</p>
<p>Mordecai met his gaze, a flicker of apprehension in his eyes. "They may not respond today, my lord."</p>
<p>"We are throwing a stone into a very deep ocean."</p>
<p>"They have been increasing their signal frequency for three weeks, Mordecai."</p>
<p>"They are starving for a response," Rex countered, his voice brimming with a terrifying, arrogant confidence. "They will respond and they won’t have a choice."</p>
<p>Mordecai bowed his head, accepting the inevitability of the gamble.</p>
<p>"After the reconstruction review," Rex commanded, his eyes sweeping over the assembled court, his expression one of cold, triumphant dominance. "The canyon... this afternoon..."</p>
<p>"We go to wake the gods."</p>
<p>"The throne room session is concluded," Rex announced, his voice cutting through the heavy atmosphere like a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>He didn’t wait for a reaction; he simply dictated the new reality. "Pavellia commences the reconstruction review in exactly two hours."</p>
<p>"By the end of her first session, every council member will have submitted their department’s primary briefing document."</p>
<p>"And let there be no confusion: those documents go directly to her desk... They do not pass through your peers."</p>
<p>"There will be no collusion, no shared narratives, only the raw, unvarnished truth of your performance."</p>
<p>His gaze drifted to the six council members lined along the periphery of the room, his eyes cold and predatory. He wasn’t looking at subordinates; he was looking at assets that had yet to prove their long-term value.</p>
<p>"Do not mistake your presence here for security," Rex added, a cruel, mocking lilt entering his tone. "You have survived the initial pass, which is a mere functional assessment, not a permanent status."</p>
<p>"The review determines what is vital and what is dead weight. And the review will continue until Pavellia decides there is nothing left to dissect."</p>
<p>The threat hung in the air, thick and suffocating. From her position against the wall, Thessaly bowed her head, her voice steady but respectful. "Understood, my lord."</p>
<p>The other five council members responded in a synchronized, silent acknowledgment of the practiced, collective deference of those who knew exactly how thin the ice beneath them truly was.</p>
<p>Rex rose from the throne. The movement was fluid, effortless, and carried the weight of absolute authority.</p>
<p>As he stood, the entire court rose in a wave of rustling silk and armored shifting, a reflex of survival.</p>
<p>"Gorvasha," Rex said.</p>
<p>He didn’t move from his central position, his presence still dominating the room even as he stood. She stepped forward, her eyes meeting his with the unwavering intensity of the earth itself.</p>
<p>"The formation survey," he commanded, cutting straight to the marrow of the matter. "Give me the specifics."</p>
<p>"What is the exact geological composition at the fault line intersection?"</p>
<p>Gorvasha had clearly been anticipating the strike. There was no hesitation, no fumbling through mental archives; the data was etched into her very consciousness.</p>
<p>"A three-layer composition," she began, her voice resonant. "The upper layer is compressed volcanic material, the standard substrate of the underlayer."</p>
<p>"The middle layer, however, is a transition period stone. It is a rare, volatile medium formed during long-cycle geological events, where two distinct substrate types have been forced into adjacency for so long they have begun to exchange fundamental properties."</p>
<p>"And the lower layer?" Rex pressed, his eyes narrowing.</p>
<p>"The transition stone acts as a shroud," Gorvasha replied, her expression tightening. "It blocks all passive reads."</p>
<p>"To know what lies beneath that middle layer, the Earthen Authority cannot simply observe; it must make direct, physical contact at the site."</p>
<p>"Which is why the survey demands a physical presence," Rex noted, his voice a low, dangerous hum.</p>
<p>"Precisely," she said.</p>
<p>"Tell me," Rex leaned in slightly, his intellect searching for the flaw in the plan, "what does that transition stone do to a worker that attempts to pass through it?"</p>
<p>Gorvasha paused, her mind calculating the physics of the strike. "It depends on the nature of the application."</p>
<p>"Standard elemental workings will attenuate the energy, which will bleed out and dissipate into the stone, which is why the passive reads fail."</p>
<p>"However... force-based applications that harmonize with the stone’s own internal structure, rather than attempting to pierce it, will suffer far less resistance."</p>
<p>A dark, triumphant glint flickered in Rex’s eyes. "The Earthen Authority is force-based in that exact sense."</p>
<p>"Yes," Gorvasha conceded, a rare spark of excitement touching her voice. "You wouldn’t be pushing through the transition layer; you would be speaking to it in its own tongue."</p>
<p>"The attenuation shouldn’t just be minimized; it should be bypassed entirely."</p>
<p>"—Should not," Rex corrected, his tone a sharp reminder of the thin line between theory and catastrophe.</p>
<p>"I have never engaged in direct contact with a transition period layer before," Gorvasha admitted, her voice dropping into a register of brutal honesty. "I can provide the geological theory, and I can give you a highly confident estimate of the outcome, but in the face of such a variable, certainty is a luxury we do not have."</p>
<p>Rex stared at her for a long moment, the silence in the throne room becoming deafening. He wasn’t looking for a sycophant; he was looking for a weapon that wouldn’t lie to him about its own edge.</p>
<p>"An honest assessment," Rex finally said, a ghost of a smirk playing on his lips.</p>
<p>"It is the only kind that matters," she countered with her trademark directness.</p>
<p>Rex gave a single, decisive nod. "The honest estimate is useful, Gorvasha."</p>
<p>"The uncertainty... is noted."</p>
<p>Gorvasha offered no nod, no smile, no softening of her features. She simply accepted the remark with a stony, unyielding silence, the highest form of respect she could afford, signaling that the truth had been spoken and there was nothing left to debate.</p>
<p>Rex’s gaze swept the room one final time, a cold, sweeping predator surveying his territory. The air in the throne room felt heavy, pressurized by his presence, as if the very atmosphere were waiting for his next command.</p>
<p>"Two hours," he declared. The words were short, sharp, and final.</p>
<p>He turned and strode from the dais. The court remained frozen, a tableau of statues in the dim light, holding their breath and their positions until the massive doors groaned shut behind him.</p>
<p>They knew the rule: in this room, you did not move until the shadow of the throne had passed.</p>
<p>Pavellia was already a blur of efficient motion, gliding toward the side corridor that led to her administrative sanctum. She moved with the terrifying grace of a machine, the documents clutched in her hand like a weapon.</p>
<p>To her, the throne room session was merely the preamble; the true work, the dissection of the city’s soul, was already beginning in her mind.</p>
<p>Rex stepped into the corridor outside the main entrance, breaking the silence of the hall with the rhythmic click of heels. Lilith fell into step beside him, her presence seamless, unsummoned, and entirely unapologetic.</p>
<p>"The canyon this afternoon," she said. It wasn’t a question; it was a statement of intent.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex replied, his voice a low, disinterested rumble.</p>
<p>"I’m coming," she stated.</p>
<p>Rex slowed his pace just enough to cast a sidelong, piercing look at her. He didn’t offer an invitation; he offered a challenge.</p>
<p>"The second stratum contact is Mordecai’s diplomatic moment," he reminded her, his tone laced with a hint of a warning. "He is the voice. He is the one standing in the breach."</p>
<p>"Mordecai is the voice, but he is not the bridge," Lilith countered instantly.</p>
<p>Her eyes were bright, her expression devoid of sentiment. "The site requires a conduit, someone capable of running high-fidelity surface-to-underlayer communication the moment a response is received."</p>
<p>"If the second stratum screams, we cannot afford a delay in the relay. That is my function. That is what I do."</p>
<p>She spoke with a chilling, operational precision, stripping away all emotion to present her argument as a cold logistical necessity. Rex watched her, a flicker of dark amusement dancing in his eyes.</p>
<p>He recognized this version of Lilith as the one who had learned that he had no patience for the whims of the heart and had instead armored herself in the logic of the mind. She had realized that to reach him, she had to speak the language of utility.</p>
<p>"You’re coming," Rex said. It wasn’t an invitation; it was a decree.</p>
<p>"I contributed to the multiplier," she added, a subtle, almost imperceptible hint of pride slipping through her clinical veneer.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even turn his head as he continued his ascent toward the castle’s upper levels.</p>
<p>"That is not a relevant criterion for this decision," he said, his voice dismissive, cutting through her attempt at merit.</p>
<p>"I know," she replied smoothly, her pace never faltering. "I said it anyway."</p>
<p>A ghost of a smirk touched Rex’s lips, a rare, fleeting moment of recognition for her audacity. They climbed higher, leaving the heavy silence of the throne room behind, while below them, the Underlayer began to groan and stir.</p>
<p>The sounds of the reconstruction—the rhythmic hammering, the hum of magical conduits, and the distant shouts of workers—rose up like a heartbeat. The city was waking, beginning the long, violent process of shedding its old skin to become whatever Rex intended it to be.</p>
