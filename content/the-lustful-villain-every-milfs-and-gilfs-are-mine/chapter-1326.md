<p>Rex had barely finished the syllable before the air itself seemed to rupture.</p>

<p>There was no thunderclap, no dramatic warning of displaced air. Evelyn had bypassed the physical limitations of sound entirely; she had become a phenomenon of pure velocity.</p>

<p>One moment she was twenty paces away, a golden specter amidst the rubble; the next, she was simply there, occupying the space directly in front of Rex’s sternum.</p>

<p>Her fist was already a blur of white gold energy, drawn back with the coiled tension of a siege engine. When her knuckles connected with the center of his chest, it wasn’t just a punch.</p>

<p>It was a cosmic transaction.</p>

<p>CRACK BOOM.</p>

<p>The Scales of Retribution activated with terrifying, mathematical precision. In that singular microsecond, the universe balanced the ledger.</p>

<p>Every ounce of agony she had endured in the last hour, every violation of her flesh, was forcibly extracted from her body and detonated inside his.</p>

<p>The recoil was instantaneous and catastrophic.</p>

<p>The sternum Rex had shattered at the beginning of their confrontation didn’t just break again; it exploded inward like glass under a sledgehammer.</p>

<p>The jaw he had twisted with such casual cruelty snapped sideways with a wet, grinding sound as bone fragments shredded through muscle. The ribs he had systematically kicked in succession collapsed into his lungs like crushed soda cans.</p>

<p>But the worst was the core: the devastating hole he had punched through her stomach during their battle at sea roared back into existence within his own torso.</p>

<p>Thirty-seven distinct impacts each one a precise, calculated echo of his own brutality detonated inside his ribcage simultaneously.</p>

<p>Rex didn’t have time to scream. The air was punched out of his lungs in a spray of crimson mist.</p>

<p>He didn’t feel himself leave the ground; he only felt the impossible reality of his body becoming a projectile.</p>

<p>The first thing his consciousness registered was the bone-jarring impact of passing through a solid stone wall.</p>

<p>Then came the second.</p>

<p>Then a third.</p>

<p>CRUNCH. SHATTER. SPLINTER.</p>

<p>He became a human cannonball of meat and malice, tearing through the district like a wrecking ball. He punched through the front of a merchant’s house, the wooden support beams disintegrating around him like brittle parchment.</p>

<p>The world became a nauseating kaleidoscope of splintering timber, cascading plaster, and the screams of startled civilians.</p>

<p>He tore through five buildings in rapid succession; each impact was followed by a wet thud and the shriek of collapsing architecture. The debris cloud followed him like a storm.</p>

<p>Finally, the momentum ran out against something immovable.</p>

<p>Rex’s back slammed into the massive, ancient marble pillar at the entrance of the Grand Academy with the force of a meteor strike.</p>

<p>KRA KOOOM!</p>

<p>A sound like a thunderclap rang across the entire plaza as the pillar spider webbed from its base to its capital, a hairline fracture racing up its surface. Dust and ancient stone particles rained down from the wound in the monument.</p>

<p>For three seconds, there was only the ringing silence of shock.</p>

<p>Then, slowly, Rex’s broken form began to slide down the cracked marble, leaving a thick, dark smear of blood behind him as he crumpled onto the cobblestones.</p>

<p>Rex collapsed against the cobblestones, his body convulsing in involuntary spasms. His mouth was a flooded reservoir of copper-tasting blood; every ragged breath he drew sent arterial spray painting the cracked marble.</p>

<p>The damage was beyond conventional injury; it was anatomical desecration. Three jagged shards of his own ribs had punched through his pectoral muscles, gleaming white and wet against his torn flesh.</p>

<p>Inside his torso, his organs felt like they had been liquified and stirred by a hurricane. Most horrifying was his left eye: the internal pressure of the impact had ruptured the globe, leaving it a ruined, crimson crater hanging grotesquely in its socket.</p>

<p>Deep within his soul, the twin engines of his godhood ignited.</p>

<p>[Supreme Healing] and [Infinite Regeneration] roared to life simultaneously, flooding his circulatory system with a searing, desperate warmth.</p>

<p>But something was wrong.</p>

<p>For the first time in his existence, the healing stumbled.</p>

<p>The divine blessing of the Scales wasn’t just healing tissue; it was enforcing a state of injury.</p>

<p>At every point of impact, the blessing acted like an invisible hand pressing down on a flame, holding his flesh in its broken, mangled shape, refusing to let the cells knit back together.</p>

<p>The retribution was still active, still weighing, still denying him the comfort of recovery.</p>

<p>A cold, mechanical voice flickered across his consciousness, his own system struggling to process the impossible.</p>

<p>[WARNING: Divine Authority detected. Standard adaptive protocols failing.]</p>

<p>[Full Potential Adaptation: First contact with Scales of Retribution.]</p>

<p>[Analysis running... Estimated completion: 11.2 seconds.]</p>

<p>[First contact with Judgment Lightning. Estimated completion: 9.6 seconds.]</p>

<p>’Eleven seconds,’ Rex thought, his mind reeling through the haze of agony. ’Eleven seconds to survive a god.’</p>

<p>The numbers felt agonizingly slow. He understood why his systems were choking.</p>

<p>Every enemy he had ever faced—every mage, every warrior, every monster—had operated on the laws of physics. They used energy, they used force, and they used patterns.</p>

<p>There was always a shape to study, a frequency to mimic, a weakness to exploit.</p>

<p>But this? This wasn’t energy.</p>

<p>This was a Ruling.</p>

<p>This wasn’t a spell he could unravel or a technique he could counter. The blessing didn’t need to hit him with a specific angle or a certain magical frequency.</p>

<p>It simply required that the pain be true.</p>

<p>And how do you analyze something that possesses the absolute authority of truth?</p>

<p>You cannot dissect a fact.</p>

<p>You cannot adapt to the inevitable.</p>

<p>Gack cough!</p>

<p>Rex lurched forward, pushing his broken body up against the cracked marble pillar. He spat out a glob of blood and bone fragments, his fingers clawing at the stone for purchase.</p>

<p>The regeneration finally began to win the war against the divine pressure, knitting his ribs back into place with audible, wet clicks and grinding pops, though his face remained a grotesque mask of half-healed tissue and raw nerve endings.</p>

<p>He forced himself upright. His legs shook, but he stood.</p>

<p>He wiped a crimson smear from his chin with the back of a blood-slicked hand and turned his ruined gaze toward the center of the plaza.</p>

<p>There she stood. Evelyn.</p>

<p>She looked less like a girl and more like a cataclysm incarnate. The golden fire dancing across her fists cast long, flickering shadows across the destruction she had wrought, and the symbol of the scales burned on her chest with a steady, unblinking light.</p>

<p>A sound began to build in his chest, a wet, tattered noise that scraped against his throat like broken glass.</p>

<p>Then, he began to laugh.</p>

<p>It was a low, rasping chuckle that carried the weight of a madman’s ecstasy.</p>

<p>In that moment, beneath the pain, beneath the shattered bones and the ruined eye, Rex felt something he hadn’t experienced since the fall of Kaeleth.</p>

<p>It wasn’t fear. It was something far more dangerous.</p>

<p>It was pure, unadulterated respect.</p>

<p>"Finally," he rasped, his voice cracking as he stared at the golden flame in her eyes. "That’s exactly what I wanted."</p>
