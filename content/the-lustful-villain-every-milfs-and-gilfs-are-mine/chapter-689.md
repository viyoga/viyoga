<p>Lilith stared at him, the realization hitting her like a physical blow. "You didn’t just send Zane to report the news... you released Zane specifically to test her reaction speed."</p>
<p>"You’re literally baiting her."</p>
<p>"I released Zane to provide Celestina with accurate information," Rex corrected, though the arrogance in his eyes betrayed the truth. "How she responds to that information is the real data point."</p>
<p>"Someone who reacts to last night’s report by launching an immediate, aggressive strike against Aethelgard is a different kind of player than someone who pulls her assets back to reassess the board."</p>
<p>"I need to know what kind of predator she is."</p>
<p>"You want to know her temperament," Lilith summarized.</p>
<p>"I already know she is sophisticated," Rex said, his voice filled with a dark, begrudging respect. "The Legion’s history, the relay network, the way Kregg described their methodology... it all points to a woman who thinks in decades, not days."</p>
<p>"She doesn’t react to events; she builds toward outcomes."</p>
<p>"So she moves fast," Lilith pushed.</p>
<p>"She moves when the window is optimal," Rex countered, his gaze turning toward the distant canyons. "The canyon engagement is the window, and that event is six weeks away."</p>
<p>"If she is building a grand design around that moment, she is already moving her pieces into place."</p>
<p>"Which means her assets are already in transit," Lilith whispered, the dread finally settling in her bones.</p>
<p>"Some of them," Rex conceded, his expression turning grimly thoughtful. "The ones that require the most lead time."</p>
<p>"The specific, specialized capability required for what she actually intends to do with Aethelgard... that isn’t something you can position in twenty-four hours. It’s a slow-moving tide."</p>
<p>Lilith was quiet, the silence between them heavy with the weight of impending war. She looked at the beautiful, sunlit island, realizing it was no longer a home but a chessboard.</p>
<p>"What does she actually want to do with Aethelgard, Master?" she asked, her voice barely audible above the wind. "What is her end game?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t answer immediately. He turned his gaze back to the island, his eyes narrowing as if he could see through the stone, through the people, and straight into the heart of the coming storm.</p>
<p>"The substrate didn’t just map the terrain when the Authority read the island this morning," Rex began, his voice dropping into an unnervingly calm register, almost clinical. "It mapped the foundational architecture."</p>
<p>"The very bones of this place."</p>
<p>He turned his head slightly, the sunlight catching the sharp, predatory angles of his face. "The divine intervention that lifted Aethelgard from the depths used a specific compression methodology to keep the mass stable."</p>
<p>"It wasn’t a brute force levitation; it was a mathematical miracle. And every compression methodology has a principle."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the silence stretch, heavy and suffocating, between them.</p>
<p>"The principle is the singular logic that holds a compressed structure coherent," he continued, his eyes narrowing as if he were looking at a blueprint written in the air itself. "If you introduce a contradiction to that principle, the structure doesn’t slowly erode."</p>
<p>"It doesn’t gradually weaken or crumble."</p>
<p>"It fails instantly, catastrophically, at the exact point where the logic breaks... The entire system collapses inward on its own flaw."</p>
<p>Lilith felt the blood drain from her face. The logic was terrifyingly simple.</p>
<p>"The island falls," she whispered, the words feeling like lead in her mouth. "It falls into the Convergence Waters."</p>
<p>"The Convergence Waters have no floor," Rex added, his voice devoid of pity, as if he were discussing the weather rather than a mass extinction event. "It is a vertical descent into an infinite void."</p>
<p>For a long, agonizing moment, the morning air was unnervingly still. Even the wind seemed to hold its breath.</p>
<p>Lilith looked down at the ground beneath her feet, then back at the sprawling landscape of the island. Her expression was no longer that of a strategist but of someone who had just realized the very floor of the world was a lie.</p>
<p>The scale of reality had shifted; the ground was no longer solid but a fragile shell suspended over nothingness.</p>
<p>"She isn’t just going to attack us," Lilith said, her voice trembling with the sheer magnitude of the horror. "She is going to try to drop the island."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, a small, dark, and utterly arrogant smirk playing on his lips.</p>
<p>He looked entirely too comfortable with the idea of total annihilation.</p>
<p>"And everything on it," Lilith choked out, her eyes wide. "Every single living soul."</p>
<p>"The Apostle network," Rex listed, his voice cold and methodical, as if he were conducting an inventory of assets. "The Academy, along with the entire population of reincarnators, has been lost."</p>
<p>"Even the political and military infrastructure of the most significant power center between the four surrounding nations. All of it, gone in a heartbeat."</p>
<p>"And you," Lilith added, her voice rising in desperation.</p>
<p>"And the Starlight household," Rex countered, his gaze hardening, the smugness momentarily replaced by a fierce, territorial possessiveness. "And Diana, and Lily, and Elizabeth, and Mara, and Marceline..."</p>
<p>"Everyone else on this island who is woven into the tapestry of what I have built here. Everything is on the line."</p>
<p>Lilith stared at him, a sudden, sharp realization piercing through her panic. She saw the way he stood unmoved, unshakeable, as if he had already lived through the catastrophe and found it wanting.</p>
<p>"You knew this," she said, her voice a mixture of awe and accusation. "When you were reading the substrate this morning... you found the compression principle, and you understood exactly what it meant the moment you saw it."</p>
<p>"The Authority mapped the architecture in forty seconds," Rex said, his tone dismissive of the terror the revelation should have commanded. "The implication was immediate."</p>
<p>"And you waited?" Lilith snapped, her composure finally fracturing. "You waited until the middle of explaining your golem deployment plan to tell me that the entire world is about to fall into an infinite abyss?"</p>
<p>Rex turned to her, his expression one of supreme, maddening confidence. "The golem deployment is relevant to addressing the problem, Lilith."</p>
<p>"The two are inextricably connected. One is the diagnosis; the other is the surgical response."</p>
<p>"You could have started with the fact that the island is falling into the ocean!" she cried, the absurdity of his composure hitting her like a physical weight.</p>
<p>"I could have," Rex conceded with a shrug, his eyes gleaming with a terrifying, charismatic light.</p>
<p>He looked like a man who was not just prepared for the end of the world but was actively looking forward to the challenge of defying it.</p>
<p>Lilith looked at him for a long time, her eyes searching his for a hint of doubt, a flicker of fear, or even a shred of human empathy. Finding none, she simply exhaled a long, defeated breath.</p>
<p>It was the look of a woman who had decided to file a silent, internal objection rather than press the point because she knew, with a sinking certainty, that arguing with a god in the making was a waste of precious time.</p>
<p>"What do you need from me?" Lilith asked, her voice dropping the pretense of small talk.</p>
<p>The air between them was electric, heavy with the looming shadow of a catastrophe that felt both impossible and inevitable.</p>
<p>"The golems will handle the reincarnator sweep," Rex said, his eyes already scanning a mental horizon only he could see.</p>
<p>He sounded less like a man preparing for war and more like a conductor preparing a symphony. "What I need from you is the Underlayer’s communication relay."</p>
<p>"The moment the canyon timing becomes clear, I need Cassandra and Gorvasha to have the data in their hands."</p>
<p>"They need to adjust the engagement plan in real time, or the entire underlayer will be blind when the hammer falls."</p>
<p>"You think the two operations are synchronized," Lilith realized, her mind racing to keep pace with his terrifying intellect.</p>
<p>"If Celestina is as sophisticated as her reputation suggests, the Aethelgard operation is timed to strike the moment the canyon engagement commits the Apostle network’s local force," Rex said, a dark, smug grin spreading across his face.</p>
<p>It was the look of a man who had just seen the enemy’s hand before they even reached for their sword. "She wants the network depleted."</p>
<p>"She wants the defenders stretched thin, exhausted, and distracted by the canyon."</p>
<p>"The canyon engagement is the distraction; her operation is the execution."</p>
<p>"They are two halves of a single, lethal pincer."</p>
<p>"Two simultaneous crises," Lilith whispered, the weight of the arithmetic hitting her. "A pincer movement on a continental scale... For you."</p>
<p>"For the island and for the Underlayer," Rex corrected, his voice ringing with a cold, terrifying clarity. "Simultaneously. A total, synchronized collapse."</p>
<p>Lilith stood silent, her eyes narrowing as she ran the operational math. The numbers didn’t just add up; they screamed. The sheer scale of the coordination required to survive this was staggering.</p>
<p>"The golems are already moving through the outer sectors," she said, forcing her voice into a professional, tactical clip. "The morning is progressing."</p>
<p>"According to your timeline, the non-flagged consolidation begins in exactly twenty minutes."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, his gaze fixed on the distance.</p>
<p>"Then we should move," Lilith commanded, turning to prepare the orders.</p>
<p>"After one more thing," Rex said and the tone of his voice stopped her mid-step. It wasn’t a request; it was a command of absolute necessity.</p>
