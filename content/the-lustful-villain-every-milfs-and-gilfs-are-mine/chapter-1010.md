<p>Iris grew quiet then, her eyes fixed on the flickering lanterns. The light played across her features, revealing a vulnerability she rarely allowed herself to show.</p>
<p>"I have been thinking about Veylor less than I expected to since that night," she admitted, the name hanging between them like a ghost. "It isn’t that I’ve forgotten him."</p>
<p>"It’s just... the thing I was carrying that constant, gnawing ache it finally has somewhere to rest."</p>
<p>"I don’t have to hold it up alone anymore."</p>
<p>Rex turned his head to study her. He knew what she meant. He had been the anvil upon which her grief had been hammered into something useful, something survivable.</p>
<p>"That was the purpose of the work," he said, his tone uncharacteristically gentle. "The training was designed to give you something else to focus on."</p>
<p>"To give the pain a container."</p>
<p>Iris turned to meet his gaze, a sad, knowing smile touching her lips.</p>
<p>"I know," she said. "But I truly did not expect it to work."</p>
<p>"Most things in my life have never worked quite so... effectively."</p>
<p>Iris turned her head to meet his gaze, and for a moment, the carefully constructed armor of the Nightwing household’s confidante slipped entirely.</p>
<p>She gave him a look that was raw and unguarded, a rare glimpse into the quiet spaces of her soul that she never permitted in the presence of others.</p>
<p>In her eyes, there was something far more profound than mere appreciation; there was a steady, simmering warmth that had been forging itself in the crucible of the last thirty days, growing from a simple respect into something she no longer had the energy or the desire to pretend was simple.</p>
<p>"I am glad you walked into that training hall a month ago," she said, her voice barely more than a breath against the night air. "Whatever your true motives were, whatever game you were playing or whatever debt you were settling..."</p>
<p>"I have stopped needing to know the full shape of them."</p>
<p>"Because I know that whatever brought you here, the result has made me whole in ways I didn’t know I was breaking."</p>
<p>Rex studied her. Beneath the practiced, warm humility he had offered the courtyard throughout the evening, something flickered behind his eyes.</p>
<p>It was a private acknowledgment, a silent vow that only Iris, standing close enough to feel the heat radiating from him, could truly catch. He didn’t offer her a platitud or a reassurance.</p>
<p>He offered her truth.</p>
<p>"Neither of us needs the full shape of it tonight," he said, his voice carrying an undertone of something almost possessive, almost protective. "Tonight is for what we have become, not for what we were before."</p>
<p>Iris’s mouth curved into a small, knowing smile, the satisfied expression of a woman who understood exactly what remained unspoken and was perfectly content to let those unspoken truths breathe between them.</p>
<p>She didn’t push for more; she simply allowed the moment to settle like dust after a storm. They stood together in that liminal space at the courtyard’s edge until Diana’s voice cut through the darkness, calling Iris back toward the main gathering where a new toast was being organized.</p>
<p>Diana was the last to find him.</p>
<p>By the time she approached, the structured ceremony of the evening had dissolved into the loose, comfortable haze of a celebration winding toward its natural conclusion.</p>
<p>The sharp edges of the Nightwing discipline had softened; small clusters of people huddled together in intimate conversation, and from a distant table, the unpolished, genuine strains of a household song drifted through the air as several junior guards attempted to coax melody from a borrowed instrument.</p>
<p>Diana didn’t ask permission. She didn’t offer an opening gambit.</p>
<p>She simply stepped into his space and took his hand, her grip firm and certain. There was no longer any need for a preamble between them; the month of shared intensity had burned away the necessity of social niceties.</p>
<p>Without a word, she led him further into the garden’s encroaching darkness, pulling him away from even the fading amber glow of the courtyard’s perimeter.</p>
<p>"You have been drifting to the edges all evening," she said once they were safely swallowed by the shadows of the ancient trees.</p>
<p>Her voice was low, carrying a trace of accusation wrapped in affection. "Saying quiet, private goodbyes to everyone individually instead of one grand, sweeping farewell to the room."</p>
<p>Rex looked ahead into the darkness where the garden met the unknown.</p>
<p>"That is how I prefer to do it," he said, his words precise, each one cutting through the humid night air. "A room full of people receiving the same farewell all at once is efficient, yes, but it is also dishonest."</p>
<p>"It reduces a month of blood, sweat, and genuine transformation into a single, convenient performance."</p>
<p>He turned his hand in hers, tightening his grip. "Each person here gave something different this month."</p>
<p>"Some gave their fear, some gave their loyalty, and some gave their breaking points."</p>
<p>"I would rather acknowledge each of those gifts properly than compress them into one hollow speech that means nothing to anyone."</p>
<p>Diana turned to face him fully, stepping into the deepest pocket of shadow where the moonlight couldn’t reach.</p>
<p>In the absence of the courtyard’s prying eyes, the practiced composure she maintained as a pillar of the Nightwing household finally crumbled. Her expression was raw, stripped of its usual steel, leaving something startlingly soft and unguarded in its place.</p>
<p>"You gave this household something real, Rex," she said, her voice carrying a tremor of conviction that cut through the humid night air. "I know what your actual reasons were for coming here."</p>
<p>"I haven’t been blind. I’ve known for weeks now I’ve seen the strings you’re pulling, the way you position yourself, and the way you calculate every move like a gambit on a board." A pause followed, and she took a breath that seemed to steady her soul. "And I have made my peace with that."</p>
<p>"I refuse to lie to myself and pretend you came here out of some sudden burst of altruism. But what did you build here?"</p>
<p>"What you forged in the fire of this last month? That wasn’t part of any plan. That was real."</p>
<p>She stepped closer, her eyes searching his. "I watched it happen, Rex."</p>
<p>"I watched the transformation. Rilena doesn’t just respect you; she adores you."</p>
<p>"Sela would follow you into the throat of the Underlayer itself if you commanded it. Even Elaris, who I suspect understood exactly what kind of dangerous, complicated man you are by the end of the second week, has never once looked at you with anything but genuine warmth."</p>
<p>Rex’s jaw tightened. His inner mind, always running calculations and assessing threats and advantages, momentarily stalled.</p>
<p>"You are telling me," he said, his voice rough, "that the operation and the sincerity are not mutually exclusive."</p>
<p>"I am telling you that I no longer need them to be mutually exclusive," Diana countered, her gaze fierce. "I love you, Rex."</p>
<p>"I have loved you long enough now that I no longer flinch at the darker parts of you, the parts that a more naive version of myself would have found terrifying."</p>
<p>"I don’t need you to be a saint... I simply want you to know that I see all of it."</p>
<p>"I see the cold calculation and I see the genuine warmth."</p>
<p>"I see them sitting side by side in the same space inside you, and I have chosen you anyway. With my eyes wide open, knowing exactly what I am choosing."</p>
<p>The honesty of it hit him harder than any blade he had faced in the training hall. For a man who built his entire existence on layers of deception and controlled outcomes, Diana’s acceptance was the only thing that could truly disarm him.</p>
<p>Rex reached out, his hands finding her waist, and pulled her flush against him. The distant, amber glow of the courtyard’s lanterns caught the edge of her face, revealing a steady, unshakable certainty.</p>
<p>It was the same certainty she had carried since that night on Sable Island, the night she had first laid her heart bare to him, and the night he had realized that some things could not be controlled by strategy alone.</p>
<p>He kissed her then, not with the practiced ease of a seducer, but with a slow, unhurried hunger that spoke of months of restrained tension.</p>
<p>In the velvet silence of the garden, Diana’s hands found their familiar place, her fingers curling into the fabric of his shirt. She wasn’t pulling him closer; she was holding on, anchoring herself to him, because there was no closer they could possibly get.</p>
<p>When they finally broke apart, their breath mingled in the cool air. Diana leaned in, her lips brushing against his in a ghost of a touch.</p>
<p>"Come find me later," she murmured against his mouth, her words a command wrapped in a promise. "Properly."</p>
<p>"Once the household has finally gone to sleep and the last lantern has burned out."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes darkened, the strategist surrendering entirely to the man.</p>
<p>"I will," he promised.</p>
