<p>For the better part of the afternoon, two conversations ran in perfect, deadly parallel. One voice spoke in two rooms separated by hundreds of meters of unforgiving stone and ancient shadow, two distinct realities connected by a single, invisible thread.</p>
<p>They were perfectly synchronized because both rooms, despite their vastly different atmospheres, were hearing from the exact same source. One was a performance of heroism; the other was a rehearsal for genocide.</p>
<p>In the sun-drenched council chamber of Aethelgard, Valentina moved the discussion into the cold mechanics of logistics. Her movements were methodical, her eyes sharp as she traced troop estimates across a massive map spread across the central table.</p>
<p>She placed small, carved tokens—relics of Academy strategy passed down through generations—to mark projected formations. The room smelled of parchment, expensive incense, and the desperate hope of a dying nation.</p>
<p>The Avatar sat among them, a perfect mirror of Rex’s intellect. It listened with flawless attentiveness, its head tilting at precisely the right moments.</p>
<p>When Valentina turned to it, asking for a critical assessment of troop distribution near the Key’s anchor point, the Avatar didn’t hesitate. It didn’t offer a rehearsed script; it offered a calculated deception.</p>
<p>"The anchor point is our most vulnerable nexus, but also our greatest opportunity," the Avatar suggested, its voice carrying a calm, reasoned authority that bypassed all suspicion. "If we concentrate our primary strike force there, we can secure the passage immediately upon activation."</p>
<p>"A cautious approach might leave us exposed to ambushes from the tunnels, but a decisive, overwhelming push through the anchor point will establish dominance before the Underlayer can react."</p>
<p>’Perfect,’ Rex thought in the darkness below, his lips curving into a cruel smile as he mirrored his own suggestion. ’Push harder... Overextend.’</p>
<p>’Walk right into the throat of the beast because you think you’re the one holding the knife. ’</p>
<p>The Avatar’s suggestion was a masterpiece of manipulation; it sounded thoughtful, prudent, and strategically sound. In reality, it was a gentle shove toward the exact kind of confident overextension that would make the trap inescapable.</p>
<p>Shortly after, Apollo took control of the tactical breakdown. His enthusiasm was infectious, bleeding into every word like wildfire.</p>
<p>For him, this wasn’t just war; it was a crusade, a chance to wash away the stains of the past with the blood of his enemies. His hands moved animatedly as he divided the assembled forces into three coordinated pillars of assault.</p>
<p>"First group the Spearhead!" Apollo declared, his voice ringing with conviction. "This will be my personal command."</p>
<p>"We advance directly through the key the moment it stabilizes."</p>
<p>"We drive hard, we hit them with everything we have, and we don’t stop until we’ve broken their center."</p>
<p>"My life affinity abilities will sustain us; we will be an inexhaustible tide that refuses to break against their defenses."</p>
<p>’An inexhaustible tide of meat,’ Rex mused privately, picturing Apollo’s radiant light being snuffed out by tons of crushing rock and the wet crunch of collapsing ribcages. ’I can already taste the irony of your life sustaining you while you drown in your own blood.’</p>
<p>"Second group," Valentina continued, her voice the steady anchor to Apollo’s fire, "will hold a secure position near the Key’s surface anchor."</p>
<p>"You are our lifeline. You will manage the retreat route and provide magical support from a safe distance."</p>
<p>"If the Spearhead falters, you are the wall they retreat behind."</p>
<p>"And the third group," Apollo added, "the Nightwing reserves, under Morwenna’s command."</p>
<p>"They will hold position further back, ready to reinforce either wing... We leave nothing to chance."</p>
<p>The Avatar nodded along to each division, offering measured, experienced input that sounded to every ear in the room except perhaps to the unnerved Elizabeth exactly like the strategic counsel Rex Rexilion would have given.</p>
<p>It was a symphony of deception played on two different stages: one of light and honor, one of shadow and slaughter.</p>
<p>The trap wasn’t just being set; it was being blessed by the very people who would die inside it.</p>
<p>Deep below, in the oppressive, violet-tinged darkness of Castle Nocturna’s throne room, Rex relayed the exact same information in near real time.</p>
<p>His consciousness was split between a predator with two sets of eyes. His awareness flowed between the two rooms with a terrifying fluency, a seamless synchronization that had become effortless after months of practicing this deadly duality.</p>
<p>One Rex spoke of heroism; the other spoke of execution.</p>
<p>"They’re dividing into three pillars of assault," Rex announced to the assembled leadership of the Underlayer.</p>
<p>His voice lacked any trace of surprise or urgency; it carried only the flat, chilling certainty of a man reading from a script he had already written. "The deception has taken hold perfectly."</p>
<p>"Apollo is leading the primary strike force through the Key, intoxicated by his own sense of purpose."</p>
<p>"Valentina is positioning herself at the anchor point to secure their retreat corridor."</p>
<p>"And Morwenna’s reserves are holding position in the rear, waiting for the signal to reinforce."</p>
<p>The air in the throne room seemed to grow colder as the implications settled. This wasn’t intelligence gathering; it was a countdown to a massacre.</p>
<p>Cassandra’s eyes narrowed, catching the tactical opening with the razor-sharp instincts of a veteran general.</p>
<p>"Then we don’t hesitate," she said, her voice cutting through the silence like a blade. "We let them commit."</p>
<p>"We let Apollo’s ’Spearhead’ push past the first defensive line until they are deep enough that their momentum becomes their prison."</p>
<p>"We wait until the tail of their formation passes our threshold, and then we seal the throat."</p>
<p>She leaned forward, her knuckles whitening against the obsidian table. "If Morwenna’s reserves realize the trap has sprung and try to push down to reinforce, they’ll find themselves fighting against time and stone."</p>
<p>"We let the first wave die in agony before the second wave even realizes they’re walking into a grave."</p>
<p>"Precisely," Rex confirmed, his dark eyes reflecting the cold calculation in her words.</p>
<p>Gorvasha stepped forward, her tusks gleaming in the dim light as her excitement began to manifest as a physical tremor. "But we won’t just seal them in, Cassandra."</p>
<p>"We will funnel them." The tusked woman’s voice grew husky with anticipation. "I’ve identified three specific choke points—narrow passages where the ceiling arches low and the walls constrict."</p>
<p>"If we trigger controlled collapses at these exact coordinates, we won’t just block the reinforcements; we will crush them."</p>
<p>She mimed the action with her clawed hands, her eyes wide and hungry. "Imagine it: the sound of their panicked screams as the stone ceiling descends, the wet, sickening crunch of armor flattening against ribcages, the spray of blood painting the walls as the passages become too narrow for anything but meat to pass through!"</p>
<p>"We will turn Morwenna’s ’rescue’ force into a secondary trap, layered directly atop the primary slaughter."</p>
<p>’I want to feel the vibrations through my boots,’ Gorvasha thought, her pulse hammering in her ears. ’I want to feel the moment the stone meets flesh and the screams turn into wet gurgles. ’</p>
<p>Throughout this exchange, Mordecai sat off to the side, his face pale in the unnatural light. He listened with a horrified, nervous fascination, the kind of fascination born from pure terror.</p>
<p>He had been a man of politics and half-measures, but he had never witnessed anything like this. He watched as Rex and his new inner circle discussed the systematic annihilation of thousands of lives not as a tragedy, but as a straightforward matter of geometry, timing, and structural integrity.</p>
<p>To them, this wasn’t war; it was a perfectly executed equation where the only variable was how much blood would be spilled before the equation was solved.</p>
