<p>Rex’s gaze didn’t just settle on her; it anchored her. He stood in the center of the decaying district, a man surrounded by ruin, yet appearing as the only stable point in a collapsing universe.</p>
<p>The air between them felt pressurized, thick with the scent of pulverized stone and the metallic tang of distant fires.</p>
<p>"Tonight, you are going to walk into the Grand Academy’s meeting room," Rex began. His voice wasn’t loud, but it carried the weight of a divine decree, cutting through the settling dust of the ruins.</p>
<p>"You are going to stand before the most powerful minds in the district, and you are going to strip them of their ignorance."</p>
<p>"You will tell them everything you know about the Legion’s structure."</p>
<p>"You will detail Celestina’s operational network, and you will lay bare the specific, terrifying capabilities she has been meticulously building for thirty years."</p>
<p>Ignivara felt the sheer scale of the command hit her like a physical blow. This wasn’t just an interrogation or a briefing; this was a declaration of war disguised as a lecture.</p>
<p>To reveal this much was to burn bridges, to set the very foundation of the old world on fire.</p>
<p>"Everything?" she asked. The word felt heavy in her mouth, tasting of salt and old secrets.</p>
<p>"Everything," Rex confirmed, his eyes narrowing, flashing with a cold, predatory intellect. "But there is a caveat."</p>
<p>"You will provide every scrap of truth that does not contain a single piece of actionable data that could be used to reconstruct the Legion’s network in a form that would benefit anyone in that room."</p>
<p>"You will give them the intelligence that dismantles the Legion... not the intelligence that allows them to replace it."</p>
<p>Ignivara stared at him, her mind racing through the labyrinth of her own memories. She saw the faces of the commanders she had served, the intricate web of spies and assassins, and the blood-soaked logistics of a thirty-year shadow war.</p>
<p>She saw the way Celestina moved pieces on a board, treating lives like disposable currency.</p>
<p>’He’s playing a game of surgical demolition,’ Ignivara realized, a chill tracing its way down her spine. ’He doesn’t want to give them a new weapon.’</p>
<p>’He wants to give them the knowledge of how they are being hunted, without giving them the means to hunt back.’</p>
<p>"You want them informed," she said, her voice dropping to a low, knowing murmur, "not empowered."</p>
<p>"I want them prepared," Rex countered instantly. There was no room for debate.</p>
<p>He stepped closer, his presence looming, the shadow of his silhouette swallowing the light. "There is a very specific kind of preparation that makes a network useful to me, a preparation born of desperation and a sudden, violent understanding of their own vulnerability."</p>
<p>"And then there is a different kind of preparation: the kind that makes them capable of operating independently, of building their own walls and their own armies."</p>
<p>He paused, the silence stretching, taut as a bowstring about to snap.</p>
<p>"The first kind is what tonight produces," Rex said, his voice turning as cold as the steel of a blade. "The kind that keeps them looking at the shadows, waiting for the strike, while they remain entirely dependent on the hand that guides them through the dark."</p>
<p>"And the second kind?" Ignivara asked, her golden eyes searching his, trying to find the limit of his ambition.</p>
<p>Rex looked through her, his gaze fixed on a horizon only he could see, a future built on the bones of the old order.</p>
<p>"It is not what tonight is for," he said.</p>
<p>Ignivara stood in the heavy silence, the weight of the upcoming performance settling into her bones like a slow-acting poison. She wasn’t just preparing a speech; she was preparing a battlefield of words where a single slip could turn a room of scholars into a room of executioners.</p>
<p>"The room is going to want the second kind," she said, her voice tight with the foresight of a veteran. "They won’t be satisfied with mere awareness."</p>
<p>"They are going to push and they’ll hunt for operational capacity."</p>
<p>"They’ll demand to know the Legion’s current field strength."</p>
<p>"They’ll want the exact coordinates of the remaining active operatives, and they’ll want to know how many hours it takes for Celestina to rebuild the Underlayer contact network if a cell is burned."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said. His response was a low, rhythmic pulse, a heartbeat of certainty that offered no comfort, only direction.</p>
<p>"And my task is to answer those questions," she continued, her mind already simulating the interrogation, "only up to the precise point where the answer stops being situational awareness and starts becoming an actionable plan."</p>
<p>"I have to feed them just enough to keep them hungry, but not enough to let them bite."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex repeated. He was a man of few words, but each one was a stone placed in a foundation.</p>
<p>"How do I know exactly where that line is?" she pressed, her golden eyes searching his for a hint of a margin for error. "How do we ensure the line doesn’t blur when the pressure mounts?"</p>
<p>Rex stepped into her personal space, his shadow eclipsing her. The air between them grew hot, charged with the intensity of a predator evaluating its most precious asset.</p>
<p>"You were a tier three operative for eight years, Ignivara," Rex said, his voice dropping to a gravelly, intimate resonance. "You have spent nearly a decade performing this exact dance with Celestina."</p>
<p>"You have mastered the art of providing intelligence to a principal in a way that informs without fully empowering."</p>
<p>"The only variable that has changed is the direction of the flow." He gripped her gaze, forcing her to look into the abyss of his intent. "You already know where the line is."</p>
<p>"You have been drawing it on the other side for eight years."</p>
<p>"You aren’t learning a new skill; you are simply switching sides of the blade."</p>
<p>Ignivara went still. The logic was brutal, undeniable, and terrifyingly efficient.</p>
<p>She felt the familiar hum of her designation’s perceptive architecture, the instinctive, rapid-fire cataloging of his microexpressions. She scanned the stillness of his pupils, the lack of a tremor in his hands, the absolute lack of hesitation in his soul.</p>
<p>’He sees me,’ she thought, a sudden, sharp realization cutting through her tactical mind. ’He isn’t just reading me.’</p>
<p>’He is looking at me as if the act of seeing is the easiest thing he has ever done.’</p>
<p>"The room will ask about you," she said, breaking the silence. The tension shifted, turning from the tactical to the personal. "When I walk into that chamber, the first thing they will do is look for the thread that connects me to the chaos."</p>
<p>"They will trace my path backward, through the ruins and the blood, and that path leads directly to you."</p>
<p>"You will be the epicenter of their suspicion."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, as if it were a triviality.</p>
<p>"What do I tell them about the tree line?" she asked, her voice dropping to a whisper. "What do they get to know about the moment the world shifted?"</p>
<p>"The truth," Rex said, his eyes unyielding as flint. "You were presented with a choice: a life of supervised residence under the Legion’s watchful, stifling eye or the uncertainty of a tree line."</p>
<p>"You performed a cold, calculated assessment of which option produced the most favorable outcome for your survival and your agency. That is an accurate, unassailable fact."</p>
<p>"It leaves out what you told me," she countered, her jaw tightening. "It leaves out the weight of the words you spoke when there was no one else to hear them."</p>
<p>"The content of our conversation in the tree line is irrelevant to the Academy’s operational concerns," Rex said, his tone turning clinical, dismissing the intimacy of the moment as if it were a waste of precious energy. "If they press you on the specifics, tell them we discussed the Legion’s recent operational failures and the specific intelligence you could provide in exchange for the terms I offered."</p>
<p>"Keep it dry. Keep it professional."</p>
<p>"That is accurate," she said, her voice regaining its soldier’s discipline.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"It is also incomplete," she added, her gaze unwavering, challenging him to acknowledge the hollow space left by his pragmatism.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He didn’t offer a comforting lie or a soft truth.</p>
<p>He simply stared back, a man who accepted the incompleteness of the world as the price of its control.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
