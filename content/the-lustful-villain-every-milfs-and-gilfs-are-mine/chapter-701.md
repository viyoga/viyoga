<p>Rex didn’t wait for her to recover. He didn’t even wait for her to reach him. As Kaelira surged forward, a living comet of heat and will, Rex simply tightened the reins of reality.</p>
<p>He increased the gravity field around himself, creating a localized zone of crushing density. The air itself seemed to thicken, turning into a viscous, invisible liquid that resisted her every movement.</p>
<p>She hit the edge of his field at full speed. The collision was silent but violent.</p>
<p>The resistance was immediate and absolute. Kaelira’s expression shifted from fierce determination to a mask of sheer strain in a fraction of a second.</p>
<p>She managed two more steps, her boots grinding into the cracked stone, before the weight differential became too much to bear. She stopped, her body trembling under the unnatural load.</p>
<p>Her flames flickered, the heat dimming as her body diverted every ounce of energy just to keep her lungs expanding against the crushing pressure. Rex watched her through the haze of dust, his eyes gleaming with a predatory intellect.</p>
<p>"As usual... glad to see that you’re finally well and relevant again with that Berserker class," Rex stated, his voice cutting through the heavy air with a cocky, analytical precision.</p>
<p>He didn’t look at her like a warrior; he looked at her like a puzzle. "The designation amplifies rage conversion."</p>
<p>"The more damage you take, the more output you produce. It’s a fascinating loop."</p>
<p>Kaelira glared at him through the shimmering heat distortion, her teeth gritted so hard it sounded like stone grinding against stone. She didn’t speak; she simply breathed, each inhalation a battle against the gravity that wanted to collapse her ribcage.</p>
<p>"That is genuinely impressive," Rex continued, his tone dripping with a mocking admiration. "But it also means you improve as the fight gets worse."</p>
<p>"And the rational play, from a strategic perspective, is to never let the fight get worse."</p>
<p>"Funny," Kaelira spat, the word sounding like it was being squeezed out of her throat. "Coming from the man who just tore the plaza apart for fun."</p>
<p>"It wasn’t just for fun," Rex replied, a small, arrogant smirk playing on his lips. "Well. Not entirely."</p>
<p>In a sudden, violent motion, Rex expanded the field. He pushed the gravity radius out to a four-meter circle and slammed the intensity up to eight times standard gravity.</p>
<p>The effect was brutal. Kaelira’s knees buckled.</p>
<p>With a sound like a heavy weight hitting the earth, she dropped to one knee. The ground beneath her shattered, a spiderweb of cracks blooming outward from her impact.</p>
<p>A small trickle of blood escaped her lip, a crimson stain against the dust, as the pressure forced the very fluid in her body to strive against the weight.</p>
<p>"Kaelira!" Apollo’s voice roared, a sound of pure agony and fury.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even blink. He stood there, the center of the storm, looking down at her with the calmness of a god watching an ant.</p>
<p>"Let her go!" Apollo commanded, his voice trembling with a controlled rage that was rapidly fraying.</p>
<p>He was stepping forward, his light flaring so bright it blinded the nearby soldiers.</p>
<p>"She is not in danger," Rex said, his voice flat and unyielding.</p>
<p>"She is on her knees under eight times gravity!" Apollo countered, his voice cracking with the strain of his emotions. "You are crushing her!"</p>
<p>"She is on one knee," Rex corrected, his tone almost insulting in its casualness. "She is holding it, as you can see."</p>
<p>’Most combatants would have had their bones splintered and their organs ruptured by now. But she is performing exceptionally well."</p>
<p>Apollo’s energy signature spiked so violently the air itself seemed to vibrate. "You don’t get to decide what is ’well’ for my people!"</p>
<p>"I am not deciding for them," Rex replied, his eyes locking onto Apollo’s with a defiant, cocky glare. "I am telling you what the reality is."</p>
<p>"If you want to argue with the laws of physics, be my guest. But don’t mistake my observation for disrespect."</p>
<p>Apollo’s face contorted. The light radiating from him was no longer a warm glow; it was a blinding, white-hot radiance that threatened to consume the plaza.</p>
<p>The tension between them was a physical force, a coiled spring ready to snap.</p>
<p>"You think this is a game?" Apollo hissed, his voice low and dangerous. "You think you can just walk in here and treat our lives like variables in your calculations?"</p>
<p>"I think," Rex said, his voice dropping to a low, challenging thrum, "that you are reacting with emotion where you should be reacting with logic."</p>
<p>"And in a fight, emotion is just another way to die."</p>
<p>Apollo’s light intensified, a blinding explosion of pure willpower that seemed to drown out the sun itself. The battle was no longer just about the golems or the gravity; it was a collision of two unstoppable forces, and the world was caught in the middle.</p>
<p>The air in the plaza didn’t just vibrate; it screamed. As Apollo’s light expanded, the very atmosphere seemed to undergo a biological scale reversal.</p>
<p>It was a terrifyingly beautiful display of power, a radiance so pure it felt like the reversal of entropy itself. Where the light touched the ground, dust didn’t just settle; it re-energized, dancing in a frenzy of unnatural vitality.</p>
<p>Rex stood at the center of this storm, his eyes narrowed. He could feel it.</p>
<p>The light was not just light; it was the presence of a god-like force. It was the ultimate counter to the decay of the world.</p>
<p>To anyone else, this energy would have been a blessing, a surge of life that would have made their hearts race and their veins throb with a renewed strength. But Rex knew the truth.</p>
<p>To be caught in the maw of this light was to be overwhelmed by the sheer force of life itself. But Rex was not a man of flesh and blood alone.</p>
<p>He grounded himself. Through the armor of his suit, he established a connection to the very foundation of the island.</p>
<p>The Earthen Authority surged through him, a river of stone and granite. He wasn’t just a man standing in the light; he was an extension of the crust of the planet.</p>
<p>He anchored his existence to the unyielding mass of the substrata. The stone was his shield, and the stone was his soul.</p>
<p>While the light sought to revitalize and reorder the organic, Rex had made himself a monument of the inorganic. He was an anomaly in the flow of Apollo’s power, a dense, unmoving weight of pure mineral will.</p>
<p>"That’s new," Rex said, his voice cutting through the hum of the radiance with the casualness of a man discussing the weather.</p>
<p>He looked at the blinding glare of Apollo’s aura with the clinical gaze of a scholar examining a new specimen. "You’ve gotten stronger since the canyon."</p>
<p>"Considerably stronger, actually. I’d say congratulations, but the look on your face tells me you’re not in the mood."</p>
<p>Apollo’s light flared, a pulse of pure gold that seemed to shudder the very air. The tension was so thick it felt like it could be sliced with a blade.</p>
<p>"We are not going to resolve this by talking," Rex continued, his voice dropping into a register of unshakeable certainty. "You have made that clear, and I have made it clear."</p>
<p>"The situation has a momentum now that talking cannot redirect."</p>
<p>"Then what do you want?" Apollo demanded, his voice a low thrum of controlled fury.</p>
<p>The light around him was no longer a glow; it was a predator, waiting to strike.</p>
<p>"The same thing I wanted before this began," Rex replied, his posture as rigid as the stone he commanded. "I want the Apostle network to understand that the underlayer is no longer the prey."</p>
<p>"And I want every reincarnator on this island to have the choice that the network is currently stealing from them."</p>
<p>Apollo’s eyes burned with a fierce intensity. "And you think the way to do that is to fight me?"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. He didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>He simply looked at the man across the shattered plaza with a flat, unyielding honesty. "Yes."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was more violent than the clash of swords. It was a heavy silence, a void in which the world seemed to hold its breath, waiting to fill.</p>
<p>"That’s it?" Apollo asked, his voice laced with a bitter disbelief. "Just... yes?"</p>
<p>"No grand speech about the fate of the world? No philosophical justification about the balance of the cosmos? You just... decide to fight?"</p>
<p>"Would it change anything if I gave you one?" Rex countered, a trace of a smirk playing on his lips. It was a defiant, cocky expression that mocked the very gravity of the moment.</p>
<p>Apollo didn’t answer. He couldn’t.</p>
<p>The weight of his own power, the pressure of his resolve, and the sheer intensity of the moment seemed to choke the words from his throat. He merely stared, his jaw tight, his eyes burning with a light that could melt stone.</p>
<p>"Then let’s not waste time," Rex said, his voice turning as cold as the deep earth.</p>
<p>He shifted his weight, the armor of his body creaking with the anticipation of the clash. "I’ve got a busy day."</p>
<p>The challenge was thrown. The stage was set.</p>
<p>The very air seemed to shudder in anticipation of the collision that was about to shatter the world.</p>
