<p>The implication of his words hung in the air, heavy and blood red. He wasn’t just offering a source; he was offering them the keys to the enemy’s armory, a chance to turn the Legion’s own surgical precision against them.</p>
<p>The tension in the room shifted from the fear of internal collapse to the electric, terrifying thrill of a counterstrike.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for their approval, and he didn’t ask for it. He stood there, a pillar of cold, hard logic in a room currently drowning in the heat of suspicion, and swept his gaze across the assembled leaders.</p>
<p>He looked at them not as peers but as assets that needed to be recalibrated.</p>
<p>"That is the only information that carries weight in this room right now," Rex declared, his voice cutting through the rising murmurs like a blade through silk. "What we suspect about one another is a luxury we can no longer afford."</p>
<p>"Suspicion is a parasite; it feeds on time and leaves nothing but rot."</p>
<p>"We are currently wasting our most precious resource on shadows when we have a lighthouse standing right in front of us."</p>
<p>He leaned forward, his hands gripping the edge of the obsidian table, his knuckles white.</p>
<p>"We are talking about a woman who spent eight years in the belly of the beast."</p>
<p>"Eight years breathing their air, learning their rhythms, and seeing the true, unvarnished face of the Legion."</p>
<p>"She has decided that what she saw there requires a different response than blind obedience."</p>
<p>"Whether she is a convert or a survivor is secondary to the fact that she holds the blueprint of our destruction in her mind."</p>
<p>The divination representative leaned back, her eyes narrowing into slits of pure skepticism. "You are asking us to gamble our collective security on a whim, Rex."</p>
<p>"You believe she has genuinely turned. You believe her loyalty has shifted from the Legion to us."</p>
<p>"I don’t believe in loyalty," Rex countered, his voice dropping into a low, dangerous register. "Loyalty is a human emotion, and emotions are volatile."</p>
<p>"I believe in data... I believe she has the information we need to survive the next decade."</p>
<p>"Whether her ’turn’ is a genuine change of heart or a masterful tactical maneuver is a question the intelligence itself will answer."</p>
<p>"Accurate intelligence about an organization’s structure is self-verifying... If her maps of their network match our intercepted signals, if her timelines of their movements align with our losses, then the information is accurate regardless of whether she loves us or simply wants to live."</p>
<p>"And if it is inconsistent?" Morwenna interjected, her voice sharp, her eyes searching his for any hint of a lie. "If she feeds us a beautiful lie designed to lead us into a slaughter?"</p>
<p>"Then we will know that, too," Rex said, a grim, almost predatory smile touching his lips. "And a failed lie is still a piece of intelligence."</p>
<p>"It tells us exactly what the Legion wants us to believe. Even a poisoned well tells you where the enemy is digging."</p>
<p>"There is a profound difference," the Knowledge System Holder interjected, his voice slow, heavy, and dripping with a cautious, intellectual dread, "between a source whose motivations are shrouded in mystery and a source who descended upon this continent on the back of a dragon with the explicit, scorched earth intent to reduce this city to ash."</p>
<p>The room seemed to grow colder at the mention of it. The memory of the fire, the screams, and the smell of burning stone was still too fresh in their minds.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, meeting the man’s gaze with a steady, unblinking ferocity. "There is a difference. But in the second category, the dragon rider is a person who failed her operational objective."</p>
<p>"She is a predator who lost her prey."</p>
<p>"She is currently in our custody, she is stripped of her fire, and she has every reason to realize that the Legion’s promises are as hollow as the bones of the men they leave behind in the dirt."</p>
<p>"She has the institutional knowledge of a master architect, and we are starving for it."</p>
<p>He tightened his grip on the table, the tension in the room reaching a fever pitch, a coiled spring ready to snap.</p>
<p>"The Legion does not allow for clean failures," Rex continued, his voice rising in a crescendo of brutal pragmatism. "When an operation of this magnitude collapses, the operatives who ran it aren’t greeted with medals; they are viewed with suspicion."</p>
<p>"They are the broken tools. Ignivara is not going back to Celestina."</p>
<p>"She is a woman without a country, a soldier without a war."</p>
<p>"The question we must answer is this: Do we bring her into this room and let her bleed the truth to us tonight? Or do we spend the next several months, and the lives of countless more of our people, trying to piece together an intelligence picture that she is currently holding in the palm of her hand?"</p>
<p>He let the ultimatum hang there, heavy and suffocating, leaving the council to choose between the terrifying gamble of a traitor’s word and the slow, agonizing death of their own ignorance.</p>
<p>The Knowledge System Holder stared down at the dark, polished surface of the table, his eyes tracing the grain as if searching for a mathematical proof to settle the chaos in his mind. He said nothing.</p>
<p>In the lexicon of this council, silence was not an absence of thought; it was a heavy, calculated concession. It was the sound of a man swallowing his pride to make room for a dangerous truth.</p>
<p>"Accountability for Lady Valentina," the divination representative broke the silence, her voice sharp, a jagged edge of steel cutting through the gloom.</p>
<p>She wasn’t letting them off the hook. "The truth about the intelligence leak, the breach, the suspicion... does not simply evaporate because we have found a new operational priority."</p>
<p>"We cannot trade justice for advantage without a cost."</p>
<p>"It does not disappear," Rex countered, his voice a low, rhythmic drumbeat of certainty.</p>
<p>He didn’t flinch under her scrutiny. "It merely follows the proper sequence."</p>
<p>"The intelligence comes first. The accountability follows. Not because Valentina’s choices were trivial, but because the weight of her decisions can only be measured once we have the full scale."</p>
<p>"We cannot judge the depth of the wound until we know how large the blade truly is, and Ignivara currently holds the blade."</p>
<p>The weight of his gaze shifted, moving like a physical force until it landed on Valentina. The air between them seemed to thicken, the tension so palpable it felt as though a single misplaced word might cause the very foundations of the room to crack.</p>
<p>"Will you allow me to bring her in?" Rex asked, his voice dropping to a level of raw, unvarnished intensity. "Tonight... Right here... In this room..."</p>
<p>"Let the truth be laid bare on this table."</p>
<p>Valentina didn’t answer immediately. She sat motionless, her silhouette framed by the dim, flickering light of the chamber.</p>
<p>For a moment, the mask slipped. The composed, untouchable administrator, the woman who moved through the world with the effortless grace of a high mage, was gone.</p>
<p>In her place was someone far more human: someone exhausted, someone weathered by the sudden, violent realization that her world was built on shifting sands.</p>
<p>There was a profound honesty in her eyes, a weary clarity that spoke of a woman who had been standing on the precipice of a great, dark void and had finally found a hand to hold.</p>
<p>"Accountability after," she said.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question. It wasn’t a plea for mercy.</p>
<p>It was a command, a precise and surgical confirmation. She was marking the territory of her own judgment, ensuring that while the mission took precedence, the reckoning would not be forgotten.</p>
<p>"After," Rex echoed, a solemn vow.</p>
<p>Valentina turned her gaze to the room, her eyes sweeping over the faces of her peers. She looked at Morwenna, whose expression had shifted from suspicion to a heavy, forty-year weight of silent understanding.</p>
<p>She looked at Apollo, whose face had transitioned from the jagged, bleeding edge of shock to the cold, patient stillness of a man preparing for a long war. Finally, she looked at the knowledge system holder and the divination representative, two people who had not yet embraced the gamble but had accepted the necessity of it.</p>
<p>"Yes," she said. The word was short, final, and carried the gravity of a tectonic shift.</p>
<p>Rex stood, the movement decisive and sharp. The chair scraped against the floor, a harsh, grating sound in the quiet.</p>
<p>"Elizabeth," he said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth rose in unison with him, her movements fluid and purposeful.</p>
<p>Together, they turned and walked toward the heavy doors of the meeting room, their footsteps echoing like the rhythmic march of soldiers heading toward a breach.</p>
<p>As the doors began to groan shut, Rex heard the atmosphere of the room undergo a fundamental transformation. The frantic, fractured energy of suspicion hadn’t vanished; it hadn’t even fully healed, but it had settled.</p>
<p>The overlapping murmurs of doubt hadn’t returned; instead, there was a singular, focused tension. The council was no longer a collection of bickering factions; it had become a coiled spring, a group of people who had been given a direction and were now waiting, breathless, for the impact.</p>
<p>The edges were still broken, and the wounds were still weeping, but the room was functional.</p>
<p>For Rex, in the dark corridor outside, that was enough.</p>
