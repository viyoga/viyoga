<p>Rex finished dressing, the crisp fabric of his clothes a stark contrast to the raw intimacy of the room. He crossed back to the bed one last time, leaning down to press a brief, almost businesslike kiss to her forehead.</p>
<p>It was a gesture devoid of passion, the kind of cold acknowledgment a commander might offer a trusted subordinate before sending them into the field, and yet, as her eyelids fluttered closed at the contact, something in her shuddering breath suggested she read it as something far more intimate, something that promised possession rather than mere alliance.</p>
<p>"Rest today," he commanded, his voice dropping into that low, undeniable register. "Let your household see you calm, unshaken, and in absolute control."</p>
<p>"Whatever you decide to tell them about last night is your prerogative, though I would recommend telling them nothing at all."</p>
<p>"Let them drown in their own assumptions... Uncertainty is a more effective weapon than any lie you could manufacture."</p>
<p>Morwenna opened her eyes, the pupils dilated, dark pools of calculation and something else, something darker.</p>
<p>"And if Theo’s ghost comes up in conversation?" she asked, her voice gaining a razor-edged steadiness. "If the servants whisper, or the nobles demand to know why the matriarch of House Nightwing has suddenly found peace with the very man half the city claims tore her family apart?"</p>
<p>Rex straightened, already turning toward the door, his mind already pivoting toward the next objective. "Then you tell them that peace is a luxury you have earned after thirty years of holding this crumbling empire together alone."</p>
<p>"Tell them that grief doesn’t require permanent residence in every room of this house." He paused at the threshold, glancing back over his shoulder with eyes that held no remorse. "You’re clever enough to manage your own household’s perception, Morwenna."</p>
<p>"That was never in question... What was in question was which direction you would point that cleverness."</p>
<p>"Now, you’ve chosen."</p>
<p>She said nothing more as he exited, but in that final, fleeting glimpse before the heavy oak doors swung shut, he caught the faint, almost imperceptible shift in her expression—the ghost of something that might have eventually grown into a genuine smile, had she not already traded her soul for survival.</p>
<p>Walking through the quiet, opulent corridors of the Nightwing estate as the household began to stir like waking predators around him, Rex allowed himself a private moment of cold satisfaction.</p>
<p>Eighteen maximum bonds had become nineteen. The most resistant, most politically dangerous woman in the entire Apostle network had not just folded; she had integrated.</p>
<p>With her surrender, the last structural resistance the Nightwing household could have posed to his grand design had evaporated.</p>
<p>His thoughts drifted briefly to Theo. He remembered the wet crunch of cartilage and the way the boy’s blood had sprayed across the cobblestones—a messy, visceral end to a lineage that had failed to adapt.</p>
<p>He thought about the version of this story where the boy might have grown into something formidable, someone capable of protecting his family through strength rather than watching it get swallowed by Rex’s ambition.</p>
<p>But that version of reality had been excised weeks ago, a failed variable eliminated with brutal efficiency. Rex felt no guilt, only the clinical satisfaction of a problem solved.</p>
<p>He was already three steps into orchestrating the next move, planning how to leverage Morwenna’s compliance to seize control of the household’s private military assets under his informal authority as acting combat director when the pull came.</p>
<p>It was different this time. Every previous summons to Lustia’s domain had arrived like a barbed hook through his ribs, a violent, nauseating yank that left him gasping for air and disoriented.</p>
<p>This summons, however, lacked the violence. It felt like sinking into warm, viscous water. The world around him didn’t shatter; it softened.</p>
<p>The marble walls of the estate bled into gold and deep, arterial crimson light, dissolving the physical world until there was nothing left but the approaching presence of something ancient, hungry, and inevitable.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Rex opened his eyes in Lustia’s domain, and for the first time in what felt like an eternity, the immediate sensation wasn’t pain.</p>
<p>There was no phantom ache in his ribs, no burning in his lungs from inhaling divine essence, and no grinding agony in his joints. There was only a terrifying, unnatural stillness.</p>
<p>The chamber remained, as it always was, an impossible monument to decadence. Ornate golden pillars spiraled upward toward a ceiling that defied every law of physics and architecture, stretching into an infinite amber void.</p>
<p>Deep, arterial red silk draped across low, curved furniture that served no practical purpose other than to scream of excess. The entire space hummed with a low-frequency vibration, a warmth that felt less like heat and more like the pulse of a living thing.</p>
<p>But something had shifted. The atmosphere wasn’t heavy with her usual languid boredom.</p>
<p>Lustia herself was different.</p>
<p>She wasn’t reclining on her divan in a haze of pleasure and indifference; she was standing. Her posture was straight, her shoulders squared, her eyes burning with a brightness that Rex found disconcerting.</p>
<p>It wasn’t just interest. It was pride.</p>
<p>"There he is," she said.</p>
<p>The lazy, teasing drawl that usually clung to her words like honey had evaporated. Her voice was immediate, sharp, and warm.</p>
<p>"The god slayer himself."</p>
<p>Rex straightened from where he had landed, his boots silent on the plush crimson floor. His body felt foreign to him—too quiet, too functional.</p>
<p>"That’s an exaggeration," he said flatly, though his mind was still replaying the visceral memory of Kaeleth’s divine blood spraying across his face like molten gold. "He’s alive."</p>
<p>"Diminished, but alive."</p>
<p>"Alive and utterly humiliated," Lustia countered, stepping into his space. "And for a being like Kaeleth, humiliation is a wound that never heals."</p>
<p>"It is a slow death sentence." She began to circle him, her movements fluid and predatory, the way a master craftsman might circle a finished blade to inspect the edge. "Do you even comprehend what you accomplished?"</p>
<p>Rex remained motionless, but his inner mind was racing, cataloguing the residual adrenaline.</p>
<p>’What I accomplished was survival,’ he thought bitterly. ’Survival through slaughter.’</p>
<p>"A god who has held dominion over time itself for two thousand years," Lustia continued, her voice growing more animated, almost reverent. "A being who has never once in two millennia faced a challenge he couldn’t simply erase from the timeline and rewrite with a flick of his wrist."</p>
<p>"And you? You didn’t just defeat him... You adapted." Her eyes flashed as she leaned closer, her breath warm against his ear. "You took his throne apart with your bare hands, Rex."</p>
<p>"You tore through his temporal defenses like they were wet parchment, and then you walked out of his own domain carrying a fragment of his authority like a trophy." She broke into a rich, delighted laugh that echoed off the golden walls like shattering glass. "I have been alive since before this world had a name, Rex, and I have never witnessed anything quite as satisfying as that carnage."</p>
<p>Rex’s jaw tightened. "You watched it?"</p>
<p>"Every second." She sank onto one of the low couches with exaggerated grace, gesturing for him to join her.</p>
<p>Rex remained standing, his muscles coiled, his instincts screaming that even in this sanctuary, he was never truly off guard. "I couldn’t interfere; the domain’s laws are absolute, but I saw everything."</p>
<p>"I saw the blood, the broken bones, the way you tore through his reality."</p>
<p>Her expression softened into something almost scholarly, though the hunger in her eyes remained. "The moment you realized what Full Potential Adaptation meant against a temporal specialist when you stopped fighting his time and started fighting with it, I nearly lost my composure."</p>
<p>She leaned forward, her gaze locking onto his with terrifying intensity. "Do you understand how rare that is?"</p>
<p>"To watch a mortal, even one as enhanced as you, systematically dismantle a god’s entire toolkit through pure, iterative exposure?"</p>
<p>"You didn’t just win a fight, Rex. You rewrote the rules of engagement."</p>
<p>"That kind of thing gets talked about. Quietly, in the spaces between heartbeats, in places you’ll never hear directly, but the echoes are spreading."</p>
