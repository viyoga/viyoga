<p>A wave of raw, primal despair rolled through the plaza like a physical tide. Some citizens collapsed to their knees, weeping silently into their hands; others clung to one another with a desperate, white-knuckled grip, as if physical contact could anchor them to a world that had just been torn apart.</p>
<p>In one corner of the square, the remnants of Aethelgard’s leadership—Valentina, Morwenna, Elliot, and Evelyn—stood like statues carved from grief. They watched the announcement not with outrage, but with a hollow, bone-deep resignation.</p>
<p>Their spirits had been broken long before their armies; they were simply witnessing the funeral of their identity.</p>
<p>Xerollion observed the terror, but he did not relish it with cruelty. Instead, he wielded it with the terrifying precision of a surgeon.</p>
<p>"I have not come to destroy what you have built," he continued.</p>
<p>His voice shifted. The thunderous authority retreated, replaced by something smoother, almost velvet, with a tone that sounded less like a conqueror and more like a savior offering salvation.</p>
<p>"Your lives will continue."</p>
<p>"Your markets will open."</p>
<p>"Your children will learn."</p>
<p>"Your families will remain intact."</p>
<p>"What changes is simply the hand that guides the scales."</p>
<p>The crowd stirred uncertainly. It was a poisoned honey, a promise of normalcy wrapped around the throat of their sovereignty.</p>
<p>Xerollion turned his head slightly toward the edge of the platform. "Mordecai."</p>
<p>Mordecai Vrael flinched at the call. He stepped forward, his boots clicking nervously against the wood. He looked pale, his throat working visibly as he swallowed hard.</p>
<p>He was a man being thrust into a role he had never asked for, a puppet being strung up before the audience had even finished screaming.</p>
<p>"Mordecai Vrael will remain the public face of this new administration," Xerollion announced, his voice carrying that deceptive calm. "He will oversee the transition between the Underlayer and Aethelgard."</p>
<p>"He will work alongside what remains of your own functioning structures, including Valentina Von Starlight, who will serve as your liaison."</p>
<p>At the mention of her name, Valentina lifted her head. For one heartbeat, her eyes locked onto the impassive mask of the conqueror across the plaza.</p>
<p>In that moment, a silent war passed between them, the last ember of defiance meeting the infinite void of inevitability.</p>
<p>Then, she broke. She averted her gaze, her jaw tightening as she stared at the blood-stained cobblestones.</p>
<p>Mordecai nodded repeatedly, a frantic, jerky motion as he tried to project a confidence he clearly did not possess. He straightened his spine, forcing his shoulders back, attempting to mimic the posture of a statesman while his hands trembled at his sides.</p>
<p>"New regulations will be established in the coming days," Xerollion pressed on, the velvet in his voice thinning just enough to reveal the steel beneath. "Taxes, resource allocation, and contributions from Aethelgard will be redirected to strengthen the combined structure of our two worlds."</p>
<p>"Let me be clear: compliance is not optional."</p>
<p>"Anyone who violates these new laws will face consequences considerably more severe than anything you witnessed today."</p>
<p>Thump. Thump. Thump.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was not empty; it was pressurized. It was a heavy, suffocating thing that made it difficult to draw breath.</p>
<p>Xerollion let it linger, letting the implication of his threat sink into their bones. He wanted them to feel the fragility of their own lives.</p>
<p>He wanted them to understand that their survival was now a gift he chose to grant.</p>
<p>"I understand what many of you are feeling right now," Xerollion said, his tone softening further.</p>
<p>He sounded almost paternal now—wise, understanding, and reasonable. It was the most terrifying thing he could have done. It made his conquest feel like a natural law rather than a massacre.</p>
<p>"You feel as though your world has ended. But I promise you, it has not."</p>
<p>He spread his hands slightly, an open gesture that felt like a trap.</p>
<p>"What ended today was merely a particular arrangement of power, an old system that was never going to last forever, regardless of what happened in that corridor."</p>
<p>"Every kingdom rises, and every kingdom eventually answers to something larger than itself."</p>
<p>"Today, Aethelgard simply learned what that something is."</p>
<p>Xerollion let the silence fester. He watched the crowd with the detached curiosity of a scientist observing a chemical reaction, waiting for the last echoes of their resistance to dissolve into the heavy air.</p>
<p>When he finally spoke again, his voice lacked the thunder of a god; it now carried something far more dangerous: calculated mercy.</p>
<p>"Mourn what you lost today," he said, the words landing like soft stones on graves. "Grieve your fallen."</p>
<p>"But do not let that grief become an excuse for foolish resistance that will only add more names to a list that is already far too long."</p>
<p>"Aethelgard will endure. It will breathe, it will grow, and it will persist."</p>
<p>"Only... under new leadership."</p>
<p>The speech ended not with a bang, but with the terrifying finality of a closing tomb.</p>
<p>Without waiting for applause, which would never come, Xerollion stepped down from the platform. He left Mordecai standing there like a sacrificial lamb, already surrounded by Underlayer representatives who began barking orders and coordinating the first administrative shackles of the new regime.</p>
<p>The conqueror did not linger to witness the aftermath of his words. He turned his back on the plaza and began to move, cutting through the periphery of the crowd with a predator’s grace.</p>
<p>He navigated the labyrinthine streets of the city, moving away from the open terror of the plaza toward the wounded grandeur of the Grand Academy.</p>
<p>The building stood as a scarred monument to Aethelgard’s former pride, its marble walls cracked, its stained glass shattered into glittering teeth that littered the ground.</p>
<p>He found what he needed: a narrow, shadowed alcove behind one of the Academy’s still-standing eastern wings.</p>
<p>Here, hidden by crumbling masonry and the debris of war, he could finally cease to be a god.</p>
<p>The moment he slipped into the shadows, the performance shattered.</p>
<p>Vrrr shhhhh...</p>
<p>A low, harmonic hum vibrated through the air as the violet-black energy of the Mark of Lust Authority began to retract. The oppressive, divine weight that had been crushing the atmosphere around him started to bleed away.</p>
<p>The terrifying, impassive mask of Xerollion, the symbol of absolute inevitability, began to dissolve. The magical construct liquefied like melting wax, bleeding away into the ether, revealing the true flesh beneath.</p>
<p>The transformation moved through him in a wave of settling warmth, a visceral release that felt like exhaling after holding a breath for an eternity.</p>
<p>Rex Rexilion emerged from the shell. His face was pale, lined with the genuine exhaustion of a man who had just orchestrated a world-ending event, but he was whole.</p>
<p>His breathing was ragged, his lungs burning with the effort of maintaining the illusion. Behind him, somewhere far below in the blood-soaked throat of the corridor, the ’Xerollion’ construct lay as nothing more than flickering, failing light amidst the corpses and broken bones.</p>
<p>Rex slid down the rough stone wall, his spine grinding against the masonry as he sank to the ground. He leaned his head back against the academy’s wall, closing his eyes.</p>
<p>’Fuck,’ he thought, the inner monologue sharp and raw. ’That was too close.’</p>
<p>’The pressure... it’s like my soul was being stretched across the entire city.’</p>
<p>He stayed there for several minutes, letting the blessed silence of the alcove wrap around him like a shroud. He needed to recalibrate. He needed to shed the god and become the survivor.</p>
<p>Slowly, he began to prepare for the next act. He reached down, touching the shallow cuts on his arms—wounds he had deliberately allowed to linger, wounds that burned with a real, stinging pain.</p>
<p>They were his most important props. He would emerge from this alley not as the invincible conqueror, but as the exhausted, battle-worn hero who had barely scraped through hell.</p>
<p>He would walk with a deliberate limp, his gait unsteady, his eyes carrying the haunted stare of a man who had seen too much.</p>
<p>He needed to give them a story they could live with. Because a city that has lost its gods needs a man to believe in, even if that man is the one who killed them.</p>
