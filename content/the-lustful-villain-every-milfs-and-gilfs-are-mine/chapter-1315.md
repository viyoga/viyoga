<p>The back garden of the Starlight household had ceased to be a sanctuary; it had become a slaughterhouse of architecture.</p>

<p>What was once a place of manicured beauty and gentle whispers now resembled a battlefield where two gods had collided and failed to finish the job.</p>

<p>Shallow, jagged craters pocked the lawn like fresh wounds, stretching in a chaotic trail from the shattered marble colonnade to the dried, cracked bed of the pond.</p>

<p>Between these craters stood the grotesque stumps of ancient marble pillars, sliced clean through at the waist as if by an invisible blade of impossible sharpness.</p>

<p>In a dozen places across the scorched earth, thin tongues of violet fire still licked at the air fire that burned without fuel, flickering with a hungry, unnatural intelligence that defied the laws of combustion.</p>

<p>Smoke drifted lazily through the gaps in the ruined walls, carrying the bitter taste of ash and pulverized stone.</p>

<p>But beneath that heavy haze ran something sharper, more metallic: the acrid, stinging scent of ionized air that lingers immediately after a lightning strike has torn through the atmosphere.</p>

<p>Rex stood at the epicenter of this devastation. The double spiral of his Dual Sin Authority writhed around his body like two serpents locked in a lethal embrace.</p>

<p>The crimson threads wove through the violet streams in a hypnotic, deadly braid, pulsing in rhythm with his own heartbeat. Even now, his body was a factory of reconstruction.</p>

<p>A last stubborn rib gave a wet, sickening click as it forced itself back into place beneath his shirt, and the angry crimson burn along his forearm faded to a tender pink before vanishing entirely into smooth, flawless skin.</p>

<p>His eyes remained fixed, unblinking and predatory, locked onto the girl ten meters away.</p>

<p>’She’s not slowing down,’ Rex thought, his mind analyzing the minute tremors in the air. ’Her output is increasing.’</p>

<p>’She’s not just channeling lightning; she’s becoming a conduit for something deeper.’</p>

<p>’This is the real Evelyn.’</p>

<p>Evelyn Astralight held herself with the terrifying tension of a drawn bowstring, coiled and ready to snap. The blinding white lightning that had been crawling over her now surged in violent waves, wrapping her arms and legs in a living net of crackling energy.</p>

<p>Sparks snapped off her knuckles like miniature stars, and every time she shifted her weight, the grass beneath her boots exploded in tiny puffs of smoke and fire. Her hair floated upward in an electrified halo, dancing in the unseen currents of her own power.</p>

<p>She had stopped crying somewhere in the transition from grief to fury if she had ever allowed herself the weakness of tears at all. Her face had settled into something terrifyingly plain, something steady and hollow.</p>

<p>It was a mask of pure intent, a face stripped of all human emotion, leaving behind something far more dangerous than rage: absolute, unwavering resolve.</p>

<p>"Don’t blink, Evelyn," Rex whispered to himself, his lips curling upward. "Because if you do, you might miss the moment your world ends."</p>

<p>The air between them grew impossibly heavy, the pressure building until the very stones beneath their feet began to groan and hairline fractures spiderwebbed outward.</p>

<p>The silence was no longer empty; it was a held breath, a countdown written in lightning and sin.</p>

<p>At the frayed edges of the devastation, where the garden met the encroaching shadows of the Underlayer forces, Cassandra stood like a coiled viper. Her fingers clamped around the hilt of her crimson sword with such white knuckled force that her knuckles turned ghostly.</p>

<p>Her knees bent deeply, tendons standing out like steel cables along her thighs. She was a heartbeat away from launching herself across the cratered wreckage, her blade halfway freed from its scabbard, ready to carve a path of vengeance through Rex’s back.</p>

<p>Behind her, the entire line of Underlayer soldiers leaned forward in unison, a wave of murderous intent poised to crash forward at her signal.</p>

<p>Rex didn’t even turn his head. Without breaking eye contact with Evelyn, he simply lifted a single finger.</p>

<p>The effect was instantaneous and supernatural. It was as if the very air around Cassandra had solidified into invisible granite.</p>

<p>She froze mid-lunge, her momentum cut off by an unseen barrier that made her muscles scream in protest. She hung there for several agonizing seconds, her jaw working with suppressed fury, her breath coming in ragged, frustrated gasps.</p>

<p>Then, with a shuddering exhale of defeat, she eased her blade back into its sheath.</p>

<p>Clink.</p>

<p>The sound of metal meeting leather echoed strangely in the charged air.</p>

<p>Beside her, the massive demon Gorvasha folded her thick arms across her chest, her amber eyes narrowed in grudging respect.</p>

<p>Lilith, standing nearby, had stopped breathing altogether, her entire being suspended in the terrifying tension between two titans.</p>

<p>Inside the protective ring of stone that Rex had hastily conjured around the wounded, a barrier of jagged, obsidian-like rock, the Starlight family waited in a state of suspended trauma.</p>

<p>The scene was one of quiet horror. Helena lay on the grass, half hidden beneath the blood-stained fabric of Leonidas’s traveling cloak.</p>

<p>Her face was swollen, mottled with purple bruises, and her chest rose in shallow, terrifyingly uneven gasps.</p>

<p>Beside her, Leonidas lay broken and unconscious; his arm was a mangled ruin of flesh and bone, now bound in crude, makeshift strips torn from Elizabeth’s own sleeve.</p>

<p>Lily knelt at her mother’s head, her small hands shaking violently as they hovered near Helena’s temple. Diana knelt at her father’s side, her fingers pressed against his pulse point, her face pale as death.</p>

<p>Both children were frozen mid-motion, paralyzed by the sheer scale of the violence that had erupted around them. They didn’t dare look up; they couldn’t bear to see the monster that had done this to their world.</p>

<p>Aisella remained bent over Helena, her face slick with sweat. Soft, emerald green light poured from her palms in steady streams, fighting against the darkness of Helena’s injuries.</p>

<p>She worked in a state of hyper-focused silence, each pulse of magic a desperate prayer against mortality.</p>

<p>Behind them, Valentina and Elizabeth stood as silent sentinels at the rear of the circle, their faces drawn tight with fear and exhaustion.</p>

<p>Elliot was not there to witness this nightmare. He remained trapped in the sterile confines of a medical tent in another district, strapped to a cot with bandages wrapped around his skull.</p>

<p>Evelyn had felt it; she had felt the seismic shift in the magical pressure rolling across Aethelgard like a tsunami.</p>

<p>She had felt Rex’s power vibrating in her teeth and grinding against her bones, and she had fled, leaving him in the hands of two guards before anyone could even ask what the hell was happening.</p>

<p>Rex watched the trembling children.</p>

<p>He watched the desperate healers.</p>

<p>He watched the fury in Evelyn’s eyes.</p>

<p>Then, he deliberately let the silence stretch.</p>

<p>He wanted them to feel it. He wanted the weight of the quiet to press down on them like a tombstone.</p>

<p>He wanted them to sit in the unbearable tension, to listen to the crackle of Evelyn’s lightning and the distant drip of blood, knowing that when the silence finally broke, it would be the sound of their world shattering forever.</p>
