<p>Rex turned his head to meet her gaze, his eyes shining as polished obsidian. There was no anger in his expression, only the flat, dangerous stillness of a predator who had just been given a new variable to calculate.</p>
<p>"I know what I am doing," he said.</p>
<p>The words weren’t defensive; they were a statement of fact, delivered with the absolute certainty of a man who had already rehearsed his own death a thousand times.</p>
<p>"I know you do," Lustia countered smoothly, her voice unruffled. "But you need to understand something: this is the first time you have been operating with a deadline that is defined by someone else’s timeline rather than your own."</p>
<p>"You are no longer the sole architect of this catastrophe."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He acknowledged the truth of it with a single, slow nod.</p>
<p>The reality of it settled in his gut like cold lead. He was used to being the storm, the unpredictable variable that crashed through systems.</p>
<p>Now, he was being asked to dance to a rhythm he hadn’t composed.</p>
<p>"That is going to happen more than once before this is finished, isn’t it?" Rex asked, his mind already beginning to map out the contingencies. "Kaeleth is not the only piece on this board that moves without waiting for my permission."</p>
<p>"No," Lustia said, her expression darkening. "He is not."</p>
<p>"Celestina has her own clock, ticking with lethal precision..."</p>
<p>"The other gods watching this situation have theirs, each waiting for a crack in the foundation."</p>
<p>"Even I am operating on a schedule I do not fully control." She paused, a flicker of something resembling genuine strain crossing her features. "Whatever counter frequency I used to break the working drew attention in the divine layer. Attention I am still desperately trying to manage the consequences of."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyebrows shot up, a rare crack in his composure. "You never mentioned that."</p>
<p>"I am mentioning it now," Lustia said with a sharp, almost defensive edge. "I did not want to add another burden to your pile while you were still holding me by the throat."</p>
<p>"It seemed like poor timing."</p>
<p>Rex let out a breath that might have been a laugh if there were any humor left in him. "That is fair."</p>
<p>He meant it, and he noted the mild surprise at his own capacity for concession. He was learning that in this game of gods, pragmatism was more valuable than pride.</p>
<p>"The God of Time," Rex said, shifting the focus back to the threat. "His name was Kaeleth, you say... it doesn’t sound godly at all."</p>
<p>"Indeed... Kaeleth," Lustia answered. "That is his designation in the old language the divine framework uses."</p>
<p>"He has occupied this position for approximately two thousand years. In that span, he has produced three apostles, all of whom are dead." Her voice dropped an octave. "Celestina is not his apostle; she is his contracted partner."</p>
<p>"That distinction is precisely what makes her involvement here a violation of the cosmic order."</p>
<p>"Contracted partner," Rex repeated, testing the words.</p>
<p>They sounded like something far more dangerous than a simple alliance.</p>
<p>"An arrangement that the divine framework does not formally prohibit but one that it strongly discourages," Lustia explained. "Apostles are chosen by the god and empowered through a divine blessing, a gift from above."</p>
<p>"Contracted partners, however, choose the god."</p>
<p>"They empower themselves through the god’s authority in exchange for a specific service."</p>
<p>"The service Celestina provides Kaeleth is simple and brutal: the elimination of reincarnators, whom Kaeleth’s temporal perceptive functions identify as parasites and timeline disruptions that threaten the sanctity of his domain."</p>
<p>"So she is his pest control," Rex said, a grim smile tugging at his mouth.</p>
<p>"She is his ideologically aligned contractor," Lustia corrected. "She performs a service he values deeply in exchange for divine authority she could never access through the standard Apostle system."</p>
<p>"Because the standard Apostle system," Rex interjected, his intellect cutting through her explanation like a scalpel, "would never grant the ’Balance Keeper’ designation for the purpose of genocide."</p>
<p>"The other gods would recognize the pattern immediately..."</p>
<p>"They would refuse to permit a god to officially sanction the systematic hunting of reincarnators, and it would upset the balance."</p>
<p>"Correct," Lustia said, clearly impressed by how quickly he had dissected the divine politics. "The contract arrangement allows him to bypass the god community oversight that every other divine act must endure."</p>
<p>Rex leaned back, his mind racing through the implications. "Three dead apostles in two thousand years is a pathetic record for a god of his caliber."</p>
<p>"What killed them? I want to know if there is a pattern before I decide whether I need to worry about them."</p>
<p>"Two of them died to causes unrelated to their designation," Lustia said. "One died of age; the apostle bond did not extend his mortal lifespan the way some other designations do."</p>
<p>"The second died through betrayal by a rival apostle during a period of open divine conflict two centuries ago." She paused, her eyes locking onto his. "The third is the one that matters to you."</p>
<p>Rex felt a chill crawl down his spine. "Tell me about the third."</p>
<p>"The third apostle attempted to use the temporal authority Kaeleth granted him to alter his own past," Lustia said, her voice hushed. "A personal application, not a sanctioned one."</p>
<p>"Kaeleth’s domain does not forgive that kind of transgression."</p>
<p>"A temporal alteration to an apostle’s own timeline creates a paradox, a load, a wound in reality that the domain itself must absorb to maintain stability."</p>
<p>"The apostle wasn’t killed by a weapon or an enemy. He was simply... unmade by the paradox."</p>
<p>"Erased from existence by the very power he tried to weaponize against fate."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes gleamed with a newfound, predatory interest. The horror of the story didn’t frighten him; it intrigued him.</p>
<p>"So the domain itself is a weapon that points in more than one direction," he mused aloud. "Good. That is useful."</p>
<p>"If the domain can unmake its own choice for misusing its power, then there may be a way to trick it into unmaking him."</p>
<p>Lustia stared at him for a long moment, recognizing the terrifying implications of his thought process.</p>
<p>"That is an incredibly aggressive read of what most would consider a cautionary tale," she said quietly. "But it is not a wrong one."</p>
<p>Rex rose to his full height, his shadow stretching across the floor like an inkblot. The air between them remained charged, thick with the residue of their confrontation, but the violence had passed, leaving behind something far more dangerous: clarity.</p>
<p>"I want everything," Rex said, his voice dropping into a register of absolute command. "I want every scrap of intelligence you possess regarding Kaeleth’s domain."</p>
<p>"Its dimensional coordinates, its defensive architecture, and the specific temporal mechanics that govern its internal logic."</p>
<p>"I want to know how it breathes, how it reacts to intrusion, and exactly how it enforces its own laws."</p>
<p>"Give me everything."</p>
<p>Lustia stood to meet him, her regal bearing returning, though her eyes remained fixed on his with an unblinking intensity.</p>
<p>"I will give you what I know," she said, her voice steady despite the exhaustion beneath it. "But understand this: some of it will be incomplete..."</p>
<p>"His domain is not merely hidden; it is deliberately obscured from divine observation."</p>
<p>"He has woven it into the folds of causality itself."</p>
<p>"Give me the incomplete version anyway," Rex countered immediately. "I don’t need a perfect map to begin."</p>
<p>"I will fill in the gaps with blood and experience when I arrive."</p>
<p>Lustia’s lips thinned into something resembling a smile—sharp, knowing, and almost challenging.</p>
<p>"When you arrive," she corrected him. "Not if."</p>
<p>Rex met her challenge without blinking. "When."</p>
<p>Lustia searched his face for a long moment. In that silence, the mask of the goddess slipped.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, she wore an expression she rarely allowed: a warm, vulnerable, almost painfully personal look that transcended the transactional nature of their bond. It was the look of a being who had invested something irreplaceable into a mortal she knew would likely burn the world down.</p>
<p>"He made a mistake," she said softly, the admission carrying more weight than any threat. "Kaeleth made a tactical error."</p>
<p>"He contracted a mortal to handle a reincarnator he should have recognized as something beyond mortal control."</p>
<p>"He violated the divine code; he broke the very laws he represents because he believed he had no other choice."</p>
<p>"He broke them because he was afraid, Rex..."</p>
<p>"He was terrified of what you were building."</p>
<p>Rex stared at her, the implications sinking in.</p>
<p>Fear. A god of time, a being who sees the beginning and end of all things, was experiencing fear because of a single man’s trajectory.</p>
<p>"Good," Rex said. The word was simple, but it carried the promise of inevitable retribution.</p>
<p>He fell silent then, the only sound the low hum of the domain’s ambient energy. He looked at her in the amber glow of her sanctum, and something in his expression shifted.</p>
<p>The raw fury that had consumed him earlier had cooled, but it hadn’t vanished. Beneath the anger and the cold, calculating machinery of his mind, something else emerged, something quieter, more profound.</p>
<p>"You could have handled tonight differently," Rex said suddenly.</p>
<p>His voice wasn’t accusatory; it was observational, like a surgeon noting a flaw in a procedure. "You could have given me the soft version first... eased into the bad news."</p>
<p>"Most of the gods I’ve read about in the archives manage their apostles like precious assets, things to be protected from harsh realities, shielded from the truth until they are strong enough to bear it."</p>
<p>"But you? You gave me the whole truth, raw and ugly, in the correct order..."</p>
<p>"You even included the parts where you nearly failed."</p>
