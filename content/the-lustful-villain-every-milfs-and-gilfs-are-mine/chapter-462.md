<p>Meanwhile, while Rex and Elizabeth are caught in their world of lust, Alexander remains, waiting for something that won’t arrive today.</p>
<p>In Alexander’s bedroom, the silence was deafening. It was 3:00 AM. The world was suspended in a heavy, velvet darkness, broken only by the rhythmic, lonely ticking of the clock and the occasional sigh of the wind against the stone walls.</p>
<p>Alexander lay staring up at the canopy of his bed, eyes wide and unblinking. The sheets were cold and unnatural, a blank, empty sea of linen that taunted him.</p>
<p>He instinctively turned his head to the left, his gaze landing on the empty space beside him. "Ughhh... grrgghhh... fuck...!"</p>
<p>Usually, by this hour, he would feel the familiar, comforting warmth of Elizabeth. He would feel the soft, rhythmic breathing of the woman he loved, perhaps the gentle weight of her head on his shoulder or the subtle scent of lavender and old parchment that always seemed to cling to her skin.</p>
<p>They had a routine, a silent, sacred ritual of finding solace in each other after long hours of study or the stresses of the camp.</p>
<p>But tonight, the space was hollow. The pillow was smooth, untouched, and chillingly vacant.</p>
<p>A sharp pang of loneliness pierced his chest, a sudden, visceral realization of her absence. For a fleeting, terrifying second, his mind drifted to the dark, chaotic thoughts that had plagued him all night: the image of her in Rex’s presence and the fear that he had pushed her away with his incompetence.</p>
<p>But he quickly shook the thought away, forcing himself to breathe deeply.</p>
<p>’No,’ he told himself, his jaw tightening. ’Don’t go there... and don’t you fucking dare do this shit!’</p>
<p>’Don’t let your guilt turn into madness... let it start to slowly turn you into an insane man.’</p>
<p>He squeezed his eyes shut, forcing his mind to steer back toward the light. He leaned into the optimism he had cultivated earlier.</p>
<p>"She’s busy," he whispered into the dark, the words a mantra to soothe his aching heart. "She’s being... productive."</p>
<p>"She’s doing what needs to be done for us."</p>
<p>He tried to visualize her, not in a state of distress, but in a state of purpose. He imagined her sitting in a dimly lit room, perhaps with Rex, but in a professional capacity discussing the logistics of the expedition, negotiating the terms of the underlayer’s stabilization, or perhaps even working on a way to salvage the broken key herself.</p>
<p>He pictured her as the brilliant, capable woman he knew her to be, taking charge of the chaos he had created.</p>
<p>"She’s probably exhausted," he thought, a small, bittersweet smile forming on his lips. "She’s working so hard to correct my mistake."</p>
<p>"She’s being so strong... so selfless."</p>
<p>He tried to find comfort in the idea of her strength. If she were out there, navigating the complexities of Rex’s temperament and Lady Valentina’s expectations, then she would be the hero of their story.</p>
<p>She was the one holding the pieces together while he sat here, useless and broken.</p>
<p>"She’s doing it for our future," he murmured, closing his eyes and trying to conjure the feeling of her warmth, even though she was miles away. "She’s making sure we survive this ordeal."</p>
<p>"When she returns to this bed, it will mean the danger has passed. The key will be fixed, and we will finally be whole again."</p>
<p>He drifted toward a shallow, restless sleep, clinging to the beautiful lie he had constructed. He fell asleep dreaming of a triumphant Elizabeth returning to his arms, her eyes bright with success, completely unaware that the woman he was dreaming of had been replaced by a creature of pure, unbridled lust, currently screaming Rex’s name in a fever of broken, blissful submission.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The cycle of restless sleep and jarring wakefulness became a torment. Every time Alexander drifted into a shallow slumber, a sudden, instinctive lurch in his chest would snap him awake.</p>
<p>’One minute passed.’ He sat up, squinting into the shadows of the doorway, his heart hammering against his ribs. ’Is that her?’</p>
<p>’Is that the soft scuff of her slippers on the stone?’ He waited, breathless, but there was only the silence of the dead of night.</p>
<p>’Five minutes passed.’</p>
<p>He lay back down, but his eyes wouldn’t stay closed. The emptiness of the bed felt like a physical void, a vacuum sucking the air from his lungs.</p>
<p>Every creak of the house sounded like her footsteps, and every gust of wind sounded like her sigh.</p>
<p>But the door remained shut. The space beside him remained cold.</p>
<p>"She’s not here yet," he whispered, his voice cracking. "She’s... she’s still working."</p>
<p>"She’s still being the pillar we need."</p>
<p>To keep the creeping dread at bay, he began to talk to himself, his voice a low, frantic murmur in the dark.</p>
<p>He needed to remember why he was doing it. He needed to remember the foundation of the man he was trying desperately to be.</p>
<p>"It was the Academy," he murmured, a faint, nostalgic smile flickering on his lips before dissolving into a grimace of longing. "The Great Library... the smell of old leather and dust..."</p>
<p>He closed his eyes, letting the memory wash over him. He could see it so clearly: a younger, less burdened version of himself, sitting amidst a mountain of scrolls.</p>
<p>And there she was. Elizabeth.</p>
<p>Even then, she had possessed an aura of untouchable grace, but he had seen the slight furrow in her brow, the way her fingers had trembled ever so slightly as she stared at a particularly complex theorem of arcane geometry.</p>
<p>She had been struggling. The brilliant, poised Elizabeth had been lost in the labyrinth of ancient mathematics.</p>
<p>"She appeared so small in that moment," he whispered to the empty room. "So human. So beautiful."</p>
<p>He remembered the courage it had taken to approach her. He had been a mere student then, but the sight of her frustration had stirred something in him, a primal need to be the one to provide the answer, to be the one to steady her hand.</p>
<p>He had stepped in, offering his help with a humility that had felt entirely natural.</p>
<p>"I just wanted to help her," he said, his eyes glistening. "One equation. One translation. Then another. And then... everything."</p>
<p>He remembered the way her eyes had lit up when he finally explained the concept, the way her grateful smile had felt like a sunburst in the dim library. It hadn’t been a sudden explosion of passion but a slow, steady erosion of his defenses.</p>
<p>Every time he helped her, every time he saw her succeed because of his support, he felt a surge of pride. He had fallen in love with her intellect, her tenacity, and the quiet strength she carried.</p>
<p>And as he fell, a new, more perilous longing began to take root: the desire to be her hero. He envisioned himself as the man capable of solving any problem, the one who could shield her from every hardship, the person who would consistently leave her in awe.</p>
<p>"I wanted to impress her," he admitted to the shadows, his voice trembling with emotion. "I wanted to be so perfect, so indispensable, that she would never have to look at another man."</p>
<p>"I wanted to be her everything."</p>
<p>He gripped the edge of the blanket, his knuckles white. The irony was a bitter, jagged pill to swallow.</p>
<p>In his desperate, clumsy attempt to be that perfect man who would prove his worth by seizing the key, he had instead become the man who broke her world. He had tried to impress her with strength, only to show her his weakness.</p>
<p>"I’m still trying, Elizabeth," he choked out, leaning his head back against the pillow. "Every minute you’re gone, every hour you’re away..."</p>
<p>"I’m still trying to be the man you deserve."</p>
<p>"Just... please... come home soon."</p>
<p>He lay there, a man haunted by a memory of a girl in a library, completely oblivious to the fact that the woman he was trying to impress was currently being broken into a thousand pieces by a man who didn’t care about her intellect at all, only the way her body screamed under his weight.</p>
<p>"Tomorrow... I do hope she can talk to me."</p>
