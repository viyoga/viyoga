<p>Rex found her in the training hall exactly one hour later. The space was cavernous, filled with the sharp tang of sweat and floor wax, lit only by the pale, sickly glow of the moon filtering through the high windows.</p>
<p>Aurelia was alone, a solitary figure caught in the rhythm of violence. She was running the same brutal conditioning drills he had run through himself the week before—drills designed to break a person before they were built.</p>
<p>Her movements were sharp, mechanical, and surgically controlled, but beneath that technical precision lurked something feral. Every strike against the heavy leather sandbag carried the echo of a scream; every pivot was too fast, too desperate, as if she were trying to outrun the ghost of her own choices.</p>
<p>Rex watched her from the arched doorway, his silhouette motionless. He didn’t speak immediately, letting the wet thwack of her fists against leather fill the silence.</p>
<p>He could see the way her muscles coiled and snapped, the way her knuckles were already beginning to split and weep crimson droplets onto the training mat.</p>
<p>"I heard," he said quietly as he finally crossed the floor, his boots making no sound against the reinforced wood.</p>
<p>Aurelia did not stop. She unleashed a devastating combination of a left hook, right cross, and spinning elbow; each strike was delivered with the specific, focused brutality of someone using physical agony to process an emotional wound that refused to clot.</p>
<p>The sandbag groaned under the assault.</p>
<p>"The entire household heard," she snapped, her voice ragged.</p>
<p>She didn’t turn. "I did not exactly manage the volume of it."</p>
<p>"I am not here to judge how you managed it," Rex said, stopping just outside her striking radius. His voice was steady, a calm anchor in the storm of her exertion. "I am here because you should not be doing this alone... Not tonight."</p>
<p>Aurelia stopped abruptly. Her chest heaved in violent, uncontrolled spasms, and her breath came in harsh, whistling gasps that tore at her throat.</p>
<p>Her knuckles were raw, the skin split and weeping blood that streaked down her wrists like macabre bracelets. She turned to face him fully, and the exhaustion that met his gaze was terrifying.</p>
<p>It wasn’t just physical fatigue; it was the hollow, sunken look of someone whose soul had been scraped raw.</p>
<p>"He accused you," she said, the words tumbling out like broken glass. "Of being Tremor."</p>
<p>"Of getting Iris pregnant." Her eyes searched his, desperate and accusatory. "He said it with such conviction, Rex."</p>
<p>"Such absolute, terrifying certainty..."</p>
<p>"For one terrible moment, standing there in that doorway while I held him down, while I struck him... I found myself actually wondering."</p>
<p>"The doubt felt like a blade between my ribs."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He met her gaze with the careful, unnatural stillness of a man who had learned to weigh the gravity of every lie.</p>
<p>He studied her, calculating exactly how much pressure the truth could withstand before it shattered.</p>
<p>"And what did you decide?" he asked, his voice dropping to a low murmur.</p>
<p>"I decided he was broken," Aurelia said, though her voice wavered. "I told myself that in the night in that guest room, the blood, the blade—it broke something fundamental in him."</p>
<p>"That it created a fracture that hasn’t stopped spreading... I told myself that a broken man will reach for any narrative, any twisted story that lets him believe he was righteous in his madness."</p>
<p>"I decided that believing him would mean accepting that my son might have stumbled onto something true, and I decided I had to decide that I could not survive that thought while standing in the wreckage of my own home."</p>
<p>"You made the correct decision," Rex said firmly. His tone left no room for argument.</p>
<p>"Did I?" Aurelia whispered.</p>
<p>Something in her voice cracked, not with the fury she had shown Theo, but with an aching, poisonous uncertainty. "I do not know anymore, Rex..."</p>
<p>"I have struck my own son twice in one week."</p>
<p>"I have looked at him and told him he is unrecognizable. And perhaps that was fair... Perhaps he is unrecognizable."</p>
<p>She took a stumbling step toward him, her hands shaking, the blood from her knuckles smearing across her training gear. "But then there are the pieces that don’t fit... the abilities you possess."</p>
<p>"The knowledge you carry, the names, the places, the secrets of people you should have no way of knowing."</p>
<p>’I told him none of it added up to a conspiracy."</p>
<p>"I told him you were the exception, the miracle." Her eyes welled with tears she refused to let fall. "But I am standing here now, Rex, and I am less certain of that than I was when I was screaming in his face."</p>
<p>Rex closed the remaining distance between them, his movements deliberate and unhurried, like a predator approaching something fragile rather than something hostile.</p>
<p>He reached out and placed his hand against the leather-bound post beside her, the contact sending a dull vibration through the wood that Aurelia could feel in her own aching bones.</p>
<p>He didn’t touch her; he knew better than to invade her space while she was still bleeding from the inside, but his proximity was overwhelming. He leaned in close enough that his breath stirred the stray hairs damp with sweat at her temple, and his voice dropped to a murmur that required no effort to hear.</p>
<p>"You are exhausted," he said. His tone was devoid of pity; it was worse.</p>
<p>It was a simple statement of fact, delivered with the clinical precision of a surgeon. "You have spent seven days holding back a flood, managing a crisis that would have shattered a dozen lesser men before they even reached the breaking point."</p>
<p>’Exhaustion has a way of eroding even the most solid ground, turning certainties into shadows..."</p>
<p>"What you are feeling right now isn’t weakness, Aurelia... It is simply the inevitable collapse of a body that has been asked to carry more than any one person was ever designed to bear."</p>
<p>Aurelia stiffened, her jaw clenching so hard she feared her teeth might crack.</p>
<p>"Do not manage me, Rex," she spat, but the venom was gone.</p>
<p>The words lacked the bite of her earlier rage; they sounded hollow, the desperate protest of someone who was already drowning. "I am not one of your twelve subordinates."</p>
<p>"I am not some asset to be steadied or a problem to be solved... I do not need you to talk me down from a difficult morning."</p>
<p>"I am not managing you," Rex countered immediately, his voice cutting through her protest with terrifying calm. "I am speaking the truth."</p>
<p>"And the truth is that you have spent thirty years cultivating a persona of invulnerability."</p>
<p>"You have made it your life’s work to be the strongest person in every room, the unshakable foundation upon which this entire household rests."</p>
<p>He shifted slightly, his shadow engulfing her. "But this week has demanded more than even that legendary strength was built to provide."</p>
<p>"This week has demanded a price." His eyes locked onto hers, unblinking. "Locking Theo away in that room, striking him until your own hands bled, coming here in the dead of night to punish a training post because you have nowhere else to put the fury—none of that makes you weak."</p>
<p>"None of that makes you less of a leader."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the silence of the training hall press in around them, thick with the metallic tang of her blood and the unspoken horrors of the evening.</p>
<p>"It makes you human, Aurelia," he said softly, each word landing like a hammer blow. "Which is something I suspect you haven’t permitted yourself to not be in front of your staff, not in front of your son, and certainly not in front of me for a very long time."</p>
<p>Something in Aurelia’s composure, that ironclad fortress she had spent three decades constructing, finally shattered. The admission of her own humanity had cracked the foundation, and now the walls were crumbling.</p>
<p>When she looked at Rex, the expression beneath her gaze was raw, terrifyingly unguarded, and stripped of every aristocratic pretense. She didn’t look like a matriarch; she looked like a wounded animal seeking a worthy adversary.</p>
<p>"Spar with me," she commanded, though the words came out as something closer to a plea. "One last time..."</p>
<p>"Properly."</p>
