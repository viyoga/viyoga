<p>Iris nodded, and the tension that had held her spine rigid for days finally gave way to something resembling release. It wasn’t happiness; she was beyond such simple emotions, but it was the specific, hollow relief of someone who had finally named their demon and found that the demon did not flinch.</p>
<p>She had laid her vulnerability bare, and instead of exploitation, she had found partnership.</p>
<p>That evening, they trained in the hunting grounds beyond the household’s outer walls. The terrain was unforgiving: a tangle of ancient roots, treacherous shale slopes, and dense thickets that demanded a predator’s patience rather than a soldier’s speed.</p>
<p>They moved through the twilight like two ghosts, tracking a deliberately difficult quarry through the deepening shadows.</p>
<p>Iris found herself performing a strange, unconscious recalibration of her own boundaries. More than once, she positioned herself closer to Rex than the exercise strictly required.</p>
<p>She wanted to be within striking distance of his voice, that low, quiet timbre that cut through the rustle of the wind to give instructions on reading a broken branch or the minute disturbance of a patch of moss. When he moved to redirect her aim, his hand brushing hers to adjust the angle of her bowstring, she didn’t recoil.</p>
<p>A month ago, she would have flinched away from the contact as if burned; now, she simply absorbed the heat of him, letting the contact anchor her to the present moment.</p>
<p>’I am letting him in,’ she thought, a flash of alarm sparking in her mind before she pushed it down. ’And the terrifying thing is, I don’t want to stop...’</p>
<p>While Iris and Rex navigated the physical shadows of the hunting grounds, a different kind of darkness was festering in the bowels of the household.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The whispering had begun three nights ago.</p>
<p>At first, Theo had dismissed it as the auditory hallucination of a broken mind. He told himself it was merely his own thoughts running loose in the dark, the overactive imagination of a man whose sleep had been fractured by trauma and sleeplessness for longer than he cared to admit.</p>
<p>He had convinced himself it was just the wind through the eaves or the settling of old stone.</p>
<p>By the third night, the lie had crumbled.</p>
<p>The voice, if something that felt like cold mercury sliding through his brain could be called a voice, did not arrive in words. It arrived in certainty.</p>
<p>It was an unearned, absolute conviction that settled into his chest at three in the morning, a poison that tasted like truth. It told him things he would never have dared to voice in the sunlight.</p>
<p>’Rex didn’t just defeat you, Theo,’ the certainty whispered, coiling like a snake in his gut. ’He dismantled you...’</p>
<p>’He took your position, your pride, and your purpose with the methodical cruelty of a predator claiming territory.’</p>
<p>And look at them. Look at this household... Their admiration for him isn’t respect; it’s cowardice.’</p>
<p>’They are all blind, stumbling through a fog of their own making, unable to see the usurper standing at their hearth.’</p>
<p>It whispered of Diana, too. ’Her happiness is a performance, Theo...’</p>
<p>’A costume she wears to convince herself that she hasn’t been abandoned...’</p>
<p>’She is as hollow as you are.’</p>
<p>And then, the most dangerous whisper of all: ’There is still TIME to fix it.’</p>
<p>Each night, the certainty grew sharper, more invasive. It felt less like a thought and more like something being poured thick, black, and irreversible into the hollow spaces where his own will used to reside.</p>
<p>Exhaustion had become his enemy; it had stripped away the defenses of his rational mind. By the second night, he had stopped fighting the intrusion.</p>
<p>By the third, he had begun, with a terrifying passivity, to listen.</p>
<p>Tonight, the certainty reached its crescendo. It crystallized into a single, lethal directive.</p>
<p>’Accidents happen, Theo,’ the voice purred, as warm as blood. ’Stone stairs are treacherous when the candles burn low.’</p>
<p>’A man as powerful as Rex, as physically dominant as he is... surely his heart could fail him in his sleep.’</p>
<p>’Surely a man consumed by grief could become careless... A stumble in the dark...’</p>
<p>’A misplaced foot on a narrow ledge. A tragedy born of ordinary human frailty.’</p>
<p>’No one would question it... They would simply weep for the fallen lion, never knowing he was slaughtered by the boy he overlooked.’</p>
<p>Theo sat upright in the darkness of his room, his breathing shallow and ragged. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird, but his hands were unnaturally still.</p>
<p>The exhaustion was gone, replaced by a cold, vibrating clarity that made his bones ache.</p>
<p>’Tonight is the night,’ the certainty commanded. ’The window is closing...’</p>
<p>’The opportunity will not repeat itself...’</p>
<p>’You have already lost everything that mattered... You have nothing left to protect...’</p>
<p>’The only thing left to do is burn it all down.’</p>
<p>Theo rose from his bed in the suffocating darkness, but he did not feel the cold stone beneath his feet. His mind was operating in a strange, detached register, a distant, mechanical frequency that made his own body feel like a foreign object.</p>
<p>He watched his hands move through the gloom as though they belonged to a marionette, puppeted by something far more ancient and cruel than his own will.</p>
<p>His fingers fumbled with the lock of the heavy oak chest at the foot of his bed. When the lid creaked open, he reached in and retrieved the ceremonial paladin’s blade.</p>
<p>The steel was ancient, passed down through the Nightwing heirs for three generations, designed not just to kill but to execute with divine finality. As his grip closed around the leather-wrapped hilt, the familiar weight of the weapon didn’t feel like a burden; it felt like an anchor.</p>
<p>For the first time in his fractured life, the blade felt like the only thing in the universe that made sense.</p>
<p>’This is the only truth,’ the whisper shrieked in his skull, drowning out the sound of his own ragged breathing. ’Everything else is a lie... Everything else is rot...’</p>
<p>He did not remember walking the corridor. He had no memory of the creak of the floorboards, the way his shadow stretched unnaturally long against the torch-lit walls, or the way his own pulse throbbed in his ears like a war drum.</p>
<p>There was only a tunnel of tunnel vision, a singular, driving purpose that burned through his consciousness like acid.</p>
<p>Suddenly, he was there. He was standing before the heavy door of the guest room.</p>
<p>His breath came in shallow, silent hitches as he pressed the handle down. The door swung open with an agonizingly slow whisper of wood against stone.</p>
<p>The room beyond was swallowed in shadow, smelling faintly of lavender and sweat. In the dim moonlight filtering through the window, he saw them: the two shapes that had become the twin monuments to his humiliation.</p>
<p>Diana was curled against Rex’s side, her breathing soft and rhythmic, her face pressed against the solid plane of his shoulder. Rex lay there with the terrifying stillness of a predator at rest, one arm draped possessively over her waist, his fingers curled near her hip.</p>
<p>They looked peaceful. They looked safe.</p>
<p>They looked like they belonged to each other.</p>
<p>’Look at them,’ the voice hissed, venomous and triumphant. Look at how they sleep while you bleed. ’Look at how they steal what was yours...’</p>
<p>’End the theft, Theo... End it now...’</p>
<p>A guttural, wordless sound threatened to rise from Theo’s throat, a sob of grief mixed with a howl of rage, but he strangled it. His entire existence narrowed down to the point of the blade.</p>
<p>His muscles coiled, tendons straining beneath his skin as he drew the sword back. His vision blurred at the edges, the world dissolving until there was nothing left but the silver edge of the Nightwing steel and the exposed throats of the two people he had once loved.</p>
<p>The whispering in his skull reached its breaking point, escalating into a screaming, deafening certainty. It wasn’t just a thought anymore; it was a physical force, a psychic hammer pounding against his skull.</p>
<p>’This is mercy! This is justice!’</p>
<p>’This is the only way to stop the bleeding!’</p>
<p>’Kill them both and let the world finally be quiet again!’</p>
<p>His body obeyed. Three generations of Nightwing conditioning, years of brutal training designed to turn muscle into instinct, took over.</p>
<p>His shoulders rotated, his core tightened, and his arms began the devastating downward arc. It was a perfect strike, a killing blow honed by centuries of bloodline memory, designed to cleave through flesh and bone in one clean, merciless motion.</p>
<p>His mind screamed in terror even as his body executed the slaughter with flawless, mechanical precision.</p>
<p>The blade began its descent through the air, whistling toward their necks.</p>
<p>The blade never completed the arc.</p>
