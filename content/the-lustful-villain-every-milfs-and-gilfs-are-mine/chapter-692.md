<p>Brant Hollow's gaze drifted from the towering monolith of stone to the ruined earth at his feet. He looked at the jagged scars in the soil, the evidence of eighteen months of painstaking, rhythmic labor.</p>
<p>Every inch of this field was a testament to his system, a silent, invisible masterpiece of cultivation that the villagers had mistaken for divine luck.</p>
<p>"Are the others getting this?" Brant asked, his voice trembling with a sudden, sharp realization of his own vulnerability. "The other reincarnators... are they getting a visit like this today?"</p>
<p>"The unflagged are receiving consolidation instructions," the golem replied, the vibration of its voice rattling the very air. "The flagged are receiving a different kind of contact."</p>
<p>"Different how?" Brant pressed, his eyes searching the golem's unmoving face for a shred of humanity.</p>
<p>"The flagged are being addressed," the golem stated.</p>
<p>It was the perfect, hollow answer, the exact linguistic mask Rex used when the truth was too heavy or too brutal for a civilian to carry.</p>
<p>Brant fell silent. He looked away, staring at the horizon as if searching for a ghost.</p>
<p>"Eighteen months," he whispered, more to himself than the construct. "I've been in this village for eighteen months."</p>
<p>"I chose this place because a cultivation system needs a context where its output is useful but unremarkable."</p>
<p>"A village where the crops had been failing for six seasons... a place where my 'blessings' would just look like good farming."</p>
<p>"I wasn't trying to be a god... I was just trying to be useful without being noticed."</p>
<p>"The village benefited," the golem observed.</p>
<p>"It did," Brant agreed, a grim, hollow smile touching his lips. "That wasn't an accident."</p>
<p>"I didn't pick a place to hide and produce nothing."</p>
<p>"I picked a place where my power was actually needed." He looked back at the stone giant, his eyes narrowing. "I don't know if that matters to whatever is making decisions about me right now."</p>
<p>"It is a data point," the golem countered, its tone chillingly analytical. "The Earthen Authority reads the substrate."</p>
<p>"The substrate shows eighteen months of continuous cultivation enhancement distributed across forty percent of this sector's active acreage."</p>
<p>"The pattern is unmistakable: you did not use your system to demonstrate capability; you used it to maximize the stability of the environment."</p>
<p>"You were optimizing the land."</p>
<p>Brant felt a cold shiver race down his spine. "You're telling me... the very ground under my feet has been reading my farming methods for a year and a half?"</p>
<p>"Yes," the golem said.</p>
<p>A heavy silence followed, one that felt like the weight of a mountain. "Central market square. Thirty minutes."</p>
<p>"Twenty six now," the golem added, the countdown feeling like a ticking bomb.</p>
<p>Brant looked at his disrupted rows one last time. The root systems, which he had nurtured with such delicate precision, were now mangled and broken.</p>
<p>A strange, weary calm settled over him, the look of a man who had finally realized that the world he thought he knew was merely a thin crust over a much deeper, much more dangerous reality.</p>
<p>"Those rows are going to need resetting," Brant said, his voice regaining a shred of its old pragmatism. "The root systems took heavy impact damage."</p>
<p>"The reconstruction accounting will address all infrastructure damage caused by this morning's operations," the golem stated.</p>
<p>"It's a crop row, not infrastructure," Brant snapped, a flash of his old spirit returning. He paused, his eyes darkening. "I'll reset them myself when I get back."</p>
<p>"Assuming... assuming I get back."</p>
<p>"You will get back," the golem promised, the statement sounding less like a comfort and more like a mathematical certainty. "The consolidation is for notification. Not for collection."</p>
<p>Brant stared at the construct, weighing the truth of the words. He didn't know if he believed it, but he knew he had no choice but to act.</p>
<p>"Twenty five minutes," he muttered, turning his back on the machine and beginning the long walk toward the main road to Aethelgard.</p>
<p>Miles away, Rex released his primary focus from the Caldmere contact. He let the golem operate on its own, its instructions burned into its stony consciousness.</p>
<p>He didn't need to watch the farmer anymore; the data was already being filed away in the grand architecture of his plan.</p>
<p>The second contact was resolved. The third was proceeding as scripted. But the fourth... the fourth was becoming something far more captivating.</p>
<p>The fourth golem had surfaced in the lower residential district of Aethelgard, erupting in the center of a private courtyard. Rex's mental map flared with a sudden, intense crimson light.</p>
<p>The target was a reincarnator whose system signature was unlike the others. It wasn't a bright, pulsing light of growth or a frantic flicker of magic; it was a dense, heavy, layered resonance, the unmistakable signature of a combat class system operating under intense, deliberate suppression.</p>
<p>Most combat system users let their energy bleed outward; it was a natural byproduct of their power.</p>
<p>But this signature was being squeezed, held tight, and muffled by an incredible effort of will. This wasn't just a warrior; this was a predator trying to look like a sheep.</p>
<p>The identification sweep provided the name Veran Caulstow.</p>
<p>Two years on the island. Current occupation: independent contractor in security and logistics.</p>
<p>Rex's mental grin was predatory. The man had spent two years pretending to be a mere laborer, burying a war machine beneath a layer of mundane existence.</p>
<p>But the Earthen Authority didn't care about disguises. The ground knew exactly what Veran Caulstow was, and now, the golem was standing in his garden to remind him.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The courtyard was a tomb of silence when the earth decided to break.</p>
<p>There was no gradual tremor, no warning vibration through the soles of the feet. Instead, the stone pavers of the courtyard simply surrendered, erupting upward in a violent, jagged geyser of debris.</p>
<p>The golem tore through the foundation, a monolithic slab of unyielding geological force that stood dead center in the private space, its presence an absolute violation of the morning's stillness.</p>
<p>It was the early hour, that precise window where the residential district was a ghost town, the population either still retreating into the warmth of their homes or already flowing through the bustling market arteries of the middle district.</p>
<p>The building itself was a fortress of privacy; the ground floor windows were shuttered tight, a deliberate architectural choice by an occupant who clearly valued the luxury of being unobserved.</p>
<p>The golem stood there, a silent, stony sentinel in the center of the wreckage.</p>
<p>Then, a sound: the rhythmic, heavy slide of a second-story window being thrown open.</p>
<p>A man leaned out into the morning air. By surface appearance, he looked to be in his late thirties, but his physicality betrayed a much deeper truth.</p>
<p>He possessed the coiled, effortless tension of a combat system holder, a man whose muscles had been conditioned by a system's passive aura for so long that his very resting posture was a weapon. He didn't flinch at the sound of the explosion.</p>
<p>He didn't scramble for a weapon or recoil in shock. He simply looked down at the massive construct occupying his courtyard with an expression of chilling, analytical calm.</p>
<p>'That was the first significant data point,' Rex thought, his consciousness feeding off the man's lack of surprise. 'He wasn't expecting a god to burst through his floor, yet he wasn't afraid of it either.'</p>
<p>"You are Veran Caulstow," the golem announced, its voice a low, tectonic rumble that seemed to vibrate the very walls of the building.</p>
<p>The man didn't blink. His gaze was steady, piercing through the golem's stony visage as if he were looking at the man behind the machine.</p>
<p>"I am," he replied, his voice a controlled, gravelly baritone.</p>
<p>"Reincarnator," the golem continued, the recitation of his life's secrets sounding like a death sentence. "Combat system... Suppressed signature."</p>
<p>"Two years on this island. Not a Legion contact."</p>
<p>Veran didn't deny it, and he didn't ask how the creature knew. He simply narrowed his eyes, his stance shifting almost imperceptibly into a more grounded, defensive equilibrium.</p>
<p>"You have been reading the island's substrate," he stated, and it wasn't a question; it was an observation of a predator recognizing another.</p>
<p>"Since this morning," the golem answered, its voice devoid of pride or apology. "The Earthen Authority mapped the island at the moment of first contact."</p>
<p>"And you came to me specifically," Veran said, his voice dropping an octave as the calmness began to fray, revealing a lethal edge.</p>
<p>"The suppressed signature was notable," the golem countered, the bluntness of the statement cutting through the tension. "Someone who invests the constant, taxing effort of active suppression has a reason for it."</p>
<p>"You are hiding a mountain behind a veil of dust."</p>
<p>Veran Caulstow went quiet. The silence that followed was heavy, thick with the unspoken weight of two years spent in a carefully constructed lie and the sudden, terrifying realization that the lie had just been stripped bare.</p>
<p>"What do you want?" he said.</p>
<p>"The same thing the golem is telling every non-flagged reincarnator on the island this morning," Rex said through the relay. "Central market square."</p>
<p>"Notification of the Underlayer's changed governance structure."</p>
<p>"Thirty minutes from the contact point."</p>
<p>"I know what happened in the Underlayer last night," Veran said.</p>
<p>The second significant data point.</p>
