<p>"It is not the framing I would have chosen," Lustia said, her voice carrying the detached cadence of someone discussing the weather rather than the transactional nature of human intimacy. "But it is not inaccurate either."</p>
<p>"The system you were given ties its most significant unlocks to the specific, primal act of creating life..."</p>
<p>"I did not design that mechanism, Rex..."</p>
<p>"I inherited a domain and a designation with mechanics that were already carved into the bedrock of reality the same way every god inherits the bones of whatever came before them."</p>
<p>Rex stared at her, his mind churning through the implications. The coldness of it, the sheer, mechanical cruelty of a universe that demanded such a price, made something hot and angry coil in his gut.</p>
<p>"You are telling me you did not build the pregnancy mechanic," he said, the accusation sharp enough to draw blood. "You’re telling me you’re just the administrator of a system designed to exploit the most vulnerable parts of human connection."</p>
<p>"I am telling you the Lust domain has always operated on these principles, long before you existed to weaponize it," Lustia countered, her eyes unblinking. "Desire that produces new life carries more metaphysical weight in the divine layer than desire that merely consumes."</p>
<p>"It is a fundamental law of our physics..."</p>
<p>"I simply administer the domain; I did not invent its gravity."</p>
<p>"Convenient administration," Rex muttered.</p>
<p>His inner voice was a snarl. ’A system where power is bought with flesh and seed...’</p>
<p>’It’s obscene... But if this is the currency of gods, I will spend every cent I have until I’m bankrupt...’</p>
<p>"For me, at least."</p>
<p>"I have never pretended the arrangement does not benefit you," Lustia said, her tone shifting to something almost approving. "I would be a poor patron if I did not provide you with the tools to achieve your impossible goals."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t acknowledge the compliment. His focus had already shifted from the cost to the clock.</p>
<p>The enemy wasn’t just a god; it was time itself.</p>
<p>"The timeline," he demanded, leaning forward until he was mere inches from her divine presence. "How long before the God of Time makes another attempt?"</p>
<p>"How long before he decides to erase me along with everything else?"</p>
<p>"I do not know with certainty," Lustia admitted, and for the first time, Rex saw a genuine shadow of uncertainty cross her features. "His violation of the divine framework’s operational code has consequences that ripple through the celestial hierarchy."</p>
<p>"The other gods are now aware that he acted directly, that he bypassed the protocols."</p>
<p>"That creates political pressure and that also creates friction... It will limit his immediate ability to repeat such a blatant transgression."</p>
<p>"So there is a window," Rex said, his voice dropping to a lethal whisper.</p>
<p>"A window," she confirmed. "Its duration depends on two variables: how quickly he can navigate the political fallout among his peers and whether Celestina’s next operational move creates a justification, a divine pretext, for him to act again."</p>
<p>The name Celestina struck Rex like a physical blow. His mind flashed back to the communication crystal, the way it had felt beneath his grip before he crushed it into worthless glittering dust.</p>
<p>He remembered the specific, venomous message he had sent her. It had been a calculated provocation: aggressive enough to force her hand, but measured enough to ensure her response didn’t arrive before he had finished his preparations.</p>
<p>He was playing a game of chicken with a goddess, and the stakes were his own existence.</p>
<p>’I can’t hesitate,’ he thought, his pulse hammering against his ribs like a trapped animal. ’Every second I spend thinking is a second he spends calculating how to kill me...’</p>
<p>’I need those skills...’</p>
<p>’I need those anchors...’</p>
<p>’Now.’</p>
<p>His thoughts pivoted with practiced, ruthless efficiency toward his targets. He had already been mapping them out, analyzing their vulnerabilities, and studying their patterns.</p>
<p>He had been planning them for reasons that had nothing to do with divine warfare—reasons of personal ambition and raw, unadulterated want.</p>
<p>Iris and Aurelia.</p>
<p>The God of Time situation had not changed his targets; it had simply stripped away the luxury of patience. The hunt was no longer about conquest; it was about survival.</p>
<p>"Full potential adaptation," Rex said, the words tasting like iron on his tongue. "What does it do beyond the God of Time’s domain?"</p>
<p>"Give me the full scope, Lustia..." He crossed his arms. "Don’t spare the technicalities."</p>
<p>Lustia straightened, her divine aura flickering with a sudden, sharp intensity. "It does exactly what its name implies, Rex."</p>
<p>"It is the ultimate evolutionary response..."</p>
<p>"Every ability, every spell, and every divine manifestation that an opponent hurls at you, your body and your system will analyze upon first contact."</p>
<p>"They will dissect the fundamental frequency of that attack and integrate the necessary defense into your operational parameters."</p>
<p>"The second time that same ability touches you, you will find yourself adapted to it."</p>
<p>"The adaptation is permanent and it is cumulative..."</p>
<p>"You become a living countermeasure to everything that has ever tried to harm you."</p>
<p>Rex stared at her, his mind already running through his existing toolkit.</p>
<p>"I already have Infinite Regeneration even though it’s a fucking scam," he challenged, his voice rough. "Why would I need both?"</p>
<p>"Because you misunderstand the nature of your own survival," Lustia said, her voice dropping to a cold, pedagogical register. "Infinite regeneration is reactive."</p>
<p>"It heals damage after it has already occurred... It mends torn flesh, knits broken bones, and seals ruptured arteries."</p>
<p>"It is a brilliant tool for enduring a massacre, but it still requires you to suffer the massacre first."</p>
<p>She stepped closer, the blue fire in her eyes burning brighter. "Full potential adaptation is proactive."</p>
<p>"It prevents the damage from occurring in the first place after the initial exposure. And more importantly, adaptation covers what regeneration cannot: ability suppression, designation interference, temporal distortion, and the specific category of divine working that operates on the soul rather than the biological system."</p>
<p>"Infinite regeneration cannot heal a soul that has been rewritten."</p>
<p>A cold chill traced Rex’s spine.</p>
<p>"The God of Time’s attacks operate on the soul," he whispered, the realization sinking in like a poisoned dagger.</p>
<p>"His domain operates on the soul," Lustia corrected grimly. "Everything within his reach is subject to his temporal authority."</p>
<p>"That authority works at a level deeper than biology, deeper than cellular structure..."</p>
<p>"Infinite Regeneration cannot regenerate something that has been temporally altered, something that has been erased from its own timeline."</p>
<p>"You cannot heal what no longer has a history."</p>
<p>Rex sank back into his thoughts, the weight of the revelation pressing down on his shoulders. He could almost feel it: the terrifying sensation of his own existence being unraveled, his memories dissolving into static, his very bones turning to ash not from heat, but from the simple fact that he had never been born.</p>
<p>"Say... I get all three skills," Rex said finally, his voice steadier now, though his eyes remained dark with calculation. "Presence Manipulation, Dimensional Manipulation, Full Potential Adaptation."</p>
<p>"Say I walk into his domain wearing all of them like armor..." Rex held his chin. "What actually happens when I find him?"</p>
<p>"What does killing a god look like in practice? Because I have killed armies, I have killed monsters, and I have killed kings, but none of them were something that has existed as a fundamental constant for two thousand years.</p>
<p>Lustia fell silent. She considered the question with more care than she had given any before.</p>
<p>There was a long, pregnant pause, the kind of silence that precedes a catastrophe. She was weighing how much truth was useful and how much truth was simply too frightening for a mortal mind to process without breaking.</p>
<p>"Gods do not die the way mortals die," she said at last.</p>
<p>Her voice was hollow. "A god’s existence is not merely flesh and bone; it is tied to belief, to conceptual authority, and to the domain they command."</p>
<p>"If you reach him and you damage that body enough, what you produce is not death in the way you are picturing it..."</p>
<p>"You do not produce a corpse."</p>
<p>"Then what do I produce?" Rex demanded.</p>
<p>"You produce a wound to his authority," Lustia said. "His domain destabilizes."</p>
<p>"His grip on reality slips and his ability to act in the mortal layer weakens, sometimes for years... sometimes permanently depending on how thoroughly the wound is inflicted."</p>
<p>"So I cannot kill him the way I killed Kregg and the others, huh?" Rex said, a bitter edge creeping into his voice. "I can’t just drive steel through his heart and watch him bleed out."</p>
<p>"No," Lustia said firmly. "But you can end him functionally."</p>
<p>"A god with no authority and a destabilized domain is not meaningfully different from a dead one to anyone who would have suffered under his continued action."</p>
<p>"The distinction matters to theologians and scholars..."</p>
<p>"It does not matter to Celestina’s next reincarnator target, and it does not matter to the child you and all your maximum bond women are going to have."</p>
<p>Rex’s jaw set. The anger that had been simmering beneath his skin crystallized into something harder, something sharper.</p>
<p>The need for poetic justice died in his throat, replaced by a cold, utilitarian hunger for vengeance.</p>
<p>"Then that is the version I am planning for," Rex said, his gaze fixed on some distant, bloody horizon. "I do not need a poetic ending."</p>
<p>"I do not need to hear hymns sung at his funeral because I need him unable to touch anything I have built... Ever again."</p>
<p>"That is achievable," Lustia said, her expression one of grim respect. "It will be difficult, though."</p>
<p>"It will be excruciatingly costly. And it will almost certainly require far more than the three skills we are discussing tonight."</p>
<p>"But Rex... it is achievable."</p>
