<p>He sat with that for a long moment. Then Morwenna Nightwing said her name, and the ambiguity he had cultivated so carefully over so many years simply evaporated.</p>
<p>It didn’t crumble and it didn’t burn. It just left, quietly, the way a fog lifts when the wind finally decides to move.</p>
<p>He stood up. He turned toward the sound of combat to the north.</p>
<p>"Damn it," he said, with feeling.</p>
<p>He checked his blade. And then he walked.</p>
<p>In the market square, Brant Hollow stood paralyzed as the golem loomed over him like a mountain of sentient rock.</p>
<p>It had been advancing for nearly a minute with a grinding, inevitable approach that had rooted him completely in place, every survival instinct he possessed screaming two contradictory things simultaneously: run and you will not outrun it.</p>
<p>His mouth had gone dry. The weapon in his hand felt like a child’s toy.</p>
<p>Valentina’s voice hit him between one heartbeat and the next.</p>
<p>"I know you are afraid."</p>
<p>Something loosened in his chest.</p>
<p>"He is giving you the chance to walk into your cage with your head held high."</p>
<p>Something else loosened.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer does not want partners. It wants fuel."</p>
<p>Brant Hollow looked up at the golem. He looked at the golem’s stone face, which reflected a dead, borrowed light behind its eyes—not the light of something that chose to be here, but the light of something that had been placed here, aimed, and instructed to continue.</p>
<p>A weapon. Just a weapon. An expensive, enormous, terrible weapon.</p>
<p>He exhaled once, slow and deliberate, and the fear in his eyes died.</p>
<p>It didn’t become courage exactly. It became something quieter than courage and harder than it, like a grim, settled certainty that if this was the moment, then this was the moment, and he would not spend it on his knees.</p>
<p>"Alright," he said softly, almost to himself. "Alright."</p>
<p>He moved.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Rex watched this unfolding rebellion with a smirk that didn’t reach his eyes, reflecting pure, narcissistic amusement. The individual persuasion approach, his elegant, subtle chess game, was failing.</p>
<p>He had tried to win their hearts, but Valentina and Morwenna were winning their souls. The roar of a collective awakening drowned out his single, grand speech.</p>
<p>He tracked Ren Askar’s re-engagement. He tracked Veran Caulstow’s direction change.</p>
<p>He tracked the energy signature from the market square shifting from paralytic to kinetic. He watched Morwenna’s golem relay dismantling in the north with the dispassionate precision of someone cataloguing data that no longer surprised him.</p>
<p>"The platform was wrong," he said aloud, quietly, analytically.</p>
<p>There was no heat in it. It was simply the cold, analytical process of a mind discarding a failed variable.</p>
<p>"The approach was too soft."</p>
<p>He had spent his first hours on this island playing a game that rewarded subtlety. He focused on the careful application of choice and the engineering of voluntary compliance.</p>
<p>It had been elegant. He had enjoyed it the way he always enjoyed work that required more intelligence than force.</p>
<p>But Valentina and Morwenna weren’t engaging with the game. They were burning the board.</p>
<p>His jaw tightened. Just slightly once.</p>
<p>"I have been playing politics," he said, and his voice dropped to a lower register—not louder, but fundamentally different, like the distinction between a clear sky and the moment before lightning. "I should have been playing a completely different game."</p>
<p>He turned his gaze to the full extent of his golem network, spread across the island like a nervous system waiting for a signal.</p>
<p>The smirk faded. What replaced it was not anger, and it was not rage.</p>
<p>Those were noisy things, and Rex, at his most dangerous, was very hushed. If they wanted a war of ideals, he would give them a war of extinction.</p>
<p>He made a different decision.</p>
<p>Rex’s smirk deepened into a jagged, predatory grin. The time for the subtle dance of diplomacy was dead, buried under the weight of his growing impatience.</p>
<p>He didn’t just want their obedience; he wanted their absolute, trembling realization that they were standing on the very thing that could crush them into a red paste.</p>
<p>With a violent, mental shove, Rex forced the Earthen Authority through all eleven golems simultaneously.</p>
<p>He wasn’t concerned about finesse. He wasn’t concerned about the delicate calibration of their movements.</p>
<p>He cranked their operational output to a level that bypassed precision and entered the realm of sheer, overwhelming presence.</p>
<p>The golems ceased to be eleven individual sentinels. In a terrifying display of synchronized power, they merged into a singular, terrifying consciousness: eleven jagged expressions of a single, geological god.</p>
<p>It was as if the island itself had woken up, grown teeth, and decided to bite back. They weren’t merely hostile; they were monumental.</p>
<p>The ground began to groan, a deep, tectonic shriek that vibrated in the marrow of every living soul on the island. Around each golem, the very substrate of the earth erupted.</p>
<p>Massive slabs of granite and basalt tore themselves from the soil, spiraling upward in a violent, swirling vortex of debris. Within seconds, each golem was anchored to a twenty-foot-wide base of jagged, unyielding stone.</p>
<p>They weren’t just standing on the land; they were part of it, an unstoppable extension of the island’s own crushing weight. Then, Rex unleashed the true horror.</p>
<p>He couldn’t gift them his system-granted divinity, but he could do something far more visceral. He channeled the Earthen Authority’s geological essence, the ability to command the very medium of stone, and flooded it into the golems’ construction.</p>
<p>He turned the island’s crust into a conductor for his will. The stone wasn’t just a tool anymore; it was an active, hungry weapon.</p>
<p>The carnage began with a roar of shifting earth.</p>
<p>At Caldmere, the golem looming over Brant Hollow didn’t just move; it commanded the world to close in. With a deafening crack of splintering masonry, the nearby stone walls surged forward like the jaws of a beast.</p>
<p>They didn’t just block the market square; they slammed shut, sealing the Academy fighters and the Apostles inside a claustrophobic tomb of grinding rock. The air was instantly thick with pulverized dust and the terrified screams of those caught in the sudden, violent reconfiguration of their world.</p>
<p>In the northern agricultural fields, the slaughter was more explicit. The Apostle fighters, emboldened by Valentina’s psychic surge, had surged forward to reclaim the land, their magic flaring in defiance.</p>
<p>But they had underestimated the new, brutal reality of the golems. The golems didn’t just strike; they pulverized.</p>
<p>A massive stone fist slammed into a fighter, the sound of a ribcage shattering like dry kindling echoing across the fields. Blood sprayed against the grey granite, a vivid, sickening crimson against the cold stone.</p>
<p>The "re-engagement" was a massacre; the fighters’ hope was being systematically ground into the dirt by the sheer, unyielding mass of the earth.</p>
<p>Near the outer residential district, Ren Askar was a man possessed. Driven by the hero system’s call to action, he moved toward the plaza with a grim, singular purpose, his eyes burning with the fire of a man who had found his crusade.</p>
<p>He was a whirlwind of motion, a beacon of resistance cutting through the chaos.</p>
<p>He didn’t see the ground ripple. He didn’t feel the subtle shift in the vibration beneath his boots.</p>
<p>The golem he thought he had neutralized, the one he had bled for, the one he thought was a corpse of stone wasn’t dead. It had been waiting. It had been repositioning.</p>
<p>As Ren took a heavy, determined stride, the earth beneath him didn’t just crack; it exploded. The massive, jagged form of the golem erupted from the bedrock directly under his feet, a violent geyser of stone and fury.</p>
<p>The sudden, upward thrust caught him mid-stride, the sheer force of the emergence threatening to snap his spine as the world turned into a chaotic blur of rising rock and sudden, terrifying height.</p>
<p>Rex watched the chaos through the eyes of his stone titans, his chest heaving with a dark, intoxicating exhilaration. The sight of the earth erupting, of the blood spraying like festive confetti against the grey granite, sent a surge of pure, unadulterated dopamine through his veins.</p>
<p>He wasn’t just a leader anymore; he was a god of the carnage, and the sheer, beautiful violence of it made him want to laugh until his lungs burned.</p>
<p>"Look at them!" Rex roared, his voice booming not just through the air but vibrating through the very golems he commanded, a psychic tremor of pure malice. "Look at these pathetic, crawling insects!"</p>
<p>"They think they can choose?!"</p>
<p>"They think they can negotiate with the inevitable?!"</p>
<p>"I’ve given all of you a chance, and now this is what you all give me?!"</p>
<p>"PAY. THE. FUCKING. PRICEEEE!!!"</p>
