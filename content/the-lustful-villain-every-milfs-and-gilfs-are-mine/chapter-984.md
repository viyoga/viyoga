<p>Her voice took on a desperate edge, cutting through the stale air of the hall. "I do not want to talk about Theo."</p>
<p>"I do not want to hear about Tremor, or conspiracies, or the lies that are rotting this house from the inside out."</p>
<p>"I want to hit something that will actually hit back..."</p>
<p>"I want to feel the impact of a blow that doesn’t break. And I want it to be you, Rex... because you are the only person left in this godforsaken household strong enough that I do not have to hold back."</p>
<p>Rex studied her for a long moment. He saw the tremor in her hands, the way her pupils were blown wide with adrenaline and grief.</p>
<p>He knew that if he refused, she would either collapse or destroy herself against the training post. He stepped back into the center of the open floor, rolling his shoulders, his stance shifting from passive observer to lethal combatant.</p>
<p>"Come then," he said, his voice dropping into the low, focused register of a man preparing for violence.</p>
<p>She exploded toward him.</p>
<p>There was no warm-up, no measured approach. Aurelia launched herself at him with a ferocity that made the air whistle.</p>
<p>Every ounce of her accumulated rage, every sleepless hour spent wondering if her son was a monster, and every drop of blood she had spilled that morning was channeled into a single, devastating opening gambit.</p>
<p>Her strikes were clean decades of flawless mastery holding her form together, but they were fueled by a desperation that bordered on suicidal.</p>
<p>Rex met her in the center of the ring. He didn’t match her recklessness; foolishness got you killed, but he matched her intensity.</p>
<p>When her fist connected with his forearm in a lightning-fast jab, the impact sounded like a hammer hitting stone.</p>
<p>When he countered with a controlled elbow to her ribs, he felt the solid, terrifying strength behind her movement.</p>
<p>The engagement stretched on, becoming a brutal dance of bone against bone. The training hall echoed with the wet slap of skin, the grinding of teeth, and the ragged, guttural sounds of two people pushed to their physical limits.</p>
<p>Aurelia was relentless. She pressed sequence after sequence, spinning back fists; delivered devastating knee strikes; and palm-heeled aimed at his solar plexus, each one delivered with the intent to maim.</p>
<p>She wasn’t fighting to win a point; she was fighting to exorcise a demon.</p>
<p>’Strike. Breathe. Strike. Breathe.’</p>
<p>Her inner monologue was a storm of static: ’Hit him. Break something.’</p>
<p>’Make the pain outside match the pain inside.’</p>
<p>’Don’t stop. If I stop, the truth catches up.’</p>
<p>’If I stop, I have to face what I did to Theo.’</p>
<p>She was magnificent and terrifying. But grief is a poison that clouds even the sharpest mind. As the minutes ticked by, her technique began to fray at the edges.</p>
<p>The fury that gave her strength was also eating her discipline. She became aggressive in ways that were surgically precise but tactically suicidal, leaving microfractures in her defense gaps that a lesser fighter would have died in.</p>
<p>Rex saw them. He saw the way her weight shifted too heavily on her left foot during a roundhouse kick; he saw the way she lowered her chin when she lunged for a finishing blow.</p>
<p>More than once, he could have shattered her ribs with a precise counter or swept her legs to end the fight instantly. But he didn’t.</p>
<p>He chose not to exploit the openings. He allowed her to work through the exchange on her own terms, playing the role of the immovable object so she could continue to be the unstoppable force.</p>
<p>He was giving her the only thing she could still understand: a struggle she could control.</p>
<p>Then came the breaking point.</p>
<p>Exhaustion finally breached her defenses. Aurelia committed to a vicious three-part combination: left hook, right cross, and spinning elbow, the same sequence that had served her so well throughout the night.</p>
<p>But on the final rotation, her foot slipped fractionally on a patch of sweat and blood on the mat. Her balance shifted too far forward, her center of gravity betraying her.</p>
<p>Rex caught the moment with predatory timing. He didn’t strike her; he simply moved.</p>
<p>As she came spinning into the space where he had been, he stepped inside her arc and redirected her momentum with the cold efficiency of a lever. He planted his foot and executed a controlled, devastating sweep.</p>
<p>The world tilted violently for Aurelia. Her legs were swept from under her, and her own momentum carried her downward like a felled oak.</p>
<p>She hit the reinforced wooden floor hard, a bone-jarring impact that sent a shockwave through her spine and forced every last molecule of air from her lungs in a strangled wheeze.</p>
<p>She lay there, sprawled on the polished boards, the silence of the hall rushing back in to fill the space where her fury had been. Her chest heaved in violent, shuddering spasms as she fought to reclaim her breath.</p>
<p>Her raw, split knuckles rested against the wood, stained dark with blood, while she simply stared up at the high stone ceiling, watching the moonlight dance across the rafters while the echoes of the fight died away in the hollow ache of her bones.</p>
<p>Rex crossed the blood-slicked distance between them, each footfall heavy against the floorboards that still hummed with the violence of their exchange. He didn’t tower over her; instead, he knelt beside her, bringing himself down to her level.</p>
<p>Slowly, deliberately, he extended his hand. His knuckles were scraped and his forearm bore the angry red welts where her strikes had landed, but his grip remained unshakable.</p>
<p>Aurelia stared at his outstretched palm. For several heartbeats, she didn’t move, her pupils dilated as she stared at the hand that had just been her adversary.</p>
<p>Her breathing was still a series of ragged, wet gasps. Then, with a tremor she couldn’t quite suppress, she reached up and grasped it.</p>
<p>When he pulled her up, the movement was deceptively gentle. He anchored her as she found her footing, steadying her against the dizziness that follows such primal exertion.</p>
<p>For several long moments, they simply stood there, two predators caught in the aftermath of a hunt, their bodies close enough to share heat.</p>
<p>The space between them wasn’t empty; it was charged, vibrating with the residual adrenaline and the raw, bleeding truths they had just hammered out of each other.</p>
<p>Aurelia finally turned her gaze toward him, and the look she gave him was devastating. It wasn’t anger, nor was it gratitude.</p>
<p>It was a weary, corrosive disgust, a self-directed contempt that seemed to seep from her bones. It was the expression of a woman who had just realized she had exposed her most vulnerable nerve to someone who might not be worthy of seeing it.</p>
<p>"I hate how much better I feel," she whispered, her voice cracking like dried parchment. "I hate that after all that... after all that violence... I feel lighter."</p>
<p>"I hate that doing this with you was more effective than any hour I spent alone."</p>
<p>"That is not something to hate, Aurelia," Rex said. His voice was a low rumble in the cavernous hall, steady as a heartbeat.</p>
<p>"Is it not?" she challenged, a bitter laugh catching in her throat. "I have spent thirty years being the pillar."</p>
<p>"The unmoving stone... The one everyone else leans on when their own worlds begin to fracture."</p>
<p>"I have forgotten how to be the one who needs support..."</p>
<p>"I do not know what to do with the reverse of that feeling, Rex, and I resent you deeply for how effortlessly you have made me want to surrender to it."</p>
<p>She studied him then, her eyes raking over his face, searching for the lie, searching for the crack in his composure. Her inner monologue was a battlefield of its own.</p>
<p>’Why does he feel like the only solid thing left?’</p>
<p>’Why does his presence make the chaos outside this room seem manageable?’</p>
<p>’I should push him away. I should reassert control. But I am so goddamn tired of being strong.’</p>
<p>A silence fell between them, heavy and suffocating, broken only by the distant drip of water somewhere in the bowels of the estate. Finally, something in her expression shifted.</p>
<p>The defensive posture collapsed, replaced by something terrifyingly honest.</p>
<p>"Tonight," she said, her voice gaining a sudden, dangerous clarity. "Come find me tonight."</p>
<p>"Do not send a message... Do not send an aide... Come to my quarters." She took a step closer, close enough that he could see the flecks of gold in her exhausted eyes. "I am done pretending."</p>
<p>"I am done pretending I have the strength left to manage this madness on my own."</p>
<p>"I am tired, Rex... genuinely, bone-deep tired of holding this entire household together while everyone around me crumbles into pieces."</p>
<p>Her hand moved, almost involuntarily, brushing against the fabric of his sleeve. "Whatever it is you want from me."</p>
<p>"Whatever truth you have been holding back."</p>
<p>"Say it to me tonight. Say it plainly, without the games, without the strategy."</p>
<p>"Say it like you say everything else. And I promise you... I will actually listen."</p>
<p>Then, something happened that Rex had never seen from the matriarch of this house. A slow, private smile spread across her lips.</p>
<p>It wasn’t the smile of a diplomat or a mother; it was the smile of someone who had finally stopped fighting a losing war and had decided to embrace the enemy.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a kind smile.</p>
<p>It was something far more dangerous. It was hungry.</p>
