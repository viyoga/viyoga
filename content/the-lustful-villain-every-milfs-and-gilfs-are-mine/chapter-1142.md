<p>While the front lines became a meat grinder of divine steel and demonic blood, Mordecai Vrael was fighting a different kind of war, a desperate, sweaty, terrified scramble for relevance.</p>
<p>He fought somewhere in the secondary rear formation, and frankly, he fought badly. If anyone had asked him honestly how he was doing, he would have admitted he was one bad swing away from becoming a stain on the cobblestones.</p>
<p>’Please work, please work, please God, just let something good come out of this stupid system,’ Mordecai screamed internally.</p>
<p>His hands were shaking so violently he could barely maintain the magical focus required to interface with the Infinite Gacha System. The air around him smelled of sulfur and panic.</p>
<p>"Come on! Give me something! Anything!" he shrieked, his voice cracking as an Aethelgard crossbow bolt whistled past his ear, embedding itself in the stone with a deadly thunk.</p>
<p>With a grunt of pure desperation, he triggered three summons in rapid succession. Three pillars of blinding gold light erupted across the rear formation, momentarily drowning out the crimson chaos of Cassandra’s massacre.</p>
<p>Through the haze of light, the units resolved: two SSR-ranked combatants materialized with a thunderous crash of displaced air, their armor gleaming with celestial authority.</p>
<p>Thanks to the residual energy Rex had been pumping into the battlefield; these weren’t just standard summons, they were juggernauts.</p>
<p>A third unit, a support class priestess, immediately planted her staff, casting a shimmering barrier that stabilized a section of the line that had been seconds away from collapsing under a barrage of enchanted arrows.</p>
<p>The summons tore into the Aethelgard advance with a ferocity that Mordecai could only dream of. They moved like living storms, their weapons carving through steel and bone with effortless cruelty.</p>
<p>Mordecai himself? He killed exactly two soldiers during the entire engagement.</p>
<p>Both times, it had been by complete accident: the first when he tripped and his dagger accidentally found a gap in a knight’s gorget, and the second when a stray magical discharge from one of his summons blew a soldier backward into his path.</p>
<p>’I’m going to die, I’m going to die, I’m going to die,’ his mind looped as an Aethelgard sergeant charged him, sword raised high.</p>
<p>Mordecai froze, eyes wide with terror, only for two Orc warriors to suddenly materialize between them. The Orcs didn’t say a word; they simply stepped into his space, their massive bodies forming a living wall that absorbed the sergeant’s blow.</p>
<p>They weren’t protecting him out of loyalty; they were protecting their summoner because if he died, the summons died. Mordecai didn’t notice; he just huddled behind them, breathing in ragged, terrified gasps.</p>
<p>In stark contrast to Mordecai’s shambolic terror, Pavellia Vostara moved through the mid formation like a lethal whisper. Where others were covered in gore and sweat, she remained eerily pristine, a vision of deadly elegance amidst the slaughter.</p>
<p>Fwish. Fwish. Fwish.</p>
<p>The sound of her feathers cutting through the air was almost musical. The iridescent plumage along her arms and back detached at her whim, transforming into razor-sharp projectiles.</p>
<p>Each feather found its mark with unerring accuracy: an eye socket here, a jugular there, and a gap in the visor there. She didn’t need to close the distance; she simply reaped from afar.</p>
<p>An Aethelgard squad attempted to close the gap on her, charging with shields raised. Pavellia didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>With a subtle flick of her wrists, she sent a volley of feathers cascading downward. They didn’t just strike the soldiers; they carpeted the stone in front of her in a bristling field of deadly shafts.</p>
<p>The charging knights tripped over the makeshift barrier, their footing lost on the blood-slicked stone, and before they could recover, Pavellia’s next volley descended.</p>
<p>Thwack thwack thwack.</p>
<p>The feathers drove through flesh and bone like poisoned needles, pinning the fallen men to the ground.</p>
<p>"Hold the third formation."</p>
<p>"Three paces left... Close the gap," she commanded.</p>
<p>Her voice was quiet, crisp, and utterly devoid of the adrenaline-fueled screaming that defined the rest of the battle. Her subordinates obeyed instantly, moving like clockwork under her direction.</p>
<p>Behind his mask, Rex Rexilion watched her. He was calculating trajectories, energy expenditures, and casualty rates, but his attention kept drifting back to her.</p>
<p>His system, that omniscient, unerring tool that could read the deepest desires and darkest secrets of any living thing, had never once produced a reading for Pavellia Vostara.</p>
<p>He had scanned her countless times. He had looked for fear, for ambition, for hunger, for love.</p>
<p>But there was nothing. No number. No error message. No ’Null.’ It was as if the very concept of ’desire’ was a language she didn’t speak.</p>
<p>She existed outside the framework of need.</p>
<p>’What are you?’ Rex wondered, his eyes narrowing as he watched her pluck another feather from her wing to send it spiraling through a soldier’s throat. ’Are you even alive, or are you something else entirely wearing a human shape?’</p>
<p>It was a puzzle, a dangerous, profound anomaly in his perfect calculations. But as a fresh wave of Aethelgard cavalry thundered toward them, Rex pushed the thought aside.</p>
<p>He would solve her later. Right now, he had a war to win.</p>
<p>From the rear of the formation, Viscaria Amorphis conducted her symphony of gelatinous horror. The slime contingent was not merely a defensive line; they were a living, breathing counter spell.</p>
<p>Aethelgard’s mages unleashed torrents of concentrated fire and jagged ice magic, expecting to incinerate the rear ranks. Instead, the spells struck the translucent green mass of the slime soldiers and simply... vanished.</p>
<p>Sssssss Fwoosh.</p>
<p>The magical projectiles were swallowed whole. The slimes absorbed the energy with a contemptuous ease, their bodies rippling as they dissolved the spell structures on contact, only to reconstitute seconds later, thicker and more resilient than before.</p>
<p>Viscaria herself was a force of fluid devastation. When a concentrated barrage of enchanted crossbow bolts threatened to pierce the demon rear ranks, she threw her hands forward.</p>
<p>"Rise and shield!" she commanded, her voice echoing with a wet, gurgling undertone.</p>
<p>Massive liquid walls erupted from the ground, twenty meters across, surging upward to intercept the volley. The bolts struck the viscous barrier with wet thwips, embedding themselves harmlessly in the gelatinous mass.</p>
<p>But Viscaria fought with a new, guarded caution. Between each massive exertion of will, her hand would instinctively drift to the visible curve of her midsection, a subconscious gesture of protection that betrayed her altered priorities.</p>
<p>Twice, she deliberately pulled back from positions where she could have held the line with absolute certainty, choosing survival over martyrdom. She knew now that she was more valuable alive than as another splatter of gore on the corridor floor.</p>
<p>While Viscaria held the space, Lilith Nocturne owned the darkness.</p>
<p>Moving through the shadows at the corridor’s edge like smoke poured from a vial, Lilith was a ghost in the machinery of war. She was invisible to both armies, a predator watching prey that didn’t know she existed.</p>
<p>When two Nightwing scouts attempted to creep along a side seam toward a section of the corridor where Rex’s trap mechanisms were dangerously exposed, Lilith intervened.</p>
<p>She didn’t kill them yet. Instead, she leaned into the darkness, her lips curling into a cruel smile.</p>
<p>"Lost, little birds?" she whispered, though her voice was nothing more than a ripple in the air.</p>
<p>She wove a whispered succubus illusion across their minds. To the scouts, the corridor suddenly seemed familiar, safe, and already surveyed.</p>
<p>They turned around, walking confidently back toward their own lines, convinced they had already completed their mission. Lilith watched them go, her heart thrumming with a dark, intoxicating satisfaction.</p>
<p>She would never admit it, not even under the most exquisite torture, but she felt a fierce, possessive pride: Rex had trusted her with the unseen. He had given her the most dangerous job on the battlefield, and she was savoring every moment of it.</p>
<p>Yet, amidst the screaming metal and the wet sounds of slaughter, there were moments of eerie, unspoken connection moments that would never appear in any official war report because no one outside the immediate participants could possibly understand them.</p>
<p>High above on a jagged rock shelf, Talyra drew back her bowstring, the wood creaking ominously. Her target was a demon commander who was currently cleaving through an Aethelgard knight, sending a spray of arterial blood across the stones.</p>
<p>But as she lined up her shot, her sightline crossed directly with Cassandra Vexmoor’s.</p>
<p>For half a heartbeat, the two women locked eyes across the hellscape of the corridor. Talyra, the cold precision of the archer; Cassandra, the crimson fury of the Demon Queen.</p>
<p>Neither moved. No challenge was issued; no hostility was expressed. The air between them seemed to freeze, a silent acknowledgment of two predators operating in the same territory.</p>
<p>Then, as quickly as the connection formed, it shattered. Talyra shifted her aim to a different target with practiced indifference, and Cassandra turned her back to resume her butchery.</p>
<p>Nothing was acknowledged, because in this war, acknowledgment was a luxury they couldn’t afford.</p>
<p>A few meters away, the connection was even more visceral. Nerith thrust her hands toward the eastern wall, summoning a wall of thorned vines to trap a squad of Aethelgard soldiers.</p>
<p>As the green tendrils surged upward, she caught Gorvasha’s gaze. The Orc chieftain was watching her from twelve meters away, her face splattered with blood, her eyes burning with something that looked suspiciously like approval.</p>
<p>Nerith’s breath hitched. Her concentration wavered.</p>
<p>The vine wall she summoned came up three feet shorter than it should have, with a deliberate, almost imperceptible flaw.</p>
<p>"Now!" Gorvasha roared, though not to her own troops.</p>
