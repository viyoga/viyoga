<p>The first four of the Vanguard were already breaking into a sprint, their boots thundering against the hardwood. They were the hammer, designed to engage Rex directly, forcing him into a defensive posture and creating chaos through sheer physical pressure.</p>
<p>The next four in the support layer were moving into flanking positions, angling themselves to exploit any opening the Vanguard created. Their role was to intercept Rex if he attempted to slip past the first wave, cutting off escape routes and applying pressure from the angles.</p>
<p>In the final four, the Reserves remained slightly behind the others, moving with a controlled urgency. They were the safety net, the tactical response force ready to plug any gaps or reinforce a failing position.</p>
<p>’Four... four... four...’ Rex analyzed, his breathing slowing even as his adrenaline spiked. ’A textbook encirclement pattern optimized for single target neutralization.’</p>
<p>’They expect me to defend, to retreat, to get trapped...’</p>
<p>’They are fighting a human opponent and they have not yet realized they are fighting a catastrophe.</p>
<p>His inner monologue remained eerily calm, a stark contrast to the storm brewing in the room. ’The Vanguard will come fast, high-impact, low-finesse...’</p>
<p>’The Support will try to hamstring my mobility...’</p>
<p>’The Reserves will wait for the moment of exhaustion...’</p>
<p>’I need to shatter the first wave quickly to disrupt the entire geometry...’</p>
<p>’If the first four fall, the architecture collapses.’</p>
<p>Aurelia leaned forward, her breath catching in her throat. She could see it the way Rex wasn’t bracing for a fight but simply occupying the space where the fight would happen.</p>
<p>He looked less like a combatant and more like a fixed point around which the world was about to break.</p>
<p>The first wave was thirty feet away. Their faces were masks of grim determination, some hungry for the glory of the struggle, others hollowed out by the dawning realization of what was about to unfold.</p>
<p>The air screamed as they closed the distance.</p>
<p>Rex planted his feet, his weight settling into the floor as the system calibrated his center of gravity for the inevitable impact. He waited for the first blow, the first crack of bone, the first taste of the violence he had been invited to unleash.</p>
<p>The first wave struck like a coordinated detonation.</p>
<p>Marren was the first to breach Rex’s perimeter. He moved with the terrifying, controlled explosiveness of a predator, all kinetic energy channeled through a perfectly synchronized chain of muscle and bone.</p>
<p>His strike was clean, a textbook entry designed to overwhelm a target with pure momentum.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t retreat. He didn’t brace for impact.</p>
<p>His Martial Arts Mastery flashed a critical data point in his peripheral vision: Leverage Point: Right Brachial Axis. Redirection Efficiency: 98%.</p>
<p>Rex stepped inside the arc of Marren’s strike, his movement so precise it seemed almost ghostly. He didn’t block; he simply placed a single hand at the exact pivot point where Marren’s momentum was most vulnerable.</p>
<p>With a subtle, violent twist of his wrist and shoulder, Rex redirected the force.</p>
<p>Marren didn’t just miss; he was violently slung sideways. His own momentum became his enemy, hurling him across the hardwood as if he had been struck by a runaway carriage.</p>
<p>He crashed into the floor with a bone-jarring thud, skidding several feet before his training instincts kicked in, allowing him to roll and absorb the impact rather than breaking his neck.</p>
<p>"First point," Aurelia’s voice rang out, cold and clinical as she scored the exchange from the sidelines. "Marren, your entry was flawless, but your recovery was sluggish."</p>
<p>"You left your flank exposed for three tenths of a second too long."</p>
<p>Marren gasped for air as he pushed himself up, his ribs aching from the sudden deceleration. He said nothing. In this arena, words were wasted energy; only survival mattered.</p>
<p>The second and third fighters Mael and Sela erupted from opposite angles simultaneously. This was the Support Layer’s immediate response: if the first man failed, the next two would overwhelm the target before he could reset.</p>
<p>To any other fighter, this would require a parallel defense, splitting attention between two threats. But Rex’s processing speed allowed him to treat them as a sequence.</p>
<p>Mael came in with a straight, direct thrust, a committed strike meant to punch through Rex’s guard. Rex didn’t telegraph his movement.</p>
<p>He waited until Mael was within the ’Red Zone,’ the range where the cost of redirection was minimal, and then he pivoted. With the economy of a predator, Rex caught Mael’s wrist and shoulder, turning his forward momentum ninety degrees.</p>
<p>Mael flew past Rex like wind caught in a sail, stumbling past without touching a single inch of Rex’s body.</p>
<p>Half a second later, Sela arrived. She was faster than Mael, her mind already processing the failure of her comrade.</p>
<p>She saw Rex’s position shifting and immediately adjusted her trajectory mid-sprint, angling her strike to intercept where Rex would be. It was a brilliant tactical adjustment, one that would have caught any ordinary master.</p>
<p>But Sela was calculating based on a human’s reaction time. Rex was operating beyond it.</p>
<p>Her strike carved through empty air. Rex had already vacated the space she was targeting, his body having already begun its rotation toward her before she even finished her adjustment.</p>
<p>The gap between her assumption and his reality was a chasm.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for her to recover. He punished her instinct before she could capitalize on it.</p>
<p>As she stumbled forward from the missed strike, Rex’s palm connected with her solar plexus not with a punch but with a precise, explosive burst of force designed to collapse the diaphragm.</p>
<p>Sela hit the floor hard, the air leaving her lungs in a pained wheeze. Even as she collapsed, her eyes remained locked on Rex, her mind already racing to formulate a counterstrategy even as her body screamed in protest.</p>
<p>"Sela," Aurelia called out, her voice carrying a rare note of grudging approval. "That recovery instinct is the only reason you aren’t still unconscious on the floor."</p>
<p>"Your adjustment was perfect, but your timing was human... Against him, ’human’ is a death sentence."</p>
<p>Rex stood motionless in the center of the hall, his breathing as steady as if he were standing in a garden. His inner monologue remained cold and analytical. ’Three down... Nine to go.’</p>
<p>’The Vanguard is depleted, but the Support Layer is already recalibrating.’</p>
<p>’They aren’t panicked; they’re learning...’</p>
<p>’That makes them more dangerous...’</p>
<p>’They’re beginning to realize that they can’t fight him; they can only hope to survive him.’</p>
<p>The other nine fighters began to close in, their movements no longer just coordinated but desperate. The air in the room grew thick with the metallic tang of sweat and the unspoken promise of breaking bones.</p>
<p>The fourth fighter came low, moving like a shadow across the hardwood. Rex recognized the signature of his approach immediately through the system’s analysis: this wasn’t brute force; this was technical predation.</p>
<p>The man was the unit’s specialist in close-quarters grappling and ground-level takedowns. His technique was a sophisticated trap, a lightning-fast ankle sweep designed to sever Rex’s foundation, immediately followed by a body grab to pin him while he was off balance.</p>
<p>It was a move designed to exploit the momentary weight shift a fighter makes after engaging three consecutive opponents.</p>
<p>But Rex didn’t play by the rules of human momentum.</p>
<p>In the quarter second before contact, his Martial Arts Mastery triggered a micro adjustment. His weight didn’t just shift; it redistributed with impossible fluidity, rising slightly onto the balls of his feet.</p>
<p>The sweep struck air. The specialist’s leg passed harmlessly beneath Rex’s lifted calf.</p>
<p>Desperate to complete the sequence, the fighter lunged upward to execute the body grab. His hands tore at Rex’s clothes, but instead of finding the soft give of the torso, they collided with the iron-hard cordage of Rex’s forearm.</p>
<p>The leverage differential was catastrophic. The grab was calibrated to crush ribs and control a center of mass; against a forearm, it was useless, leaving the specialist exposed and overextended.</p>
<p>In one smooth motion, Rex’s hand shot down like a piston. He didn’t punch; he seized the fighter’s wrist and collar in a vice grip.</p>
<p>The specialist’s eyes widened as he realized he was no longer the hunter but a puppet caught in a snare.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t throw him. A throw would have sent him crashing into the stone walls with a bone-splintering impact.</p>
<p>Instead, Rex performed a controlled deposition; he simply placed the man back on the floor with the terrifying precision of someone who had decided that the lesson was not in the pain of the impact but in the absolute helplessness of the failed technique.</p>
<p>"That was generous," Aurelia’s voice cut through the sudden silence, colder than before. "Do not mistake restraint for weakness."</p>
<p>"Do not get used to generosity because the world outside this hall will not set you down gently; it will grind you into the dirt."</p>
<p>The specialist stayed on one knee, breathing hard, staring at his own shaking hands. He had been neutralized without Rex even breaking a sweat.</p>
<p>Then, the second wave arrived.</p>
<p>These four moved differently. The raw, reckless confidence of the Vanguard had evaporated, replaced by something far more dangerous: professional caution.</p>
<p>They moved in a staggered formation, each keeping a watchful eye on their companions while maintaining a measured distance. They were assessing, probing, waiting for the moment Rex overextended.</p>
<p>Rex watched them through the amber HUD of his mind.</p>
<p>’Caution,’ he thought bitterly. ’A double-edged blade...’</p>
<p>’You’ve learned not to rush into his reach, but in doing so, you’ve lost the one thing that could hurt him: commitment.’</p>
<p>’You’re hesitating. And in a fight against someone who moves faster than thought, hesitation is where you die.’</p>
