<p>"Rex Rexilion," Diana intervened before the guard could find his voice, her tone clipped and absolute. "Aurelia invited him yesterday."</p>
<p>The assessment cycle lasted barely two seconds, a silent calculation of authority and recognition before the massive mechanisms of the gate groaned and began to part.</p>
<p>As they crossed the threshold into the courtyard, they found Elaris. Whether it was sheer coincidence or simply the Elaris-esque talent for appearing exactly where the atmosphere was most charged, she was already there, seated in the morning light.</p>
<p>She held a teacup with deceptive delicacy, her expression stripped of the formal armor she wore in public. When her eyes landed on Rex, that familiar, genuine brightening touched her features, the look of someone who had just encountered something unexpectedly refreshing in a world of tedious obligations.</p>
<p>"Rex Rexilion," she said, a smile playing at the corners of her mouth. "You are early."</p>
<p>"Aurelia made an offer," Rex replied, his voice carrying the blunt honesty of a man who had no interest in games. "I am following through."</p>
<p>Elaris lowered her cup, studying him over the rim with a sharp, appreciative intelligence. She wasn’t just looking at him; she was cataloguing his readiness, his intent, and the quiet intensity that seemed to radiate from him like heat from stone.</p>
<p>"She will be pleased," Elaris said. "She was discussing it at breakfast this morning."</p>
<p>"Specifically, she noted that she didn’t expect anyone to follow through before the end of the week."</p>
<p>"She expected me to take time," Rex said.</p>
<p>"She expected most people to take time," Elaris corrected, her gaze softening with a warmth that danced on the edge of something more personal than mere professional respect. "An offer like hers carries a specific social weight, a debt of confrontation."</p>
<p>"Most people respond by inventing delays, by waiting until the urgency has faded and the obligation feels less like a threat."</p>
<p>"You, however..." she paused, her eyes bright. "You are not most recipients."</p>
<p>Rex met her look with an easy, unforced expression, the one he reserved for Elaris, acknowledging her teasing without the need to deflect or perform discomfort.</p>
<p>"Is she at the training hall?" Rex asked, cutting through the pleasantries.</p>
<p>"Since the fifth hour," Elaris said, her tone shifting back to information. "She is instructing the household’s elite unit this morning."</p>
<p>"Twelve of their finest... She typically runs them through two hours of hell before she moves to individual work."</p>
<p>Rex’s mind immediately began to calculate: if she had been there since the fifth hour and was running two-hour drills, she would be approaching the individual sessions precisely when he arrived.</p>
<p>The timing was perfect. The opportunity was raw.</p>
<p>"Mrs. Elaris," Diana said, stepping forward with the precise register of respect owed to a family elder.</p>
<p>"Diana," Elaris replied warmly. "I am glad you’re here."</p>
<p>"Theo has been hunting for an excuse to be anywhere else this morning, and you are exactly the kind of ’anywhere else’ he requires."</p>
<p>Diana’s expression flickered through several rapid emotions before settling into something sharper. "Where is he?"</p>
<p>"The garden," Elaris said, gesturing vaguely with her cup. "Pretending to read... but we both know he isn’t reading a single word."</p>
<p>Diana turned to Rex, her eyes betraying a conflict: the pull between her own objectives and her instinct to intervene in Theo’s avoidance. She hesitated, caught between two desires.</p>
<p>"Go," Rex said, his voice gentle but firm, cutting through her indecision. "I know where the training hall is."</p>
<p>Diana lingered for another heartbeat, weighing the cost of her absence.</p>
<p>"Go," Rex repeated, adding a note of reassurance that assured her he didn’t need a guardian, but then he starts to get close to her just to whisper right to her ear. "Make sure you’ll act like you’ve forgiven him because I want his hope to at least come back just for a bit."</p>
<p>"I still hate it..."</p>
<p>"You’ll get a reward..."</p>
<p>"Alright then..." Diana agreed right away.</p>
<p>With one final, complicated look, Diana turned toward the garden.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait. He turned toward the inner gate, his stride purposeful as he moved toward the rear of the estate.</p>
<p>Somewhere ahead, the sounds of disciplined violence, the wet slap of flesh against flesh, and the harsh breathing of elite warriors were beginning to echo through the stone corridors. He was walking into the lion’s den, and he intended to make it count.</p>
<p>The training hall loomed at the rear of the estate, a brutalist monument to violence. This wasn’t some gilded gymnasium for nobles to posture in; it was a dedicated facility designed with the cold, architectural investment of a family that viewed combat training as their primary survival instinct.</p>
<p>The walls were thick, unadorned stone, built to absorb both magical backlash and the literal impact of bodies being hurled against them. The entrance was not a door but a wide, yawning archway designed for rapid deployment, allowing warriors to stream in and out without the hesitation of ceremony.</p>
<p>Rex could hear the violence before he saw it. Even twenty meters away, the sounds reached him raw, rhythmic, and terrifyingly disciplined.</p>
<p>It wasn’t the performative clatter of soldiers showing off for an audience. These were the wet, concussive sounds of genuine training: the bone-deep thud of a gauntlet connecting with ribs, the sharp exhale of air forced from lungs by a precise strike, and the occasional, sickening crack of a wooden training weapon splintering against armor.</p>
<p>Aurelia wasn’t merely supervising; she was orchestrating. Her voice cut through the din, flat, commanding, devoid of cruelty but saturated with an exacting intensity that left no room for error.</p>
<p>She taught with the desperate investment of someone who believed that a single moment of hesitation in the field meant death.</p>
<p>Rex came to a halt at the mouth of the archway, his boots silent on the stone.</p>
<p>Iris was there.</p>
<p>She wasn’t on the training floor, immersed in the sweat and blood of the drills. Instead, she sat perched at the very edge of the hall’s perimeter, a solitary figure carved from shadow and stillness.</p>
<p>She looked like someone who had been observing the chaos not as a participant but as a witness to something she couldn’t quite touch. When Rex appeared, her head turned slowly.</p>
<p>She wore an expression that suggested her internal monologue had been abruptly interrupted by a sudden, tangible reality.</p>
<p>There was no performative pleasantry in her gaze. Instead, Rex saw something far more vulnerable: a quiet, unguarded relief.</p>
<p>It was the look of someone who had spent a sleepless night wrestling with ghosts and had finally seen a face that didn’t require any emotional labor to understand.</p>
<p>"Rex," she said, her voice barely carrying over the distant sounds of a sparring match that ended in a grunt of pain.</p>
<p>"Iris," he replied.</p>
<p>Instead of standing at attention like a stranger or a subordinate, Rex crossed the distance and sank onto the stone beside her. It was a deliberate invasion of her solitude, a statement that he wasn’t passing through but staying.</p>
<p>Iris’s eyes flickered with a brief, unguarded flash of surprise before she smoothed her features back into their customary calm.</p>
<p>"You did not have to sit," she said quietly.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said, his gaze fixed on the training floor where a soldier had just been thrown into the wall with enough force to send a cloud of dust cascading from the stones. "I wanted to."</p>
<p>"You look like someone who has been sitting alone with her own company for longer than is healthy, and I would rather be bad company than let you continue that uninterrupted."</p>
<p>"You are not bad company," Iris said, the words almost lost to the wind.</p>
<p>"I have received different feedback from those who know me much better than you do," Rex said, a ghost of a smile touching his lips. "But I will take the compliment while it is offered."</p>
<p>A sound escaped her, a small, involuntary noise that was almost a laugh. It was sharp and unexpected, the first genuine crack in her composure Rex had witnessed since the funeral.</p>
<p>She looked startled by her own reaction, as if she had forgotten that joy was still a permissible human emotion.</p>
<p>Her attention drifted back to the floor. In the center of the hall, Aurelia was moving through the second half of the session.</p>
<p>The elite unit moved like a single organism, their movements precise and lethal. They didn’t need to think about where their feet landed or how to brace for a blow; the geography of the hall was etched into their muscle memory.</p>
<p>A fighter took a punishing blow to the shoulder, ribs audibly grinding under a strike, but he didn’t falter; he simply reset, his face a mask of disciplined agony.</p>
<p>"Is she always like this after a significant engagement?" Rex asked, watching Aurelia systematically dismantle a student’s guard with terrifying efficiency.</p>
<p>"When she does not know what to do with herself," Iris answered, "she teaches."</p>
<p>"Because teaching is something she knows how to do perfectly. And knowing how to do something is what she reaches for when everything else, when the world feels uncertain."</p>
<p>"That is not so different from what I do," Rex mused, his inner mind already analyzing the tactical implications of Aurelia’s movements. "I plan... Same instinct, different tool."</p>
<p>"I noticed," Iris said, turning her head to study him.</p>
