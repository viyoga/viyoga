<p>Without warning, she drove her fist into his solar plexus. The impact was wet and sickening, the sound of air being forcibly expelled from a body.</p>
<p>Theo doubled over, his lungs seizing, but she refused to let him collapse. She maintained her death grip on his collar, using his own weight to keep him upright, forcing him to endure the full agony of the next moment.</p>
<p>"My best friend’s daughter... You stood over her and over the man who has saved this entire household more times than I can count, and you raised three generations of Nightwing steel over their throats while they slept!"</p>
<p>She swung again. This time, her knuckles found his jaw with a brutal, grinding force.</p>
<p>The sound of teeth clicking together echoed off the stone. Theo’s legs finally betrayed him, buckling beneath the assault, but Aurelia went down with him.</p>
<p>She refused to release him, slamming him down onto the unforgiving stone floor of the corridor. She pinned him there, her body a vise, her fists becoming instruments of retribution.</p>
<p>Her strikes were not the wild flailing of a grieving woman; they were delivered with the terrible, controlled precision of a master instructor who knew exactly where the bone was thinnest and where the nerves burned brightest.</p>
<p>Crack. Thud. Crack.</p>
<p>"You do not get to be forgiven for this!" she screamed, each word punctuated by the wet slap of flesh against flesh. "You do not get a formal apology to cleanse your conscience!"</p>
<p>"You do not get a household ceremony where we all bow our heads and pretend the debt has been paid!"</p>
<p>"There is no ceremony for this, Theo! There is no account that settles what you almost did tonight!"</p>
<p>Theo made no attempt to defend himself. The toxic fog that had guided his hands in the darkness had burned away completely, leaving behind a raw, bleeding clarity that was worse than any wound.</p>
<p>He felt every impact: the sharp sting of split skin, the nauseating jar of bone against bone, the warm copper taste of blood pooling in his mouth. But he did not raise his arms.</p>
<p>He did not flinch. He offered no resistance, his arms hanging loose at his sides like dead weight, as if he were welcoming the pain as the only honest thing left in his life.</p>
<p>He wanted the punishment. He craved the breaking of his body to match the breaking of his soul.</p>
<p>Diana walked to the doorway, her face ghostly in the dim light. One hand was pressed against her mouth as she watched, her expression a devastating tapestry of horror and grief—not fear for her own life, but a profound mourning for the man Theo had clearly and irrevocably become.</p>
<p>Rex stood beside her, his palm resting protectively against the small of her back. He made no move to intervene.</p>
<p>His eyes were fixed on Aurelia’s fury with the patient, grim attention of a man who understood that some wounds cannot be healed with words or restraint and that this violence was a necessary purging.</p>
<p>The final exchange was a blur of motion and muffled sounds. Theo stopped responding somewhere between the third and fourth strike.</p>
<p>His head lolled to the side, his eyes rolling back into his skull as consciousness fled. His body went limp beneath his mother’s relentless grip.</p>
<p>Aurelia remained frozen over him for one more heartbeat. Her chest heaved in ragged, desperate gasps that sounded like breaking glass.</p>
<p>Her knuckles were split and crimson, blood dripping from her hands onto her son’s pale face. She stared down at the unconscious wreck of her child, her eyes burning with a fire that had consumed everything else.</p>
<p>Then, with a shudder that shook her entire frame, she let go.</p>
<p>Aurelia collapsed backward onto her heels in the center of the corridor, the stone floor cold beneath her. She sat there in the aftermath of her own storm, exposed before the entire household, the servants trembling in the shadows, the guards standing like stone monuments, and Elaris, who had appeared during the crescendo of the violence, now standing with one hand pressed against her sternum as if trying to physically hold her own heart together.</p>
<p>Aurelia did not look away from the carnage. She stared down at her hands.</p>
<p>Her knuckles were split open, weeping crimson droplets that stained the ancient stone, her skin raw and swollen from the impact of bone against bone. The fury that had sustained her, that molten, terrifying energy, had evaporated, leaving behind nothing but a hollow, aching exhaustion so profound it felt like her bones had turned to ash.</p>
<p>Slowly, she lifted her head. Her gaze swept across the gathered faces, meeting each one with the unflinching honesty of a condemned woman.</p>
<p>"Everyone here saw what he did," she said.</p>
<p>Her voice was steadier now, though it carried the jagged, broken edge of someone who had just survived a shipwreck. "I will not ask any of you to lie."</p>
<p>"I will not ask you to wear a mask of politeness or to pretend that the air in this house isn’t poisoned tonight."</p>
<p>"And I will not punish anyone for speaking the truth of what they witnessed if they are asked of them later." Her jaw tightened. "What happened tonight happened... I will not build a story on top of this atrocity to make it smaller or more palatable than it truly was."</p>
<p>Her head turned toward Rex and Diana. The movement was slow, deliberate, and heavy with shame.</p>
<p>"I owe you both more than an apology," she said.</p>
<p>And there, beneath the steel of her composure, the first real crack appeared, a fracture in the dam that let a sliver of raw, maternal grief spill through. "I trained him... I raised him within these very walls, under this roof, and taught him how to hold a blade and how to carry himself with honor."</p>
<p>"And something in him broke something so deep and so twisted that he stood over the two of you with steel raised to your throats while you slept..."</p>
<p>"I did not see it... I did not see it in time to stop the rot before it reached the surface." Her voice dropped to a whisper that carried to every corner of the hall. "That failure is mine as much as it is his."</p>
<p>"I do not expect either of you to simply set that aside because I have said the words."</p>
<p>"Aurelia," Rex began, his voice low and warning.</p>
<p>"Let me finish," she cut him off, her eyes flashing with one last spark of defiance. "I am the acting head of this household while Leonidas is away."</p>
<p>"That means the blood on these stones is my responsibility... I will not hide behind the excuse of a ’broken mind’ as though his madness absolves this house of its accountability."</p>
<p>"If you want sanctions against this family, if you want retribution against me specifically for what was almost unleashed under my roof, I will accept them without argument."</p>
<p>"I would rather you name the price now than have it sit like unspoken poison between us forever."</p>
<p>A silence descended upon the corridor, so absolute it felt as though the house itself had stopped breathing. Rex studied her.</p>
<p>His face remained a mask of controlled calm, but behind those dark eyes, something was moving: a quiet, calculating assessment. He was not merely listening to her words; he was weighing the soul of the woman before him, measuring the terrifying utility of a leader who would sacrifice her own blood to preserve the integrity of her honor.</p>
<p>Finally, Rex spoke.</p>
<p>"No sanctions," he said.</p>
<p>The words were flat and final. "Not tonight, and likely not at all."</p>
<p>He stepped forward slightly, his presence filling the space between the grieving mother and the horrified witnesses. "What I saw in this corridor tonight was not a failure of leadership."</p>
<p>"It was a mother protecting the people she loves with the same ferocity she would use against an invading army."</p>
<p>"That is not a household that requires punishment, Aurelia." His gaze hardened, turning toward the unconscious Theo and then back to her. "That is a household doing exactly what it must do when something inside it breaks badly enough to become a threat."</p>
<p>Aurelia stared at Rex, her expression caught in a painful limbo between genuine gratitude and a profound, disbelieving shock that mercy was even being offered after such a violation of sacred trust. Her lips parted as if to speak, to protest, to demand further punishment for herself, but the words died in her throat.</p>
<p>"Get him to the healer," Rex commanded, his voice cutting through the heavy atmosphere like a blade.</p>
<p>He gestured toward Theo’s crumpled, bloodied form. "Whatever punishment continues from here, let it continue once he can actually stand to receive it."</p>
<p>"A broken body cannot learn from a broken spirit."</p>
<p>Aurelia gave a single, stiff nod the only movement she could manage. Two guards stepped forward with practiced neutrality, their faces grim as they hoisted Theo’s limp body between them.</p>
<p>They carried him away like a corpse, his head lolling uselessly with every step. Gradually, the corridor began to empty.</p>
<p>The staff dispersed with a hushed, ghostly quiet, moving like shadows in the periphery. They understood the unspoken law of the household: they had witnessed a private apocalypse, a moment that would define the family’s history for years to come, and it was something they would never not once, not in a whisper discuss openly again.</p>
