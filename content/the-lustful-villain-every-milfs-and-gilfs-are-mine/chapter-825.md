<p>Valentina paused, her pen hovering over the parchment. She looked at him, her eyes narrowing slightly.</p>
<p>"The agenda is nearly exhausted, Apollo. Is this a matter of immediate strategic necessity?"</p>
<p>"It is the foundation of the necessity," Apollo replied. He leaned forward, his shadow stretching long across the table. "We have been dissecting Tremor’s connection to the Underlayer and the Legion as if they were isolated strategic variables."</p>
<p>"We have treated the Legion as a force of nature, a storm to be weathered. But the presence of Ignivara, the precision of Zane’s movements, and the specific architecture of the operation they were running... it points to a singular, terrifying truth that this network can no longer afford to ignore."</p>
<p>The room went unnaturally still. Even the flickering candlelight seemed to freeze in place.</p>
<p>"The Legion has a founder," Apollo continued, his voice dropping into a lower, more ominous register. "The intelligence we extracted from the canyon, the decrypted logs, and the behavioral patterns of their command confirmed it."</p>
<p>"A single architect... A single entity who established the organization’s structure thirty years ago and has been pulling the strings from the shadows ever since."</p>
<p>He paused, the weight of the revelation pressing down on everyone present. He looked down at the table, his eyes dark.</p>
<p>"The name we have is Celestina Von Starlight."</p>
<p>The silence that followed wasn’t just a lack of sound; it was a vacuum. It was a sudden, violent theft of breath from the lungs of everyone in the room.</p>
<p>Rex did not look at Valentina. He knew the physics of the moment too well.</p>
<p>To look at the head of the table now would be to draw a line of sight directly to the epicenter of the earthquake. To look at her would be to signal to everyone in the room that her reaction was the only thing that mattered, and in this moment, her reaction was everything.</p>
<p>Instead, he kept his eyes fixed on the dark grain of the table in front of him, maintaining a mask of calm, scholarly focus.</p>
<p>But his senses were hyperattuned to the carnage of the silence. He heard the quality of the stillness shift; it was no longer the polite silence of a closing meeting; it was the suffocating, pressurized silence that occurs when a fundamental truth is dropped into a room, reorganizing the reality of everyone present.</p>
<p>He heard Apollo’s quiet, heavy coda: "I thought everyone should know that."</p>
<p>He heard Aurelia’s sharp, jagged intake of breath, a sound of pure, unadulterated shock.</p>
<p>He heard Morwenna murmur something in the ancient, formal register of the Nightwing household, a dialect reserved for secrets so heavy they could not be carried by common speech.</p>
<p>He heard the subtle, frantic rustle of Elizabeth shifting in her chair, her body tensing as if preparing for a blow.</p>
<p>And then, he heard nothing from the head of the table. The silence from Valentina stretched, growing longer and more significant with every passing second, until it became a living thing, a monster sitting in her chair.</p>
<p>Rex finally allowed himself to look up.</p>
<p>Valentina was frozen. Her hands were pressed flat against the table, her knuckles white, her posture rigid.</p>
<p>She looked like a statue carved from ice, her expression the terrifying mask of a woman who had just realized she had been standing on a trapdoor for thirty years. The room was no longer looking at the agenda; they were looking at the headmaster of the Grand Academy, waiting for her to breathe, waiting for her to deny it, waiting for her to crumble.</p>
<p>She had no response ready. The architect of the world’s most powerful network had just been named, and the woman tasked with leading the network had been caught in the lie.</p>
<p>Rex watched her from his position beside Elizabeth. He did not look away, but he did not stare.</p>
<p>He performed the perfect role of the attentive colleague, offering a measured, warm, and patient gaze, the look of a man who was simply waiting for a leader to finish her thought. He was the picture of stability in a room that had just lost its footing.</p>
<p>The world waited for Valentina to speak.</p>
<p>Morwenna was the one to shatter the suffocating stillness.</p>
<p>"Celestina Von Starlight," she said.</p>
<p>The name didn’t roll off her tongue; it was placed there, heavy and cold, with the surgical precision of a blade being laid upon a table. She spoke with the flat, terrifying clarity of someone who had just seen the final piece of a grand, bloody puzzle click into place.</p>
<p>"The Headmaster’s family name."</p>
<p>The atmosphere in the room didn’t just change; it curdled. The quiet shifted from a state of shock to a state of accusation.</p>
<p>Aurelia’s breath was a ragged, audible thing, a small, desperate sound in a room that had suddenly become a courtroom.</p>
<p>Valentina remained a statue. Her hands were still pressed flat against the obsidian surface, her knuckles pale, her fingers unmoving.</p>
<p>She was practicing a feat of monumental willpower, a desperate, internal war to contain the tidal wave of emotion threatening to breach her composure. She was a dam holding back thirty years of secrets, straining to keep the flood from drowning everyone in the room.</p>
<p>"Lady Valentina," Aurelia whispered.</p>
<p>The voice was fragile, trembling with the weight of a daughter realizing the foundation of her world was built on sand.</p>
<p>Valentina turned her head. Her movement was slow, almost agonizing.</p>
<p>"Did you know?" Aurelia asked.</p>
<p>Three words. They were small, but they carried the force of a thunderclap.</p>
<p>It was the most devastating question ever uttered in the history of the Grand Academy, a question that demanded a reckoning for every lie, every shadow, and every silent moment of the last three decades.</p>
<p>Valentina held Aurelia’s gaze for a long, harrowing moment. The silence stretched until it felt as though the very air might crack.</p>
<p>"Not in the way you mean," Valentina finally said. Her voice had transformed.</p>
<p>It had reached that crystalline, guarded register she used when she was navigating a minefield, the voice of a woman selecting words not to communicate but to survive. "I knew the name..."</p>
<p>"I have known that name for a very long time. But I did not know the full, terrifying scope of what that name represented."</p>
<p>"And what I suspected... what I feared... I chose not to confirm. Because confirmation produces obligation, Aurelia."</p>
<p>"And for thirty years, I was not ready for the obligation."</p>
<p>The room absorbed the confession like a sponge absorbing blood. It was a heavy, visceral realization.</p>
<p>"Thirty years," Morwenna interjected.</p>
<p>Her voice was a cold instrument of arithmetic, stripping away the emotion to reveal the naked, brutal timeline beneath. "The Legion’s operational history is approximately thirty years."</p>
<p>"The headmaster’s tenure at this academy is approximately thirty years."</p>
<p>"Yes," Valentina said, the word a weary surrender.</p>
<p>"You have been presiding over the Academy," Morwenna continued, her eyes narrowing like a predator sensing a kill, "during the exact same period that the Legion has been festering beneath it."</p>
<p>"You were the sun, and they were the shadow, moving in perfect, synchronized orbit."</p>
<p>"Yes," Valentina said.</p>
<p>"And you did not confirm it," Morwenna pressed, her voice sharpening into a weapon. "You sat in this very chair, watched the reports come in, watched the casualties mount, and you did not confirm."</p>
<p>"I did not confirm!" Valentina’s voice rose, a sudden, sharp crack in her composure that sent a jolt of electricity through the room.</p>
<p>She looked down at the table, her eyes burning. "I watched. I monitored. I maintained this institution as the bastion it was designed to be, and I managed the razor-thin tension between what I suspected, what the evidence could prove, and what I was willing to sacrifice to find the truth!"</p>
<p>"I was not ready for what confirmation would have required me to do... To confirm was to declare war on my own blood and to... confirm was to tear the world apart."</p>
<p>Rex sat in the center of the storm, listening to the echoes of thirty years of deception vibrating in Valentina’s voice. As she spoke, his mind drifted back to the private letter he had sent her months ago, the letter containing the name he had whispered into the dark.</p>
<p>He thought of the calculated risk of that message and the profound satisfaction of seeing her response. She hadn’t ignored him; she hadn’t retreated.</p>
<p>She had waited. She had prepared.</p>
<p>He felt a dark, quiet triumph. He had placed a seed in the most fertile soil in the world, and now, he was watching the harvest arrive exactly as he had designed it.</p>
<p>He kept his face a mask of perfect, attentive neutrality. To the others, he was just another witness to the tragedy.</p>
<p>To himself, he was the architect of the moment.</p>
<p>"What happens now?" Apollo asked.</p>
<p>His voice was the embodiment of pragmatism. He didn’t ask with grief or anger; he asked with the cold, efficient hunger of a soldier who had just found the enemy’s flank exposed.</p>
<p>He was looking for the edge of the blade, the point where the talking ended and the blood began.</p>
<p>Valentina looked down at the obsidian table, her reflection staring back at her from the dark surface. She looked exhausted, a woman who had finally been forced to stand in the light after a lifetime of shadows.</p>
<p>She had no answers. She had no grand strategy to offer.</p>
<p>The world was spinning, the lies were bleeding out, and for the first time in thirty years, the Headmaster of the Grand Academy had nothing to give them.</p>
