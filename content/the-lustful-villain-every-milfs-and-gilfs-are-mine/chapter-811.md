<p>"You have been reading forty years’ worth of compiled, high-level underlayer intelligence in secret."</p>
<p>"I have been reading it very carefully," Rex corrected, his gaze unwavering.</p>
<p>"And the specific operational details?" she pressed, her voice gaining a sharp, interrogative edge. "Lord Xerollion’s governance transition?"</p>
<p>"The massive military redistribution across the sectors? The nuances of the second stratum contact situation? These aren’t just ’patterns,’ Rex."</p>
<p>"These are specific, volatile, current events... Are these also in the forty-year archive?"</p>
<p>Rex felt the pressure of her mind, a relentless force trying to find the fracture in his foundation. He leaned into the truth, twisting it just enough to remain functional.</p>
<p>"The historical patterns are in the archive," Rex said, his tone authoritative. "The exact condition of the underlayer was just a guess, based on those long-term patterns and the information Ignivara shared during our talk at the tree line."</p>
<p>"What Ignivara communicated," Elizabeth repeated.</p>
<p>The way she said it was clinical, a surgeon noting a specific symptom to be dissected later. She wasn’t accusing him yet, but she was cataloging the variable.</p>
<p>"She was in the underlayer four months ago," Rex said, providing the final layer of the shield. "She departed just before the communication blackout began."</p>
<p>"Her firsthand knowledge of the governance state is recent enough to be operationally relevant to the patterns we see today."</p>
<p>"And she communicated all of this to you," Elizabeth said, her eyes locking onto his, her voice dropping to a low, dangerous silkiness. "During the ’conversation’ that consumed the better part of your night."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t blink. He met her intensity with a stillness that was almost unnerving, a man who had already accounted for her suspicion and found it manageable.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>Elizabeth stared at him, her gaze not merely looking at him but dissecting him. The air in the room seemed to thin, the silence stretching until it felt like a physical weight pressing against their chests.</p>
<p>"Rex," she said.</p>
<p>The way she uttered his name wasn’t a question, nor was it a greeting. It had the heavy, decisive quality of a gavel striking wood, the sound of someone who had been weighing a truth in the dark for the entire duration of their conversation and had finally, irrevocably, decided to bring it into the light.</p>
<p>"Yes," he replied, his voice steady, though he could feel the invisible tension of her intellect tightening around him like a vise.</p>
<p>"For the last six months, I have been the primary observer of your trajectory at this academy," she said, her voice gaining a razor-sharp clarity. "I have been the one who knew the most about your movements."</p>
<p>"I authorized your research access..."</p>
<p>"I personally reviewed your archive requests."</p>
<p>"I sat in the sessions where you presented your findings, watching the way you laid out your data. I have been close enough to your shadow to know its exact shape."</p>
<p>"Yes," he said, acknowledging the truth of her proximity.</p>
<p>"And yet," she continued, her voice dropping an octave, vibrating with a controlled intensity, "I do not have the full shape."</p>
<p>"I have the silhouette you have allowed me to see."</p>
<p>"I have the version of you that fits within the parameters of an academy student. And the most frustrating part, Rex, is that I have known the distinction between the shadow and the man was there since the very beginning."</p>
<p>Rex remained silent. He didn’t offer a defense, nor did he attempt to smooth over the jagged edges of her observation.</p>
<p>He simply stood there, a monolith of secrets, letting her words strike him.</p>
<p>"I am not asking you to correct the record," she said, stepping closer, her eyes burning with a fierce, analytical light. "I am not demanding you fill in the blanks or offer me the missing pieces."</p>
<p>"I am telling you that I know the blanks exist."</p>
<p>"I am telling you this because you should know that my awareness is total. And because..." she paused, her expression softening just enough to be dangerous, "...because my support tonight is not contingent on your transparency."</p>
<p>"I do not need to know everything to stand by you."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her, truly looked at her, searching for the motive behind such a profound concession. In a world of politics, power, and calculated alliances, such a gesture was an anomaly.</p>
<p>"Why?" he asked.</p>
<p>The single word carried the weight of a man accustomed to the idea that everything has a price.</p>
<p>She met his gaze with that terrifying, flat attention of hers, the kind of unwavering focus that most people found exhausting to endure but which Elizabeth wielded with the effortless grace of a master.</p>
<p>"Because the work you are doing is real," she said, her voice ringing with conviction. "The research is not a fabrication; it is a foundation."</p>
<p>"The capability development is not a game; it is a transformation... The knowledge you have been hoarding and the reasons you have been accumulating it... those are real, tangible forces, even if the full architecture of your intent remains hidden from me."</p>
<p>She took a breath, the tension in her shoulders finally breaking as she leaned into the raw emotion of the previous day.</p>
<p>"And because," she whispered, her voice trembling with the memory of the violence, "the morning yesterday produced outcomes that I witnessed with my own eyes."</p>
<p>"I watched the world tear itself apart, and I watched it from the inside of an engagement where the blood was flowing, where bones were snapping, and where the cost of failure would have been total annihilation."</p>
<p>"The outcomes could have been significantly worse... they could have been catastrophic. And the specific man responsible for the narrow, brutal gap between what happened and the slaughter that should have happened... is the man standing in front of me right now."</p>
<p>Rex fell into a profound silence. The weight of her words settled into his bones, heavier than the divine mineral material integrated into his very marrow, leaving him momentarily anchored in the gravity of her recognition.</p>
<p>"I know that too," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice was low, a resonant vibration that seemed to ground the electric tension in the room. It wasn’t a boast, nor was it a plea for validation; it was a simple, brutal acknowledgment of the reality she had laid bare.</p>
<p>Elizabeth stared at him, her features settling into a mask of profound, weary clarity. She looked like someone who had finally stepped into the center of a storm she had been predicting for months.</p>
<p>She had reached for a truth she had hoped might be different, perhaps a truth that would bridge the gap between them, but instead, she received the exact confirmation she had expected. And, in a display of her formidable mental discipline, she decided that the cold, unyielding truth was sufficient.</p>
<p>She didn’t need the warmth of a lie to feel secure.</p>
<p>The moment of emotional vulnerability passed, replaced instantly by the sharp, cold steel of her strategic mind. The researcher was back.</p>
<p>"The meeting tonight," she said, her voice regaining its professional, razor-edged precision. "Is there anything Morwenna is going to raise that you cannot account for? Or are we walking into a tactical minefield?"</p>
<p>Rex closed his eyes for a fraction of a second, mentally simulating the upcoming confrontation. He could almost see Morwenna’s face, the skepticism, the sharp, probing intellect that left no stone unturned.</p>
<p>"Morwenna is going to target my absence during the engagement’s initial phase," Rex said, his voice hardening as he mapped out the enemy’s strike. "She is going to point out the mathematical improbability of my arrival."</p>
<p>"She will note that I appeared at the exact moment the tide turned, a timing that is far too convenient for the narrative of the engagement’s resolution."</p>
<p>"She is going to suggest that this convenience isn’t just luck, but rather a calculated, suspicious intervention."</p>
<p>"And?" Elizabeth prompted, her eyes narrowing, waiting for the ’but’ that would define his defense.</p>
<p>"And the truth is that I was at the eastern agricultural district during the early phase of the engagement," Rex said, his tone unapologetic. "I wasn’t idling."</p>
<p>"I was positioned there because the Earthen Authority’s geological awareness is a finicky, massive sensory input."</p>
<p>"The island’s substrate is far more clearly readable from the outer perimeter, where the seismic noise is minimal, than from the chaotic, crushing density of the city’s central district."</p>
<p>"I went there to establish a clean observation point to see the battlefield through the earth itself before the chaos became too loud to interpret."</p>
<p>Elizabeth leaned back, her mind instantly deconstructing his logic, testing its structural integrity.</p>
<p>"So, let me summarize your tactical position," she said, her voice laced with a hint of incredulity. "You went to the outer perimeter miles away from the epicenter to observe a high-intensity combat engagement occurring in the very heart of the city’s central district."</p>
<p>"Through the geological substrate," Rex corrected, his gaze locking onto hers with the intensity of a man who had stared into the abyss of the earth and survived. "The earth doesn’t lie, Elizabeth."</p>
<p>"And the signal reads differently from the perimeter than it does from within the density..."</p>
<p>"From the outskirts, the vibrations are a clear, distinct language, while from the center... it’s just a deafening, unintelligible scream."</p>
