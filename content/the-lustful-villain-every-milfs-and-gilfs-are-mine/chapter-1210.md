<p>The air in the corridor was thick with the copper tang of blood and the acrid stench of scorched stone. Xerollion could feel the heat of his own blood trickling down his cheek, a warm reminder of his own mortality, or at least, the mortality he had so carefully simulated.</p>
<p>Behind him, the third corpse, a young soldier whose face was twisted into a permanent mask of agony, lunged with clawed fingers aimed at his spine.</p>
<p>SHHHH TICK!</p>
<p>Without so much as turning his head, Xerollion flicked two fingers behind his back. A razor-thin blade of compressed air screamed through the air.</p>
<p>FIIIIIIIIIIII ZIP!</p>
<p>The invisible edge sliced clean through the necromantic threads connecting the corpse’s joints. The body didn’t just fall; it collapsed like a marionette with its strings cut, hitting the stone with a wet, hollow THUD.</p>
<p>The golem, sensing its opening, roared in a sound of grinding tectonic plates.</p>
<p>KRAAAAA RUMBLE!</p>
<p>It swung its fourth attack, a massive, overhead hammer fist designed to pulverize anything beneath it.</p>
<p>This time, Xerollion didn’t retreat. He didn’t roll.</p>
<p>He stood his ground.</p>
<p>BOOM!!!</p>
<p>"Enough of this," Xerollion growled.</p>
<p>As the stone fist descended, he punched upward. His fist was encased in a corona of violet-black energy that shrieked like a dying star.</p>
<p>VROOOOOOM!</p>
<p>The impact was catastrophic. His knuckles met the golem’s elbow joint with the force of a meteor strike.</p>
<p>CRUNCH SHATTER!!!</p>
<p>The stone elbow exploded into a thousand jagged fragments. The golem’s arm didn’t just break; it disintegrated, leaving the massive limb hanging uselessly by a few pathetic strands of enchanted mortar.</p>
<p>The construct’s balance shattered along with its arm; it staggered, its entire form lurching dangerously to one side.</p>
<p>’Too predictable,’ Xerollion thought, his mind working with the cold efficiency of a supercomputer. ’You are mimicking my power, but you lack my mastery.’</p>
<p>’You are playing with fire without understanding how to burn.’</p>
<p>In one fluid motion, Xerollion unleashed hell.</p>
<p>SHHH BOOOOOOM!!!</p>
<p>A massive telekinetic burst erupted from his entire body in a 360-degree shockwave. The golem was caught in the epicenter.</p>
<p>The stone construct didn’t just break; it imploded, then exploded outward.</p>
<p>KRA KRA KRA BOOM!!!</p>
<p>Chunks of granite the size of boulders rained down across the corridor in a lethal arc.</p>
<p>THUD. CRASH. SMASH.</p>
<p>Several pieces slammed into the floor with enough force to send tremors through the entire complex; if any soldier had been foolish enough to stand within thirty feet, they would have been crushed instantly into a red paste.</p>
<p>Simultaneously, Xerollion sent out a secondary pulse, a precise, surgical wave of anti-magic energy.</p>
<p>VREEEEEE!</p>
<p>The necromantic connections animating the remaining corpses were severed instantly. The last shambling horror froze mid-step, its jaw hanging open, before its muscles lost all tension and it slumped into genuine, silent stillness.</p>
<p>The necromantic horror was over. The battlefield was suddenly, eerily quiet, save for the settling of dust.</p>
<p>Xerollion turned his full attention back to Rex Rexilion. The construct stood amidst the ruin, its breathing labored, its systems sparking, its options dwindling to nothing.</p>
<p>Xerollion’s voice changed. The cruelty was still there, but it was layered beneath something more profound: a cold, serious acknowledgment.</p>
<p>It was a private conversation between a god and his shadow.</p>
<p>"You’re fighting hard," Xerollion said, stepping through the settling debris, each footfall echoing in the hollow silence. "I will give you that."</p>
<p>"Your tenacity is... admirable... almost human."</p>
<p>He stopped a few paces from the construct, his violet eyes glowing with an unsettling intensity.</p>
<p>"But you already know how this ends, don’t you?" He tilted his head, studying the flickering energy in Rex’s eyes. "You were built from my behavioral patterns."</p>
<p>"My tactics. My history. But you were built from my data, not my experience."</p>
<p>"You have the map, but you have never walked the terrain."</p>
<p>Xerollion leaned forward, his shadow stretching long and dark over the broken hero.</p>
<p>"You never truly fought Kaeleth..."</p>
<p>"You never felt the true terror of a soul being unmade."</p>
<p>"You have never fought anything that tested the real, jagged edges of what I am truly capable of."</p>
<p>Rex Rexilion offered no rebuttal. There was no defiance in its silence, no wounded pride; there was only the terrifying, mechanical processing of a machine trying to solve an unsolvable equation.</p>
<p>It stood amidst the carnage of its own failed gambit, breathing in that same unnecessary, perfectly simulated rhythm: Haaa... Haaa... a sound that felt like a mockery of life in the middle of a graveyard.</p>
<p>Then, its fingers scraped against something metallic.</p>
<p>SKREEEE.</p>
<p>With a sudden, violent wrench, the construct tore a three-foot length of broken iron railing from the corridor wall. The stone around the socket exploded in a shower of sparks and grit.</p>
<p>CRUNCH.</p>
<p>The construct didn’t swing it like a club; it wielded it like a rapier, then a mace, then a cleaver. Weapons mastery was overriding every limitation of the improvised tool.</p>
<p>The railing became an extension of Rex’s own lethal intent, each swing carrying a surgical precision that defied the crude, rusted metal.</p>
<p>WHOOSH!</p>
<p>The first strike whistled past Xerollion’s ear.</p>
<p>WHAP!</p>
<p>The second strike tore through the air so close that the displaced wind slapped against Xerollion’s collar, ruffling his cloak.</p>
<p>Xerollion didn’t flinch. He simply waited for the third strike, a vicious thrust aimed directly at his throat.</p>
<p>CLANG!!!</p>
<p>He caught the iron rod directly in his open palm. The sound was deafening, a metallic shriek that reverberated off the walls.</p>
<p>KRAAAA TANG!</p>
<p>The iron didn’t just stop; it buckled and bent visibly under the supernatural pressure of his grip, the metal groaning as it deformed around his palm. With a contemptuous twist of his wrist, Xerollion tore the weapon away.</p>
<p>CLATTER CLATTER CLATTER.</p>
<p>The railing fell uselessly across the broken stones.</p>
<p>"Creative," Xerollion purred, his violet eyes tracking the construct’s empty hand as it curled instinctively into a fist, still expecting the familiar weight of a weapon. "But you’re making a mistake, Rex."</p>
<p>"You didn’t need a weapon in the first place... You know that."</p>
<p>"I know that."</p>
<p>He stepped closer, the blood on his cheek now drying into a dark smear. "The fact that you reached for something external, something to bridge the gap between your power and mine, tells me something about your programming that you probably didn’t mean to show me."</p>
<p>"It tells me you’re afraid of failing."</p>
<p>The construct’s systems seemed to shudder. It wasn’t fear, not truly, but something close enough to trigger a cascade of emergency protocols.</p>
<p>Rex Rexilion drew itself upright one final time, its joints popping with mechanical finality.</p>
<p>CRACK POP.</p>
<p>It began to gather everything.</p>
<p>The air around its fists started to shimmer. At first, it was a faint, ghostly glow, but it thickened with every passing microsecond, becoming something dense, something physical.</p>
<p>VROOOOOOOOOM.</p>
<p>The light became so intense it cast razor-sharp shadows across the rubble, turning the corridor into a hellscape of stark blacks and blinding whites.</p>
<p>Then came the gravity.</p>
<p>RUMBLE... HUMMMM...</p>
<p>The ground beneath the construct’s feet began to fracture as a localized gravitational well formed.</p>
<p>CRACKLE. SHATTER.</p>
<p>Loose stone, splinters of marble, and fragments of the fallen golem were drawn inward, swirling around the construct in a trembling, violent halo of debris.</p>
<p>WHIRRRRRR.</p>
<p>The soldiers watching from the sidelines felt it before they understood it. A crushing pressure settled over their lungs, a physical weight that pressed down on their very souls.</p>
<p>THUMP. THUMP.</p>
<p>Their hearts hammered against their ribs as the atmosphere itself seemed to thicken. Several of them took involuntary steps backward, their faces pale with a primal, instinctive terror, as the air grew charged with enough energy to ignite the very oxygen in their lungs.</p>
<p>The construct was no longer just a machine. It was becoming a singularity.</p>
