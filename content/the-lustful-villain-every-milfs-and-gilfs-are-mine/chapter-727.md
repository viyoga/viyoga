<p>Veylor’s voice was breaking, turning into a wet, gargling whistle as his lungs filled with fluid. His eyes were wide, rolling back in his head, showing only the bloodshot whites as he stared into the abyss of his own demise.</p>
<p>Rex wasn’t finished. He wanted the spectacle to be absolute.</p>
<p>He gathered a massive amount of gravitational energy into his palm, turning it into a dense, black sphere of crushing force.</p>
<p>VREEEEEEEEEEEEE!</p>
<p>"This is the end of the show, little bird!"</p>
<p>"BOOOOOOOOOM!"</p>
<p>He slammed the gravity sphere directly into Veylor’s midsection. The sheer pressure didn’t just bruise; it eviscerated.</p>
<p>The abdominal wall gave way with a sound like a wet sack of grain being struck by a sledgehammer.</p>
<p>THWACK SQUISH!</p>
<p>Intestines, slick and steaming in the cool air, were forced out of the gaping wound, spilling onto the blood-soaked stones.</p>
<p>SLITHER... SPLAT!</p>
<p>Veylor’s screams had faded into a rhythmic, pathetic whimpering. His body was a ruin of shredded meat, exposed bone, and pulsing organs.</p>
<p>He was a hollowed out shell of the man who had just moments ago danced through the clouds.</p>
<p>"He’s... he’s dying... he’s really dying..." Iris whispered, her voice a hollow shell of its former self.</p>
<p>She watched, paralyzed by a mixture of horror and a strange, hypnotic fascination, as the light in Veylor’s eyes began to flicker like a dying lamp.</p>
<p>Rex stood back, breathing heavily, his hands drenched in the warm, red life of his enemy. He looked down at the mess at his feet with a look of profound boredom. "Pathetic!"</p>
<p>"A waste of a good scream."</p>
<p>Veylor tried to lift a hand, the one that wasn’t completely shattered, toward Iris. His fingers twitched in the dirt, leaving red streaks in the dust.</p>
<p>"Hhh... hhh... I... sss..."</p>
<p>But there was no strength left. The connection between his mind and his broken body finally snapped.</p>
<p>The last spark of mana flickered out, and the overwhelming weight of his injuries finally claimed him.</p>
<p>The tension in his broken frame vanished. The frantic, agonizing twitching stopped.</p>
<p>And then, slowly, helplessly...</p>
<p>He fell.</p>
<p>The air was no longer air; it was a thick, metallic soup of pulverized stone and atomized blood. The silence that followed Veylor’s final, shuddering collapse was more deafening than the roar of the battle had ever been.</p>
<p>The stone below his fallen form didn’t just sit there. As Veylor descended, a mangled heap of meat and broken bones, the ground itself seemed to lunge upward to meet him.</p>
<p>Rex, with the cold, calculated precision of a god playing with clay, manipulated the very tectonic plates of the plaza. A massive, jagged golem formation of obsidian-like rubble rose with predatory intent, meeting Veylor’s descent.</p>
<p>CRUNCH THUD!</p>
<p>The impact was sickeningly precise. The stone didn’t cushion him; it crushed him further into the earth, the jagged edges of the rising formation acting like a grinding stone against his already shattered ribs.</p>
<p>Veylor hit the formation and did not bounce. He did not groan and he did not even twitch.</p>
<p>He simply became part of the wreckage. And he also did not get up.</p>
<p>Rex stood over the ruin of the man, his chest rising and falling in a slow, rhythmic tempo. He didn’t look exhausted; he looked satisfied, like a man who had just finished a tedious chore.</p>
<p>He glanced at the flickering holographic interface of his [Foresight], watching the data streams confirm the absolute cessation of Veylor’s vital signs. The red lines had gone flat, and the variable was zeroed out.</p>
<p>Then, Rex turned his gaze toward Iris.</p>
<p>She was a wreck. The legendary assassin, the woman who could move through a room of killers without a sound, was now a trembling, broken thing.</p>
<p>Her energy signature was fluctuating wildly, not with the explosive, chaotic roar of a berserker, but with the terrifying, razor-sharp intensification of a predator pushed to the brink of madness. It was a cold, sharpening grief, a silent scream vibrating in the very atoms of her being.</p>
<p>"Veylor..." Iris whispered. The name was a plea, a prayer to a god that had clearly abandoned them. "VEYLOORRRRR! "</p>
<p>Her voice rose, cracking, losing all the lethal, controlled grace she was known for. She began to crawl, her fingers digging into the blood-soaked cracks of the plaza, dragging her body toward the mound of gore that used to be the man she loved.</p>
<p>"NOOO! VEYLOOORRRRRR! HWAAAAHHHHHHHH!" A strangled, guttural sob tore from her throat, a sound so raw it felt like it was ripping her own vocal cords apart. "VEYLOR! PLEASE...! WAKE UP! WAKE UUUUUUPPPP!"</p>
<p>She reached the edge of the crater, her eyes widening as she saw the reality of him. The sight of his mangled limbs, the hollowed-out cavity of his torso, the way his blood had pooled into a dark, stagnant lake around his head—it was too much.</p>
<p>She collapsed at the edge of the carnage, her hands hovering over him, terrified that if she touched him, he would simply dissolve into red mist.</p>
<p>"You... you monster..." she breathed, her eyes locking onto Rex.</p>
<p>The grief in her gaze was so heavy it felt like it had its own gravitational pull. "You didn’t have to do that...! You didn’t have to make him... you didn’t have to kill him like an animal!!!"</p>
<p>Each word was a jagged shard of glass, delivered with a terrifying, deliberate cadence. She was holding herself together by a single, fraying thread of sheer willpower, her entire being structured around the refusal to scream until her lungs gave out.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at her, his expression as impassive as a mountain. There was no remorse in his eyes, no flicker of guilt, no shadow of an apology.</p>
<p>There was only the flat, chilling acknowledgment of a completed task.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex said. His voice was a low, steady hum in the wake of her hysteria. "I did it anyway."</p>
<p>"Why?" Iris shrieked, the sound echoing off the ruined walls of the plaza, a high, piercing wail of pure, unadulterated agony. "Why him?! Why this way?!"</p>
<p>"Was it not enough to win?!"</p>
<p>"Did you have to destroy him?!"</p>
<p>Rex stepped closer, his shadow falling over her like a shroud.</p>
<p>"Because he was the most efficient way to remove your support without removing you," he stated, his voice devoid of any warmth. "You were the one I was managing for, Iris."</p>
<p>"He was a variable in that equation... An unpredictable, sentimental variable that threatened the stability of the outcome." Rex clenched his fist. "So, I solved for the variable."</p>
<p>"He’s not a variable!" Iris roared, her voice breaking into a sob that sounded like a death rattle.</p>
<p>She slammed her fist into the bloodstained ground, spraying crimson across her own face. "He’s a person! He’s a man! He fought by my side for years! He was--"</p>
<p>She choked on the word. The reality of the silence hit her all over again.</p>
<p>The "was" hung in the air, heavy and suffocating, a tombstone for the man who had just been slaughtered for the sake of a calculation.</p>
<p>"He was," Rex corrected softly.</p>
<p>The small, clinical correction landed on her like a final, crushing blow, more brutal than any of the physical strikes he had dealt Veylor.</p>
<p>Iris turned her head toward Rex, and for the first time since their meeting, the legendary shadow of the battlefield vanished. There was no lethal grace left in her, no calculated poise of a master assassin.</p>
<p>Her face was a ruin of raw, unbridled fury and catastrophic heartbreak. Her eyes, once sharp enough to track a heartbeat from a mile away, were now bloodshot, overflowing with tears that mixed with the grime and blood on her cheeks.</p>
<p>She looked less like a warrior and more like a woman who had just watched the entire world bleed out in front of her.</p>
<p>She opened her mouth to scream a sound that promised a vengeance so deep it would hollow out the earth, but the scream caught in her throat, turning into a jagged, choked sob that made her entire frame shudder violently.</p>
<p>"Hhh... hhh... you... you fucking..." she gasped, her voice trembling with the weight of a thousand unspoken agonies.</p>
<p>Before the outburst could shatter the heavy air, Aurelia moved. Aurelia, ever the tactician, processed the mathematics of the moment faster than Iris’s grieving mind could.</p>
<p>She saw the way Rex’s eyes were still glowing with the residual, cold light of his gravitational aura, the look of a predator that hadn’t quite finished the kill. She saw the way the air around Rex was still vibrating with the violent energy of the slaughter.</p>
<p>With a fluid, decisive motion, Aurelia stepped into the space between them. She didn’t stand in front of Iris like a shield; she positioned herself with the precise, calculated geometry of a redirection, a barrier meant to guide a falling star back into its orbit.</p>
<p>"We need to go," Aurelia commanded. Her voice was low, a sharp blade cutting through the thick, metallic fog of the plaza. "Now!!!"</p>
<p>Iris didn’t even blink. Her gaze remained locked on the mangled, unmoving heap that was Veylor, her eyes tracing the dark, widening pool of crimson that was slowly swallowing his remains.</p>
<p>She was anchored to the carnage.</p>
<p>"Iris," Aurelia said, her voice hardening, injecting a sense of urgent survival into the air. "We need to go... NOW!"</p>
<p>"He just..." Iris started, her voice a broken whisper, a fragile thread of a sentence that died in the heavy silence. "He was just... there... and now he’s..."</p>
<p>She couldn’t finish. To finish the sentence was to accept the finality of the blood and the bone.</p>
<p>"I know," Aurelia intercepted, her eyes darting briefly to Rex, then back to her friend. "I know. But not here. Not like this!"</p>
<p>"Not while the scent of him is still in the air! Come on!"</p>
