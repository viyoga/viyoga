<p>Rex didn’t wait for the god to process the threat. He moved before the last syllable had even left his lips. His Lustful Villain register triggered a cold, predatory shift in his consciousness that collapsed the distance between intent and execution to absolute zero.</p>
<p>He wasn’t running; he was a blur of violence, a streak of crimson and tan cutting through the divine haze at a speed that Kaeleth’s depleted temporal reserves could no longer track.</p>
<p>The god’s eyes widened, pupils contracting as he realized his reaction time was now measured in seconds while Rex was operating in milliseconds.</p>
<p>What followed was not a fourth exchange. This was not a tactical back-and-forth exchange.</p>
<p>It was an execution wearing the mask of a fight.</p>
<p>Kaeleth had made the fatal error of every immortal who had never tasted defeat: he had treated Rex like a puzzle to be solved rather than a catastrophe to be survived.</p>
<p>He had spent the whole fight trying out different divine powers to see which one could finally defeat Rex, ignoring the clear warning from the first sign of trouble: that none of his powers could control something that didn’t follow the usual rules of cause and effect.</p>
<p>Rex’s fingers slammed shut around Kaeleth’s throat.</p>
<p>The contact was brutal. Rex’s knuckles scraped against the divine flesh, the pressure so immense that the god’s neck tendons strained like steel cables.</p>
<p>Rex wasn’t trying to choke him; he knew divine anatomy didn’t require oxygen. He was anchoring himself. He needed that point of contact.</p>
<p>He needed to bridge the gap between his own corrupted reality and the god’s perfect one so that when he unleashed what came next, there would be no distance for the divine authority to deflect it.</p>
<p>Up close, the illusion of Kaeleth’s divinity shattered completely. Rex’s Divine Perception pierced through the golden radiance and the regal robes, reading the truth that the god’s face couldn’t hide.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was terrified.</p>
<p>Rex could see it in the minute tremors of the god’s eyelids, in the way his divine aura flickered like a dying candle, and in the hollow, desperate emptiness behind those ancient eyes.</p>
<p>The ruler of two millennia was suddenly reduced to something small, something fragile, something mortal.</p>
<p>Rex leaned in, his breath hot against the god’s ear, his voice dropping to a whisper that carried more menace than any roar. The performance was over.</p>
<p>The bravado, the taunts, and the theatricality—all of it fell away, leaving only the raw, unfiltered hunger of the predator.</p>
<p>"This," Rex said, the words dripping with a cold, inevitable finality, "is the part where you find out what happens after."</p>
<p>The domain itself seemed to recoil. The very fabric of space held its breath, the specific, suffocating silence of a reality that knows its owner is about to lose control.</p>
<p>The architecture groaned, the light dimmed to a sickly amber, and for one suspended moment, time itself seemed to freeze in anticipation of the slaughter.</p>
<p>Then, the fourteen minutes ended.</p>
<p>The slaughter began not with a bang, but with the terrifying precision of a god realizing he was being hunted by something that learned faster than he could kill.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s desperation manifested first as a Temporal Slowtemporal slowdown. He threw his will against the fabric of reality, attempting to drag Rex’s personal timeline into thick, viscous molasses.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, Rex felt the universe turn to honey, every movement requiring an impossible expenditure of effort. But Rex’s adaptation was already screaming.</p>
<p>The system analyzed the god tier implementation the way Kaeleth manipulated the fundamental constant of speed, and within five point eight agonizing seconds, Rex’s consciousness had mapped the divine architecture of the ability.</p>
<p>He didn’t just resist it; he learned how to exist within it.</p>
<p>"Is that it?" Rex’s inner voice growled through the mental static. "Is that all the power of two millennia gets you?"</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s face contorted with fury. He reached deeper, pulling at the seams of causality itself.</p>
<p>He unleashed a timeline fracture.</p>
<p>This was something Rex had never encountered. Theo’s mortal-level abilities hadn’t touched this level of atrocity.</p>
<p>As the fracture struck, Rex’s sense of self didn’t just slow; it shattered. His consciousness splintered into a thousand jagged shards.</p>
<p>One version of Rex was screaming; another was already dead; a third was still standing frozen. His very existence became incoherent, a kaleidoscopic nightmare of simultaneous, conflicting presents.</p>
<p>It felt like his brain was being fed through a meat grinder of pure information.</p>
<p>’Four point nine seconds.’</p>
<p>Four point nine seconds of absolute, existential horror. Then, with a psychic wrench that felt like tearing his own soul back together, Rex snapped back into coherence.</p>
<p>The adaptation had done its work. It had tasted the incoherence and learned how to stitch itself back together.</p>
<p>The coverage was extended: Timeline Fractures acquired.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t pause to recover. He channeled his fury through his Telekinesistelekinesis, not as a blunt instrument, but as a scalpel of pure rage.</p>
<p>He slammed the force against Kaeleth’s position with the specific, directed violence of someone who had just survived being unmade.</p>
<p>The impact was devastating. Kaeleth was hurled backward eight meters, his divine robes fluttering like wounded wings as he skidded across the sanctum floor.</p>
<p>"You monster," Kaeleth hissed, blood golden and luminous seeping from the corner of his mouth. "You are not a man...!"</p>
<p>"You are a cancer on reality!"</p>
<p>"Then consider yourself infected," Rex spat.</p>
<p>Kaeleth rose, his eyes blazing with a cold, apocalyptic light. He didn’t try to slow or fracture this time.</p>
<p>He went for the kill. He deployed the Temporal Freeze.</p>
<p>Unlike Theo’s area of effect stop, this was a surgical strike. Kaeleth targeted Rex’s personal timeline with god-tier precision, attempting to halt Rex’s existence entirely.</p>
<p>For three point one seconds, Rex ceased to be. He didn’t just stop moving; he stopped happening.</p>
<p>He became a statue in the middle of a rushing river, a fixed point of nothingness in a moving world.</p>
<p>’Three point one seconds.’</p>
<p>The adaptation seized the pattern before the freeze could become permanent. Rex’s consciousness reconnected with a jarring, nauseating lurch, the sensation of a mind being forcibly yanked back into a body that had forgotten how to breathe.</p>
<p>His lungs burned, his heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped animal, and his vision strobed with residual temporal afterimages.</p>
<p>But Rex was already moving. He was done playing with time.</p>
<p>He reached down with the Earthen Authority, seizing the very bones of the domain. The stone-analogous substance beneath his feet groaned and shrieked as he tore it apart.</p>
<p>With terrifying precision, he fused the debris into a massive, jagged geological construct—a projectile of compressed mineral density designed to shatter anything in its path.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a temporal attack. It wasn’t a concept or a law.</p>
<p>It was pure, unadulterated physical force. And in the calculus of combat, physical force possessed a unique advantage: it operated on a different dimensional plane than temporal defenses.</p>
<p>You could slow time, but you couldn’t slow the momentum of a mountain once it was already moving.</p>
<p>Rex launched it. The projectile tore through the air with a sound like a collapsing building, a screaming comet of stone and fury aimed directly at Kaeleth’s chest.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s eyes narrowed. With a flick of his wrist, he raised a barrier of compressed light, deflecting the projectile.</p>
<p>The impact sent sparks of divine energy cascading across the chamber like fireworks, but the god remained unbowed.</p>
