<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He stood his ground in the empty sky, a man who had seen the bottom of the world and wasn’t afraid of the heights.</p>
<p>"You are not here because Celestina sent you," Rex said, his voice cutting through her fury like a blade. "You are here because Kregg is dead, and Celestina gave you an operational directive that happened to point directly at the man responsible."</p>
<p>"Those are not the same thing, Ignivara..."</p>
<p>"The motivation is different, and so is the fighting style."</p>
<p>"A true operative of Celestina would be patient."</p>
<p>"She would be methodical. But you?" Rex laughed. "Oh boy... you are pushing this engagement forward with a reckless, violent urgency because you don’t just want the mission accomplished... you want it resolved."</p>
<p>Ignivara’s next assault was a frantic, jagged thing. The disciplined, economical grace of her aerial combat had vanished, replaced by a desperate, unrefined ferocity.</p>
<p>She was no longer fighting like a soldier; she was fighting like a wounded animal, her movements wide and heavy, her emotional instability bleeding into her technical output. The air around her didn’t just whistle; it screamed with the turbulence of her rage.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t dodge. He didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>He let her close the distance, his eyes cold and analytical, watching the flaws in her technique widen with every heartbeat. He redirected the first strike with a flick of his will, caught the second on a rebuilt stone shield that groaned under the impact, and as the two of them collided in a mess of heat and kinetic force, he spoke with a chilling, detached clarity.</p>
<p>"He was important to you," Rex said, his voice cutting through the roar of the wind like a scalpel. "Not professionally. Not as a commander. He was actually important."</p>
<p>"A sentimental attachment in a profession that demands none."</p>
<p>"He was my mentor!" she snarled, the words tearing from her throat as she slammed a fist toward his chest.</p>
<p>The close-range physicality of the fight had neutralized her aerial advantage; there was no room for her wings to build momentum, forcing them into a brutal, grinding struggle of pure strength. "He was the one who found me!"</p>
<p>"Before Celestina... before the Legion... He was the one who explained what the hell I was and why it meant something!"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t offer a comforting look. He didn’t even offer a moment of silence to respect her grief.</p>
<p>He simply held her at bay, his telekinesis acting as a buffer between her fury and his skin, his expression one of mild, almost bored observation.</p>
<p>"Then I am sorry," Rex said, though the words held zero warmth.</p>
<p>They were as hollow as a tomb. "That he died the way he did... It was a pathetic way for a man of his supposed caliber to go out."</p>
<p>Ignivara recoiled as if he had physically struck her. She broke the close distance deliberately, soaring backward and upward, her wings beating a frantic rhythm as she fought to create space and find the air she needed to breathe again.</p>
<p>"You killed him," she accused, her voice trembling with a lethal, vibrating tension. "YOU FUCKING KILLED HIM!!!"</p>
<p>"Well, duh, he was going to kill more people who couldn’t defend themselves," Rex countered, his voice devoid of any moral weight.</p>
<p>To him, it was a simple equation of efficiency. "Kregg was a man of grand ambitions and small morals."</p>
<p>"The choice he made in that canyon, the choice to prioritize his own tactical advantage over innocent lives, is why he is a corpse."</p>
<p>"I was merely the mechanism that executed the consequence," Rex smirked. "Do not confuse the tool with the cause."</p>
<p>"It is close enough!" she screamed, her golden eyes flashing with a terrifying, primal light.</p>
<p>"It really is not," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice wasn’t hard or furious; it was worse. It was the voice of a man stating a mathematical fact, a man who had weighed the lives of hundreds against the life of one man and found the trade to be a bargain.</p>
<p>"If you had arrived at that canyon and found those same people, and Kregg had made the same call, would you have stopped him?"</p>
<p>"Would you have stood in the way of his ’glory’ to save a few peasants?"</p>
<p>"But fuck it... you’re one of them anyway." Rex laughed.</p>
<p>Silence fell between them, heavy and suffocating, broken only by the distant, mournful cry of the wind. Ignivara remained still, her wings poised, her chest heaving.</p>
<p>"And that is the honest version of the question," Rex continued, his eyes narrowing with a hint of predatory disdain. "Don’t give me the ’justified’ bullshit just because you’re right in your point of view!"</p>
<p>"Tell me if you would have done exactly what I did with a different point of view when you didn’t discover the Legion... given the same information and the same options."</p>
<p>"Or are you just a hypocrite clinging to the memory of a man who was too weak to be truly great?"</p>
<p>"I would not have been in that position!" she snapped, her voice defensive, a desperate attempt to shield her ego from his scrutiny.</p>
<p>"That is not an answer," Rex said, his lip curling into a faint, mocking sneer. "That is an escape."</p>
<p>"It is the only answer I have!" she hissed.</p>
<p>The flatness in her voice was the sound of a cornered creature, the sound of someone who realized they were being mentally dismantled, maneuvered into a corner where the only way out was to confront the ugly truth they were terrified to face.</p>
<p>Rex let the silence stretch, watching her struggle, enjoying the way her certainty was eroding under the weight of his logic. He let the tension hang, a predator waiting for the final, inevitable crack in her resolve.</p>
<p>The silence didn’t last long. Rex broke it, not with a counterattack, but with a sound that was far more insulting: a low, guttural chuckle that rapidly escalated into a full, mocking laugh.</p>
<p>It was a sound of genuine amusement, the kind a man makes when watching a child struggle to stand against a gale.</p>
<p>"You want more explanation about the escape I just said...? Well, yes. Exactly as you thought," Rex said, his eyes dancing with a sadistic light. "You’re clinging to a ghost, Ignivara!"</p>
<p>"You’re fighting for a man who was nothing more than a glorified butcher with a sentimental streak."</p>
<p>"Shut up!" she screamed, her voice cracking under the sheer pressure of her rage.</p>
<p>Her whole body became a projectile of gold and fury as she lunged. She unleashed a flurry of wing strikes so rapid they blurred into a singular, devastating arc of kinetic energy.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even bother with a shield this time. He danced.</p>
<p>He moved with a fluid, almost insulting ease, his body tilting and weaving through the air as if he were performing a choreographed routine. A massive wing swept toward his head; he leaned back just enough for the wind of the strike to ruffle his hair.</p>
<p>As a second strike aimed at his ribs, he spun on his axis, the edge of the wing whistling past his chest by a hair’s breadth. He was dodging her most lethal blows with the nonchalance of a man walking through a light rain.</p>
<p>"He was a good teacher!" Ignivara roared, the sound a primal, agonizing scream that tore from her lungs.</p>
<p>She was losing control, her movements becoming frantic and heavy. "He taught me discipline! He taught me strength! He taught me what it meant to be a predator!"</p>
<p>"He taught you how to be a loyal dog!" Rex barked back, laughing even harder as he ducked under a sweeping blow that would have decapitated a lesser man. "He taught you how to follow orders and die for a cause that didn’t even care if you lived!"</p>
<p>"He wasn’t a teacher, Ignivara!"</p>
<p>"He was a handler. And you? You’re just the most expensive hound he ever trained."</p>
<p>"You don’t know him!" she screamed, her eyes brimming with a mixture of tears and pure, unadulterated hatred.</p>
<p>She threw herself at him, her hands glowing with concentrated thermal energy, her strikes coming in a desperate, wide arcing barrage.</p>
<p>Thwack. Whoosh. Crack.</p>
<p>Every time she thought she had him pinned, he was gone, a phantom in the sky, leaving her to strike at nothing but empty air and the echoes of her own frustration.</p>
<p>"I knew him well enough to see the rot!" Rex shouted over her screams, his laughter booming through the clouds.</p>
<p>He dodged a lunge that sent her flying past him, her own momentum nearly sending her into a tailspin. "He was a man who thought he was a god, but he died in the dirt like the rest of the cattle!"</p>
<p>"And you’re here, crying over his corpse like a heartbroken girl instead of a warrior!"</p>
<p>"Die!" she shrieked, the word a jagged shard of glass.</p>
<p>She channeled every ounce of her dragon kin’s strength into a single, massive downward strike, her wings snapping shut to drive her like a spear toward his center.</p>
<p>Rex watched her approach, the sheer violence of her movement enough to make the very air tremble. He didn’t look afraid; he looked delighted.</p>
<p>As the shadow of her descent fell over him, he let out one last, mocking peal of laughter.</p>
<p>"Look at you!" he taunted, his voice dripping with venomous glee. "All that power, all that heritage, and you’re nothing but a weeping widow for a dead man’s mistakes!"</p>
