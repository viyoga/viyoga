<p>Ignivara’s gaze drifted back to the dark wood of the table, her eyes seemingly fixed on a point miles beneath the surface.</p>
<p>"The three children were not targets," she said, her voice dropping to a hollow, haunting whisper. "The Legion is efficient; they do not waste resources on collateral unless the mission dictates it."</p>
<p>"The children were not harmed... They were simply... there."</p>
<p>"They were standing in the wreckage of a life that had just been surgically removed. And as I moved along the extraction path, as the adrenaline began to fade and the cold reality of the mission set in, the silence of that village began to scream at me."</p>
<p>"I could not stop thinking about what that village would become in a month... A month of hollow eyes and empty chairs and a month of children wondering why the light had gone out of their world, all because a machine decided their mother was a ’variable.’"</p>
<p>The silence in the room was no longer just heavy; it was suffocating, a thick, visceral thing that made it difficult to breathe.</p>
<p>"I did not quit," she said, her voice sharpening with a sudden, defensive edge. "I want to be precise about that, because the truth is far more damning than a sudden burst of conscience."</p>
<p>"I continued to work for the Legion for two more years."</p>
<p>"I completed six more assignments... I was precise and I was professional."</p>
<p>"I was a perfect instrument of their will... I did not let the cracks show, and I did not discuss the rot that had begun to fester in my mind regarding their framework."</p>
<p>She paused, her eyes flashing with a sudden, violent clarity. "But the tense changed... internally..."</p>
<p>"And once that shift happens, the organization’s logic stops being the truth you live by and starts being a foreign language you are forced to translate."</p>
<p>"You say the words they want to hear, but you no longer believe the meaning behind them."</p>
<p>Apollo leaned forward, his eyes narrowing. He was no longer just assessing a prisoner; he was weighing a soul against the divine scales of the Apostle of Life.</p>
<p>He looked at her with a terrifying, predatory intensity, searching for the moment the lie would break her.</p>
<p>"You said eleven," Apollo said, his voice low and resonant, vibrating with the authority of his station. "Can you name them?"</p>
<p>Ignivara met his gaze, her expression unreadable, though a flicker of something, perhaps a ghost of a memory, danced in her eyes.</p>
<p>"Yes," she said.</p>
<p>And then, she began.</p>
<p>She did not recite file numbers, nor did she use the cold, clinical designations of the Legion’s archives. She did not speak of ’Target Alpha’ or ’Anomaly 402.’ Instead, she spoke of people.</p>
<p>She spoke of the mercenary woman outside Valdric, a woman with a fierce laugh who had run a protection service for a starving farming village, only to be erased because her combat prowess was deemed ’too loud’ for the Legion’s delicate sensors.</p>
<p>She spoke of the scholar in the Aurelian Compact, a man whose eyes had lit up when he discovered a new way to weave magic from his knowledge-user system, a man whose brilliance was seen as a contagion that needed to be quarantined by death.</p>
<p>She named them one by one, her voice maintaining a flat, terrifying accuracy. It was the voice of a woman who had been carrying a graveyard in her mind for years and was finally, agonizingly, laying the bodies down.</p>
<p>Each name was a heavy, rhythmic tolling of a bell, a verbal accounting of a debt that could never truly be repaid.</p>
<p>When the eleventh name finally left her lips, the atmosphere of the room underwent a profound, tectonic shift. The air felt different, thinner, colder, more sacred.</p>
<p>The room had become a vessel for a truth that had cost the speaker everything she was.</p>
<p>The knowledge system holder had stopped the frantic scratching of his quill. His notes lay forgotten, a mess of ink and half-formed thoughts.</p>
<p>He was staring at the table, his professional mask shattered, looking like a man who had just realized the math he had been using his whole life was fundamentally broken.</p>
<p>Aurelia’s expression had shifted from the guarded suspicion of a general to the profound, unsettling realization of a philosopher. She had come to assess a threat, a predator to be caged, but she found herself staring at something far more dangerous and far more complex: a broken saint.</p>
<p>Even Morwenna, who had remained a statue by the window, had changed. She still faced the jagged, broken skyline of the city, but her stillness was no longer passive.</p>
<p>It was the stillness of a listener. She was no longer watching the horizon; she was listening to the echoes of the names Ignivara had just released into the air.</p>
<p>Rex, sensing the moment of vulnerability had passed and the moment of utility had arrived, broke the heavy trance. He stood tall beside Elizabeth, his presence grounding the room back into the realm of the living.</p>
<p>"The Legion," Rex said, his voice a command that demanded the transition from confession to strategy. "Walk us through the structure... Give us the architecture of the beast."</p>
<p>Ignivara drew a breath, but it wasn’t the breath of a woman seeking air; it was the breath of a soldier bracing for the impact of a heavy blow. She leaned forward slightly, the movement deliberate, as if she were physically pushing the weight of her next words into the center of the table.</p>
<p>"Celestina Von Starlight founded the Legion approximately thirty years ago," she began. The name hung in the air like a sudden drop in temperature. "And I know the weight that name carries in this room."</p>
<p>"I know it will poison the very air you breathe. So, let us strip the skin from the bone and address it directly: the surname is real."</p>
<p>"The lineage is unbroken. Celestina shares a father with Valentina Von Starlight."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was not quiet; it was a violent, sudden vacuum. Every eye in the room pivoted, a synchronized movement of shock and accusation, toward Valentina.</p>
<p>Valentina did not flinch. She did not gasp.</p>
<p>She sat like a statue carved from obsidian, her face a mask of terrifying, unreadable composure, though the air around her seemed to vibrate with the sudden, jagged tension.</p>
<p>"This is not..." Ignivara continued, her voice maintaining a razor-thin level of stability, the kind of stability required when one is walking a tightrope over a pit of vipers. "...a coincidence."</p>
<p>"This is not a piece of disinformation planted by the Legion to destabilize this network or to sow seeds of doubt."</p>
<p>"The blood connection is genuine... Which means the Legion has known about the specific, intimate relationship between the headmaster and its founder for the entire duration of the organization’s existence."</p>
<p>"They have sat on this secret, nursing it like a slow-acting venom."</p>
<p>"And they have not used it," Morwenna said, her voice cutting through the tension like a blade.</p>
<p>She had finally turned from the window, her eyes burning with a cold, predatory intelligence. "They have held the throat of this entire network for decades and chose not to squeeze."</p>
<p>"They have not used it publicly," Ignivara corrected, her eyes meeting Morwenna’s. "Celestina’s operational philosophy is not that of a gambler who plays her hand early."</p>
<p>"She is not a woman who deploys leverage visibly to win small skirmishes..."</p>
<p>"She is a strategist of the long game, and she holds her cards until the moment of maximum, catastrophic effect."</p>
<p>"The name was released into this network’s intelligence picture at this exact moment, in this specific room, during this specific crisis, and this was not an accident."</p>
<p>"It was a precision strike, and it was the specific deployment she had been sharpening for thirty years."</p>
<p>Apollo’s jaw tightened, a muscle leaping in his cheek. The realization was a physical blow to him.</p>
<p>"She expected us to fracture," he said, his voice a low growl of realization. "She didn’t want to kill us..."</p>
<p>"She wanted to break the bond that makes us dangerous."</p>
<p>"She expected it to produce exactly the chaos we witnessed in the first portion of tonight’s meeting," Ignivara said, her gaze flickering momentarily toward Rex. "Before he spoke and before he provided the anchor."</p>
<p>The collective gaze of the room shifted to Rex. He sat there, a pillar of silent strength, his expression unreadable, though the intensity of the moment seemed to settle on his shoulders like a heavy mantle of iron.</p>
<p>He said nothing, letting the gravity of the accusation settle.</p>
<p>"You are telling us," Morwenna said, her voice dropping to a dangerous, slow tempo, "that the name was not just information."</p>
<p>"It was a weapon. A blade she had been honing in the dark for three decades, waiting for the perfect moment to slide it between our ribs."</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara said.</p>
<p>"Knowing that the Headmaster’s composure was the only thing holding this entire network together," Morwenna pressed, her eyes narrowing as she pieced together the brutal geometry of the attack. "Understanding that just one piece of information, a truth that directly challenged Valentina’s trust, could create a split so deep that thirty years of direct, violent actions couldn’t have done the same."</p>
<p>"She didn’t want to fight us with armies."</p>
<p>"She wanted to destroy us with a single, whispered truth."</p>
<p>"Yes," Ignivara said, her voice final, as if the verdict had already been passed.</p>
