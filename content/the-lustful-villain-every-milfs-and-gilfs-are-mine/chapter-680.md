<p>The briefing lasted two grueling, razor-sharp hours. Pavellia moved through the reconstruction’s operational architecture with the terrifying efficiency of a grand architect executing a long-awaited design.</p>
<p>This wasn’t a mere report; it was a masterwork of logistical warfare, a forty-three-page dossier she had forged in the frantic, blood-stained hours between the purge’s final phase and the throne room session. She had worked through the night while Rex slept, her mind a relentless engine of calculation.</p>
<p>When she placed the heavy stack of documents on the table, Rex noted the slight paleness of her skin and the predatory focus in her eyes. He offered no praise; admiration was a luxury for the weak, but the silence between them acknowledged her labor.</p>
<p>The first thirty minutes were a brutal dissection of the city’s broken bones. Halvard stepped forward, presenting a material assessment that was as cold as it was comprehensive.</p>
<p>He provided a district-by-district autopsy of the structural damage, the yawning chasm between current reserves and total repair requirements, and a projected timeline that felt more like a countdown to collapse. The methodology was flawless, revealing a man who didn’t just report data but understood the weight of every stone and every life lost.</p>
<p>"The eastern residential sector is our critical path," Halvard stated, his voice cutting through the tension. "Not because the damage is the most catastrophic; the northern market district bore the brunt of the construct deployment’s kinetic impact."</p>
<p>"But the eastern sector houses... the city’s largest concentration of noncombatants."</p>
<p>"If we don’t stabilize the structural integrity there, we aren’t just rebuilding a city; we’re managing a graveyard."</p>
<p>"Habitability is our primary vulnerability."</p>
<p>Rex leaned back, his eyes tracking the data like a hawk. "Define the prioritization sequence."</p>
<p>"Eastern residential first," Halvard answered instantly. "For habitability and social stability."</p>
<p>"Northern market second; the market infrastructure is the heartbeat of the supply chain that feeds the reconstruction itself."</p>
<p>"The outer districts will follow in parallel, third and fourth, as their damage is largely cosmetic and manageable with smaller, mobile teams."</p>
<p>"The construction type summons Mordecai identified," Rex interjected, his voice dropping an octave, signaling a shift in the conversation. "Thirty available."</p>
<p>"How does that impact the math?"</p>
<p>Halvard’s eyes scanned the figures. "The forty percent resource gap shrinks to approximately twenty-two percent with thirty additional skilled laborers."</p>
<p>"The timeline on the critical path drops from nine weeks to roughly six."</p>
<p>"And the remaining twenty-two percent?" Rex pressed, the question hanging like a threat. "Where is the material coming from?"</p>
<p>"That is the second stratum contact item," Pavellia interjected, her voice a smooth segue into the heart of the strategy.</p>
<p>"Explain the specific necessity," Rex commanded.</p>
<p>Pavellia tapped the document. "The transition period stone layer identified by Gorvasha is located in the northern canyon reach."</p>
<p>"The second stratum’s upper boundary is composed of that exact material."</p>
<p>"Their entire construction methodology has been evolved specifically to manipulate and harness it."</p>
<p>"They can produce it," Gorvasha added, her voice ringing with certainty. "The second stratum has been quarrying transition period stone from their side of the boundary for generations."</p>
<p>"They don’t just have it; they have massive, untapped reserves."</p>
<p>"And they have been waiting for a reason to establish formal contact," Mordecai added from the far end of the table, his mind already weaving the political threads. "Offering to bridge our material gap is the perfect catalyst."</p>
<p>"It gives them a reason to speak without the vulnerability of a formal plea."</p>
<p>"It also establishes a precedent of trade before a single diplomatic word is even uttered," Rex said, a dark, calculating smile ghosting his lips. "Which is the only logical order of operations."</p>
<p>"A working relationship must precede a formal one... A transaction produces the evidence that a treaty is worth the ink."</p>
<p>Mordecai watched him, his expression one of intense integration, absorbing the sheer ruthlessness of Rex’s logic.</p>
<p>"You want the first contact to be a transaction," Mordecai mused. "A deal, not a declaration."</p>
<p>"A declaration is merely a statement of intent," Rex countered, his gaze turning piercing. "A transaction is a demonstration of power and utility."</p>
<p>"The second stratum has been signaling for three years."</p>
<p>"They have been patient because their timescale is geological, not human. But do not mistake patience for a lack of preference."</p>
<p>"They would much rather prove the relationship works through commerce than debate whether it should exist through politics."</p>
<p>"How can you be so certain?" Mordecai asked. It wasn’t a challenge to Rex’s authority but a genuine quest for the logic behind the instinct.</p>
<p>"The signal pattern Pavellia described," Rex said, his eyes narrowing. "Three years of testing."</p>
<p>"Increasing in frequency, but never in force."</p>
<p>"If an entity were preparing for a confrontation, they would have escalated the violence of the signal alongside the frequency."</p>
<p>"And yet... they haven’t. They aren’t trying to break the door down, Mordecai."</p>
<p>"They are waiting for someone to turn the handle."</p>
<p>Mordecai sat back, the weight of the realization settling on him.</p>
<p>"I can work with that framing," he said, his voice hardening with resolve. "When I approach the relay contact point, I won’t lead with recognition or sovereignty."</p>
<p>"I will lead with the material exchange... We will offer them a purpose before we offer them a title."</p>
<p>"Lead with the material exchange," Rex confirmed, his voice final and absolute. "Give them a reason to want us before you tell them they must obey us."</p>
<p>Cassandra had been waiting for this moment with the predatory patience of a general. When she placed her document on the table, it was noticeably thinner than Pavellia’s, but it possessed a terrifying density; every word was a sharpened blade, stripped of all fluff and compressed into pure, functional lethality.</p>
<p>"Our current military structure is a slave to Mordecai’s whims," Cassandra began, her voice cutting through the room like a cold wind. "It is built entirely around his gacha output."</p>
<p>"The composition shifts with every significant pull cycle, meaning we cannot build a cohesive unit around specific capabilities."</p>
<p>"We are forced to build around mere categories..."</p>
<p>"We are fighting a war of chance."</p>
<p>"That is the existing problem," Rex said, his tone dry, acknowledging the flaw without offering sympathy.</p>
<p>"The post-reconstruction problem is far more lethal," Cassandra countered, leaning forward. "The purge didn’t just kill people; it gutted our expertise."</p>
<p>"It wiped out a massive percentage of our highest-capability individuals within the non-summon population."</p>
<p>"What remains is a base of survivors, men and women with the raw, frantic combat experience of last night’s slaughter, but they lack the disciplined, organized training required to produce the specific quality of force needed for the canyon engagement."</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes locked onto hers, unblinking. "What timeline do you require to turn these survivors into a scalpel?"</p>
<p>"For the canyon engagement as described against an Apostle network expedition, I need a minimum of eight weeks of sustained, high-intensity training," she stated. "However, if the training can happen at the same time as getting the geological site ready, and if the team can practice using the canyon’s defenses as part of their drills instead of seeing them as extra challenges, we can shorten the training to six weeks."</p>
<p>"Six weeks, concurrent," Rex repeated, weighing the risk.</p>
<p>"Six weeks is achievable," Cassandra affirmed, "on one non-negotiable condition: the training must occur at the site itself."</p>
<p>"The canyon’s terrain isn’t just a backdrop; it is the training environment."</p>
<p>"They must bleed in the dirt they are meant to defend."</p>
<p>Gorvasha looked up from her notes, her brow furrowing. "The northern canyon reach is a three-hour march from the city’s primary gate."</p>
<p>"A deployment of that scale means the city’s standing defense is stripped of its primary muscle for the duration of the training window."</p>
<p>The tension in the room spiked. To move the army was to leave the throat of the city exposed.</p>
<p>"What is the realistic threat to the city during that window?" Rex demanded.</p>
<p>Pavellia answered before the question could even settle, her mind already three steps ahead. "If Celestina responds within the six- to eight-week window you’ve identified, she won’t strike the city head-on."</p>
<p>"Her operational pattern, according to Kregg’s intelligence, is precision extraction."</p>
<p>"She doesn’t want a massacre; she wants specific targets. She will move like a ghost through the shadows, not a hammer against the gates."</p>
<p>"Which means the city’s primary requirement during that window isn’t brute force," Rex deduced, his eyes gleaming with the cold logic of a strategist. "It’s detection."</p>
<p>"Precisely," Pavellia said. "The restructuring of the monitoring network is designed to address exactly that."</p>
<p>"We don’t need a wall of shields in the streets to maintain the detection layer."</p>
<p>"We need eyes."</p>
<p>"Then the deployment is viable," Rex declared.</p>
<p>He turned his gaze back to Cassandra. "Six weeks at the site."</p>
<p>"Give me the numbers..."</p>
<p>"What does the force composition look like?"</p>
<p>"The summoned population provides approximately three hundred combat class units in the current deployment," Cassandra said. "If Mordecai’s pulls over the next six weeks are strictly directed toward combat class outputs, that number will swell."</p>
<p>"Combined with the two hundred experienced fighters from the native population, we are looking at a core of five hundred."</p>
<p>"Five hundred at the canyon site," Rex mused.</p>
<p>"And the geological advantage multiplies that force exponentially," Cassandra added, her voice rising with a hint of tactical hunger. "If Gorvasha’s assessment of the fault line intersection is accurate, the defensive position at that site isn’t just a stronghold."</p>
<p>"With three fault lines feeding into a single pressure point and the Earthen Authority able to direct the geological output... it isn’t a defensive position in any traditional sense."</p>
<p>"It is a weapon," Gorvasha interjected, her voice low and heavy with the weight of the earth’s power.</p>
<p>"A weapon with a city-sized trigger," Cassandra corrected sharply. "And I need to master the mechanism before I build an engagement plan around it."</p>
<p>Rex turned his head toward Gorvasha, his command absolute. "The survey this afternoon."</p>
<p>"The survey this afternoon," Gorvasha confirmed.</p>
