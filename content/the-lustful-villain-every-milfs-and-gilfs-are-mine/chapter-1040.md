<p>The sky above Aethelgard did not merely change; it hemorrhaged. The morning light, once warm and predictable, began to fracture as if the medium through which it traveled had turned to cracked glass.</p>
<p>The atmosphere took on a sickly, unnatural quality, a visual distortion that signaled reality was no longer operating under standard physical parameters. The air itself felt viscous, resisting movement like invisible oil.</p>
<p>Through the lens of [Divine Perception], the true horror of the sky was laid bare. Rex didn’t just see clouds and sunlight; he saw the amber gold signature of Kaeleth’s divine authority tearing through the firmament.</p>
<p>It wasn’t the god himself, but a stolen fire—the specific, blasphemous investment of divine temporal power channeled into a mortal vessel.</p>
<p>The symbol was gargantuan, a celestial brand seared into the divine register. It hung directly above Aethelgard’s central district like the eye of a predator, claiming dominion over every second that passed beneath it.</p>
<p>Rex stared up at the burning sigil, his mind racing. His perception dove deeper, past the golden flames of authority, searching for the anchor the mortal soul that served as the conduit for this stolen divinity.</p>
<p>He found it.</p>
<p>The moment his consciousness locked onto the soul signature, a cold, flat sensation washed over him. It wasn’t fear.</p>
<p>It was the hollow, mechanical satisfaction of a predator whose trap had just sprung exactly as designed. Confirmation had arrived, but it felt different than prediction.</p>
<p>Prediction was a theory; confirmation was a cold, hard fact that demanded blood.</p>
<p>’Theo Morr Nightwing.’</p>
<p>The name resonated in Rex’s mind like a death knell. The boy was playing with fire he couldn’t possibly control.</p>
<p>Rex was still tracking the golden wound in the sky when the universe died.</p>
<p>The stop was absolute. It wasn’t a slow fading of motion or a gradual deceleration; it was a violent, instantaneous severance.</p>
<p>The ambient cacophony of the city—the distant shouts of merchants, the clatter of wheels on cobblestone, the rustle of wind—was cut off with the brutal precision of a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>One moment, the world was alive; the next, it was a corpse.</p>
<p>Pigeons were suspended mid-flap above the academy tower, their feathers frozen like carved marble. In the northern district, reconstruction crews became living statues, mid-swing with hammers that would never fall.</p>
<p>The water in the fountain behind Rex froze in mid arc, each droplet a suspended crystal suspended in a gravity that no longer applied. The silence wasn’t peaceful; it was deafening, a pressurized void that screamed in the ears.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the center of the frozen apocalypse, his pulse the only thing moving in a world of stone.</p>
<p>Immediately, [Full Potential Adaptation] roared to life.</p>
<p>He felt the attack. The temporal stop didn’t feel like stillness; it felt like an assault.</p>
<p>A metaphysical pressure slammed into his body, an invisible hand attempting to sever his personal timeline from the universal flow.</p>
<p>It felt as though his very existence was being unstitched, his atoms being told they no longer had permission to move through time. The sensation was nauseating, a psychic wrenching that threatened to tear his consciousness apart.</p>
<p>’This is it,’ Rex thought, his inner voice calm despite the cosmic violation. ’The first contact.’</p>
<p>The system worked with terrifying speed. In the seven tenths of a second between the moment the temporal authority struck him and the moment the adaptation completed its analysis, Rex endured the full, unmitigated force of the divine stop.</p>
<p>His nerves screamed as his timeline shuddered under the strain, the sensation of being ’erased’ from the moment threatening to overwhelm his senses.</p>
<p>But the system did not fail.</p>
<p>[ANALYZING TEMPORAL MECHANISM: DIVINE CLASS CHRONO SEVERANCE DETECTED]</p>
<p>[INTEGRATING DEFENSE... ADAPTATION COMPLETE.]</p>
<p>[TEMPORAL STASIS IMMUNITY GRANTED: SUBSEQUENT CONTACTS WILL BE FULLY NEGATED.]</p>
<p>The crushing pressure vanished. The invisible hands that had been trying to strangle his timeline suddenly lost their grip, sliding off him as if he were made of untouchable diamond.</p>
<p>The stasis still held the world around him captive, but Rex was no longer part of its prison. He was an anomaly, a living variable in a frozen equation.</p>
<p>A dark, hungry smile spread across Rex’s face.</p>
<p>The boy wanted to stop time? Fine.</p>
<p>Rex would show him what happens when you try to freeze something that refuses to die.</p>
<p>Rex took a step forward. His boot struck the cobblestone with a crack that sounded like a gunshot in the absolute silence of the frozen city.</p>
<p>Rex could move. The impossibility of it was a physical sensation, a low hum of defiance vibrating through his bones.</p>
<p>The temporal stop had struck him like a hammer made of absolute zero, delivering its first contact violation to his very existence, but the [Full Potential Adaptation] had already eaten the blow.</p>
<p>It had consumed the system, studied the divine structure of Kaeleth’s power, and created a lasting protection in Rex’s cells. The stasis still gripped Aethelgard, but Rex was now a ghost walking through a photograph.</p>
<p>He tilted his head back, his eyes tracking the descent.</p>
<p>Theo Morr Nightwing was dropping through the frozen sky. He descended with the terrifying, controlled intentionality of a predator who had suddenly discovered he could control the universe itself.</p>
<p>There was no fear in his movements only the intoxicating, reckless confidence of a boy who believed he had finally seized the leash of God.</p>
<p>Theo landed thirty meters away. The impact wasn’t graceful; it was violent.</p>
<p>The divine designation amplified his physical mass as he fell, and when his boots struck the cobblestones, the stone didn’t just crack; it exploded.</p>
<p>A spiderweb of fissures radiated from his feet, sending shards of granite flying like shrapnel. The sound of the impact was a dull, wet thud that echoed unnaturally in the silent city.</p>
<p>Theo stood up, his chest heaving, his eyes locked on Rex.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He didn’t adopt a stance.</p>
<p>He simply stood there, a dark monolith in the center of a dead world, watching the boy crumble.</p>
<p>Rex had seen many faces on Theo. He had seen the flaring rage of the street punch, the suffocating shame of the formal apology, and the brittle, defensive pride of their sparring matches.</p>
<p>But this was different. This was something that had burned away all those lesser emotions.</p>
<p>Theo had surrendered his humanity to become something else, something colder, something hollowed out.</p>
<p>It wasn’t peace. It was a void.</p>
<p>It was the expression of someone who had already died inside and was now simply waiting for his body to catch up.</p>
<p>"You took everything," Theo said.</p>
<p>His voice didn’t carry the tremor of grief or the heat of anger. It was flat.</p>
<p>Dead.</p>
<p>It was the voice of an executioner reading a prepared indictment, devoid of inflection, stripped of hope.</p>
<p>"My house," Theo continued, each word falling like a stone into a deep well. "Diana... Iris... My grandmother..."</p>
<p>"And my mother." He paused, and for a heartbeat, Rex saw the ghost of the boy who had been broken before the emptiness swallowed it again. "The household’s respect. My position."</p>
<p>"My room."</p>
<p>Theo took half a step forward, his boots grinding the shattered cobblestones into powder. The amber gold light of the temporal sigil above cast harsh, hellish shadows across his face, making his eyes look like empty sockets.</p>
<p>"I had years in this world," Theo said, his gaze narrowing, piercing through the thirty meters of frozen air. "A lot of years of building something... It was slow... It was pathetic."</p>
<p>"It was unimpressive. But it was mine." His fingers curled into fists, the divine energy crackling around them like static discharge. "And you came."</p>
<p>"You didn’t just break it, Rex."</p>
<p>"You tore it out by the roots!"</p>
<p>"You left nothing but ash!"</p>
