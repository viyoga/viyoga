<p>Theo turned his head one last time. His gaze didn’t just land on Rex; it pierced through him, fueled by a hatred so concentrated it felt like a physical pressure in the air between them.</p>
<p>Through the haze of pain and the blood streaming from his ruptured eye, Theo saw through the mask of the perfect assistant. He saw the architect of the catastrophe.</p>
<p>He saw the cold, calculating monster that had orchestrated every moment of this breakdown. He knew exactly that Rex was a predator wearing the skin of a protector, and he knew that every word Rex had spoken, every ’logical’ assessment, had been a calculated move in a game. Theo was only now beginning to understand.</p>
<p>Rex met that stare with terrifying neutrality. His pupils didn’t dilate; his breathing didn’t quicken.</p>
<p>He looked at Theo not as an enemy but as a specimen under glass.</p>
<p>Something passed between them in that silence, a wordless, venomous acknowledgment. It wasn’t a truce, and it certainly wasn’t a resolution.</p>
<p>It was the grim recognition that this engagement had reached a temporary stalemate, a point where continuing would require both of them to expend something they couldn’t afford: more violence, more exposure, more truth.</p>
<p>Neither of them was going to blink. Neither of them was going to give the other the satisfaction of a concession.</p>
<p>Rex was the first to break the contact. He averted his eyes with a clinical precision that felt more insulting than a physical blow.</p>
<p>He didn’t look away because he was cowed; he looked away because his calculations had shifted. His mind was already moving toward the next phase of the operation: the next twenty minutes.</p>
<p>For the plan to succeed, Aurelia needed to successfully steer this broken, volatile asset Theo into the controlled environment of the household’s interior before the window of his current vulnerability closed.</p>
<p>If Theo collapsed now, or worse, if his rage triggered another uncontrolled discharge, the entire sequence would fail.</p>
<p>Behind the impenetrable calm of his expression, behind the mask of the flawless innocent man he’s playing, Rex felt something else. A cold, dark satisfaction.</p>
<p>A private, silent laugh echoed in the deepest recesses of his consciousness. ’Hook, line, and sinker.’</p>
<p>Theo had reacted exactly as predicted. The trauma, the betrayal, the collapse—it was all part of the design.</p>
<p>He had fallen directly into the trap, driven there by the very emotions Rex had so carefully manipulated.</p>
<p>"Theo," Aurelia said, her voice breaking through his spiraling fury like a lifeline. "Move. Now."</p>
<p>She didn’t wait for his consent. She placed her hand on his shoulder not as a gesture of comfort, but as a physical anchor, a command disguised as support.</p>
<p>She began to steer him toward the courtyard gate, her body positioned to shield him from Rex’s sight.</p>
<p>Theo moved. But he didn’t move like a warrior or a god.</p>
<p>He moved with the hollow, mechanical quality of a man who had burned his entire soul to ash and was now operating on nothing but muscle memory and the singular, desperate need to obey the one person who still saw him as human.</p>
<p>Every step was a struggle against gravity and exhaustion; his boots dragged across the debris, leaving crimson smears where his blood dripped onto the cracked pavement. His shoulder slumped under Aurelia’s touch, his entire posture screaming of a profound, spiritual defeat.</p>
<p>Rex stood motionless in the center of the devastation, a solitary figure amidst the ruins. He watched them retreat, the mother carrying the broken son back into the fortress, his eyes tracking their movement with the detached interest of a chess player watching a pawn move toward its inevitable sacrifice.</p>
<p>Rex turned his head slowly. His gaze found Morwenna, who stood at the ragged edge of the street like a sentinel carved from ancient stone.</p>
<p>She wasn’t panicked; she was something far more dangerous: she was processing. Her eyes swept over the carnage, the twitching bodies, the cracked foundations, and the blood-slicked asphalt, and then she fixed her attention on him with the terrifying focus of a predator assessing a new threat.</p>
<p>"Inside," Rex said.</p>
<p>His voice was terrifyingly calm, devoid of the tremor that should accompany witnessing a massacre. "Both of them need someone who is not me."</p>
<p>Morwenna didn’t blink. She met his stare with the flat, glacial assessment of a woman who had spent forty years mastering the art of reading souls and seeing through lies.</p>
<p>She didn’t see an assistant; she saw a surgeon who had let the patient bleed out to study the arterial spray.</p>
<p>"You," she said, the word carrying the weight of a death sentence, "could have stopped this earlier."</p>
<p>"I stopped it when it needed stopping," Rex replied.</p>
<p>His tone was conversational, almost bored, as if they were discussing weather patterns rather than the psychological and physical evisceration of a young man.</p>
<p>"You could have stopped it before the children were affected," she snapped, her voice dropping to a dangerous register. "Before Theo was broken."</p>
<p>Rex looked at her. For a fraction of a second, something flickered behind his eyes—not guilt, not remorse, but something darker: the cold satisfaction of a predator watching a trap spring.</p>
<p>"Yes," he said. "I could have."</p>
<p>"But the adaptation required full expression contact to cover the area distortion’s mechanism. Full expression contact required the field to reach its operational parameters."</p>
<p>Morwenna turned her head toward the field’s outer radius. In the distance, Apollo was moving through the crowd, his hands stained with blood as he knelt beside civilians whose bones had been twisted into impossible angles by the temporal fluctuations.</p>
<p>The screams were fading into low, wet moans of agony.</p>
<p>"You used them," Morwenna said.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question. It was an accusation that stripped away every pretense of his helpfulness.</p>
<p>"You used Theo’s trauma as a test subject. You used these people as collateral for your own data."</p>
<p>Rex’s expression remained impassive. Inside his mind, his processors hummed with cold efficiency.</p>
<p>’Emotional variables: Morwenna has high hostility.’</p>
<p>’Logic parameters: Defensive deflection.’</p>
<p>’Objective: Maintain plausible deniability while framing casualties as statistical inevitabilities.’</p>
<p>"The adaptation’s coverage prevents the mechanism from reaching anyone I am actively protecting in future applications," Rex said, his words precise as a scalpel. "The first contact requirement is the cost of the coverage."</p>
<p>"I chose to absorb that cost myself rather than prevent the expression."</p>
<p>"But the field reached the civilians anyway," Morwenna challenged, stepping closer. "The people screaming in the street, the children whose minds have been fractured—they were not part of your ’cost,’ Rex."</p>
<p>"The field’s area expression," Rex countered, stepping into her space with a calm that was more threatening than rage, "was going to reach its radius regardless of what I did with the first contact requirement."</p>
<p>"Theo deployed it at scale. The radius was set by the designation’s current power level, not by my choices."</p>
<p>"My choice was whether to prevent the mechanism from reaching me specifically, which would not have affected the field’s radius or its secondary effects on anyone outside my direct protective coverage."</p>
<p>The implication was venomous: ’I saved myself. The rest was irrelevant.’</p>
<p>Morwenna’s eyes narrowed. "You could have contained the field."</p>
<p>"The Earthen Authority that you received from Tremor includes the geological work you have been developing for months."</p>
<p>"You could have anchored the reality around them. You could have—"</p>
<p>"I could have attempted it," Rex interrupted, his voice cutting through her anger like ice through flesh. "Attempting geological containment of a divine temporal working at first application against a mechanism I had not yet adapted to would have had a success probability I was not willing to stake the outcome on."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the cold logic hang between them, a shield of rationality that made her moral outrage seem hysterical. "I chose the path of guaranteed adaptation over the path of uncertain heroism."</p>
<p>"In any other equation, Morwenna, that is called being correct."</p>
