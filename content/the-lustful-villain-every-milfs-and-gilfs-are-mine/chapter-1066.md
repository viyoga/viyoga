<p>Rex stared at the number 49,200. It wasn’t just a currency; it was a scorecard of violence, a tally of every divine mechanism he had dismantled and every law of reality he had bent to his will.</p>
<p>The number felt heavy in his mind, a cold reminder of the price paid for dominance.</p>
<p>He looked out at the courtyard. The serenity of the morning was almost insulting after the carnage he had just orchestrated.</p>
<p>The grass was green, the air was still, and the world was blissfully unaware that its temporal foundation had just been violently reconfigured.</p>
<p>Moving with deliberate slowness, Rex walked toward the training hall. His muscles screamed a dull, grinding ache that spoke of microfractures in bone and the cellular exhaustion of adapting to god-tier entropy.</p>
<p>He reached the entrance and sank down into the doorway, sitting with the flat, patient stillness of a predator who had finished the hunt and was now simply allowing the adrenaline to recede. He didn’t collapse; he claimed his rest.</p>
<p>His mind wandered back to the domain.</p>
<p>He thought about Kaeleth. Not the God of Time as the legends described him—the untouchable architect of centuries—but the broken thing he had seen at the end.</p>
<p>Rex remembered the exact moment he had dropped the Presence Manipulation’s suppression. He remembered the way the divine authority had suddenly felt the full, crushing weight of Rex’s actual existence.</p>
<p>It had been a moment of pure, unadulterated terror. That specific, nauseating quality of a two thousand-year-old entity encountering something its entire model of reality had failed to predict.</p>
<p>Kaeleth hadn’t just been fighting a mortal; he had been fighting a variable that shouldn’t exist, a predator that learned how to eat time itself.</p>
<p>’The look in his eyes,’ Rex thought, a ghost of a smile touching his lips. ’That wasn’t divinity. That was panic.’</p>
<p>He thought about the temporal designation’s navigation component, that jagged shard of broken causality lying abandoned in the wreckage of the domain’s spatial layout after his throw.</p>
<p>He could still see Kaeleth staring at it, looking at that piece of discarded divinity like a man looking at his own severed limb.</p>
<p>A deep, resonant satisfaction settled in Rex’s chest. It wasn’t the cheap thrill of victory; it was the profound, quiet satisfaction of a craftsman who had spent years honing a blade and had finally found the perfect stone to test it against.</p>
<p>The working had produced the result it was built for. The machine had functioned perfectly.</p>
<p>But it wasn’t complete satisfaction. Rex was not a man of easy endings.</p>
<p>Celestina was still operational. The Seam House still stood, its foundations still rooted in the complications of the world.</p>
<p>The Legion’s surface network was a hydra; he had severed one head, but the body was still writhing, still dismantling itself in a slow, agonizing process that would take time.</p>
<p>However, the fundamental calculus had changed.</p>
<p>The God of Time’s contract with Celestina was void. The divine umbilical cord had been cut with surgical precision.</p>
<p>Rex leaned his head back against the doorframe, closing his eyes. He knew what this meant for her.</p>
<p>Celestina had been operating under a canopy of divine protection, wrapped in a safety architecture that smoothed over every temporal error and muffled every catastrophic ripple she created. She had been playing a game with loaded dice.</p>
<p>Now, she was playing alone. She was operating without the safety net, naked against the raw, unmanaged currents of time.</p>
<p>The safety architecture was gone, and in its place was only the cold, unforgiving reality of a world that no longer cared if she survived her own mistakes.</p>
<p>Rex sat in the training hall doorway, the ancient stone beneath him still holding the cool ghost of the night. He watched the morning light crawl across the courtyard stones of the Nightwing household, each golden ray marking the passage of time he had just violently reconfigured.</p>
<p>In the silence, he could feel the unmistakable shift in the air. The plan was changing shape.</p>
<p>It was shedding its preparatory skin, molting from a series of calculated preparations into something far more dangerous: an operational reality.</p>
<p>He had a month of relentless, grinding work behind him. A month of shadows, of blood, of sleepless calculations.</p>
<p>He tallied his assets with the cold detachment of an auditor.</p>
<p>Fifty thousand points in reserve, a war chest of raw potential. He had the Full Potential Adaptation, providing comprehensive coverage across his systems.</p>
<p>He had dimensional manipulation allowing him to slip between layers like a blade between ribs.</p>
<p>He had Soul Manipulation to strip away designations and command communication, and the Temporal Navigation Component gave him a skeleton key to the domains of gods.</p>
<p>The board was set. Aethelgard’s operating structure was now aligned under Apollo’s formal leadership, but it was Rex who held the informal reins, the unseen hand guiding the momentum.</p>
<p>He held the position of Acting Combat Director for the Nightwing household, a title that had transformed his relationship with them from a mere asset to something foundational and indispensable.</p>
<p>The genetic future was secured: Iris and Aurelia’s pregnancies were confirmed, their free skill selections already woven into the fabric of their potential.</p>
<p>And then there was Celestina.</p>
<p>Rex leaned his head back against the doorframe, staring up at the vast, uncaring blue of the sky. His mind drifted to her throne room.</p>
<p>He could almost see her sitting there in the sudden, suffocating silence, the Time Crystal gone dark, its divine hum extinguished, leaving her with the terrifying realization that Kaeleth was no longer on the other end of the connection.</p>
<p>The umbilical cord of divinity had been severed, leaving her exposed, raw, and dangerously alone.</p>
<p>He thought about the message he had left her on the communication crystal. He had given her a sequence of kingdoms, a specific ordering that seemed tactical on the surface but carried deeper, more intimate implications that only she and he would truly understand.</p>
<p>’What will she do?’ he wondered.</p>
<p>A woman who had spent thirty years building a formidable organization, armed with genuine personal capability and a mind that could outmaneuver most emperors, would not crumble.</p>
<p>She would adapt. She would either become the predator she was always meant to be, or she would burn in the vacuum left by the God of Time.</p>
<p>’And what will I do?’</p>
<p>The two thoughts existed in a state of perfect, tense equilibrium. They were not identical, but they were inextricably linked: the predator contemplating the prey and the prey contemplating the trap.</p>
<p>One was the reaction; the other was the catalyst.</p>
<p>Rex closed his eyes, letting the morning sun warm his face. He allowed his mind to enter that familiar, hyperfocused state of background processing.</p>
<p>His consciousness became a loom, weaving together intelligence reports, power dynamics, and potential threats into a single, coherent tapestry of future action.</p>
<p>Aethelgard was still standing. He was still standing.</p>
<p>The world hadn’t ended.</p>
<p>Everything else—the Legion, the remaining divine echoes, and the shifting loyalties of kingdoms—was merely a variable. And variables were the raw materials he used to build his empire.</p>
<p>His eyes snapped open. The moment of reflection was over.</p>
<p>Rex stood up, his joints popping with the sound of small bones settling into place.</p>
<p>The lethargy of victory was rejected; the exhaustion of the battle was compartmentalized.</p>
<p>He turned and walked back into the Nightwing household. There were people inside who required the attention of the Acting Combat Director.</p>
<p>There were training schedules to enforce, vulnerabilities to patch, and a household to fortify. An acting combat director did not linger in doorways savoring the aftermath of a god’s death.</p>
<p>He went inside to continue the work.</p>
