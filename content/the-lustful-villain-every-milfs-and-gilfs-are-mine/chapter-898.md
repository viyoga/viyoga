<p>The engagement stretched far beyond the brief, brutal massacre of the twelve fighters. What had been a demonstration of dominance had transformed into a grinding, intimate war of attrition.</p>
<p>Minute after agonizing minute, Iris circled Rex like a wolf assessing a mountain, probing his defenses with strikes that carried the venom of experience. She tested angles that no trained unit fighter would ever conceive, angles born not from textbooks but from the desperate necessity of finding a gap when the only alternative was a corpse in the dirt.</p>
<p>She lunged.</p>
<p>It was a low, compressed entry that Rex had never seen from the twelve. She didn’t just move; she seemed to collapse toward the floor, her body becoming a projectile that skimmed the hardwood.</p>
<p>The movement was grotesquely efficient, designed to exploit the blind spot beneath a fighter’s peripheral vision. Rex had to burn through his reserves to intercept it.</p>
<p>His elbow connected with her shoulder with a wet, meaty thud, forcing her to abort the strike, but the effort left him breathing harder than he had all morning.</p>
<p>His mind raced: ’This isn’t technique... This is survival instinct rendered as motion.’</p>
<p>Thirty seconds later, she struck again. Same compressed low entry, but from a completely different vector.</p>
<p>This time, Rex was ready. He caught her wrist mid-lunge, his grip tightening like a vice.</p>
<p>The sound of her tendons straining under his pressure echoed in the silent hall. He deflected the attack with a brutal efficiency that left her stumbling, but he felt the sting of her adaptation; she had already adjusted the timing of her second attempt based on his reaction to the first.</p>
<p>She adjusted a third time, her movements becoming more erratic, more dangerous.</p>
<p>"She learns fast," Aurelia murmured from the hall’s edge, her voice carrying a rare note of genuine fascination.</p>
<p>She didn’t look away from the violence unfolding. "Faster than the unit did."</p>
<p>"That is the specific advantage of training that was never systematic... Nothing about her combat language is inherited from anyone else’s assumptions."</p>
<p>"That is one way of describing years of surviving alone," Elaris replied, her tone laced with a bitter understanding. "Training in the absence of teachers."</p>
<p>"It is not a kind way to describe it," Aurelia corrected coldly. "But it is an accurate one."</p>
<p>Aurelia’s voice cut through the tension twice more, calling out commands not to Rex but to Iris. Each call was a provocation, a challenge to abandon caution and commit fully to the kill.</p>
<p>Each time, Iris responded with a visible shift in her combat posture, a sudden increase in aggression, and a willingness to sacrifice defense for lethal momentum. Rex saw the shift coming before her muscles even twitched; he read the intent in the way her center of gravity dropped.</p>
<p>The climax arrived exactly when Rex’s calculations predicted.</p>
<p>Iris had been building momentum for thirty seconds, weaving a complex tapestry of feints and strikes that drew Rex into a specific pocket of space. It was a trap designed to force him into close quarters, setting up the final element: a high-level assassin’s throw.</p>
<p>Rex waited. He saw the trap closing.</p>
<p>The first phase of the throw began with the grip. Iris’s fingers locked onto his arm with the desperate strength of a drowning person.</p>
<p>She found his leverage points with terrifying accuracy, her nails digging into his skin as she prepared to wrench his balance from under him.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t fight the grip. He embraced it.</p>
<p>The moment her leverage reached its peak, Rex moved not against the force of the throw, but with it. He twisted his entire body at the exact point of her leverage, turning her own mechanical advantage into a weapon against her.</p>
<p>The physics of the counterthrow were absolute.</p>
<p>Iris was airborne for a heartbeat before she slammed into the hardwood. The impact was sickening; the sound of her body hitting the floor was less of a slap and more of a bone-deep crunch.</p>
<p>The air rushed out of her lungs in a strangled gasp as her ribs groaned under the sudden deceleration.</p>
<p>But she was an assassin.</p>
<p>Two seconds after impact, her muscles spasmed and she rolled, forcing herself back onto her feet with a ragged, desperate energy. But then she stopped.</p>
<p>Her legs buckled slightly, and she sank back down to a seated position.</p>
<p>Her body had processed the trauma. The damage was real: the shockwave of the landing had reverberated through her spine, and her nervous system was screaming warnings.</p>
<p>Her mind was already running the grim calculus of the assassin: ’Am I broken? Can I stand again, or am I already dead?’</p>
<p>She needed thirty seconds, thirty seconds of agonizing stillness, to assess whether continuing the fight was a viable option or a suicide mission.</p>
<p>Blood trickled from a split cuticle where she had gripped him, staining the floor. Through the haze of pain and the ringing in her ears, Iris lifted her head.</p>
<p>She looked at Rex from the floor, her eyes raw, bleeding with both physical pain and the terrifying realization that she had finally met something she couldn’t simply outlive.</p>
<p>"That was the longest anyone has held out against you this morning," Iris said. Her voice was rough; each word dragged through a throat tight with pain.</p>
<p>She was breathing in ragged, uneven stabs, her chest heaving as she fought to reoxygenate blood that was pooling in her bruised tissues. Despite the sweat streaming down her face and the tremor in her hands, there was a spark in her eyes, the razor-edged pride of a predator who had been beaten but refused to acknowledge the humiliation of the defeat.</p>
<p>She had made him work. She had forced him out of his textbook perfection and into the mud.</p>
<p>"It was," Rex admitted, his own breathing measured but heavy.</p>
<p>His shirt was torn at the shoulder where her grip had nearly torn the muscle from the bone. "You made me use techniques the unit never required..."</p>
<p>"They fought to win; you fought to survive. That changes the calculus entirely."</p>
<p>Iris stared at him for a heartbeat, the silence in the hall stretching until it felt like it would snap.</p>
<p>"I accept," she said. The words were a vow.</p>
<p>Rex reached down, offering his hand.</p>
<p>For a fraction of a second, Iris stared at his palm. Then, her fingers closed around his, but it wasn’t the grip of someone accepting help.</p>
<p>It was the grip of someone seizing a weapon.</p>
<p>’Now.’</p>
<p>Her entire body coiled like a released spring. She didn’t pull herself up; she launched herself forward, using his outstretched arm as a fulcrum to swing her weight into a vicious, close-quarters headbutt aimed directly at his nose.</p>
<p>The wet crack of bone on bone echoed as she drove her forehead into his face, but Rex had already anticipated the deception.</p>
<p>The moment her fingers touched his skin, his Foresight flashed crimson: ’Deception detected. Counterstrike imminent.’</p>
<p>Instead of pulling away, Rex snapped his free hand upward in a brutal rising elbow.</p>
<p>THWACK.</p>
<p>The strike caught her just below the jawline as she came in. Iris’s head snapped back, blood spraying from her split lip in a fine crimson mist.</p>
<p>She didn’t retreat; she couldn’t afford to. Instead, she twisted mid-air, turning the momentum of the blow into a spinning back kick aimed at Rex’s solar plexus.</p>
<p>Oof!</p>
<p>Rex caught the kick with both forearms, the impact sending a shockwave through his bones that felt like being hit by a sledgehammer.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of his own tendons straining under the force filled his ears.</p>
<p>"Still learning?" Rex gritted out through clenched teeth, his muscles bulging as he fought to hold her leg in place.</p>
<p>"Still fighting," Iris hissed.</p>
<p>She didn’t wait for him to release her. She brought her free knee up with terrifying speed, aiming for his ribs.</p>
<p>CRUNCH.</p>
<p>The sound of ribs protesting the impact was sickening. Rex gasped, the air leaving his lungs in a pained wheeze, but he didn’t break his hold.</p>
<p>He redirected her momentum, twisting his torso to slam her sideways into the nearest pillar.</p>
<p>BOOM.</p>
<p>The impact against the stone column knocked the wind out of her completely. Iris slumped for a microsecond, blood streaming from her nose and mouth, painting her chin crimson.</p>
<p>But before she could slide to the floor, she drove an elbow backward into Rex’s sternum THUD and spun around, claws extended like she intended to tear flesh from bone.</p>
<p>She was a whirlwind of desperate, ugly violence. She slashed at his eyes, aimed for his throat, and threw herself at his knees.</p>
<p>Each attack was followed by a countermove that defied conventional physics: a palm strike to the throat, a fingernail rake across a forearm, a vicious knee to the thigh.</p>
<p>Rex met her with the cold precision of a surgeon. He moved through her storm of strikes with terrifying economy.</p>
<p>When she lunged with a throat strike, he parried with a forearm that sounded like two planks of wood colliding CLACK and followed immediately with a devastating liver shot.</p>
<p>Splat.</p>
<p>Iris doubled over, a strangled cry escaping her lips as the pain radiated through her entire torso. But even as she bent, she attempted to sweep Rex’s legs.</p>
<p>He jumped the sweep, but she anticipated the leap, rising from her crouch to catch him midair with a desperate, two-handed grab to his throat.</p>
