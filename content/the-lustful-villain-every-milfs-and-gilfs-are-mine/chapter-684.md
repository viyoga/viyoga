<p>The voice that bled through the crystal was not merely a sound; it was a presence. It possessed that terrifying, razor-sharp quality. Celestina always carried the tone of a woman whose intellect moved so far ahead of the present moment that her words felt like echoes of a conclusion she had already reached.</p>
<p>There was no warmth in her greeting, only a chilling, surgical precision. She didn’t waste breath on pleasantries; she used her voice like a scalpel, calibrated to dissect the silence.</p>
<p>"You went silent four months ago," she said, her voice cutting through the dawn mist like a blade. "The timing is notable, Zane."</p>
<p>"In our line of work, coincidence is just a word for a pattern we haven’t understood yet."</p>
<p>Zane swallowed hard, his eyes scanning the horizon, feeling the weight of her gaze even through the magical link.</p>
<p>"The timing was operational," he replied, forcing his voice into a steady, professional clip. "The underlayer was developing monitoring gaps."</p>
<p>"I couldn’t confirm whether they were flaws or intentional lures designed to track outgoing communications."</p>
<p>"And now?" her voice drifted, cold and expectant. "Are you confident those gaps have closed?"</p>
<p>"The monitoring network hasn’t just closed; it has been completely rebuilt," Zane said, the words tumbling out with a sense of urgency he couldn’t quite suppress. "I am on the surface."</p>
<p>"I am no longer in the Underlayer..."</p>
<p>"This transmission is not routable back to any known underlayer position. For the first time in months, the trail is cold."</p>
<p>"You left," she stated.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question. It was a cold observation of a fact he had just surrendered.</p>
<p>"Last night made staying a category error," Zane whispered, the gravity of his desertion finally hitting him. "To stay would have been to die. Or worse, to be absorbed."</p>
<p>A silence stretched between them a heavy, suffocating pause that felt like the world was holding its breath.</p>
<p>"Tell me," she commanded. It wasn’t a request for a story; it was a demand for a postmortem.</p>
<p>Zane began to speak. He did not allow himself the luxury of emotion.</p>
<p>He knew Celestina; if he came to her with fear or awe, he would be useless to her. He stripped away the terror of the night and replaced it with the cold, flat delivery of a man presenting a tactical autopsy.</p>
<p>He gave her the data, raw and unvarnished, building a model of the catastrophe in her mind one brutal fact at a time.</p>
<p>He told her about the Purge, the terrifying efficiency of the opening speech from the spire. He detailed the simultaneous, public marking of all eleven Legion contacts and the chilling, unified response of the population.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a riot; it was an endorsement.</p>
<p>He talked about the battle: the desperate and hopeless fight of the city, the alliance that was quickly broken in just eleven minutes, and the basic forces that appeared with a geological label that didn’t fit normal categories.</p>
<p>He spoke of Cassandra Vexmoor. He described the activation of her blood oath, the sheer, violent majesty of it, as he had watched from the castle’s secondary observation point.</p>
<p>He described the way the very air had curdled with her power, the environmental devastation her full expression had wrought.</p>
<p>"The Blood Oath was not sufficient," he said, his voice dropping an octave. "It was a candle against the sun."</p>
<p>He gave her the impossible data of the third SSS-class entity, the ’absence architecture,’ that had defied every known taxonomy of energy, and showed the way the Lustful Villain had resolved the anomaly through a frequency generation method that shouldn’t have been possible in this dimension.</p>
<p>He described the geological authority not as a localized spell but as a citywide event. He told her how the entire substrate of the underlayer had registered the authority’s activation, moving through the very bedrock like a thought passing through a mind.</p>
<p>And finally, he gave her the name. The name that changed everything.</p>
<p>"Lord Xerollion."</p>
<p>The silence that followed the name was different. It wasn’t the silence of listening; it was the silence of a predator processing a new, massive threat. Celestina didn’t gasp; she didn’t react.</p>
<p>She simply became a void of stillness.</p>
<p>"The reconstruction is complete," Zane continued, his voice gaining a desperate edge as he tried to drive the point home. "As of this morning, the Underlayer is a new organism."</p>
<p>"It is operating under the governance of the Lustful Villain..."</p>
<p>"Mordecai Vrael has been relegated to a secondary, formal position."</p>
<p>"The military command has been gutted and redistributed..."</p>
<p>"The administrative structure has been purged."</p>
<p>"The contact network... it’s gone, Madam... It’s all gone."</p>
<p>He paused, the realization of his own words settling in his gut like lead.</p>
<p>"The underlayer the Legion has spent fourteen months mapping does not exist anymore," he said, his voice a grim finality. "What exists now is a different thing entirely. It is built on the same geography, but it has an entirely different capability profile."</p>
<p>"It is a kingdom that is actively, aggressively hostile to infiltration."</p>
<p>He stopped, his lungs burning, waiting for her to tear the news apart or command him to return.</p>
<p>The channel remained quiet. It was a silence so profound and so long that Zane found himself checking the crystal’s connection indicator, fearing the void had finally swallowed the signal.</p>
<p>But the light remained steady. The channel was still open, and Celestina was still there, listening to the wreckage of the world.</p>
<p>"The Blood Oath was not sufficient," Celestina said.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a repetition of his words; it was a surgical extraction. She had bypassed the entire tactical report to isolate the single, most terrifying variable.</p>
<p>Her voice held that razor-sharp clarity, the sound of a grandmaster identifying the one move that renders the entire board obsolete.</p>
<p>"Cassandra Vexmoor’s full expression, at sustained output, achieved only partial penetration through the geological designation’s defense structure," Zane clarified, his voice tight as he provided the supporting data. "The engagement lasted four minutes at full Blood Oath before the authority’s novel frequency interference began to degrade the form’s expression."</p>
<p>"It dropped below the effective threshold in less than a quarter of an hour."</p>
<p>"Four minutes," she whispered. It wasn’t a lament; it was a measurement of a catastrophe.</p>
<p>"At full expression," Zane pressed, the weight of the realization pressing against his chest. "Cassandra Vexmoor is the highest-capability individual I have encountered in fourteen months."</p>
<p>"Full Blood Oath activation is the absolute ceiling of her native system. And even at her zenith, she only managed four minutes of partial penetration."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was heavy, a vacuum of sound that seemed to suck the air from the forest around him. Zane waited, his heart hammering a frantic, uneven rhythm.</p>
<p>He was braced for a command to return, for a mobilization, for a declaration of war.</p>
<p>Instead, Celestina spoke with a chilling, effortless finality. "We do not need the Underlayer."</p>
<p>The statement arrived in that flat, decisive register she used when she had moved past the messy phase of analysis and into the cold realm of conclusion. It was the voice of a woman who had just looked at a massive, complex equation and realized the answer was zero.</p>
<p>Zane felt a momentary jolt of vertigo.</p>
<p>"The Legion’s operational presence in the Underlayer was a monitoring asset," she continued, her tone devoid of sentiment. "Fourteen months of intelligence on a demon population center, its structure, its defenses, and its leadership."</p>
<p>"That entire intelligence picture has just been invalidated..."</p>
<p>"It is obsolete data, Zane. It is history."</p>
<p>"Yes," Zane said, the word feeling heavy on his tongue.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer under Mordecai Vrael was a manageable threat," she said, her charisma manifesting as a terrifying, calm authority. "A demon lord with a predictable, probability-based summon system."</p>
<p>"A population center with known vulnerabilities and a monitored leadership. But the Underlayer under Lord Xerollion..."</p>
<p>"It’s a different category," Zane finished for her.</p>
<p>"The category change happened in a single night," she cut in, her voice sharpening. "And that is the only piece of information that matters."</p>
<p>"A power structure that can undergo a total metamorphosis in a single night is not a structure that benefits from the Legion’s approach of monitored containment."</p>
<p>"Containment assumes stability. It assumes the thing being watched isn’t going to rewrite its own physics while you’re looking at it."</p>
<p>"What you have described is not stable. It is volatile."</p>
<p>Zane felt the logic snapping into place, a terrifying architecture of thought being built in the dark.</p>
<p>"You are withdrawing the underlayer from priority status," he realized.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer is going to destroy itself," Celestina said.</p>
<p>There was no satisfaction in her voice, only the cold, detached clarity of a scientist watching a chemical reaction that had already reached the point of no return. "Lord Xerollion is a reincarnator with a villain system."</p>
<p>"He has consolidated a demon population center and built a governance structure entirely around his own singular authority..."</p>
<p>"That structure has one single, catastrophic point of failure."</p>
<p>"Him," Zane said.</p>
<p>"Every decision, every pulse of power, runs through him," she said, her voice gaining a predatory momentum. "The geological designation, the military command, the intelligence network, the diplomatic ties to the second stratum... it is all just his personal capability applied to the underlayer’s problems."</p>
<p>"The entire region’s capacity is now a hostage to his continued presence and function."</p>
<p>"He removes himself, and the structure collapses," Zane whispered.</p>
<p>"The structure collapses," she agreed. "Not immediately."</p>
<p>"The puppets he has placed will maintain the illusion of continuity for a time."</p>
<p>"But the capability tier, the world is now bracing for the canyon engagement, the looming war with the Apostle network, and the second stratum diplomacy—none of it works without him specifically."</p>
