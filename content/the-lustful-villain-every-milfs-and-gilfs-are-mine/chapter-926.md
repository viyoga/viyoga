<p>She rose from her chair in one fluid motion. When she reached him, he took her hand, his grip firm and possessive, and together they turned toward the door.</p>
<p>They left the sitting room with the unhurried, casual ease of two people who had somewhere far more important to be, leaving the scholarly war council behind in their wake.</p>
<p>Theo watched them go. His eyes remained fixed on the page in front of him, but his body was coiled tight. He kept his gaze downward for exactly as long as it took for the rhythmic echo of their footsteps to fade completely from the corridor outside.</p>
<p>Then, he slowly set the volume down.</p>
<p>Elaris, watching him from the head of the table with that quiet, penetrating attention she reserved for her family, saw the muscle in his jaw clench until it looked like stone.</p>
<p>For a heartbeat, the grief and the isolation flickered across his features like lightning before he forced them back down. With visible effort, he picked the book back up and returned to the translation, his knuckles white against the parchment.</p>
<p>Nobody in the room said a word. There was no need for pity, and there was certainly nothing useful left to say.</p>
<p>The room returned to its grim work, the silence filled only by the scratching of quills and the distant, fading memory of two people walking away from the world.</p>
<p>The scratching of quills resumed, a brittle sound that failed to mask the charged silence left in Rex and Diana’s wake. Elaris took up the mantle of leadership with practiced ease, her voice steady as she pointed out a discrepancy in a logistical timeline, guiding Theo back into the mechanical weeds of the military shorthand.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was already deep in another trance of analysis, her quill moving with such aggressive precision that it seemed she was trying to carve the truth directly into the paper.</p>
<p>But for Theo, the scholarly atmosphere had become a thin, transparent veneer.</p>
<p>The words on the page began to blur. The archaic symbols he was supposed to be translating—jagged strokes and complex ligatures—seemed to writhe like insects.</p>
<p>His breathing grew shallow. His mind, treacherous and hyperaware, refused to stay anchored to the task.</p>
<p>’They went upstairs,’ his mind whispered, a venomous thought that refused to be silenced. ’Not to the training hall... not to the library... but upstairs...’</p>
<p>’Where the doors lock... Where the rooms are private...’</p>
<p>A hot, uncomfortable flush crept up his neck. He knew the layout of the house better than anyone; he knew the guest wing was silent, isolated, and designed for privacy.</p>
<p>His imagination, fueled by weeks of repressed tension and the raw, violent energy he had witnessed between Rex and Diana, began to construct scenarios that made his stomach churn with a nauseating mix of anger and something far more shameful.</p>
<p>He saw images of Rex towering, powerful, and utterly dominant, claiming Diana with the same relentless force he used in combat. He imagined the bedsheets tangled, the physical evidence of Rex’s possession marking her skin.</p>
<p>’Fuck,’ he thought, gripping the edge of the table so hard his fingernails bit into the wood. ’He’s doing it again...’</p>
<p>’He’s taking what he wants while we sit here playing scholar.’</p>
<p>Then, it started.</p>
<p>At first, it was nothing, just a faint, distant sound that seemed to travel down the ventilation shafts or through the old bones of the house.</p>
<p>A soft thud.</p>
<p>Then something else.</p>
<p>Theo froze. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird.</p>
<p>He stared fixedly at the page, but his ears were straining, every nerve ending suddenly hypersensitive.</p>
<p>A moan.</p>
<p>It was faint, almost lost beneath the crackle of the fireplace, but to Theo, it sounded like a thunderclap. It was Diana’s voice high, breathless, and unmistakably undone.</p>
<p>The sound escalated. A low, guttural growl that could only belong to Rex, followed by the unmistakable rhythm of movement: the creak of floorboards, the heavy impact of bodies meeting.</p>
<p>Then came the cry, Diana’s voice breaking into a sharp, keening moan that echoed down the stairwell, raw with a pleasure that sounded almost like pain.</p>
<p>Theo’s face went scarlet. His jaw was locked so tight it ached.</p>
<p>A wave of pure, unadulterated anxiety washed over him, a feeling of being trapped in a room where he was the only one conscious of a grotesque violation of propriety. His mind spiraled into darker places: the sound of Diana’s scream as she climaxed, the wet, rhythmic sounds of their coupling, the sheer, overwhelming physicality of it.</p>
<p>’I need to leave... I need to get out of here,’ his brain screamed, but his body was paralyzed by a morbid fascination.</p>
<p>Yet, across the table, the scene was unnervingly normal.</p>
<p>Elizabeth didn’t flinch. She simply dipped her quill into the inkwell with terrifying composure, her hand steady as she wrote a complex theorem.</p>
<p>"The resonance patterns in section four suggest a secondary containment field," she said aloud, her voice as cool as marble, entirely ignoring the distant, unmistakable sound of Diana’s hips slamming against the bedframe upstairs.</p>
<p>Lily was humming. A soft, sweet melody that seemed to deliberately cut through the air, providing a counterpoint to the increasingly vocal sounds of ecstasy drifting from above.</p>
<p>She leaned over to Elaris, pointing at a map with the casual air of someone discussing the weather. "If we move the supply lines through the eastern pass, we avoid the worst of the magical interference."</p>
<p>Elaris was the worst of all. She was the picture of serene matriarchal focus, nodding approvingly at Theo’s work. "Good, Theo..."</p>
<p>"That translation of the supply depot notation is spot on. Keep going."</p>
<p>Theo felt like he was losing his mind. The sounds were getting louder. Diana’s voice was now unmistakable, a series of fragmented, desperate cries that tore through the quiet of the sitting room.</p>
<p>"Rex! Ah, Rex, please, oh god!" The sounds of exertion were unmistakable: the heavy breathing, the slap of skin on skin, and the unmistakable wetness of their union.</p>
<p>He looked up, desperation breaking through his mask, his eyes wild as he searched for any sign of acknowledgment. But he found none.</p>
<p>Elizabeth was reading. Lily was smiling. Elaris was watching him with gentle, encouraging eyes. They were all performing.</p>
<p>They were all complicit.</p>
<p>Rex had warned them. He had asked them to ignore it, to let him have his due, and they were doing exactly that trolling Theo with a silence so profound it felt like a physical assault.</p>
<p>They were letting him drown in the sounds of their family’s private debauchery, forcing him to bear the full weight of his own jealousy and disgust alone.</p>
<p>Theo stared down at the book, the words dancing mockingly before his eyes. The moans from upstairs reached a crescendo, a shattering, vocal release that seemed to vibrate through the very chair he sat in.</p>
<p>And then, as suddenly as it had begun, the house fell silent.</p>
<p>Only the fire crackled. Only the quills scratched.</p>
<p>"Well done, Theo," Elaris said softly, breaking the silence. "Let’s move on to the next passage."</p>
<p>Theo couldn’t breathe. He couldn’t speak.</p>
<p>He simply picked up his quill with shaking hands and tried to pretend that the world hadn’t just been torn apart by the sound of his own inadequacy.</p>
<p>Theo gripped the quill so tightly that the feather trembled violently between his fingers. He stared at the parchment until the ink blotches began to resemble Rorschach tests of his own deteriorating sanity.</p>
<p>’Focus,’ he commanded himself, the word echoing hollowly in his skull. ’Focus on the syntax...’</p>
<p>’Focus on the verb conjugations... Focus on anything but the ceiling...’</p>
<p>But the ceiling was betraying him.</p>
<p>The house was old, its bones settled and creaking, but this wasn’t the settling of wood. This was something rhythmic, something muscular.</p>
<p>A wet, slapping sound, the unmistakable impact of flesh meeting flesh, punctured the scholarly silence like a gunshot.</p>
<p>Thwack. Thwack. Thwack.</p>
<p>Theo flinched, his quill skidding across the page in a jagged, ruined streak of black ink. His heart began to gallop, a frantic percussion against his ribs that made his vision blur.</p>
<p>Sweat pooled at the base of his spine, a cold, clammy discomfort that made his shirt cling to his skin.</p>
<p>Then came Diana.</p>
<p>"Rex—ah! Rex, harder!" Her voice wasn’t just loud; it was raw, stripped of all her usual soldierly discipline, reduced to something primal and desperate.</p>
<p>It tore through the air, followed immediately by a guttural, commanding growl from Rex that sounded more like an animal than a man.</p>
<p>Theo’s breath hitched. His chest felt tight, as if the oxygen in the room had been replaced by something thicker, something charged with the heat of what was happening mere yards above his head.</p>
<p>Every time Diana cried out and every time her voice climbed to that shattering, high-pitched register of ecstasy Theo felt a physical jolt go through his spine.</p>
<p>"Do you—" Theo started, his voice cracking.</p>
<p>He cleared his throat roughly, his face burning with a humiliation so deep it felt like shame. "Do you all... do you hear that?"</p>
<p>The question hung in the air, pathetic and exposed. He looked at Elizabeth, hoping for some shred of shared recognition, some acknowledgment of the obvious.</p>
<p>But Elizabeth didn’t even pause. She merely adjusted her spectacles, her eyes fixed on a map of ancient ley lines.</p>
<p>"Hear what, Theo? If you’re referring to the wind in the chimney, it is indeed a bit restless tonight."</p>
<p>"The wind?" Theo hissed, his voice rising in pitch. "It’s not the wind!"</p>
<p>"It sounds like someone is—"</p>
