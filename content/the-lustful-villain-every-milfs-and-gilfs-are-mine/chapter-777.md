<p>The specific quality of Zane’s expression when he heard that was the look of a man who had just been offered a gilded cage and was frantically trying to calculate the exact weight of the chains. It was the expression of someone being offered a way out that was technically generous, but at a cost that would strip him of every ounce of his remaining dignity.</p>
<p>He didn’t even have time to scream.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for the argument to form, for the pride to flare, or for the vengeance to manifest. With a flick of his wrist and a subtle, terrifying ripple in the local atmospheric pressure, he applied the Sleep Manipulation.</p>
<p>He didn’t use the violent, jarring version that crushed the mind; he used the surgical, gentle version at the specific depth that induced unconsciousness with the absolute completeness of a shutter closing on a camera lens.</p>
<p>Zane’s eyes didn’t flutter; they simply went dark. His body, exhausted by the morning’s carnage and the sheer mental weight of Rex’s revelation, surrendered instantly.</p>
<p>He slumped forward, the fight drained from him before his brain could even register the defeat.</p>
<p>Thud.</p>
<p>Rex caught him. It wasn’t a warm or friendly catch; it was the efficient, practiced movement of a man catching a heavy sack of grain.</p>
<p>He steadied Zane’s muscular frame, leaning him back against the gnarled roots of an ancient oak with a clinical, almost bored precision.</p>
<p>Then, Rex turned his gaze toward the treeline.</p>
<p>Ignivara had dragged herself from the dirt. She was a vision of battered, golden fury.</p>
<p>Her breathing was a wet, ragged whistle, and a thick smear of crimson stained her chin where she had bitten her lip to keep from screaming. She had forced herself up to one knee, her golden eyes tracking Rex with the predatory, hyperfocused assessment that her designation demanded, even in the throes of total physical depletion.</p>
<p>"You waited..." she rasped, her voice cracking like dry parchment, "until he had finished the thought."</p>
<p>"Finishing thoughts is important," Rex said, his voice smooth and utterly devoid of remorse.</p>
<p>He stood there, the sunlight catching the sharp lines of his face, looking more like a conqueror than a student. "Interrupted thoughts do not process correctly."</p>
<p>"If the cognitive cycle is broken midstream, the brain struggles to integrate the new data... I needed him to have finished processing the full scope of his humiliation before the sleep took hold."</p>
<p>"Otherwise, he would wake up with only half a picture, and a man like Zane is much more interesting when he understands the full scale of his own irrelevance."</p>
<p>Ignivara’s eyes burned with a hatred so intense it seemed to physically heat the air between them. She looked at Zane’s unconscious, vulnerable form, the man she had fought beside, now reduced to a mere ’data point’ in Rex’s grand calculation, and then she looked back at the monster standing over him.</p>
<p>"You think you’re so goddamn efficient," she spat, a glob of bloody saliva hitting the dirt near Rex’s boot. "You think because you can manipulate the mind and the earth, you’re above the soul."</p>
<p>"But you’re just a butcher, Rex... A butcher who likes to talk while he carves."</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even flinch at the insult. He merely tilted his head, a cocky, infuriatingly calm smile playing on his lips.</p>
<p>"A butcher is at least honest about his purpose, Ignivara."</p>
<p>"Most people pretend they aren’t consuming one another... They just call it ’society.’"</p>
<p>He took a slow, deliberate step toward her, his presence expanding, filling the clearing until the very shadows seemed to shrink away from him.</p>
<p>"You’re staring at me as if you’re waiting for the blow," Rex said, his voice dropping to a low, intimate hum. "As if you’re waiting for me to knock you out, to put you in the dark with him."</p>
<p>"You aren’t going to do the same to me," she challenged, her voice trembling not with fear, but with the sheer effort of staying upright.</p>
<p>She gripped a jagged piece of broken stone, her knuckles white, her eyes promising a death of a thousand cuts. "You won’t dare."</p>
<p>"No," Rex said simply. The word was a definitive, chilling fact.</p>
<p>Ignivara narrowed her eyes, her suspicion deepening. "Why not? Is it because you’re afraid of what a conscious mind will do to you?"</p>
<p>"No," Rex replied, his eyes gleaming with a dark, analytical hunger. "It’s because you are far more useful awake."</p>
<p>He leaned in closer, his shadow swallowing her. "Zane’s value right now is singular and static."</p>
<p>"He is the vessel for the conversation he is going to have with Apollo when he wakes up."</p>
<p>"That conversation requires him to have fully digested the reality of his failure before the first word is even spoken..."</p>
<p>"He is a completed task."</p>
<p>He paused, his gaze raking over her broken, bloodied form with the detached interest of a jeweler examining a cracked diamond.</p>
<p>"But you..." Rex whispered, a predatory glint in his eyes. "Your value is different. It is much more... dynamic."</p>
<p>Ignivara felt a chill run down her spine that had nothing to do with the wind. She tightened her grip on the stone, her heart hammering against her bruised ribs.</p>
<p>"What is my value?" she demanded, her voice a low, defiant snarl.</p>
<p>Rex looked at her, the mask held loosely at his side. Without the porcelain veneer, his face was a landscape of terrifying, calm intellect.</p>
<p>He had the specific, unsettling quality of a man about to deliver a profoundly uncomfortable truth, not because he cared about her feelings, but because he had calculated that honesty was simply the most efficient way to manipulate her.</p>
<p>"You are going to go back to Celestina," he said, his voice cutting through the humid air like a scalpel. "But not as a survivor seeking refuge..."</p>
<p>"You are going to go back as a report."</p>
<p>Ignivara’s breath hitched, a sharp, pained sound. She glared at him, her teeth bared in a snarl of pure, unadulterated loathing.</p>
<p>"A report? You think you can just... use me like a goddamn messenger bird?"</p>
<p>"I am going to use you as the most effective instrument of misinformation ever devised," Rex corrected, his tone dripping with a cocky, effortless superiority. "You are going to tell her everything that happened this morning."</p>
<p>"Every drop of blood spilled, every bone that snapped under my command, including this very conversation."</p>
<p>"You will recount the capability set you witnessed, the sheer scale of the devastation."</p>
<p>"You will provide the most accurate, most complete, and most brutal intelligence briefing the Legion has ever received about a single target."</p>
<p>He stepped into her personal space, looming over her, his eyes cold and predatory. "And the beauty of it, Ignivara, is that every single detail in that briefing will be exactly what I want her to know."</p>
<p>"You will be telling the truth, and yet, you will be lying with every word."</p>
<p>Ignivara went perfectly still. A cold, paralyzing dread momentarily eclipsed the fury in her eyes.</p>
<p>The realization was a physical weight, pressing down on her chest until her ribs felt like they might splinter again.</p>
<p>"You are not letting me go," she whispered, the words trembling with the realization of her own helplessness. "You aren’t releasing us... you are deploying us."</p>
<p>"I am merely making the observation that you are going to leave," Rex said, a smirk playing on his lips as he watched her struggle with the logic. "What you do with your departure is your decision."</p>
<p>"I am also making the observation that the most accurate report you can give Celestina is the one that describes exactly what you observed."</p>
<p>"And everything you observed—the way the earth buckled, the way the air screamed, the way you were utterly, hopelessly outmatched—was something I chose for you to observe."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the weight of his god complex sink in. "The aerial fight? The carnage? The feeling of your own strength failing you?"</p>
<p>"I did not bring you into that fight to stop you, Ignivara..."</p>
<p>"I brought you into it to teach you. Everything you saw was a curated lesson in your own insignificance."</p>
<p>She looked at him for a long, agonizing moment. Her expression was that of someone who had already crossed a threshold into a nightmare and was now standing on the far side, looking back at the world she used to belong to, realizing she could never go back.</p>
<p>"You are going to explain to Celestina," Rex continued, his voice dropping to a commanding, low register, "that the Aethelgard operation did not proceed as planned."</p>
<p>"That the variables have shifted... That the predator has become the architect..."</p>
<p>Ignivara’s eyes flashed with a final, desperate spark of defiance.</p>
<p>"I will explain it," she hissed, her voice cracking with emotion, "when I am ready. Not when you dictate it!"</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even blink at her outburst. He simply smiled, a thin, cruel line of satisfaction.</p>
<p>"I will let you explain it... when I am ready."</p>
<p>With a movement so fluid and swift it was almost a blur, he reached into her jacket. His fingers brushed against the communication crystal, and he felt the hum of active energy, the warmth of a live connection.</p>
<p>The outgoing signal was pulsing, a frantic heartbeat of light. Someone was on the other end, waiting, listening, expecting news of a victory that was actually a prelude to a massacre.</p>
<p>Rex lifted the crystal, holding it up toward the hollow, dark eyes of the Lustful Villain’s mask.</p>
<p>The connection snapped open instantly. Celestina’s voice flooded the clearing, sharp, controlled, and laced with the tension of a commander holding her breath.</p>
<p>"Ignivara?" Celestina called, her voice demanding an update, a sign of life, a report of success.</p>
<p>Rex leaned toward the crystal, his eyes glinting with a dark, triumphant mischief.</p>
<p>"No," Rex said.</p>
