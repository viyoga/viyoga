<p>Rex remained motionless, a statue of scarred muscle and coiled intent. The bathroom’s silence became absolute, broken only by the distant drip of water from a faucet, each drop sounding like a hammer blow against the anvil of his concentration.</p>
<p>Behind his eyes, the world was no longer made of marble and steam; it was a cascade of variables, a storm of tactical projections and brutal possibilities.</p>
<p>’Leonidas.’</p>
<p>The name resonated in his mind with the weight of a threat. The second strongest mage in Aethelgard, a man Rex had never stood across from in combat, but whose reputation preceded him like a cold wind.</p>
<p>He was a man of structured power and devastating arcane precision. But worse than his magic was his bloodline.</p>
<p>He was Helena’s husband, the man who would see his wife’s authority usurped and her family’s legacy dismantled. Unlike any other enemy Rex would face, Leonidas wouldn’t just fight for power; he would fight with the raw, unvarnished fury of a man protecting his own.</p>
<p>He would be a rabid dog, and Rex would have to break him, bone by agonizing bone.</p>
<p>Then there was Helena. The implications of her return were a poisoned chalice.</p>
<p>She would ride through the gates expecting the familiar rhythms of her city, only to find it occupied by a stranger who wore her husband’s respect like a trophy.</p>
<p>Then would come the second shock: the revelation that Valentina, the pillar of her family, had not only surrendered but had also knelt to become an instrument of his will. The psychological collapse would be total.</p>
<p>’How many pieces do I move?’ Rex asked himself, his inner voice cold and calculating. ’If I break Leonidas first, Helena’s spirit fractures before she can even process the betrayal. ’</p>
<p>’If I keep Valentina as my shield, Helena might hesitate.’</p>
<p>’But if I expose the truth immediately, I force their hand.’</p>
<p>’I need to decide how to position them, how to bend their trauma into usefulness.’</p>
<p>’I have until dawn to arrange the room, to set the stage for their breaking.’</p>
<p>As he calculated the slaughter, something darker lurked in the periphery of his consciousness. Deep in the back of his mind, in that shadowed place where he had been pushing his deepest anxieties for days, a figure appeared.</p>
<p>It was the silent, masked specter from the domain—the entity that seemed to exist outside of time and causality. The figure was walking toward him from a void that predated the gods themselves, each step measured and inexorable.</p>
<p>Rex had a dozen questions about its origin, its purpose, and its connection to the divine corruption he was now wielding. He had a gut deep suspicion that the goddess standing before him in the mirror held the keys to that particular mystery.</p>
<p>He drew breath, his chest expanding, his throat working as he prepared to demand answers from the divine. He would not let her leave without extracting the truth.</p>
<p>"Lustia," he began, his voice rough with suppressed urgency.</p>
<p>But the words died in his throat. The mirror began to fail.</p>
<p>The supernatural connection was hemorrhaging. The brilliant, molten gold that infused Lustia’s hair began to drain away like blood from a severed artery, leaving behind something dull and lifeless.</p>
<p>The sharp, terrifyingly beautiful edges of her features began to blur, her face growing thin, translucent, and uncertain.</p>
<p>The sheer physical strain of maintaining the breach between dimensions was finally taking its toll; she was fading, her form unraveling as the laws of reality reasserted their dominance.</p>
<p>She didn’t look afraid. Instead, she looked at him with a slow, devastatingly knowing smile, the kind of smile that acknowledged his question without granting him the mercy of an answer.</p>
<p>She saw the hunger for truth in his eyes and saw the desperation beneath his steel, and she chose to vanish rather than surrender the secret.</p>
<p>"Until tomorrow, my apostle," her voice whispered, growing faint as the silver surface of the mirror began to solidify back into ordinary glass.</p>
<p>"Be careful, my dear apostle," Lustia whispered.</p>
<p>Her voice was no longer a physical sound; it was a fading echo, thinning as her image dissolved like smoke caught in a gale. "Tomorrow will be a very long day."</p>
<p>"Pray you have the strength to survive the sunrise."</p>
<p>Then, with the finality of a closing tomb, the divine light collapsed. The golden fire, the crimson hair, and the hungry eyes vanished.</p>
<p>The mirror ceased to be a window into another realm and became nothing more than a cold, silvered surface reflecting the mundane reality of a bathroom.</p>
<p>Rex stared at his own reflection: blonde hair plastered to his forehead, eyes bloodshot and shadowed by exhaustion, and a faint, almost imperceptible smile tugging at one corner of his mouth, the smile of a man who had just been handed a death warrant and found it thrilling.</p>
<p>"Tomorrow," he murmured to the empty room.</p>
<p>The word felt heavy, like a blade being drawn from a sheath.</p>
<p>He moved with mechanical precision, a soldier operating on autopilot. He dried his face with a rough towel, the friction against his skin a grounding reminder of his own physicality.</p>
<p>He hung the towel back on its hook, his movements economical, each gesture stripped of wasted energy. His mind was already gone, already marching ahead to the gates of Aethelgard, already imagining the screams, the clash of steel, and the sound of bones snapping under his grip.</p>
<p>He walked back into the bedroom, the doorway feeling smaller than it had when he left. The sun had climbed higher in the sky while he had been communing with a goddess, casting harsh, angular shafts of light across the chamber.</p>
<p>The stripe of molten gold stretching across the bed had widened, creeping across the silk sheets until it covered most of Valentina’s shoulder.</p>
<p>She remained motionless, a fallen monument in the center of his conquest. Her breathing was still measured that slow, deep, rhythmic cadence of someone who had been utterly broken and had found a hollow kind of peace in surrender.</p>
<p>Her hand lay curled loosely against the sheet, pale against the fabric, and her face was turned toward the window as if she were unconsciously basking in the morning light.</p>
<p>Rex stood at the foot of the bed for a long moment, watching her. A part of him, the part he kept buried under layers of ambition and blood, felt something like pity.</p>
<p>But that part was dying. In its place was only the cold calculus of power.</p>
<p>Valentina slept in blissful, ignorant serenity. She had no idea how close her daughter was.</p>
<p>She had no idea that tomorrow, a gate at the edge of the city would swing open to admit the storm. She had no idea that the very foundation of her world was about to be torn apart or that the monster she had spent days trying to forget was about to walk straight through her front door and claim everything she held sacred.</p>
<p>Rex turned away from the bed and walked toward the window, looking out over the city he now owned. The conquest was over.</p>
<p>The war, however, was only just beginning.</p>
