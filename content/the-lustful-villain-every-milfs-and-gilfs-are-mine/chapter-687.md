<p>Rex stood on the jagged edge of the surface, just outside Aethelgard’s lower perimeter, looking up at the floating titan. He didn’t just see the island; he felt it.</p>
<p>Through the Earthen Authority, the island wasn’t a distant object; it was a living, breathing geological organism, and he was currently reaching into its very marrow.</p>
<p>The authority’s passive awareness began its work the instant he made contact. Within thirty seconds, a flood of data surged through his mind: the compressed stone mineral substrate, the ancient, divine architecture that had defied gravity for eight centuries to keep this mass suspended above the Convergence Waters.</p>
<p>It was a masterpiece of celestial engineering, and Rex was already finding the seams.</p>
<p>A shimmer of displaced air announced Lilith’s arrival. She landed beside him three seconds after he did, the residual light of her teleportation fading like a dying star.</p>
<p>She stood there for a moment, her lungs expanding as she drew in the crisp, morning surface air, a stark, sharp contrast to the heavy, recycled atmosphere of the Underlayer.</p>
<p>She looked up at the sprawling agricultural fields and the distant, sun-drenched silhouette of Aethelgard’s upper districts.</p>
<p>"Same island," she murmured, her voice tinged with a strange sort of vertigo. "But it looks... different from this angle."</p>
<p>"That’s because you’ve been staring at its belly for six months," Rex said, his voice smooth, laced with a smug, effortless confidence.</p>
<p>He didn’t even look at her; his eyes were fixed on the horizon, though his mind was miles deep into the rock.</p>
<p>"Everything looks different when you stop seeing it from underneath," she replied, turning her gaze toward him.</p>
<p>She saw the way his shoulders were set, the predatory stillness in his posture. "You’re already reading it, aren’t you?"</p>
<p>"The Authority doesn’t wait for an invitation," Rex said, a cocky grin tugging at the corner of his mouth. "It maps passively on contact."</p>
<p>"I don’t even have to try. The world just... confesses its secrets to me."</p>
<p>"And what is it telling you?" Lilith asked, her tone cautious.</p>
<p>Rex’s expression shifted. The playfulness vanished, replaced by the cold, calculating intensity of a man who saw the world as a collection of structural vulnerabilities.</p>
<p>"The substrate is denser in the central mass than in the agricultural sectors," he said, his voice dropping into a clinical, authoritative register. "The divine intervention that lifted this place used a specific compression method."</p>
<p>"It concentrates the structural load toward the center, creating a gradient that distributes the load-bearing capacity outward."</p>
<p>"The outer edges... they’re the lightest. The least dense."</p>
<p>Lilith’s eyes widened as the implication hit her. "Which means the outer edges are where the structural foundation is most exposed..."</p>
<p>"Where the grip is weakest."</p>
<p>"Precisely," Rex said, his eyes gleaming with a dark, intellectual hunger. "If someone wanted to find the foundational weakness, if someone wanted to make this entire divine miracle unravel, they wouldn’t strike the heart."</p>
<p>"They would strike the gradient’s end and they would pull the rug out from the edges."</p>
<p>Lilith looked at the massive, beautiful island, a sense of dread pooling in her stomach. "You’re not just mapping this for the golem deployment, are you?"</p>
<p>Rex finally turned to her, his gaze piercing and utterly devoid of warmth. A slow, dangerous smile spread across his face, the smile of a man who had already won a game no one else knew they were playing.</p>
<p>"The golems are just the noise, Lilith," he said, his voice a silky purr of pure menace. "I’m mapping everything that uses this island."</p>
<p>"The stone, the energy, the people... the whole damn thing."</p>
<p>Lilith didn’t press him. She had learned long ago that Rex’s assessments were absolute and that trying to pry information from him before he was finished was like trying to catch lightning in a jar.</p>
<p>Rex turned back to the earth. He didn’t just strike the ground; he claimed it.</p>
<p>He drove both of his heavy, gauntleted fists into the surface with a violent, controlled force.</p>
<p>"Awaken," he commanded, his voice vibrating with a sudden, terrifying power.</p>
<p>The Earthen Authority exploded into life. At full activation, the power didn’t just ripple; it roared.</p>
<p>A shockwave of geological intent surged from his impact point, tearing through the substrate and spreading outward like a digital virus. The island’s composition was different from the underlayer; the mineral architecture was tighter, the density far higher, and the pressure immense.</p>
<p>It was a challenge. And Rex lived for challenges.</p>
<p>He leaned into the power, his muscles tensing as he forced the Authority to adapt. The sheer pressure of the island’s mass pushed back against his consciousness, but he pushed harder, his ego driving the connection deeper.</p>
<p>He wasn’t just reading the rock; he was dominating it.</p>
<p>In forty seconds of intense, focused mental labor, the map was complete. His passive foresight fused with the authority’s raw data, creating a godlike, multidimensional blueprint of the entire island.</p>
<p>He saw every fault line, every load-bearing formation, and every microscopic structural concentration. He saw the very bones of Aethelgard.</p>
<p>And then, he saw the lifeblood.</p>
<p>The map flared with energy signatures. He saw twenty-three active reincarnators scattered across the surface like bright, pulsing embers.</p>
<p>Most were clustered in the Academy and the residential districts, with smaller, flickering lights in the outer settlements and the agricultural villages.</p>
<p>Rex sat in the silence of his own godhood, the massive influx of data settling into his mind like a settled debt. He let the smugness return to his features, a cold, triumphant light dancing in his eyes as he looked over his new kingdom of stone and soul.</p>
<p>"Twenty three," Rex murmured, the number rolling off his tongue like a death sentence.</p>
<p>Lilith turned to him, her eyes searching his face for a hint of hesitation, a flicker of doubt. She found none.</p>
<p>There was only that terrifying, placid certainty that made him so dangerous.</p>
<p>"Twenty three?" she repeated.</p>
<p>"Active reincarnators on the island," he clarified, his voice dripping with a casual, almost bored arrogance. "The Authority doesn’t just map the stone, Lilith; it reads the resonance."</p>
<p>"Reincarnator systems produce a specific, rhythmic vibration through the substrate at a frequency that’s distinct from the messy, chaotic signatures of native ability users."</p>
<p>"They pulse like a heartbeat in the rock. And right now, the island is beating quite loudly."</p>
<p>"Twenty three... and that includes the ones we already have on our radar?" Lilith asked, her voice tightening.</p>
<p>"The Academy’s Special Division has nine," Rex said, ticking them off with a flick of his fingers as if he were counting coins. "The Starlight family structure accounts for five."</p>
<p>"The Nightwing household has two."</p>
<p>"The remaining seven are scattered across the outer settlements and the residential districts."</p>
<p>"Seven unknowns," Lilith whispered, the weight of the variable pressing on her.</p>
<p>Rex let out a short, sharp laugh, a sound devoid of humor and full of condescension. "Not unknown, Lilith."</p>
<p>"Don’t be so fucking dramatic."</p>
<p>"The substrate gives me their exact coordinates and the energy tier of their systems. I know how much power they’re holding."</p>
<p>"I just haven’t been close enough for my passive perception to peel back the skin and see the specific system type."</p>
<p>"But they aren’t hidden. They’re just... uncatalogued."</p>
<p>"The golems will close that gap," Lilith noted, her mind racing to keep up with his tactical progression. "They’ll read them at close range."</p>
<p>"That’s exactly what they’re for," Rex said, his eyes flashing with a predatory light. "They are my eyes... my hands... and my judgment."</p>
<p>He didn’t move a muscle, yet the very air seemed to thicken with sudden, violent intent.</p>
<p>"Arise," he commanded, not a shout but a low, guttural vibration that seemed to come from the earth itself.</p>
<p>From the ground, eleven massive geological entities began to coalesce. These weren’t the clunky, theatrical stone statues of a common mage; these were nightmares of precision.</p>
<p>Through the marriage of his elemental mastery and the Earthen authority, Rex had birthed autonomous geological agents, fluid, terrifyingly efficient formations of hyperdense mineral that carried a fragment of his own consciousness. They were eleven field operatives, each possessing the raw, crushing force of a landslide and the surgical intellect of his own foresight woven into their very architecture.</p>
<p>With a subtle gesture, he sent them outward.</p>
<p>They didn’t walk across the grass or scramble over the rocks. They submerged.</p>
<p>They moved through the island’s substrate like sharks through water, utilizing the earthen authority to glide through the medium of the earth itself. They were ghosts in the machine, traveling through the veins of the island, capable of erupting anywhere they chose, bypassing every surface path, every guard, and every watchful eye.</p>
<p>Lilith stood frozen, her gaze fixed on the ground beside her feet. She felt the subtle, rhythmic thrumming of the earth, the unmistakable sensation of eleven massive, heavy presences surging through the soil beneath her.</p>
<p>The sheer scale of the movement was enough to make her breath hitch.</p>
<p>"Can you... can you feel all of them at once?" she asked, her voice barely a whisper.</p>
<p>"They are extensions of my own awareness," Rex replied, his posture relaxed, almost smug, as if he were merely stretching his limbs. "It’s not about giving full attention to each one."</p>
<p>"It’s about the connection."</p>
<p>"If a single one of them encounters a spike in resonance and if a single one of them locates a target, my attention will snap to it instantly."</p>
<p>"Like peripheral vision," Lilith surmised.</p>
<p>"Closer to proprioception," Rex corrected, his eyes gleaming with a dark, intellectual pride. "I know where they are."</p>
<p>"I know what they are touching..."</p>
<p>"I don’t need to ’look’ at them to know they exist because they are part of me."</p>
