<p>The air in the plaza didn’t just vibrate; it felt like it was being shredded by the sheer force of the clash. The tension was no longer a psychological weight; it was a physical pressure that made the very molecules of oxygen feel heavy and jagged.</p>
<p>Apollo stared at his hands, then at the armor Rex wore. The realization hit him like a physical blow.</p>
<p>The entropy reversal, the passive, radiating aura of his designation, hadn’t just been healing Kaelira. It had been working on the enemy.</p>
<p>"Your designation’s entropy reversal," Rex called out, his voice booming with a mocking, predatory clarity. "The cracks from Calivara’s working, the shattered plates from Kaelira’s cascade... they didn’t just stay broken."</p>
<p>"You’ve been repairing my armor for the last forty seconds without even trying."</p>
<p>Apollo blinked, the weight of the absurdity hitting him.</p>
<p>"You’re welcome," he grunted, the words sounding ridiculous even to his own ears.</p>
<p>"Truly," Rex replied, a smirk playing on his lips, "you are a generous man, Apollo."</p>
<p>"Such kindness in the middle of a slaughter."</p>
<p>The atmosphere shifted. The playfulness evaporated, replaced by a cold, lethal intent. Apollo’s eyes turned hard.</p>
<p>"I’m going to enjoy this part," he said, his voice dropping an octave, losing its warmth and gaining a jagged, deadly edge.</p>
<p>"I figured you might," Rex countered.</p>
<p>He settled into a stance, his body a coiled spring of pure violence. "Come on then."</p>
<p>"Let’s see what four minutes buys you."</p>
<p>Then, the world exploded.</p>
<p>Apollo and Kaelira didn’t just move; they launched. It wasn’t a dance; it was a collision of tectonic plates.</p>
<p>There was no choreography here, only the brutal, unpredictable chaos of two gods colliding with a monolith.</p>
<p>Kaelira was a blur of crimson fury. Fed by Apollo’s restoration, her body was a furnace of kinetic energy.</p>
<p>She hit Rex with a strike that sounded like a cannon shot. The impact caused a shockwave to pass through the ground, cracking the pavement for meters.</p>
<p>Rex’s Martial Arts Full Potential kicked in, his body twisting in midair to absorb the impact, his muscles rippling like steel cables under his skin. He caught her fist, the sound of their collision echoing like a hammer hitting an anvil.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of bone-on-bone impact echoed. Kaelira’s fists were like iron mauls, and Rex’s defense was a fortress.</p>
<p>But the pressure was immense. Every time they clashed, the air itself seemed to splinter.</p>
<p>While Kaelira provided the physical onslaught, Apollo was the silent storm. He didn’t just strike; he manipulated the very space around Rex.</p>
<p>Telekinetic bursts slammed into Rex’s flanks, trying to break his balance, while gravity wells threatened to crush his limbs. Rex was fighting a two-front war, a dance of death against a perfectly synchronized machine.</p>
<p>Rex’s Earthen Authority was working overtime. Every time a strike from Kaelira or a burst from Apollo chipped away at his armor, the Earthen Authority surged, the stone plates knitting themselves back together in a hiss of dust and grit.</p>
<p>He was a living fortress, constantly rebuilding himself even as he was being hammered.</p>
<p>But the cost was high. Rex’s Foresight was screaming.</p>
<p>He was seeing the next ten seconds of the fight before they happened, a constant stream of incoming violence. He saw the angle of Kaelira’s next punch and the trajectory of Apollo’s next gravity burst.</p>
<p>He was managing the chaos, but he was not unscathed.</p>
<p>His breath was heavy, a rhythmic growl in his chest. His eyes were burning with the intensity of a man holding back a flood.</p>
<p>He was tallying the blows. He was assessing the angles. He was fighting to endure.</p>
<p>He knew the clock was ticking. Apollo and Kaelira were a force of nature, a combination of speed, power, and regeneration that should have broken him.</p>
<p>But as long as he could hold the line, and as long as his foresight could predict the unpredictable, he could endure.</p>
<p>He was waiting for the moment the ceiling was reached. He was waiting for the moment the limit was hit.</p>
<p>Because he knew, deep in his marrow, that even the most mighty gods eventually run out of breath.</p>
<p>The air wasn’t just heavy; it was thick with the metallic tang of blood, the ozone of discharged energy, and the suffocating smell of pulverized stone. The battle had ceased to be a fight and had become a war of attrition, a brutal, rhythmic grinding of two-way destruction.</p>
<p>For three minutes and forty seconds, the world was nothing but a cacophonous blur of violence. There was no time for grand speeches or tactical reevaluations.</p>
<p>There was only the crack of bone meeting reinforced stone, the hiss of steam from overheated skin, and the thunderous boom of kinetic impacts. Rex was a monolith, absorbing blows that would have turned a lesser man into red mush, his body absorbing the brutal force of Kaelira’s unrelenting strikes.</p>
<p>Kaelira, in turn, was a whirlwind of fury, her body a vessel of pure rage, her skin flushing a deep crimson as she pushed past the limit of human biology. But even gods have a ceiling.</p>
<p>At the three-minute and forty-second mark, Rex saw it. The subtle shift.</p>
<p>The way Apollo’s energy signature began to flicker like a dying star. The way Kaelira’s regeneration speed slowed by a fraction of a percent.</p>
<p>The "edge" was fraying.</p>
<p>"Twenty seconds," Rex grunted, his voice a low growl hidden behind the mask.</p>
<p>He was breathing hard, his muscles screaming under the strain, but his eyes remained sharp as glass. "Give or take."</p>
<p>"You feel that? The drain?"</p>
<p>Apollo didn’t answer. He couldn’t answer because he was too busy holding the world together.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t wait for a reply. He saw the opening and he saw the moment the flow stuttered.</p>
<p>He didn’t just strike; he commanded the world to change.</p>
<p>"Gravity: sixfold!" he roared.</p>
<p>He slammed a wave of crushing force into the space between them. It wasn’t a gentle push; it was a gravitational hammer.</p>
<p>The gravity field slammed into Apollo and Kaelira like a falling ocean. It didn’t stop them, but it staggered them.</p>
<p>It broke the rhythm. And it gave Rex the seconds he needed.</p>
<p>With a predatory roar, Rex drove his fist into the earth. "Earthen Authority: RISE!"</p>
<p>The ground didn’t just move; it exploded. A massive slab of bedrock, two meters thick and five meters high, erupted from the plaza floor like a rising tombstone.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a slow growth; it was a violent geological surge. The stone groaned and screamed as it tore through the crust, a wall of pure mass slamming into the space between the combatants.</p>
<p>Apollo threw everything he had at the wall. A burst of divine energy, a concentrated blast of life force, a shriek of entropy reversal.</p>
<p>The wall took it all. The stone, being inanimate, didn’t feel the exhaustion.</p>
<p>It just stood there, a monolith of unyielding mass. And while Apollo was busy fighting the wall, Rex was already gone.</p>
<p>He was a blur of motion, circling the flank like a shark. He emerged from the right side of the barrier, his eyes locked on the vulnerable gap in their defense.</p>
<p>He saw the opening and he saw the weak point.</p>
<p>CRACK THOOM!</p>
<p>He hit Apollo with the full weight of his martial mastery. It wasn’t just a punch; it was a collision of will. The impact sounded like a cannonade.</p>
<p>Apollo’s body was thrown back, his breath escaping in a spray of blood and spit. He hit the ground hard, his limbs splayed, his energy signature flickering like a dying lamp.</p>
<p>Kaelira, still struggling against the six times gravity field, could only watch in horror. Her eyes, wide and wild, searched for the man who had been her anchor.</p>
<p>"Apollo!" she screamed.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a battle cry. It was a human sound.</p>
<p>A sound of fear. And a sound of vulnerability.</p>
<p>Rex stood over the fallen apostle, his shadow looming like a dark god. He looked down at the man who had just tried to kill him, his expression impassive, his breath steadying.</p>
<p>"Stay down," Rex commanded, his voice a low rumble of authority. "You’ve used your all, and there’s not a chance you win this one."</p>
<p>Apollo looked up, his vision blurred, his chest heaving. He had nothing left to say, and he had lost the tempo.</p>
<p>Kaelira let out a sound, a raw, guttural wail that was neither human nor animal. It was the sound of a berserker who had lost her focus, the sound of a predator whose prey had escaped.</p>
<p>But the fight wasn’t over. Not yet.</p>
<p>As the dust settled, the ground began to tremble. Not from Rex’s authority. Not from Apollo’s gravity. But from the distance.</p>
<p>From the rest of the island.</p>
<p>The earth groaned. The sky seemed to shudder.</p>
<p>The fight in the plaza had been the loudest, the most violent, the most brutal. But as the vibrations traveled through the rock, it was clear: the real war was only just beginning.</p>
