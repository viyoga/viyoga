<p>"What does the Underlayer's governance actually offer someone in my position?" Ren demanded, his voice hardening, stripping away the last vestiges of politeness. "Don't give me philosophy."</p>
<p>"Give me specifics! What is the tangible value of your 'sovereignty'?"</p>
<p>"A clear picture of reality," Rex countered, his voice dropping into a tone of chilling, absolute gravity. "Information that the Apostle network is actively withholding from you."</p>
<p>"The network will feed you a convenient narrative: that the Underlayer is a chaotic demon threat requiring containment to ensure your safety."</p>
<p>"But the truth is far more surgical... The network will tell you it is a void; I am telling you it is a city of two hundred thousand people who, last night, underwent a violent reconstruction that successfully severed a Legion contact network that has been operating in their shadows for fourteen months."</p>
<p>Ren's breath hitched. The word hit him like a physical blow.</p>
<p>"The Legion..."</p>
<p>"The Legion of Anti-Reincarnators," Rex clarified, the name sounding like a death knell. "An organization that has spent thirty years systematically eliminating reincarnators from this world."</p>
<p>"Forty three confirmed deaths, Ren. Likely many more..."</p>
<p>"The Underlayer's purge last night didn't just change their government; it decapitated the Legion's entire internal intelligence network."</p>
<p>Ren stared at the golem, his eyes wide, his combat-ready composure fracturing.</p>
<p>"The network... they don't tell their aligned reincarnators about the Legion," he said. It wasn't a question; it was the painful realization of a man who had just discovered that his entire life had been a curated lie.</p>
<p>"The network's intelligence on the Legion is compartmentalized far above the tier of an aligned combat class system holder," Rex said, his voice relentless. "Which means every hero designation, every elite soldier operating within the network's structure, has been marching toward a battlefield without knowing there is a shadow organization dedicated solely to their extinction."</p>
<p>A flicker of pure, unadulterated dread moved through Ren Askar's expression. He began to piece the nightmare together in real time.</p>
<p>"The canyon engagement..." he whispered, his voice trembling with the weight of the implication. "If the network sends an expedition... and that expedition includes the reincarnator force..."</p>
<p>"The Legion's Balance Keeper is going to use that exact window," Rex interrupted, his words cutting like a scalpel. "The moment the network's local force is committed to the canyon and Aethelgard's defensive capacity is stretched thin."</p>
<p>"She is going to strike the island..."</p>
<p>"The reincarnators who go to the canyon won't be coming back to a victory; they will be coming back to an island that is being systematically slaughtered."</p>
<p>Ren's face went pale. "If they come back..."</p>
<p>"If they come back," Rex repeated, the silence that followed heavy enough to crush the lungs.</p>
<p>The practice ground became a vacuum of sound. The wind seemed to stop; even the distant morning birds fell silent.</p>
<p>"You are telling me things that are either terrifyingly accurate and demand a total redirection of my life," Ren said, his voice regaining a desperate sort of strength, "or you are constructing the most perfect strategic narrative a rival power could dream up—a lie designed to make the opposition look self-defeating before they even fight."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said, and for the first time, there was a hint of grim respect in his tone. "Both of those are true simultaneously."</p>
<p>"I have every strategic reason in the world to want you out of the network's canyon expedition."</p>
<p>"But my interest in your position does not invalidate the data... Do not listen to my intent; evaluate the information on its own merits."</p>
<p>Ren looked at the golem, his mind racing through a thousand variables. "How do I verify it?"</p>
<p>"How do I know this isn't a ghost story meant to sow dissent?"</p>
<p>"You cannot verify the Legion's operational timeline before today," Rex conceded, "but you can verify their existence."</p>
<p>"The Academy's restricted archives contain documented incidents with the specific, unmistakable signature of Legion activity stretching back twenty years."</p>
<p>"They aren't labeled as 'Legion attacks.' They are filed under 'unexplained reincarnator disappearances. 'The pattern is screaming at you, Ren."</p>
<p>"You just haven't been looking for it."</p>
<p>"The restricted archive," Ren muttered, the gears of his mind turning.</p>
<p>"Your current access tier allows entry into the restricted section," Rex reminded him. "You have the clearance."</p>
<p>"You simply haven't had a reason to look until now... Now, you have the most pressing reason of your life."</p>
<p>Ren Askar stood paralyzed in the center of his training ground, caught between the shattered flagstones and the looming, silent golem. He looked up at the vast, indifferent morning sky above the Academy, feeling the world tilt on its axis.</p>
<p>"The market square," he said, his voice a command to himself.</p>
<p>"The market square," Rex confirmed. "But there is a condition..."</p>
<p>"I want you at the archive first... What you find in those records will determine the nature of our conversation at the square."</p>
<p>"Twenty minutes is not enough time for a full archive pull," Ren countered, the soldier in him fighting the strategist.</p>
<p>"Then come to the square when you are finished," Rex said, his voice final. "I am flexible on the timeline for flagged contacts."</p>
<p>Something fundamental shifted in Ren Askar's gaze. It was the precise, sharp recalibration of a man who had just realized he wasn't being treated as a mere soldier or a mere threat but as a variable of immense importance.</p>
<p>He wasn't just a 'flagged contact' to this power; he was a person being given the tools to choose his own destiny.</p>
<p>"You flagged me because of my designation's alignment," Ren said, his voice low, searching for the logic behind the sudden shift in his reality. "Not because of any action I've taken and not because of a threat I've posed."</p>
<p>"You flagged me because of what the system says I am."</p>
<p>"Correct," Rex replied, the word landing with the heavy finality of a gavel.</p>
<p>Ren's eyes narrowed, his mind already dissecting the tactical implications. "And yet, you are willing to hold the timeline for a flagged contact to let me slip away into the restricted archives just so I can verify your claims?"</p>
<p>"You're giving me a window of autonomy."</p>
<p>"Because you are a person making an informed decision," Rex said, and for the first time, the voice through the relay carried a profound, almost philosophical weight. "An informed decision requires the luxury of time."</p>
<p>"The alternative is a person making a frantic decision under the crushing weight of time pressure."</p>
<p>"And fast decisions made under pressure produce a kind of rigid, blind commitment—the kind of commitment that is nearly impossible to break once the truth finally catches up to it."</p>
<p>Ren Askar stared at the golem, the silent stone monolith that had become the mouthpiece of a revolution. The tension in his muscles hadn't dissipated; it had merely transmuted from combat readiness to an agonizing intellectual hunger.</p>
<p>"Tell me what happened in the Underlayer last night," Ren demanded, his voice cracking the silence like a whip. "But don't give me the strategic summary."</p>
<p>"Don't give me the version designed to sway my allegiance or manipulate my position..."</p>
<p>"Give me the raw truth."</p>
<p>"The problem, Ren, is that the raw truth is designed to make you change your position," Rex countered, his tone unyielding. "That is the nature of accurate information."</p>
<p>"When your current worldview is built on a foundation of incomplete data, the truth will naturally act as a wrecking ball."</p>
<p>"I cannot give you an account that is 'neutral' if the facts themselves demand a shift in your assessment."</p>
<p>Ren stepped closer to the construct, his gaze burning. "Then stop trying to be neutral."</p>
<p>"Just give me the truth."</p>
<p>And then, Rex began to speak.</p>
<p>He bypassed the condensed, tactical bursts the golem was programmed to deliver. He stripped away the diplomatic layers and the sanitized reports. Instead, he unleashed a torrent of high-density, terrifying reality.</p>
<p>He spoke of the purge not as a mere political shift, but as a violent, surgical necessity. He detailed the Legion's identification methodology, revealing how they had been hiding in plain sight, a shadow parasite feeding on the reincarnator population.</p>
<p>He relayed the chilling echoes of the speech from the Spire, the frantic, rhythmic cadence of the engagement sequence, and the terrifying moment the Blood Oath was fully activated and the visceral, metaphysical power it unleashed upon the city.</p>
<p>He laid bare the new governance structure, a complex web of sovereignty born from chaos. He spoke of the grim reality. Aethelgard would face in the coming six weeks: a period of unprecedented vulnerability where the lines between protector and prey would blur.</p>
<p>He spoke of the Legion's foundational philosophy, the cold, mathematical belief that reincarnators were an evolutionary error that needed to be corrected. He spoke of Celestina Von Starlight, the architect of the shadow war.</p>
<p>He spoke of the Seam House, the relay network, and the grim tally of the forty-three souls already lost to the void.</p>
<p>The information poured into Ren's mind like molten lead, heavy and transformative. Every detail was a hammer blow to his understanding of the world.</p>
<p>By the time Rex finally fell silent, the air in the practice ground felt different, thinner, and colder, as if the very atmosphere had been stripped of its illusions.</p>
<p>The practice ground was silent, the weight of the truth hanging between them like a physical shroud.</p>
