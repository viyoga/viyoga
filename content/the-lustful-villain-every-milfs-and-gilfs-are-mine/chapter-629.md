<p>In the depths of his consciousness, Mordecai had felt the limiter snap. It hadn’t been a mechanical click but a psychic rupture. For six months, he had been a man trying to hold back a tidal wave with a dam made of sand.</p>
<p>He had been the "responsible" one, the "structured" one, the one who counted every coin and every drop of mana to ensure the city’s stability. He had been a prisoner of his own caution.</p>
<p>But as the eighteen hundreds pulled through the architecture of his soul, the dam didn’t just break; it evaporated.</p>
<p>"HRAAAAAAAGGGGGGGGGHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!!!"</p>
<p>The scream that tore from his lungs was the sound of a man reclaiming his divinity. It was the sound of twenty-two years of being a bystander finally erupting into a protagonist.</p>
<p>It was a primal, soul-shattering roar that carried the weight of every suppressed insult, every swallowed pride, and every moment he had chosen "safe" over "true."</p>
<p>Then, the world broke.</p>
<p>The bioluminescent ceiling, the very sky of the underlayer, didn’t just crack; it shattered. A hundred and twenty rifts tore through the geological substrate of the cavern, sounding like a thousand glass cathedrals collapsing at once.</p>
<p>A terrifying, abyssal darkness instantly swallowed the amber and blue light of the city. The ceiling was gone. In its place was a jagged, weeping mosaic of the multiverse.</p>
<p>Cold, alien light colors that had no business existing in a three-dimensional space leaked from the rifts, casting long, distorted shadows over the two hundred thousand terrified souls below. The pressure was immense; the air grew thin and tasted of ozone and ancient, starlight dust.</p>
<p>Rex, who had been standing in a posture of casual, blood-soaked dominance, felt the shift in the atmosphere like a physical blow to the chest. The mocking grin vanished.</p>
<p>The predatory amusement died in his eyes, replaced by wide-eyed, primal shock. He looked up, his head tilting back, his gaze lost in the swirling, chaotic void of the ceiling.</p>
<p>"What the... what the fuck is this?" Rex whispered, his voice barely audible over the roaring hum of the rifts.</p>
<p>For the first time, he didn’t sound like a god. He sounded like a man realizing he was standing in the path of a hurricane.</p>
<p>"Mordecai! What the hell did you just do?!"</p>
<p>Mordecai stood at the center of the courtyard, the epicenter of the madness. He was no longer a man.</p>
<p>He was a conduit. His skin was etched with glowing, violet ley lines, and his eyes were no longer eyes; they were windows into the very mechanism of the gacha, spinning with the frantic, beautiful energy of a thousand simultaneous summons.</p>
<p>"I stopped managing, Lustful Villain!" Mordecai yelled, his voice cutting through the dimensional roar like a blade.</p>
<p>He looked up at the weeping sky, his face illuminated by the terrifying light of a hundred different worlds. "I stopped being the architect of a stable life!"</p>
<p>"Tonight, we see what happens when the foundation is gone!"</p>
<p>Rex stepped back, his hand instinctively clenching both his gauntlets, his breath coming in short, jagged gasps. The sheer scale of the energy was overwhelming; it was a weight that pressed against his very soul, threatening to crush his essence.</p>
<p>"You’re insane!" Rex shouted, a frantic, hysterical edge creeping into his tone. "You’re going to tear the whole fucking world apart just to prove a point!"</p>
<p>"You’ll kill everyone! You’ll kill yourself!"</p>
<p>"Maybe!" Mordecai roared back, a wild, terrifying joy dancing in his eyes. "But at least we’ll die as something more than just pieces on your goddamn board!"</p>
<p>As he spoke, the first of the summons began to emerge. Not as single, elegant entities, but as a chaotic, violent deluge of power. From the rifts, shadows, titans, gods, and monsters began to pour, a torrential rain of cosmic entities descending upon the Underlayer.</p>
<p>The ground groaned, the buildings shuddered, and the very fabric of reality began to scream under the weight of the sudden, massive influx of existence.</p>
<p>The battle for the Underlayer had ceased to be a fight between two men. It had become a collision of universes, and at the center of the storm stood Mordecai, the man who had finally decided to stop being a spectator and start being a force of nature.</p>
<p>The air in the courtyard didn’t just vibrate; it screamed. The sheer, unadulterated chaos of a hundred and twenty simultaneous dimensional breaches turned the atmosphere into a churning soup of ozone, cosmic dust, and raw, unrefined mana.</p>
<p>Rex stood amidst the carnage, his silhouette a dark, sharp anchor in a world that had lost its geometry. He watched the ceiling or what was left of it as it wept gods and monsters into the underlayer.</p>
<p>His mind, a hyper-efficient engine of calculation and foresight, was working at a fever pitch. He had expected a storm; he had prepared for a tempest.</p>
<p>But this? This was a goddamn apocalypse of probability.</p>
<p>’One hundred and twenty,’ Rex thought, his eyes darting upward, tracking the jagged, bleeding edges of the rifts. ’The math was wrong...’</p>
<p>’The reserve wasn’t just a stockpile; it was a fucking supernova waiting to happen.’</p>
<p>He felt a flicker of something he rarely allowed himself to experience: genuine, unmitigated shock. It wasn’t fear; Rex didn’t do fear, but it was a profound, jarring recognition of a variable he had underestimated.</p>
<p>He had modeled Mordecai as a man who would eventually grow, a man who would eventually use his tools. He hadn’t modeled him as a man who would decide to break the universe just to stop being a "bum."</p>
<p>"You magnificent, suicidal bastard!" Rex roared, his voice cutting through the cacophony of the descending storm.</p>
<p>A grin, wide and terrifyingly cocky, split his face even as the sheer pressure of the rifts threatened to buckle his knees. "You actually did it!"</p>
<p>"You stopped playing house and decided to burn the whole fucking neighborhood down!"</p>
<p>"You’re not just pulling the lever, Mordecai; you’re ripping the whole machine out of the ground!"</p>
<p>Despite the madness, Rex’s confidence remained a towering, arrogant thing. He felt the thrill of the hunt, the intoxicating rush of a world suddenly becoming infinitely more dangerous and infinitely more interesting.</p>
<p>"Finally! A challenge that doesn’t feel like babysitting a goddamn toddler!"</p>
<p>Then, the first wave hit.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a controlled descent; it was a bombardment. The SR class outputs began to cascade through the rifts like a torrential rain of nightmare and wonder.</p>
<p>The crystalline arthropod, a gargantuan structure of refracting light and jagged geometry, tore through a rift with a sound like a thousand mirrors shattering. Its massive limbs scraped against the city’s architecture, sending showers of stone and bioluminescent dust raining down on the terrified populace.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t hesitate. His eyes flashed with a cold, calculating light.</p>
<p>"Don’t just stand there and watch the show, you idiots!" he commanded his elemental constructs, though his gaze remained fixed on the falling titan.</p>
<p>He thrust his hands forward, his telekinetic aura erupting from him in a violent, invisible wave. He caught the crystalline arthropod mid-stride, the sheer mass of the creature pushing back against his will with the force of a mountain.</p>
<p>The redirection required a massive, sustained expenditure of energy; the creature’s exoskeleton didn’t just absorb the force, it dispersed it laterally, sending ripples of kinetic energy through the courtyard that threatened to liquefy the very ground.</p>
<p>Rex gritted his teeth, the muscles in his arms corded and straining. "Heh!"</p>
<p>"Is that all you’ve got, you overgrown fucking jewel?" he spat, his voice a mix of exertion and exhilaration.</p>
<p>He felt the immense pressure of the creature’s biology and the way it fought his control, and he found himself laughing a short, sharp bark of pure, adrenaline-fueled joy.</p>
<p>He watched as the sound-based entity drifted through the air like a ghost of a dying symphony and as the three fragmented organisms began their desperate, lurching march toward one another across the city streets. The world was no longer a structured city; it was a chaotic, living battlefield of impossible biologies.</p>
<p>Rex noted the dispersal mechanic of the arthropod, the way the force bled out through its crystalline lattice, and he filed it away in the cold, efficient archives of his mind. He was in the thick of it now. The "management" was over. The "structure" was dead.</p>
<p>The real game had finally begun.</p>
