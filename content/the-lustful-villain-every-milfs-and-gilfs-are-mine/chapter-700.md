<p>The tension in the plaza was no longer just a feeling; it was a physical weight, a pressure in the lungs of everyone present. Rex watched Apollo through the slit of his stone visor, his mind working with the cold, calculating precision of a grandmaster surveying a board.</p>
<p>He knew Apollo better than most. Over the months of their manufactured friendship and strained coexistence, Rex had learned that Apollo was no brute.</p>
<p>He wasn't just a man of light; he was a man of strategy. He understood the geometry of power. The Apollo standing there now wasn't the man from the canyon; he was something sharper, something hardened.</p>
<p>The Apostle of Life had undergone a metamorphosis in the weeks since their last encounter, and the energy signature radiating from him was blindingly dense. The recovery period hadn't been a time of rest; it had been a crucible of refinement.</p>
<p>"You said you came from the Underlayer," Apollo said, his voice cutting through the dust like a blade.</p>
<p>His eyes were narrowed, stripping away Rex's arrogance to find the truth. "You speak of it like it's a sovereign nation."</p>
<p>"Mordecai has been running the shadows down there for years, and the network barely even noticed him."</p>
<p>"So tell me, Tremor. Is the world changing, or is the truth changing?"</p>
<p>Rex let a smirk tug at the corner of his mouth. He didn't even bother to hide the satisfaction in his voice.</p>
<p>"The truth is the only thing that doesn't change, Apollo. Only the players do."</p>
<p>"Mordecai's been running the Underlayer for decades," Apollo pressed, his posture shifting into a combat-ready stance that made the very air tremble. "If you're suggesting he's been replaced, you're suggesting a total collapse of the order we knew."</p>
<p>"The order didn't collapse," Rex said, his voice dropping into a low, resonant thrum that echoed the stone. "It was overwritten."</p>
<p>"And you took it?" Apollo asked, the question hanging in the air like a death sentence.</p>
<p>"I took it from everyone who thought they claimed it," Rex replied, his eyes gleaming with a predatory light. "Mordecai is still alive, if that makes you feel better."</p>
<p>"He's just... no longer the architect. But he's just another part of the foundation now."</p>
<p>"How many?" Apollo asked.</p>
<p>There was a hitch in his voice, a momentary lapse in his composure that revealed the weight of the answer he was dreading. He was bracing himself, his body tensing for the blow.</p>
<p>"Enough to change the map," Rex said, his voice devoid of mercy. "There is only one Demon Lord left in the Underlayer. And it isn't him."</p>
<p>As the words left his lips, the Academy fighters began to react. The tension had reached a breaking point.</p>
<p>A group of six elite soldiers, sensing the shifting tide, began to move. They moved with the coordinated grace of a strike team, closing the distance toward Rex with the intent to neutralize the anomaly.</p>
<p>They were moving as a single unit, a wall of steel and will, intending to smother the threat before it could erupt.</p>
<p>Rex didn't even blink. He simply raised a single hand, a gesture of casual dismissal.</p>
<p>"Enough theatrics," Rex muttered.</p>
<p>The ground didn't just shake; it screamed. Without a moment's warning, twenty-three golems erupted from the cracked earth.</p>
<p>They didn't explode upward in a clumsy display of force; they slid through the fractures with the lethal efficiency of serpents. They emerged from the veins of the island, their stone bodies interlocking with the precision of a fortress.</p>
<p>In a heartbeat, a wall of living granite stood between Rex and the advancing soldiers, a monolithic barrier that felt less like a defense and more like a statement of fact.</p>
<p>The attacking soldiers skidded to a halt, their weapons raised, their breath catching in their throats. They were staring at a wall of stone that breathed.</p>
<p>"I am not here to slaughter your men," Rex said, his voice booming from the center of the golem line, dripping with a cocky, effortless grace. "But if you insist on testing the foundation, you will find it is harder than it looks."</p>
<p>"The golems are here because it is easier than me having to personally deal with each of you. It saves us all a great deal of unnecessary effort."</p>
<p>Apollo stared at the impenetrable wall of stone, his eyes flashing with a mixture of fury and respect.</p>
<p>"How generous of you," he spat, the sarcasm thick enough to cut.</p>
<p>Rex let out a short, sharp laugh, his eyes locking onto Apollo's with a defiant brilliance. "I think it's the most generous thing you've heard all day."</p>
<p>The air in the plaza was no longer just air; it was a pressurized soup of tension, dust, and the scent of ozone. As the golem wall stood its ground, the chaos erupted.</p>
<p>From the periphery of the crowd, three reincarnators, realizing the sheer scale of the scale-shifting power Rex possessed, broke into a panicked sprint. They fled in different directions, their footsteps a frantic rhythm against the stone.</p>
<p>Rex didn't even turn to watch them. His awareness, guided by the geological signals from the ground, followed their paths through the stone until they disappeared into the commercial district, leaving only the sound of their escape behind.</p>
<p>He let them go. They were small-scale variables; he had larger weights to consider.</p>
<p>Near the Academy gate, a cluster of four fighters made a different choice. These were not the green soldiers; these were the veterans.</p>
<p>They bore the scars of real combat and the aura of trained masters. They moved with a lethal synchronicity that made the air hum. Without a word, they launched a coordinated strike.</p>
<p>A torrent of roaring flame collided with a cyclone of compressed wind, slamming into the golem frontline with the force of a tempest. The impact sent a spray of shattered granite across the plaza, a thunderous explosion of dust and stone.</p>
<p>Rex didn't flinch. He simply adjusted the gravity.</p>
<p>In a heartbeat, the laws of physics shifted. The gravity in the immediate radius of the four fighters tripled.</p>
<p>It wasn't a gradual increase; it was a sudden, violent crushing weight.</p>
<p>The fighters, mid-strike, were slammed toward the earth. The first woman, whose flame was mid-bloom, saw her fire dissolve into a disorganized haze as her concentration shattered under the weight.</p>
<p>The second fighter, attempting to launch a gust of wind, found the pressure forcing the air sideways, the compressed blast smashing into a nearby decorative planter and turning it into shrapnel.</p>
<p>They were forced to their knees, their bodies straining against an invisible hand that demanded their total submission.</p>
<p>"You are not the primary target," Rex said, his voice cutting through the roar of the clash with a chilling, unbothered clarity.</p>
<p>He didn't look at them; he looked through them. "You can end this at any time simply by stopping."</p>
<p>"This is the polite version of the conversation."</p>
<p>One of the fighters, a man with a scarred jaw and the rank markings of a senior officer, gritted his teeth, his muscles trembling as he fought to lift his head against the crushing force.</p>
<p>"Polite?" he grunted, his voice strained. "This is FUCKING polite, you say?!"</p>
<p>"Compared to what comes after, yes," Rex replied, his tone as cold as the deep stone. "I would suggest you take the offer while it is still on the table."</p>
<p>But while the fighters struggled, a shadow moved.</p>
<p>Rex had been tracking her. He had been sensing her since the moment Apollo's group arrived.</p>
<p>Kaelira. She was not like the others.</p>
<p>She was a presence of stillness, a predator waiting for the moment the world wavered. He had felt her energy, a quiet burner beneath the surface of the chaos.</p>
<p>She moved.</p>
<p>She didn't just run; she vanished into a blur of motion. She crossed the twenty-meter gap of the broken plaza in a flash of speed that defied the logic of her injuries.</p>
<p>She was a streak of purpose, her energy a roaring heat signature that bypassed the golem wall entirely. This wasn't the standard fire magic; it was a concentrated, high-temperature output, a combustion of will that turned the air around her into a shimmering haze.</p>
<p>Rex reacted. He couldn't let her reach him at full velocity.</p>
<p>As she closed the distance, he deployed a gravity field. He didn't crush her; that would be wasteful, but he tripled her effective mass just as she reached the eight-meter mark.</p>
<p>The impact of the gravity well hit her like a physical wall.</p>
<p>Kaelira didn't stumble, and she didn't break. She hit the ground with a thud that echoed the heaviness of her own momentum, her body absorbing the shock of the impact like a coiled spring.</p>
<p>She rolled, her eyes burning with a fury that matched the heat of her flame, and as she came up from the dust, her eyes were already locked onto his.</p>
<p>"Kaelira!" Apollo's voice roared across the plaza, a command filled with dread and warning.</p>
<p>But Kaelira was already moving again. And she wasn't done.</p>
<p>She was just beginning.</p>
