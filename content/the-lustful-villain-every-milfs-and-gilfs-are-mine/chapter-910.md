<p>Rex saved Diana for last. It was a calculated move, a deliberate choice that served both his training regimen and the unspoken power dynamics simmering beneath the surface of the hall.</p>
<p>"Your stance is already correct," he announced, his voice carrying across the room with clear authority.</p>
<p>He wanted the spectators, the curious cousins, and the nervous junior guards to hear it. He wanted them to understand that ’correct’ was not some abstract concept reserved for legends; it was a tangible reality that could be achieved through sweat and discipline.</p>
<p>Then, he stepped closer, lowering his voice until it was a private murmur meant only for her ears, cutting through the ambient noise of the hall. "But your weight shifts a fraction too early when you commit to a strike."</p>
<p>"You’re telegraphing your intent before your muscles even contract... It has been costing you precious milliseconds of speed for as long as I have watched you fight."</p>
<p>Diana didn’t flinch, and she didn’t deflect with a quip. Her eyes locked onto his, burning with a focused intensity.</p>
<p>"Show me," she commanded.</p>
<p>Rex moved behind her. He positioned himself close enough that his presence was undeniable, close enough that the heat from his body could be felt, but he maintained a clinical detachment.</p>
<p>This was not an invitation to intimacy; it was a demonstration of mechanics. He placed his hand firmly at her hip, his fingers finding the exact point of tension where her balance wavered.</p>
<p>"Wait for the coil," he whispered, his breath ghosting near her ear. "Don’t lean into the strike."</p>
<p>"Let the strike come from the floor."</p>
<p>He guided her through the motion, physically forcing her body to delay the shift by a heartbeat. He felt the micro adjustments in her muscles as she resisted the old habit, the way her core tightened to compensate.</p>
<p>When she finally executed the movement, the correction was instinctive.</p>
<p>"There," he said, withdrawing his hand. "That is the difference between a strike that lands and a strike that kills."</p>
<p>Diana launched the strike again, alone this time. The difference was visceral.</p>
<p>Even to the untrained eyes of the household staff watching from the sidelines, there was a new, lethal quality to her movement. There was an added snap, a whip crack of energy, that came from the weight arriving at the exact microsecond the force required it, rather than bleeding off prematurely.</p>
<p>"That is going to matter," Diana murmured, more to herself than to him.</p>
<p>Her jaw was set, her mind already cataloging the adjustment as a permanent part of her arsenal.</p>
<p>From the shadows at the rear of the hall, Rex could feel Theo’s gaze like a physical weight. The heir was watching the exchange with an expression that was performing the exhausting labor of appearing professionally interested, but he was failing.</p>
<p>Beneath the mask of neutrality, Rex could sense the corrosive burn of resentment and the dangerous calculation of a wounded predator watching a rival claim territory.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The morning dissolved into the familiar, brutal rhythm of the training hall. Rex circulated through paired sparring sessions like a surgeon moving between patients.</p>
<p>He watched Iris as she was paired with a nervous junior guard; she demonstrated a rare, unspoken grace, dialing back her lethal intensity just enough to allow the boy to learn without being maimed, all while maintaining the razor-sharp quality of her technique.</p>
<p>He intervened twice during a session between two of the twelve elites, stopping them mid-swing because their footwork, while technically flawless, was tactically lazy; they were fighting like athletes, not survivors.</p>
<p>He allowed Aurelia to conduct her own segment without interference. There was no need for his critique there; she was a force of nature in her own right, and both of them understood that his role today was to observe, not to usurp.</p>
<p>As she worked, Rex remained a silent sentinel, his mind working like a ledger, cataloguing who was truly evolving and who was merely coasting on the reputation they had earned yesterday.</p>
<p>Finally, the time came for the closing ritual. Rex stepped into the center of the circle and gestured to Diana.</p>
<p>The unspoken understanding between them hung heavy in the air, a tension so palpable that several of the onlookers instinctively took a half step back. No one questioned it; in this house, competence was the only currency that mattered, and the chemistry between them was irrelevant compared to the lethal potential they shared.</p>
<p>"Full intensity," Rex said, his voice dropping into a register that signaled the end of instruction and the beginning of combat. "Do not expect me to hold back."</p>
<p>"I would rather you discover the true ceiling of your capabilities now than find out in the middle of a slaughter when you realize you’ve been training against an artificially soft version of me."</p>
<p>Diana’s lips curled into a genuine, dangerous smile, the smile of a wolf recognizing another.</p>
<p>"Good," she said, rolling her shoulders as she settled into a deadly crouch. "I was going to ask anyway."</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>Diana was transformed. It wasn’t that a single morning of technical adjustment had rewritten her entire combat identity—that would be impossible—but she possessed the rare, terrifying quality of a predator who could absorb a lesson and weaponize it instantly.</p>
<p>Most fighters required hours, sometimes days, to translate verbal correction into instinctive muscle memory. Diana did it in heartbeats.</p>
<p>Her weight shift was no longer a tell; it was a coiled spring. Her recovery between exchanges was seamless, her movements stripped of any wasted motion that might betray her next intent.</p>
<p>She pressed him harder than she ever had before. Every strike carried more conviction and venom.</p>
<p>She wasn’t just sparring; she was testing the limits of the new reality Rex had handed her. Rex allowed the engagement to stretch, letting the intensity climb until the air in the hall felt charged with static.</p>
<p>Watching her evolve in real time provided a grim satisfaction that bypassed his tactical mind entirely; it was the raw appreciation of a craftsman watching steel become unbreakable.</p>
<p>The climax of the exchange came with brutal efficiency. Diana launched a desperate, high-velocity strike aimed at his throat, a move that would have been fatal in any other context.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He stepped inside the arc of her arm, his movement a blur of controlled violence.</p>
<p>He caught her wrist, pivoted his hips, and used her own momentum against her. With a precise, controlled application of force, he redirected her trajectory into a guided fall.</p>
<p>There was a sharp thud as her back hit the mats, but she absorbed the impact with the fluid grace he had drilled into her, rolling to dissipate the energy rather than fighting it.</p>
<p>The hall fell into a suffocating silence. It was the kind of quiet that occurs when witnesses realize they are watching something primal, something that defies the polite vocabulary of training and enters the realm of something far more dangerous.</p>
<p>Diana lay on the floor for a heartbeat, chest heaving, sweat slicking her skin. Then, she looked up at him and grinned.</p>
<p>It was an unguarded, feral expression, one she never permitted herself when the household’s eyes were upon her.</p>
<p>"Better...?" she panted, the word punctuated by her ragged breathing.</p>
<p>"Much better," Rex replied, his voice carrying an unfamiliar note of genuine approval.</p>
<p>He reached down, offering his hand.</p>
<p>When she took it, he didn’t simply pull her up; he hauled her close, the motion deliberately violating the distance expected between instructor and student. For several seconds, the hundreds of eyes watching them—the guards, the servants, the scheming cousins—simply ceased to exist.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at her flushed face, her pupils dilated with exertion and something else, and felt something that had absolutely nothing to do with strategy, training, or control.</p>
<p>"You are doing that thing," Diana whispered, her voice low enough to be private despite the silence of the hall. "That thing where you look at me like I am the only person in the room."</p>
<p>"You are... currently," Rex said, his gaze unblinking, holding hers with an intensity that felt like a physical claim. "The room will still be here in a minute, but this moment will not."</p>
<p>Then, he kissed her. It was not a tentative gesture; it was unhurried, possessive, and utterly defiant of the audience surrounding them.</p>
<p>In front of the entire training hall, Rex Rexilion claimed her. Diana didn’t recoil; she didn’t hesitate.</p>
<p>Her hand came up to rest against his chest, fingers splaying over his heartbeat, not to push him away but to anchor herself to him.</p>
<p>Across the hall, Aurelia remained motionless, but the corners of her mouth lifted in a small, knowing smile, the approval of a matriarch who understood that power was often forged in the heat of passion as much as in battle.</p>
<p>Elaris, watching from the sidelines, pressed a hand to her mouth to stifle a delighted gasp. Iris offered a brief, warm smile of amusement before returning to the solitary work of stretching out her bruised shoulder.</p>
<p>Only one person refused to witness it. Theo turned his head away first, his jaw working silently, his eyes fixed on the far wall as he retreated into the bitter shadows of his own resentment.</p>
