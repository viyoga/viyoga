<p>The tension in the plaza didn’t just break; it shattered.</p>
<p>Apollo raised his hand, and the light that erupted from his palm was no longer the soothing, rejuvenating glow of a healer. It was a lance of compressed divinity, a concentrated beam of reality-warping force.</p>
<p>The strike slammed into Rex’s stone armor with the sound of a mountain splitting. The force was immense, enough to crater the ground beneath his feet and slide his heavy frame backward a half step.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes narrowed behind his mask. He felt the vibration of the impact rattling his very marrow, but he didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>The armor held and the anchor also held.</p>
<p>"Is that everything?" Rex asked, his voice dripping with a casual, almost insulting nonchalance.</p>
<p>The mask hid his expression, but the sheer arrogance of the question was palpable. "Because if so, this is going to be over a lot faster than I expected."</p>
<p>Then, the predator moved.</p>
<p>Rex surged forward, and the world seemed to groan under the sudden shift in momentum. When Apollo engaged, it wasn’t a fight of blades or fists; it was a collision of fundamental physics.</p>
<p>Apollo’s offense was a violent clash of realities. By reversing entropy in a localized zone, he created a ’boundary condition,’ a jagged, violent edge where the laws of physics simply refused to agree.</p>
<p>It was like slamming two different universes together.</p>
<p>The first forty seconds were a blur of destruction. The plaza, already scarred, was systematically dismantled.</p>
<p>Rex took three direct strikes, the impacts sounding like thunderclaps. The stone armor groaned, pitting and cracking under the pressure of Apollo’s divine output, but it held.</p>
<p>In retaliation, Rex unleashed a telekinetic surge of lethal precision. He caught Apollo in a grip of gravity, lifting him six meters into the air.</p>
<p>"Four seconds," Rex remarked, his voice cutting through the roar of the clash.</p>
<p>He was analyzing, calculating, his mind as cold as the stone he commanded. "Last time, you needed three."</p>
<p>"You’re slowing down, Apollo."</p>
<p>"Shut up and fight!" Apollo roared, his voice a commanding thunder.</p>
<p>He broke the hold with a burst of light that shattered the air itself.</p>
<p>As the two titans clashed, the battlefield became a meat grinder of elemental force. Nearby, Calivara, seeing the opening, moved with the lethal grace of a predator.</p>
<p>She didn’t target Rex; she knew her target was the golems. She understood the geometry of the chaos.</p>
<p>She unleashed her blood affinity, a visceral, terrifying power. Her first strike was a surge of corrosive energy that melted the stone of the first golem like acid on flesh.</p>
<p>The second golem, a massive construct of granite, was caught in a pressure differential. She forced the blood like energy into the seams of the stone, and with a sickening CRACK, the golem exploded from the inside out, its internal structure pulverized by the sudden expansion of pressure.</p>
<p>But the third golem was different. It wasn’t an autonomous construct; it was a vessel of Rex’s own will, a living extension of the Earthen Authority.</p>
<p>It moved with a purposeful, unyielding weight that the other golems lacked.</p>
<p>Calivara poured everything into her defense, her blood affinity forming a shimmering, viscous barrier. But the golem didn’t care about physics or logic.</p>
<p>It was a wall of pure mass, driven by the will of a god-like entity.</p>
<p>The collision was brutal. The golem smashed through her defensive field like a hammer through glass.</p>
<p>The impact sent her flying backward, a streak of motion across the shattered plaza. She slammed into the remnants of a decorative wall with a bone-jarring thud, the stone splintering around her as she was swallowed by the debris.</p>
<p>The dust settled for a fraction of a second, leaving only the sound of Rex and Apollo’s breathing and the smell of burnt ozone and crushed stone. The fight was far from over; it was only just beginning to bleed.</p>
<p>The air in the plaza didn’t just hum; it screamed with the friction of colliding realities.</p>
<p>As Eryndra, the astral summoner, began her work, the very fabric of the dimension seemed to shudder. Her energy didn’t strike the world like a hammer; it unraveled it like a thread.</p>
<p>Rex felt the sensation through his armor, not a physical impact, but a structural dissonance, a vibration that rattled his very essence. The golems near her began to lose their coherence, their stone-like forms blurring as the fundamental principle holding them together was stripped away.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t hesitate. He was a maestro of chaos.</p>
<p>He reasserted his authority, lacing the geological relay with his own will to stabilize the crumbling formations. But the cost was heavy. Managing the Apollo clash, the Kaelira gravity field, and now the astral instability was a mental drain that would have shattered a lesser man.</p>
<p>"Too many variables," Rex muttered, his voice a low growl of pure focus.</p>
<p>With a violent gesture, he tore seven new golems from the shattered rubble of the plaza. They rose like jagged monoliths, born of dust and fury.</p>
<p>He didn’t waste a second; he sent four of them screaming toward Eryndra.</p>
<p>She was swift—terrifyingly fast. Before the golems could crush her, she was already airborne, her body defying gravity through astral buoyancy.</p>
<p>She moved like a ghost in the wind, a shimmering afterimage of presence.</p>
<p>Rex saw his opening. He released the grip on Kaelira.</p>
<p>He didn’t let her up slowly; he dropped the gravity field instantly. The sudden release of eight times the pressure was like a vacuum snapping shut.</p>
<p>Kaelira, who had been bracing against the crushing weight, surged upward with a violent, uncontrolled force. She staggered, her balance momentarily shattered by the sudden void of resistance.</p>
<p>"Now," Rex hissed.</p>
<p>He was a blur of stone and intent. He ignored the distractions, his eyes locked on Apollo.</p>
<p>He moved across the thirty meters of broken rubble in a heartbeat, his boots thundering against the earth. He was a force of nature, a landslide given human form.</p>
<p>As he ran, he reached into the very substrate of the world, pulling compressed earth and stone into his momentum, augmenting his mass and power with every stride.</p>
<p>Apollo met him in the center of the carnage. The collision was a tectonic event.</p>
<p>Apollo unleashed a directed working of pure light, a lance of divine energy. Rex didn’t dodge.</p>
<p>He summoned a wall of compacted stone from the ground, a shield of granite that caught the impact. The shield shattered under the force, sending a spray of shrapnel and stone shards flying like bullets across the plaza, but the momentum was preserved.</p>
<p>Rex pushed through the debris. Apollo rolled, his movements fluid and desperate, launching a close-range strike.</p>
<p>Rex took the hit directly on his chest plate. The sound was like a mountain collapsing.</p>
<p>The armor cracked, a spiderweb of fractures blooming across the stone surface, but the inner layer held.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t even blink. He reached out.</p>
<p>His gauntleted hands, heavy with the weight of the Earthen Authority, clamped onto Apollo’s arm. It wasn’t a strike; it was a capture.</p>
<p>The grip was absolute, a lock of stone on flesh that felt like the closing of a tomb.</p>
<p>The tension in the air was suffocating. Apollo was straining, his muscles bulging, his breath coming in ragged gasps as he fought against the unyielding strength of the man who seemed to have the weight of the world in his fingers.</p>
<p>"You’re stronger up close than you let people believe," Apollo gritted out, his voice strained, his eyes burning with a mixture of exhaustion and respect. "All those matches... you were holding back."</p>
<p>Rex leaned in, his face inches from Apollo’s, his expression a mask of arrogant triumph. A dark, charismatic smirk pulled at the corner of his lips.</p>
<p>"Every single one," Rex replied, his voice a low, deadly rumble. "You should be flattered, Apollo."</p>
<p>"Most men would have been crushed by the weight of my presence alone. But you? You actually made me work for it."</p>
<p>He tightened his grip, the stone of his gauntlets grinding against the air itself.</p>
<p>"I gave you a fair fight," Rex added, his eyes gleaming with the pride of a predator. "But don’t mistake my mercy for weakness."</p>
<p>"In this arena, there is only one truth: the one who stands wins."</p>
