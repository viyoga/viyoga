<p>[Flashback]</p>
<p>The contract had not been signed with the pedestrian formalities mortals clung to for security. There was no parchment to yellow with age, no ink carved from oak galls, and no quill scratching across a table to seal a pact between two desperate souls.</p>
<p>The covenant between Celestina Von Starlight and Kaeleth, the God of Time, was forged in something far more visceral: a convergence of raw intent that pierced the divine framework.</p>
<p>It was binding not because of law but because the universe itself recognized the absolute, uncoerced alignment of two wills.</p>
<p>Celestina had been forty-three years old when she hunted him.</p>
<p>She had not knelt before a gilded altar. She had not wasted her breath on the sanctimonious rituals of the Apostle system, those pathetic displays of incense, spoken invocations, and gathered witnesses that served as little more than theater for the desperate.</p>
<p>Those ceremonies were designed for the masses: mortals begging for recognition, seeking the crumbs of divine worthiness while submitting to a god’s absolute authority.</p>
<p>Celestina had no interest in being worthy. She had no desire to be a chosen servant.</p>
<p>She wanted a transaction. And she wanted leverage.</p>
<p>For three years, she had been a ghost in the archives, her mind burning with the fever of obsession as she dissected the divine temporal framework. She had calculated every heartbeat of the cosmos until she found the crack in the armor: the exact hour when Kaeleth’s attention shifted from the glacial management of primary domains to his secondary observation function.</p>
<p>That was the moment the god became a predator, scanning the physical world for temporal anomalies, the jagged, bleeding disruptions in the timeline that demanded his attention.</p>
<p>Reincarnators were those anomalies. They were foreign bodies stitched into the flesh of reality, splinters of other worlds that refused to heal.</p>
<p>To the God of Time, they were wounds in the fabric of existence.</p>
<p>Standing atop the highest spire of the Seam House’s eastern tower, the wind howling like a dying beast around her, Celestina prepared to become a wound herself. Her heart hammered against her ribs, a frantic, rhythmic reminder of her mortality, but her hands remained steady.</p>
<p>She was gambling with a currency most could not comprehend: her own place in causality.</p>
<p>Using the forbidden mechanisms of her Balance Keeper designation, she began to twist her own temporal signature. It was a nauseating, violent process.</p>
<p>Inside her mind, it felt as though her very soul was being shredded, pulled apart by invisible hooks that sought to drag her across dimensions.</p>
<p>’Just a little further,’ she screamed internally, her teeth grinding until they threatened to crack. ’ Make them see me... Make him notice the infection...’</p>
<p>She forced her essence to mimic the jagged, wrong frequency of a reincarnator, a false note played on a cosmic instrument. She held that dissonance, that agonizing lie, until her vision blurred with blood vessels bursting in her eyes.</p>
<p>She pushed through the psychic agony, holding the shape of a foreign element until the universe finally blinked.</p>
<p>Then came the arrival.</p>
<p>Kaeleth did not descend from the heavens in a burst of light. He arrived as a sudden, crushing absence of reality.</p>
<p>The air didn’t just grow cold; it became heavy, saturated with a pressure that felt like being submerged in liquid mercury. The tower beneath her feet didn’t shake; it seemed to shudder in terror, the stone itself recognizing that something existed here that defied the linear progression of seconds and centuries.</p>
<p>The weight was not physical, yet it pressed against her bones with the force of an approaching avalanche, a divine gravity that demanded her knees buckle and her spirit break.</p>
<p>"You are not from another world."</p>
<p>His words did not travel through the air as sound; they reverberated through the marrow of her bones, a temporal vibration that bypassed her ears entirely. It was the voice of an ancient mechanism clicking into place, cold and absolute.</p>
<p>Celestina steadied her breathing, though every nerve in her body was screaming at the proximity of something so fundamentally alien.</p>
<p>"No," she replied, her own voice sounding thin and fragile against the cosmic weight pressing down on her.</p>
<p>"You fabricated the wound," he stated, the accusation carrying no emotion, only the clinical observation of a surgeon examining a tumor. "You made yourself appear as a disruption."</p>
<p>"I did," she admitted, forcing herself to meet the crushing pressure of his presence. "I made myself a target..."</p>
<p>"To get your attention."</p>
<p>Silence descended. It was not merely the absence of noise; it was a temporal vacuum.</p>
<p>Seconds stretched into minutes; minutes felt like centuries of suspended animation where the very concept of ’now’ began to dissolve. Her heart throbbed in her ears, thump-thump, thump-thump, the only clock left ticking in a world that had suddenly gone still.</p>
<p>"You have it," Kaeleth finally spoke, the vibration shifting from accusation to something darker, something almost predatory. "Understand this, mortal: very few have the audacity to deliberately draw my attention and leave this encounter with their sanity or their existence intact."</p>
<p>"Most who seek to matter to me are desperate to be seen to be acknowledged as worthy. But you?" Kaeleth laughed. "You made yourself into a problem... That is... a more interesting approach."</p>
<p>"I did not come here to be seen," Celestina said, her jaw tightening despite the tremor in her hands. "I came to be useful..."</p>
<p>"There is a fundamental difference between those two desires, though most mortals who grovel at your feet confuse them."</p>
<p>"Elaborate," Kaeleth commanded.</p>
<p>The pressure increased, a subtle tightening around her skull, as if his curiosity itself had physical mass. "Tell me what a mortal who has spent three years dissecting my schedule believes to be the distinction."</p>
<p>"Being seen is a plea for recognition," Celestina said, each word measured, each syllable a shield. "It is asking you to notice that I exist and then hoping you remember."</p>
<p>"But being useful? Being useful is offering you a reason to keep noticing long after the first moment has passed."</p>
<p>"Gods forget mortals who exist only as a single pleasantry in an eternal conversation."</p>
<p>"I did not come here for a conversation... I came to offer a solution."</p>
<p>She had rehearsed this for three years. She had lived this moment in her mind ten thousand times.</p>
<p>But as she spoke, the rehearsed script fell away, replaced by something far more dangerous: the raw, unvarnished truth of a woman who had gambled everything on a single throw of the dice.</p>
<p>"You have a problem, Kaeleth," she said, using his name, a transgression that would have shattered a lesser being. "The world is hemorrhaging timeline disruptions..."</p>
<p>"The reincarnator population has been hemorrhaging into this reality for two centuries, and the rate isn’t just increasing; it’s accelerating."</p>
<p>"Your observation function is drowning..."</p>
<p>"You’re spending more time patching holes than managing the flow. And you cannot intervene directly; the divine framework’s operational code binds your hands, preventing you from striking at individual mortals."</p>
<p>"Your assessment is accurate," Kaeleth said. The words felt like grinding tectonic plates.</p>
<p>"How accurate?" Celestina pressed, leaning into the divine pressure, her eyes blazing. "Am I merely touching the surface of your frustration, or have I pierced the core of it?"</p>
<p>"The frustration is deeper than your pathetic records could ever comprehend." Now, there was something new in the vibration, a crack in the divine composure. A tremor of something that sounded dangerously like rage. "Two hundred years ago, I could account for every anomaly in a single observation cycle."</p>
<p>"Now? The cycle cannot complete before new disruptions have already festered behind it."</p>
<p>"I am in the specific sense that matters to a being who defines existence through accounting for falling behind my own domain."</p>
<p>The admission hung between them like a guillotine blade.</p>
<p>"That is a dangerous confession for a god to make to a stranger," Celestina whispered.</p>
<p>"You already suspected it," Kaeleth countered, the pressure suddenly spiking, making her bones creak audibly. "I am not confessing; I am confirming."</p>
<p>"And in our arrangement, confirmation carries a price far higher than suspicion."</p>
<p>Celestina didn’t flinch. "Then let us discuss the price... I have a solution."</p>
<p>"It is not a cure; I am not naive enough to claim I can heal time, but it is a management system."</p>
<p>"For twelve years, I have built an organization dedicated to one purpose: identifying and eliminating reincarnators."</p>
<p>"In those twelve years, we have hunted down and erased forty-three of them." Her mind flashed to the blood, the screaming, the way the bodies of those who didn’t belong seemed to unravel when they died. "The mechanism works... and the blade is sharp..."</p>
<p>"Its only failure is its reach."</p>
<p>"Reach," Kaeleth repeated, the word echoing like a death knell.</p>
<p>"The reincarnator population is not scattered randomly like seeds in the wind," Celestina said, her voice cutting through the divine pressure like a blade through silk. "They concentrate."</p>
<p>"They cluster around nodes of opportunity. Aethelgard is the most dangerous among them."</p>
<p>The God of Time’s presence seemed to ripple at the mention of the city.</p>
<p>"The neutral city on the floating island is a magnet," she continued, stepping closer to the precipice of the tower, staring into the impossible void where Kaeleth’s essence resided. "It offers exactly what a foreign element craves: stability to plant roots and opportunity to exploit."</p>
<p>"Aethelgard has, at any given moment, between twenty and forty active reincarnators festering within its borders."</p>
