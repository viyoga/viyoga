<p>The collective gaze of the room shifted toward Ignivara, a heavy, expectant weight that seemed to demand more than just tactical data. They were looking for a sign of sentiment, a hint of lingering loyalty, or perhaps a warning of a trap.</p>
<p>"I am not asking you to protect him," Ignivara said, her voice cutting through the tension with a sharp, pragmatic edge.</p>
<p>She wasn’t pleading for a life; she was presenting a variable. "I am simply asking you to consider that he might be worth a conversation before the alternative—the violent, inevitable alternative—is taken."</p>
<p>"Do not let a potential asset be slaughtered simply because you were too eager to strike."</p>
<p>A profound silence descended upon the chamber. It was the kind of silence that felt like a held breath, a moment where the fate of a single man in the Thornweald Collective seemed to hang in the balance of their collective will.</p>
<p>"Noted," Apollo said finally. His voice was calm, but his eyes were calculating, already weighing the cost of a conversation against the efficiency of an assassination.</p>
<p>Ignivara held his gaze for a heartbeat longer, searching for a flicker of doubt, then she simply stood.</p>
<p>The meeting began its natural dissolution, the heavy atmosphere fracturing into smaller, hushed clusters of conversation. The group’s edges began to fray as people stood, whispered, and drifted toward the exits, the intense focus of the last few hours breaking into the mundane murmurs of post-meeting debriefs.</p>
<p>Across the expanse of the table, Rex’s eyes remained fixed on Valentina.</p>
<p>For the first time since the meeting had begun, the mask of the weary strategist had slipped, and the administrator had returned.</p>
<p>The poise of the woman who commanded the academy was back in full force—that terrifying, crystalline organizational clarity of a leader who already knew exactly what the next twelve hours required and was mentally ordering the soldiers, the scholars, and the logistics to meet the demand.</p>
<p>She was a machine of pure efficiency, and in this light, she looked formidable.</p>
<p>She caught him watching.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t flinch. He held her gaze for a single, heavy moment, a silent acknowledgment between two people who understood the weight of command, and then he let his eyes drift away with the practiced, unloaded glance of a man who was communicating absolutely nothing whatsoever.</p>
<p>Valentina turned her attention toward the exit, where Ignivara was being escorted by an Academy staff member who had appeared with the silent, practiced timing of a shadow.</p>
<p>"Ignivara," Valentina called out.</p>
<p>The defector stopped and turned, her expression guarded.</p>
<p>"I want to speak with you," Valentina said, her voice carrying a weight that demanded attention. "Tomorrow morning. Privately."</p>
<p>Ignivara studied her, the silence stretching between them.</p>
<p>"About Celestina," Valentina added.</p>
<p>The way she said the name was telling. It was the voice of a woman delivering a sentence of immense cost, a statement made with the precise emotional economy of someone who knew exactly how much a secret was worth and was willing to pay the price to hear it.</p>
<p>Ignivara didn’t blink.</p>
<p>"Yes," she replied. "I will tell you everything. Every fragment of her mind that I managed to grasp."</p>
<p>She paused, her eyes narrowing as a memory surfaced, unbidden and sharp.</p>
<p>"She asked about you," Ignivara said, the revelation landing like a stone in a still pool. "Celestina..."</p>
<p>"During the founding session, when the topic of family connections arose."</p>
<p>"She asked whether the headmaster of the academy knew she existed."</p>
<p>The air in the room seemed to chill. Valentina’s outward expression remained a fortress of composure, but behind the eyes, something shifted: a microscopic fracture in the granite, a flicker of something ancient and perhaps dangerous.</p>
<p>"What did Kregg tell her?" Valentina asked, her voice low, almost dangerous.</p>
<p>"He told her the intelligence suggested you did not," Ignivara said. "She didn’t react and she didn’t argue."</p>
<p>"She simply moved to the next item on the agenda, as if the answer were a mere footnote."</p>
<p>Ignivara leaned in slightly, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial, haunting tone. "But I have thought about her expression in that moment many times in the years since."</p>
<p>"I have replayed it in my mind a thousand times. I still do not know what it meant... but it was not the expression of someone who had received an answer they were satisfied with."</p>
<p>Valentina stood perfectly still, her eyes absorbing the weight of Ignivara’s words as if she were physically pulling the information into her very marrow. The implication hung in the air, a silent, invisible thread connecting the headmaster of the academy to the most dangerous woman in the world.</p>
<p>"Tomorrow morning," Valentina said. It wasn’t a request; it was a command, spoken with a finality that brooked no argument.</p>
<p>"Tomorrow morning, got it..." Ignivara confirmed, her voice a steady anchor in the sudden, swirling tension of the room.</p>
<p>Valentina gave a single, sharp nod, the unmistakable, decisive gesture of the headmaster, and Ignivara turned to depart.</p>
<p>Rex watched her go, his mind working with the cold, terrifying efficiency of a grandmaster surveying a chessboard. He didn’t just hear the conversation; he filed it.</p>
<p>He placed the news of the private meeting into the deep, secure vaults of his mind, alongside the names of enemies and the coordinates of targets. This was a variable that changed everything.</p>
<p>His gaze drifted across the room, scanning the wreckage of the people left behind. He saw Apollo leaning toward Kaelira, his shoulders slumped with the crushing fatigue of a man who had spent the day staring into the sun.</p>
<p>Apollo’s expression was one of profound mental exhaustion, the look of someone who had processed a mountain of data and was finally feeling the gravity of its implications.</p>
<p>Nearby, Morwenna stood like a statue by the window, her silhouette framed against the city’s scarred and broken skyline. She wasn’t looking at the lights; she was looking at the damage, her eyes reflecting the hollow, bitter realization of someone who had spent forty years building a life on a foundation of lies trusting the wrong person for all the right reasons.</p>
<p>He saw the knowledge system holder, hunched over his scrolls and notes, his eyes moving with a flat, mechanical focus. He was no longer a man; he was a processor, converting raw intelligence into the cold, unyielding steel of tactical planning.</p>
<p>Then, his eyes settled on Valentina. She was already moving, speaking in low, rapid tones to an academy staff member.</p>
<p>The administrative machine had reengaged. The grief, the shock, and the revelations were all being pushed into the background, replaced by the ruthless efficiency of a leader who was already ordering the next twelve hours of existence.</p>
<p>Rex felt a flicker of something dark and predatory in his chest. Months ago, he had handed her a name.</p>
<p>She had sent a letter to an unknown recipient, a secret transaction wrapped in the guise of Academy business. That letter sat in a mental category of his own: secrets to be harvested.</p>
<p>He intended to know every word of its contents, every shadow it cast, but he knew the value of patience.</p>
<p>’Not tonight,’ he thought.</p>
<p>Tonight was a masterpiece of his own design. This entire day had been a controlled fracture, a crack he had induced, a break he had managed, and a wound he had now sealed with the perfect architecture of trust and dependency.</p>
<p>He had broken them open, and in the breaking, he had made them rely on him. He had created the very vacuum that his next phase of operations would fill.</p>
<p>He looked at the room one last time, feeling the grim, cold satisfaction of a craftsman looking at a machine that was finally, beautifully, functioning exactly as intended. The pieces were in place. The trap was set.</p>
<p>He turned his gaze to Elizabeth. His voice was low, a sharp command that cut through the lingering murmurs of the room.</p>
<p>"Our job here is done," he said. "Let’s go home."</p>
<p>Elizabeth looked around the room, her eyes catching the exhaustion of Apollo, the bitterness of Morwenna, and the iron resolve of Valentina. She took a breath, her expression hardening into something unbreakable.</p>
<p>"Okay," she said.</p>
