<p>The chamber descended into a heavy, suffocating stillness, broken only by the distant, rhythmic roar of the purge echoing from the city below. Rex stood motionless in the center of the wreckage, the single drop of blood on his fingertip a vivid, crimson stain against the cold metal of his mask.</p>
<p>The displaced jawplate of his helm sat slightly askew, a jagged testament to the violence he had just endured, but his posture remained unnervingly stable. He wasn’t just standing; he was calculating.</p>
<p>The data from the exchange was already being integrated. Cassandra and Gorvasha had achieved the impossible: they had produced temporal ambiguity within his foresight.</p>
<p>They hadn’t just countered his speed or matched his raw power; they had struck at the very foundation of his predictive framework. They had introduced a variable that made his foresight flicker like a dying candle.</p>
<p>It was a revelation. Predictive frameworks could be refined; they could be rebuilt, but they were never truly infallible.</p>
<p>He felt the weight of the final tool in his arsenal, the one he had kept tucked away, shielded by layers of strategic intent. To deploy it was to change the fundamental nature of the game.</p>
<p>It was a tool that belonged to a different identity, a different era of his existence, and using it meant revealing a version of himself that the Underlayer was not prepared to face. He had been weighing the cost of this revelation since his first step into the depths, but the brutal honesty of the last seven minutes had provided the answer.</p>
<p>The Underlayer could no longer afford the luxury of a partial truth. If he was to build what he intended, the inhabitants of these lower chambers needed to understand the sheer, terrifying scale of the force that had arrived in their midst.</p>
<p>Partial demonstrations only built half-hearted loyalty; he needed a goddamn epiphany.</p>
<p>Across the debris-strewn floor, Cassandra had not lowered her sword. Her knuckles were white around the hilt, her eyes locked onto him with a predatory, evaluating intensity.</p>
<p>She was a veteran of a thousand skirmishes, and she knew the weight of a man’s silence. She could feel the atmospheric pressure shifting, sensing that the man standing before her was no longer merely recovering, but transforming.</p>
<p>"You’re thinking about something," she said, her voice cutting through the gloom, sharp and wary.</p>
<p>"I’m deciding something," Rex replied, his voice echoing with a new, chilling resonance.</p>
<p>"There’s a difference," she countered, her stance tightening.</p>
<p>"Thinking is incomplete," Rex said, stepping forward, the movement so deliberate it felt like a tectonic shift. "Deciding is the end of thinking."</p>
<p>He fixed his gaze on her, his eyes burning behind the gap in his mask. "I’ve been carrying a weight since before I arrived in the Underlayer tonight, something I haven’t used..."</p>
<p>"The question was whether tonight was the moment to finally let it loose."</p>
<p>"And?" she pressed, her heart hammering against her ribs.</p>
<p>"The answer is yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>The air in the room seemed to vanish. Cassandra stared at him, her expression hardening into the grim satisfaction of someone who had just seen their darkest suspicions confirmed.</p>
<p>The tension was a physical thing, a coiled spring ready to snap and shatter the very foundations of the room.</p>
<p>"You held back something else," she said, her voice dropping to a whisper of pure realization. "The whole fight... it was a mask within a mask."</p>
<p>"I’ve been holding back this specifically," Rex said, his voice gaining a terrifying, melodic edge. "The rest of what you saw tonight was the complete version of the man you know."</p>
<p>"But this? Oh~! Better prepare your asses because this is something else entirely."</p>
<p>"I’ve been saving it to wreck havoc in Aethelgard, but I’m going to test it here..."</p>
<p>On the floor, Gorvasha began to rise. Her movements were heavy and labored, the grit of the stone scraping against her skin as she braced herself with a trembling hand.</p>
<p>Her lineage allowed her to process the massive trauma of the exchange at a supernatural rate, but even she could feel the sudden, violent shift in the room’s energy. She looked up at him, her eyes wide with a mixture of exhaustion and primal dread.</p>
<p>"What... is it?" she demanded, her voice a low, guttural rasp that demanded an answer before the world changed forever.</p>
<p>Rex flexed both hands, and the very air in the chamber seemed to scream in anticipation.</p>
<p>’Time to use those fucking gauntlets... and finally... I get the chance to use them now.’</p>
<p>The gauntlets did not manifest as armor being donned or equipment being summoned; they were far more intimate, far more terrifying. They materialized as an eruption of pure, integrated authority.</p>
<p>From the microscopic pores of his skin, a dense, compressed stone-mineral material began to surge outward, flowing from the molecular level like a liquid mountain. It was the signature of the Earth Sovereign’s long-term integration, a geological metamorphosis that saw the minerals of the world rising through his flesh to redefine his anatomy.</p>
<p>The substance climbed from his knuckles to his mid-forearms, weaving itself into a seamless, obsidian-dark extension of his own skeletal structure. This was not a covering; it was a structural evolution.</p>
<p>It was as if his hands had finally decided to stop pretending to be flesh and bone and had instead embraced the eternal permanence of the earth itself.</p>
<p>The moment the transformation was complete, the atmosphere in the room underwent a violent, fundamental shift. The chamber did not feel heavier, but it felt infinitely more present.</p>
<p>The stone floor, the crumbling walls, the jagged ceiling—everything groaned in a low, subsonic frequency. The geological substrate of the underlayer was responding to a divine-tier claim, a passive and involuntary acknowledgment of its true master.</p>
<p>Then, Rex let out a roar.</p>
<p>"HRAAAAGGGGHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!!!"</p>
<p>It was not a scream of pain, but a primal, earth-shaking release of pure, unadulterated power. He threw his head back, his lungs expanding to their absolute limit as he channeled the tectonic fury surging through his veins.</p>
<p>The sound was a thunderclap made manifest, a sonic boom that rattled the teeth of everyone in the room and sent fresh cracks spiderwebbing across the ceiling. As he screamed, the energy radiating from him became visible: a shimmering, golden brown aura of pressurized earth that pulsed in sync with his heartbeat, forcing the very dust in the air to dance in violent, geometric patterns.</p>
<p>Gorvasha felt the impact before she could even process the sight.</p>
<p>Her orc lineage carried a deep, ancestral memory of the earth’s wrath, a genetic instinct for the weight of the world. Her blood sang a song of ancient terror, a recognition that bypassed her rational mind and struck directly at the primal core of her soul.</p>
<p>Ten thousand years of underground warfare had taught her how to fight the earth, but she had never felt the earth obey a man like him.</p>
<p>She stared at his hands, her breath catching in a throat tight with sudden, instinctive dread.</p>
<p>"What... what are those?" she managed to choke out, her voice barely a whisper against the receding echo of his roar.</p>
<p>"A test," Rex replied.</p>
<p>The smugness had returned, but it was no longer the playful arrogance of a man playing a game; it was the terrifying confidence of a god conducting an experiment. "I haven’t used them at full output since the integration was finalized."</p>
<p>He turned his gaze toward her, his eyes burning with a light that seemed to originate from the core of the planet itself. He looked at her with the clinical, detached attention of a master craftsman deciding whether a piece of raw ore was worthy of being struck by a hammer.</p>
<p>"You’ll do," he said, his voice a low, tectonic rumble. "Let’s see if you can survive the calibration."</p>
<p>Gorvasha’s eyes changed. "I don’t want to be in a denial state anymore, but... that’s the gauntlets where the God of Earth once used."</p>
<p>"If he has that... it means the Earth Sovereign’s power runs deep inside his blood."</p>
<p>Cassandra took a step back. "What...? So... the one that his Undead Army took was the real thing...?"</p>
<p>What Cassandra did right now was not a retreat. It was the specific repositioning of someone who had been in enough significant engagements to understand when an environment was about to change in a way that required different geometry, and who had the professional discipline to move rather than wait for the change to make the decision for her.</p>
<p>"How long have you had those," Gorvasha asked.</p>
<p>"I steal it from the island assessment where Rex Rexilion and both his woman almost trying to get this divine power," Rex said. "I say... months, but that’s not important for both of you."</p>
<p>"You’ve been in this kingdom for several hours causing a lot of because," she said, "And now... with those on your hands.</p>
<p>"Why haven’t you used them?"</p>
<p>"I used it already since the moment I lay my hands on it, but not in the active mode right now where the gauntlets are visible," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Then... what’s the passive mode...?" she said.</p>
<p>"The fault-line mapping," Rex said. "The substrate awareness..."</p>
<p>"The sealed exits before the speech." He looked at her steadily. "Every structural decision tonight came from the passive read."</p>
<p>Cassandra absorbed this.</p>
<p>"And the active mode...?" she said.</p>
