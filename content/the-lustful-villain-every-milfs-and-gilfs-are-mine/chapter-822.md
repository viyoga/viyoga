<p>Morwenna didn’t look away. She leaned into the space between them, her eyes dissecting him with the cold, unblinking intensity of a surgeon.</p>
<p>"You are very precise," she said, her voice a low, rhythmic hum of suspicion. "With your words... With the way you frame every single truth you offer us."</p>
<p>"I strive for accuracy," Rex replied, his tone as level as a horizon line.</p>
<p>"Accuracy is not the same as precision," Morwenna countered, her lip curling in a microscopic display of skepticism. "Accuracy is merely being correct."</p>
<p>"Precision is about sharpness... It is about the calculated intent to cut..."</p>
<p>"You are consistently both, Rex. It is a pattern of terrifying efficiency that has been visible to me since the canyon. I’ve heard about you."</p>
<p>"You don’t just tell the truth; you weaponize it."</p>
<p>The room fell into a heavy, suffocating silence. It was the kind of stillness that occurs when a predator has stopped moving and the prey is waiting to see if the next sound will be a growl or a strike.</p>
<p>The other members of the council sat frozen, absorbing the weight of her accusation, sensing that the air in the room had suddenly become electrically charged.</p>
<p>Rex held her gaze. He didn’t blink or shift his weight.</p>
<p>He maintained that unnerving, steady calm, the composure of a perfectly comfortable man being scrutinized as if he were a specimen under a microscope, waiting to see if the observer would find a flaw in his armor.</p>
<p>"I appreciate the observation," he said simply.</p>
<p>"It was not a compliment," she snapped.</p>
<p>"I know," Rex replied, his voice devoid of any defensive heat. "I received it as information."</p>
<p>Morwenna let the exchange hang in the air, letting the tension simmer until it was almost unbearable. She wasn’t finished; she was merely reloading.</p>
<p>"The Underlayer," she said, pivoting with sudden, violent grace.</p>
<p>Rex waited, his silence a silent challenge.</p>
<p>"Your preliminary report describes a level of operational intelligence regarding the Underlayer’s internal architecture that borders on the impossible," Morwenna continued, her voice gaining a predatory momentum. "Lord Xerollion’s specific governance protocols."</p>
<p>"The exact nature of the military restructuring..."</p>
<p>"The precise status of the second stratum contact situation."</p>
<p>"You described these things with the intimate, granular detail of someone who hadn’t just read about them but had stood in the heart of them during the engagement."</p>
<p>"I have been synthesizing the academy’s compiled intelligence for six months," Rex said, his voice a calm countercurrent to her rising tide. "Forty years of intelligence operations, patterns of movement, and structural data."</p>
<p>"The changes in operations you mentioned, Xerollion’s rise, and the shift of forces were all understandable when you compared those historical patterns with the information Ignivara shared during our talk in the tree line."</p>
<p>"What Ignivara provided," Morwenna repeated, the words tasting like ash.</p>
<p>"She has lived in the Underlayer," Rex stated, a fact as simple as gravity. "She left four months ago."</p>
<p>"She was willing to trade her firsthand observations for the terms of survival outlined in my report."</p>
<p>"And the Legion operatives," Morwenna pressed, her eyes flashing. "Ignivara and the void user..."</p>
<p>"They didn’t just encounter you, Rex... They recognized you."</p>
<p>"Ignivara knew my name," Rex said, his voice remaining unshakeable. "She had been briefed on the man who eliminated Kregg in the canyon."</p>
<p>"It was a matter of intelligence, nothing more."</p>
<p>"She didn’t just know your name," Morwenna shot back, her voice sharpening into a blade. "She recognized your presence before she even spoke."</p>
<p>"The moment her eyes landed on you, her entire posture shifted..."</p>
<p>"It was the instinctive reaction of a soldier identifying a high-tier threat they had been specifically warned to avoid at all costs."</p>
<p>"She had been briefed on my physical description," Rex countered, his eyes narrowing slightly. "Kregg’s final, dying transmission would have included a visual profile."</p>
<p>"Or," Morwenna said, and her voice took on a flat, terrifyingly final quality, the sound of a trap snapping shut.</p>
<p>She had arrived at the precipice of her argument, and she had no intention of stepping back. "Or, she recognized you because she had encountered you before."</p>
<p>"Not in a briefing, and not in a report..."</p>
<p>"She recognized you because you have been in the Underlayer... in a capacity that explains the impossible detail of your intelligence and the bone-shattering similarity between your power and the power of Tremor."</p>
<p>The silence that followed was not merely a lack of sound; it was a physical, suffocating weight. It was a pressure that seemed to compress the very oxygen in the room, making every breath feel labored, as if the atmosphere itself were thickening with the gravity of Morwenna’s accusation.</p>
<p>Apollo’s gaze was a physical force, fixed on Rex with the predatory intensity of a wolf weighing the strength of a rival before the kill.</p>
<p>Beside him, Aurelia’s eyes were equally unyielding, searching Rex’s face for the microscopic tremor of a lie, the slight dilation of a pupil, or the frantic pulse in a neck—the telltale signs of a man caught in a web of his own making.</p>
<p>Even Elizabeth, the pillar of composure, had averted her eyes, staring down at the dark, polished obsidian of the table as if the ancient patterns in the stone could provide the answers Rex was so calculatedly withholding.</p>
<p>Rex did not flinch. He did not shift.</p>
<p>He met Morwenna’s gaze with the terrifying, clinical calm of a man who had just been presented with a complex mathematical equation rather than a lethal insult. To him, this was not a threat to his honor; it was a problem to be solved.</p>
<p>"What you are describing," Rex said, his voice cutting through the heavy tension like a cold, surgical blade, "is a tripartite accusation."</p>
<p>He didn’t wait for her to confirm. He laid the cards on the table with brutal efficiency.</p>
<p>"You are suggesting that I am Tremor. Or that I am operating in direct, clandestine coordination with him..."</p>
<p>"Or maybe," he paused, his eyes darkening, "there exists a fundamental, undisclosed connection between me and the entity that slaughtered fifty percent of this network’s forces in a single morning."</p>
<p>"Yes," Morwenna said, her voice a low, dangerous rasp that vibrated in the quiet room. "That is exactly what the data is screaming at us."</p>
<p>"It is screaming it in the face of anyone with eyes to see."</p>
<p>"Then let us be precise," Rex countered, leaning forward.</p>
<p>The movement was subtle, but it commanded the room, forcing the attention of every high-ranking official present to lock onto him. "I will not respond to this as though it were an unreasonable conclusion."</p>
<p>"To do so would be a lie, and a lie would be a waste of our collective time."</p>
<p>"The pattern you have identified is real..."</p>
<p>"The questions you are generating are entirely legitimate..."</p>
<p>"If I were sitting in your chair, Morwenna, looking at this same data, I would reach the exact same conclusions."</p>
<p>The room fell silent again, but the texture of the quiet had shifted. It was no longer the tension of an interrogation; it was the stunned, uneasy stillness of people who had expected a man to defend his honor with passion and instead found a man defending the cold, hard logic of his own suspicion.</p>
<p>"What I am telling you," Rex continued, his voice dropping into a tone of absolute, unshakeable gravity, "is that the explanation I have provided is the accurate one."</p>
<p>"It may not be the complete one, because I do not provide exhaustive accounts of my own capability development to anyone, including the people at this table."</p>
<p>"That is not a deception; it is simply the way I operate."</p>
<p>He paused, letting the weight of that admission settle like silt in a river.</p>
<p>"But it is accurate... The partial integration is a physical reality."</p>
<p>"The archive access is a documented fact, and the timeline is ironclad..."</p>
<p>"What I demonstrated in the plaza was the raw, violent output of a partial integration of a divine-tier geological designation, amplified by compound elemental mastery and a working set that has been honing its lethality far longer than the engagement suggested."</p>
<p>Morwenna didn’t blink. She stared at him, her eyes searching for the crack in the monolith, the single flaw in the stone.</p>
<p>"You are telling me," she said, her voice dripping with a skepticism so thick it was almost tangible, "that the thing I am observing the terrifying, bone-shattering, landscape-altering similarity in power with is merely a coincidence of capability class?"</p>
<p>"That you are not him, but simply a mirror reflecting him?"</p>
<p>"I am telling you that two entities with access to the same designation tier and the same elemental compound capacity are mathematically destined to produce similar outputs," Rex said, his logic as relentless and crushing as a landslide. "The Earth Sovereign designation is the source."</p>
<p>"The Earthen Apostle designation is its derivative, and the outputs are similar because the source material is identical."</p>
<p>"Two entities," Morwenna repeated, her voice rising with a sharp, incredulous edge that cracked the silence. "Two entities with access to the same extremely rare, historically singular, and nearly mythic designation tier?"</p>
<p>"The designation is transferable through prolonged contact with the mineral divine material," Rex said, his voice remaining a calm, steady anchor amidst her storm. "That material sat in the sealed chamber at Sable Island for at least two hundred years."</p>
<p>"The methodology of transfer is documented in the very archives you trust."</p>
<p>"There is no architectural reason why two practitioners, in the same era, could not have acquired a partial or full integration independently."</p>
<p>Morwenna leaned back, her eyes narrowing until they were mere slits of predatory intent. She looked like a hunter who had cornered a beast and was deciding whether to strike or to study it.</p>
<p>"No architectural reason," she conceded, her voice a low, chilling whisper. "Only a probabilistic one."</p>
<p>"The odds of such an event being a coincidence are infinitesimally small, Rex."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex replied, his gaze unwavering, his expression as unreadable and eternal as the deep stone he commanded. "Only that."</p>
