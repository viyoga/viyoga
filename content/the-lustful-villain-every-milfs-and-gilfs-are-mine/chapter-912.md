<p>Rex sank onto the wooden bench along the hall’s side wall, the muscles in his legs protesting the sudden cessation of movement. Diana didn’t hesitate; she knelt between his knees, claiming the space beside him with the casual certainty of someone who belonged there.</p>
<p>She began working the towel over his shoulders and neck, her movements unhurried and deliberate. This wasn’t the efficient wiping of sweat from a soldier; it was the meticulous care of a woman tending to the man she loved.</p>
<p>Her hands moved with a gentleness that bypassed all tactical training, a tenderness born from the raw honesty of that first night when they had stopped pretending and admitted exactly what they wanted from each other.</p>
<p>"You worked harder than you needed to today," she murmured.</p>
<p>Her thumbs found the knots of tension that had calcified along his traps during those final, punishing drills. She pressed in, working the muscle loose with a precision that was both therapeutic and intimate.</p>
<p>"You didn’t have to run that final conditioning set... Nobody was watching by then. Even the guards had gone."</p>
<p>"I was watching," Rex said, his voice gravelly from exertion. "That was reason enough."</p>
<p>Diana’s hands faltered for a heartbeat, that infinitesimal pause that signaled she was absorbing the weight behind his words. She didn’t speak, but the way she resumed her work, pressing deeper into his shoulders with a renewed firmness, spoke volumes.</p>
<p>She moved down his spine, each pass of the towel a caress, each press of her palms a silent acknowledgment of the burden he carried.</p>
<p>"You’re getting good at this," Rex noted, closing his eyes as the relief began to spread through his overheated frame.</p>
<p>"I’ve had excellent motivation to practice," Diana replied, a trace of amusement softening her tone. "You come home looking like you’ve gone through a meat grinder more often than either of us would ever admit aloud."</p>
<p>Silence settled between them as she continued, her hands tracing the rugged lines of his shoulders before moving down to his forearms, wiping away the salt and grime.</p>
<p>In this moment, Rex allowed himself something he granted almost no one else: he allowed the performance to drop. The director, the strategist, the weapon—all of it receded, leaving only the man beneath.</p>
<p>He let himself simply exist in her presence, anchored by the steady rhythm of her breathing and the heat of her touch.</p>
<p>When she finally finished, she didn’t rise. She remained kneeling between his legs, looking up at him.</p>
<p>Her face was flushed, her eyes bright with a quiet, satisfied hunger that mirrored his own. She reached up, her palm splaying across his damp chest where his heart still hammered against his ribs, and pulled him down into a kiss.</p>
<p>It was a slow, unhurried, and devastatingly honest kiss that tasted of sweat and shared secrets. Rex met her halfway, his hands finding her waist, giving himself to her with the kind of vulnerability that only existed in the spaces where they were truly alone.</p>
<p>The moment was absolute. Until it wasn’t.</p>
<p>Near the far entrance of the hall, where the shadows pooled thickest, a figure froze.</p>
<p>Theo had returned. It was a genuine errand; he needed the training log Aurelia kept on the shelf by the door, a mundane administrative task that fell to him as the heir whether he desired it or not.</p>
<p>He had expected an empty hall, a place of echoing stone and fading echoes. He had not expected to walk into the raw, unguarded aftermath of something so private, something so achingly obvious.</p>
<p>From his position in the doorway, he saw them: the way Diana leaned into Rex, the way Rex’s fingers curled possessively against her skin, the way they seemed to form a single, impenetrable unit of heat and devotion.</p>
<p>The sight hit him harder than any physical blow, a visceral ache that settled in his chest like broken glass.</p>
<p>He should have turned around immediately. He should have retreated into the darkness and pretended he had seen nothing.</p>
<p>But he remained motionless, trapped in the doorway, a ghost watching the living claim a happiness he could never touch.</p>
<p>Theo remained frozen in the doorway, a silent voyeur to a reality he could no longer claim. He stood there for several heartbeats longer than any sane man would, long enough to see the way Diana’s fingers curled possessively against Rex’s damp skin, long enough to see the unguarded softness in her jaw that he had never once been able to provoke.</p>
<p>Watching them was like watching a language he had forgotten how to speak being spoken fluently by two strangers. There was an ease between them, a seamless synchronization of breath and touch that had never existed between him and Diana, not even during those feverish summers when they had been inseparable.</p>
<p>Even then, there had always been a barrier, an unspoken distance that neither had dared to bridge. Now, seeing that barrier obliterated by Rex, Theo felt something in his chest tighten with the excruciating ache of witnessing something you desired belong completely and irrevocably to someone else.</p>
<p>’It isn’t fair,’ a small, venomous part of his mind hissed.</p>
<p>It was the same furious, wounded voice that had spoken to him years ago on the rain-slicked streets when he had thrown a desperate punch he couldn’t back up, a punch he was still paying for in blood and reputation.</p>
<p>It wasn’t fair that Rex had simply walked into this house and claimed the territory Theo had spent half his life trying to map. Rex hadn’t fought for it; he had simply taken what Theo had spent years failing to build.</p>
<p>But as he watched Diana, really watched her, the fury began to curdle into something more painful: honesty. He saw the way her eyes closed when Rex kissed her, a surrender that wasn’t weakness but a profound choice.</p>
<p>He saw the genuine, unforced light in her expression. A quieter, more devastating part of him understood the truth that his pride refused to acknowledge: fairness had nothing to do with this.</p>
<p>Diana was not a trophy to be seized or a territory to be conquered. She had never been his to lose in the first place.</p>
<p>He had spent years operating under the quiet, arrogant assumption that she was simply waiting for him to become the man she needed, never realizing she had already found him in someone else.</p>
<p>The humiliation burned in his throat like bile, but he forced himself to move.</p>
<p>With movements practiced and precise, he stepped toward the shelf. His fingers brushed the leather binding of the training log, retrieving it without making so much as a whisper of sound against the wood.</p>
<p>He clutched it to his chest like a shield, a pathetic substitute for the connection he craved.</p>
<p>He turned and retreated into the shadows of the corridor, walking back the way he had come. He moved like a ghost, careful not to let his boots scrape or his breathing falter.</p>
<p>Behind him, in the warm glow of the training hall, Rex and Diana remained locked in their own private world, oblivious to his presence and pain.</p>
<p>Neither of them noticed him leave. Neither of them looked up. And as the darkness of the hallway swallowed him whole, Theo realized that their indifference was the most brutal answer of all.</p>
<p>...</p>
<p>The night did not bring relief; it only brought silence, and in the silence, the echoes were louder.</p>
<p>Theo lay flat on his back in the darkness of his chambers, the moonlight filtering through the high windows in cold, silver ribs across the ceiling. His room, designed with the austere elegance of a Nightwing heir, felt less like a sanctuary and more like a gilded cage.</p>
<p>He stared upward at the vaulted rooftop, his eyes tracing the familiar patterns of stone and shadow, but his mind was leagues away, trapped in the memory of the training hall.</p>
<p>Every time he closed his eyes, he saw them. He saw the way Diana had looked at Rex, not with the guarded respect she gave the guards or the polite distance she gave him, but with a raw, terrifying vulnerability.</p>
<p>That look had been a blade, twisting in the wound of his own inadequacy.</p>
<p>’I was too late,’ he thought, the realization settling in his gut like poisoned lead. ’I was always too late...’</p>
<p>He replayed the years like a broken film reel. He remembered the moments he had mistaken for connection, the times he had stood near her, offering nothing but the hollow expectations of his station.</p>
<p>He remembered how he had been so consumed by his own training, his own rise to power, and his own desperate need to prove he was worthy of the House that he had failed to see the girl standing right beside him.</p>
<p>He had been so insensitive, so utterly blind to the quiet ways she had reached for him, only to have him recoil into duty or retreat into arrogance. He had assumed she would always be there, a constant, a fixed point in his orbit, never imagining that even the most patient flame eventually burns out when left in the cold.</p>
<p>A memory surfaced, unbidden and cruel. They were fourteen.</p>
