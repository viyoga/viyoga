<p>He straightened his shoulders, meeting the god’s hollow gaze with a defiance born of absolute desperation. If he were to be a weapon, he would at least know the target.</p>
<p>"If you’re going to use me, tell me what you want."</p>
<p>"I want Rex Rexilion addressed," Kaeleth stated. "He has become a disruption in the timeline of this world, a cancer that has exceeded the threshold that the timeline’s natural self-correction mechanisms can manage."</p>
<p>"He is an anomaly that refuses to be smoothed away."</p>
<p>"You can’t kill him yourself?" Theo asked, knowing the answer but needing to hear it.</p>
<p>"I cannot touch him directly," Kaeleth explained. "He has a patron deity."</p>
<p>"That divine protection creates a barrier I cannot breach without violating the operational code that governs divine activity in the mortal sphere."</p>
<p>"To interfere directly would trigger a celestial conflict that would unravel the very fabric I seek to preserve."</p>
<p>"So you need a proxy," Theo said, the pieces clicking together with brutal clarity.</p>
<p>"I require an Apostle," Kaeleth said. "Someone who carries the temporal domain’s authority through the mortal-divine designation relationship."</p>
<p>"Someone who can act in the physical world with a capability set that transcends mortal limitations."</p>
<p>Theo’s blood ran cold. "And that capability set... it’s designed to address Rex Rexilion."</p>
<p>"Specifically, yes," Kaeleth said, and for the first time, the symbols around him flared with a violent, hungry intensity. "The Apostle designation grants the ability to deliver strikes that operate at the soul’s temporal anchor rather than at the biological level."</p>
<p>"Rex Rexilion’s biological healing is near limitless; he can regenerate flesh and bone as if they were water."</p>
<p>"But his soul-level defenses... that is a different question entirely."</p>
<p>Theo stood motionless, the implications of Kaeleth’s words sinking into him like molten lead. The air in the amber void felt heavier now, charged with the static of a coming storm.</p>
<p>The abstract concepts of ’Apostles’ and ’temporal anchors’ were stripped away, leaving only the raw, ugly truth beneath.</p>
<p>"You want me to kill him," Theo said.</p>
<p>His voice was flat, devoid of inflection. He wasn’t asking for confirmation; he was stating a fact he had already calculated.</p>
<p>"I want you to be the instrument through which the disruption is corrected," Kaeleth replied.</p>
<p>The God of Time spoke with the detached clinical interest of a surgeon discussing a scalpel.</p>
<p>"That is the same thing," Theo countered, his eyes narrowing. "The instrument exists to cut."</p>
<p>"The correction requires his death."</p>
<p>"Yes," Kaeleth said. The agreement was simple, terrifyingly honest.</p>
<p>Theo turned his gaze back to the temporal register. His eyes burned as he studied the two signatures again.</p>
<p>Rex’s signature was a roaring inferno of causality, a sun that refused to set, consuming everything in its gravitational pull. And then there was Theo’s—a thin, wavering thread, a ghost of a timeline that seemed to be fraying at the edges.</p>
<p>The disparity between them was not just a gap in power; it was a fundamental violation of cosmic balance.</p>
<p>’He is the anomaly,’ Theo thought, his mind working with desperate, cold precision. ’And I am the wound that will cauterize him.’</p>
<p>"What do I get?" Theo asked.</p>
<p>The question wasn’t born of greed. It wasn’t even calculated. It was the hollow, direct demand of a man who had already stepped off the cliff and was simply asking what kind of parachute he would be given before he hit the ground.</p>
<p>He had already decided to say yes; now he wanted to know the price of the weapon he was becoming.</p>
<p>"The temporal domain’s designation," Kaeleth said, the amber symbols around him beginning to pulse in rhythm with Theo’s own heartbeat. "Everything that entails."</p>
<p>"You will no longer be a prisoner of the present."</p>
<p>"You will gain perception of the timeline as it actually is raw, unfiltered, and absolute rather than the distorted illusion perceived by those trapped inside its flow."</p>
<p>The symbols flared brighter, casting jagged shadows across Theo’s face.</p>
<p>"You will gain control over the local scale of causality," Kaeleth continued, his voice growing more resonant, more commanding. "Temporal acceleration and deceleration."</p>
<p>"You will learn to treat time not as an immutable constraint that binds you, but as a medium, a fabric you can grasp, fold, and reshape to your will."</p>
<p>The god paused, letting the magnitude of the power sink in. The silence was deafening.</p>
<p>"And," Kaeleth added, the last words carrying a weight that made the very void tremble, "you will gain the capacity to stop time entirely."</p>
<p>Theo’s breath hitched.</p>
<p>"Not permanently," Kaeleth clarified quickly, sensing the instinctive recoil. "Not in a way that would unravel reality."</p>
<p>"But at durations and scales that the designation’s development supports."</p>
<p>"Initially, you will achieve brief stutters and fractured moments of stillness within a limited radius around yourself."</p>
<p>"But as the designation develops, as you prove your utility... the periods will lengthen."</p>
<p>"The radius will widen until the world becomes nothing more than a frozen gallery of statues at your command."</p>
<p>Theo didn’t respond immediately. He couldn’t.</p>
<p>His mind was already racing through the possibilities: the tactical advantage of a frozen moment, the ability to strike a killing blow while the target’s heart was suspended between beats, and the sheer, terrifying potential to dismantle Rex Rexilion while he was trapped in a single, eternal instant.</p>
<p>The power offered was godlike. It was the power to rewrite the rules of the game that had broken him.</p>
<p>Slowly, Theo turned his head away from the God, looking one last time at his own timeline.</p>
<p>He looked at that pathetic, flickering line that ’nothing’ had been waiting for him at the cliff’s edge. He saw the darkness, the inevitable collapse, the hollow emptiness of a life that had run out of meaning.</p>
<p>He looked at the void where his future should have been, and then he looked back at the burning fire of Rex’s signature.</p>
<p>’Better to die as a god’s weapon,’ Theo thought, a dark, vengeful resolve hardening in his chest like cooling steel, ’than to fade away as a footnote in his legend.’</p>
<p>"And after?" Theo asked, his voice cutting through the amber hum of the void like a blade through silk. "After Rex Rexilion is nothing but a memory etched into a corrected timeline."</p>
<p>"What then? What becomes of the weapon once the war is won?"</p>
<p>Kaeleth turned his attention fully to him. The God’s voice shifted, taking on a quality that Theo’s temporal awareness recognized as something terrifyingly rare: sincerity.</p>
<p>It was the measured cadence of a being making a vow, a promise that carried the weight of cosmic inevitability.</p>
<p>"Then," Kaeleth said, "the timeline corrects itself.</p>
<p>"The wound is cauterized... The world stabilizes, returning to its natural rhythm, free from the gravitational distortion Rex Rexilion has imposed upon it."</p>
<p>"And you... you will retain the temporal domain’s designation permanently."</p>
<p>"You will remain the established Apostle of Time."</p>
<p>"For how long?" Theo pressed, his mind already constructing the terrifying architecture of such an existence. "How long does this power last? How long am I tied to you?"</p>
<p>"The designation’s duration is woven into the fabric of your own causality," Kaeleth replied. "It is tied to the holder’s life."</p>
<p>"As long as you draw breath, the authority of Time remains yours to wield."</p>
<p>Theo closed his eyes for a moment, feeling the immense pressure of the offer. "And what do I do with it? After?"</p>
<p>"When Rex is dead, when the world has settled into its new equilibrium, and I am left standing there with this... this godlike perception."</p>
<p>"When I can see the seconds as physical barriers and the centuries as mere layers of dust."</p>
<p>"What becomes of a man who can perceive time as a medium but has no reason to move through it?"</p>
<p>"What you want," Kaeleth said simply. "You will be the architect of your own eternity."</p>
<p>Theo opened his eyes and looked.</p>
<p>He looked at his own trajectory that flat, dead line that had promised nothing but oblivion. He looked at Rex’s signature, that bloated, cancerous explosion of power that threatened to drown reality.</p>
<p>And then he looked at the cliff he had already stepped off. There was no going back; there was no climbing up. The fall had already begun.</p>
<p>His mind became a storm of fragmented memories, each one a sharp pang of what had been stolen.</p>
<p>He remembered his mother’s voice the way she spoke Rex’s name with that terrible, reverent cadence, the voice of someone who had already decided that Rex was the correct answer to every question the world asked.</p>
<p>The betrayal wasn’t just in the loss; it was in the acceptance.</p>
<p>He remembered Diana. He could still see her standing on the training floor, her eyes fixed on Rex as he demonstrated some impossible feat of strength or skill.</p>
<p>He remembered the specific quality of her expression, that mixture of awe and something darker, something that looked dangerously like devotion.</p>
<p>He thought of Iris in the quiet, blue light of the early morning training sessions, the shared glances that meant nothing now. He thought of Elaris’s warmth, a heat that now felt like a fading ember from a house that had already burned down.</p>
<p>He thought of the household staff, the way their deference had shifted, their eyes sliding past him to acknowledge the new hierarchy that Rex had constructed with terrifying ease.</p>
