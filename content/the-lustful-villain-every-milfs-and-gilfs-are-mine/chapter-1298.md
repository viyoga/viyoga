<p>They stood amidst the apocalyptic ruin of the garden, separated by ten yards of scorched earth and fused glass. Both were panting, their breaths coming in wet, ragged gasps that sounded like tearing silk.</p>
<p>The once pristine pond was nothing more than a dry, cracked crater of steaming mud. The ancient oaks that had shaded the Academy for centuries had been stripped to blackened, skeletal stumps, their bark vaporized by the elemental clash.</p>
<p>Helena was a ghost of herself. Her arms hung limp at her sides, fingers twitching spasmodically from magical overload.</p>
<p>Her wings, those magnificent instruments of wind, were gone, leaving only raw, red welts along her shoulder blades where the mana had burned away. Her face was a slick mask of sweat, soot, and tears that carved pale rivers through the grime.</p>
<p>She had nothing left.</p>
<p>Not a drop of energy, not a shred of conviction.</p>
<p>Her last great spell had been a scream into the void, and the void had screamed back.</p>
<p>Across from her, Rex was a vision of terrifying resilience. Blood matted his hair, dripping down his neck to mingle with the pink, raw tissue of his regenerating face.</p>
<p>His chest rose and fell with genuine, human effort—a rare sight. For one long, suspended heartbeat, the world stopped.</p>
<p>The distant screams of the city, the settling of rubble, and the crackle of dying embers—all of it faded into a deafening silence.</p>
<p>Her eyes met his. And in that moment of terrible clarity, the truth struck her harder than any spell.</p>
<p>It came to her slowly, a cold realization dawning in the center of her chest as she surveyed the wreckage.</p>
<p>In all the fury of this hour, in every lightning bolt she had hurled and every storm she had summoned, he had never aimed to kill her.</p>
<p>Not once.</p>
<p>’He was playing,’ she thought, a fresh wave of horror washing over her. ’All this time... he was testing me.’</p>
<p>Every devastating blow he had taken, he had allowed. Every spell he had returned had been meticulously calibrated, measured, and shaped not to annihilate, but to wound.</p>
<p>To test.</p>
<p>To teach.</p>
<p>She was standing in a garden she had nearly destroyed, facing a man who held her entire city in his palm, a man who was still alive despite her best efforts to erase him from existence.</p>
<p>And he had not finished it. This fair fight, this honest, awful, beautiful contest, had been a courtesy.</p>
<p>A gift from a god to a mortal.</p>
<p>And now, the gift was over.</p>
<p>"That was your finest magic, Helena," Rex said quietly.</p>
<p>His voice was stripped of its earlier manic glee, carrying instead a strange, almost paternal softness. "Truly... I’m proud of you."</p>
<p>For one heartbeat, the words felt warm.</p>
<p>They felt like recognition.</p>
<p>For a second, she believed she had earned something more than death.</p>
<p>Then, something shifted.</p>
<p>The warmth drained from his face like water down a drain, leaving behind something hollow and terrifying. The light in his eyes didn’t dim; it crystallized into something hard, something ancient and unfeeling.</p>
<p>His expression settled into a cold, steady smile the kind of smile a predator gives when the prey finally stops running.</p>
<p>"Now," he said, his voice dropping to a register that seemed to vibrate in her very bones, "it’s time you saw what I’ve been keeping."</p>
<p>He didn’t raise his hands.</p>
<p>He didn’t chant.</p>
<p>He simply willed it.</p>
<p>The air didn’t move; it collapsed. The atmosphere itself seemed to flee from him, rushing toward his center as if he had become a vacuum.</p>
<p>Then, the pressure arrived.</p>
<p>Gravity came down on the garden like the lid of a coffin slamming shut.</p>
<p>Rex did not move. He did not shout.</p>
<p>He did not even raise his hands. He simply ceased to hold back.</p>
<p>In that instant, the fundamental physics of the garden shattered. A field of crushing, absolute weight erupted from his body in a perfect, invisible sphere, expanding outward with the inevitability of an avalanche.</p>
<p>It swallowed the scorched lawn, the ruins of the hall, the blackened stumps of the trees, and the stone wall where four women stood frozen in terror. The air itself didn’t just thicken; it became liquid, heavy as mercury, pressing against everything with murderous intent.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>The sound of stone splitting under unseen pressure echoed through the crater. Helena’s knees didn’t just buckle; they collapsed as if a mountain had been dropped onto her shoulders.</p>
<p>A wet, pained gasp tore from her throat as she slammed face first into the dirt. Her palms slapped against the earth with enough force to bruise, her fingers digging uselessly into the soil.</p>
<p>Her hair fell forward in a tangled curtain, veiling her face as she fought for every molecule of oxygen.</p>
<p>When she tried to lift her head, her neck tendons corded and strained like steel cables, her breath coming in ragged, wet rasps that sounded like a saw cutting through bone.</p>
<p>"I... can’t..." she wheezed, the words barely audible beneath the groan of the earth. "Rex... please..."</p>
<p>But there was no mercy in the gravity.</p>
<p>Then came the violation of her autonomy. An invisible, merciless grip clamped around both of her wrists.</p>
<p>With a sickening pop of tendons stretching to their limit, her arms were dragged wide apart, pinned to either side of her body as if she were being crucified on the very ground itself.</p>
<p>Telekinesis seized every joint, her elbows, her shoulders, and her wrists, holding them with the cold, mechanical precision of a puppeteer’s strings.</p>
<p>She was paralyzed. The thing she had spent forty-five years mastering—the delicate, precise art of somatic spellcasting—had been stripped from her in a single heartbeat.</p>
<p>She couldn’t weave a gesture; she couldn’t even curl a single finger to spark a cantrip. The most powerful mage in Aethelgard had been reduced to a helpless doll, her body no longer her own.</p>
<p>’It’s over,’ her mind screamed, a frantic whisper behind her closed eyes. ’I’m nothing.’</p>
<p>’I’m nothing against him.’</p>
<p>Deep within the abyss of Rex’s consciousness, something ancient and slumbering stirred. Something that had been patient, something that had been observing from the shadows of his own mind.</p>
<p>Full Potential Adaptation had slept through the flames, through the ice, and through every clever gambit and desperate trap Helena had thrown at him.</p>
<p>But now, the predator was awake. The process began not as a conscious thought, but as a cascade of data.</p>
<p>In the span of three heartbeats, Helena’s entire existence was dissected. The Flame Lances, the Ice Shards, the Stone Fists, and the Lightning Bolts—every spell she had ever cast was pulled apart molecule by molecule.</p>
<p>The Phoenix, the Frost Mirror Labyrinth, and the Four Seasons Cataclysm were each measured, analyzed, broken down into their mathematical and magical components, and filed away into a vast, silent library of absolute immunity.</p>
<p>He felt the knowledge settle into his soul like a lock clicking into place. He didn’t just know what she could do; he knew how to make it fail.</p>
<p>He knew the exact frequency to cancel her magic, the exact angle to deflect her power, and the exact moment to shatter her will.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at her. His face was calm, almost serene, standing in the center of the devastation he had wrought.</p>
<p>His voice drifted down to her, flat, emotionless, and terrifyingly gentle.</p>
<p>"You showed me everything, Helena," he said, each word carrying the weight of a tombstone. "Every secret, every technique, every ounce of your soul."</p>
<p>He took one step closer, the ground cracking beneath his boot, his shadow falling over her broken form.</p>
<p>"I understand all of it now..."</p>
<p>"There’s nothing left that you can do."</p>
