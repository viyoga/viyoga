<p>"He has built a kingdom of glass around a single pillar."</p>
<p>"He built it deliberately," Zane said, marveling at the sheer audacity of the man.</p>
<p>"All power structures are built around a single axis until they are old enough to survive without it," Celestina replied, her tone almost philosophical in its ruthlessness. "He has not had the time to build redundancy."</p>
<p>"What exists in the underlayer right now is an extraordinary, godlike capability trapped in a fragile, brittle container."</p>
<p>"And the Apostle network is going to strike that container directly," Zane said, the implication chilling his blood.</p>
<p>"Exactly," Celestina said, and for a moment, Zane could almost hear the predatory smile in her voice. "The Apostle network will strike the container."</p>
<p>"They will find the geological designation, the Blood Oath, the elemental mastery, and the physical godhood you described."</p>
<p>"They will strike it with everything they have, and they will fail. And in that failure, they will shatter the container."</p>
<p>"It will cost the network a significant portion of its Aethelgard adjacent operational capacity, and in the process, both the Apostle network and the Underlayer will be left significantly, irrevocably weaker."</p>
<p>Zane fell silent, the sheer, brutal logic of her vision settling over him. He wasn’t just listening to a report anymore; he was listening to the blueprint of a coming apocalypse.</p>
<p>"You are going to let them fight each other," Zane said, the realization hitting him with the force of a physical blow.</p>
<p>It wasn’t just a strategy; it was a calculated abandonment.</p>
<p>"We are going to take advantage of the fact that they are going to fight each other regardless of anything we do," Celestina corrected him.</p>
<p>Her voice was smooth, terrifyingly calm, the sound of a master weaver pulling a single thread to unravel a tapestry. "There is a distinction, Zane. To ’let’ it happen implies passivity."</p>
<p>"We are not passive. We do not control the collision, but we will position ourselves to harvest the wreckage."</p>
<p>"The result being two weakened parties," Zane whispered, the math of her cruelty unfolding in his mind.</p>
<p>"Precisely," she said, and he could almost feel the predatory satisfaction radiating through the crystal. "Aethelgard’s Apostle network will be depleted, and the Underlayer will be left dependent on a single reincarnator who has, by his own hand, made himself the primary target for both our organization and the network’s inevitable second engagement."</p>
<p>"With both powers bleeding out, the city on the floating island, the prize we actually care about, becomes significantly more accessible."</p>
<p>A cold dread pooled in Zane’s stomach. "You are moving against Aethelgard."</p>
<p>"I am moving against Aethelgard," she said.</p>
<p>The weight of the statement was absolute. This wasn’t a proposal or a contingency plan; it was a decree.</p>
<p>The conversation wasn’t about making a decision; it was the final verification of a machine that had already begun to turn.</p>
<p>"When?" he demanded, his voice cracking the professional veneer he had fought so hard to maintain.</p>
<p>"Soon," she replied, her tone as unyielding as granite. "The window that opens when the canyon engagement strips the network of its local force is the window we have been building toward for three years."</p>
<p>’The problem with Aethelgard has never been the Apostle network’s raw capability; it has been their depth."</p>
<p>"We don’t need to defeat them in a war of attrition; we only need to deplete their local reserves."</p>
<p>"Once the canyon engagement drains them, the recovery timeline for their external capacity will be long enough for our purposes."</p>
<p>"And what are those purposes?"</p>
<p>"The island," she said, her voice dropping into a register of pure, cold ambition. "Specifically, its structural foundation."</p>
<p>"The geological platform holding Aethelgard above the Convergence Waters is not a natural phenomenon, Zane."</p>
<p>"It is a divine construction, forged eight hundred years ago. And divine constructions possess a singular, elegant vulnerability: they are built around a foundational principle."</p>
<p>"They do not fail because they are overstressed; they fail because that principle is contradicted."</p>
<p>Zane felt the air leave his lungs. He saw the vision: she was painting a world where the very ground beneath a civilization’s feet was turned into a weapon.</p>
<p>"You want to contradict the foundational principle," he said.</p>
<p>"I want Aethelgard to fall," she said, and the sheer, unadulterated menace in her voice made the hair on his arms stand up. "Not metaphorically. Not politically. But... physically."</p>
<p>"I want the island to descend into the Convergence Waters, dragging the Apostle network, its reincarnator population, and its divine designation holders into the abyss with it."</p>
<p>"The Convergence Waters have no floor," Zane said, his voice barely a breath. "There is nothing to catch them."</p>
<p>"Correct," she said, a chillingly brief confirmation.</p>
<p>The channel fell into a heavy, expectant silence. Then, Celestina spoke again, her voice shifting into the mode of command.</p>
<p>"Ignivara has been briefed on the foundational analysis, and she is ready to move."</p>
<p>"The canyon engagement provides our timing. Once the network’s forces are committed to the underlayer, Aethelgard will be running on a hollowed-out defense."</p>
<p>"That is when Ignivara strikes."</p>
<p>The name hit Zane like a physical strike to the solar plexus. The operational register he had maintained, the cold, detached shield of a Legion analyst, cracked.</p>
<p>"Ignivara," he breathed.</p>
<p>"Ignivara Ashenwyrm," Celestina said, her eyes, if he could see them, undoubtedly narrowing. "You know her."</p>
<p>It wasn’t a question. It was a test.</p>
<p>"She was stationed in the Underlayer for the seven months before the purge," Zane said, forcing his voice back into a flat, disciplined line, though his heart was racing. "We operated in the same contact network."</p>
<p>"She left the Underlayer four months ago when the monitoring conditions shifted," Celestina said, her voice cutting through his internal turmoil. "She has been at the Seam House since then."</p>
<p>"Her analysis of the island’s structural foundation is the very basis of our technical methodology."</p>
<p>Zane said nothing. He couldn’t.</p>
<p>The thought of Ignivara, of her fire, her intellect, and the way she moved through the world being the architect of such a cataclysmic descent was almost too much to process.</p>
<p>"You are going to work with her," Celestina commanded.</p>
<p>"I understand," Zane said.</p>
<p>"You are going to work with her because she is the only other person at operational distance from Aethelgard with the technical expertise to execute the foundational contradiction," Celestina continued, her logic relentless. "And because your existing operational familiarity will reduce the coordination overhead during the timing window."</p>
<p>"We cannot afford a single second of hesitation."</p>
<p>"I understand," he repeated, though the word felt like ash in his mouth.</p>
<p>"Then tell me clearly," Celestina said.</p>
<p>Her voice had shifted again; it was no longer a strategist but a commander addressing a potential flaw in the ranks. It was the voice she used when she was looking for a weakness to prune.</p>
<p>"Tell me, Zane. Is the personal relationship a liability?"</p>
<p>Zane closed his eyes, the weight of the entire impending apocalypse resting on the answer he was about to give.</p>
<p>"It is not a liability," Zane said. The words were clipped and precise, a defensive wall of logic built in an instant.</p>
<p>"That answer was too fast," Celestina countered.</p>
<p>There was no warmth in her voice, only the razor-sharp intuition of a predator sensing a tremor in its prey. She wasn’t looking for a fact; she was looking for a lie.</p>
<p>"The relationship was operational in its context," Zane pushed, his voice hardening as he leaned into the professional mask. "We were both running contact work in the same environment."</p>
<p>"The proximity produced a personal component..."</p>
<p>"That component did not compromise the operational work then, and it will not compromise it now."</p>
<p>"The personal component," Celestina repeated.</p>
<p>She used his words like a scalpel, dissecting them to show how shallow they were. The implication was clear: your terminology serves as a shield, but it is an ineffective one.</p>
<p>Zane felt the heat rise in his chest, the sheer, raw weight of the truth threatening to shatter his composure. He stopped playing the analyst.</p>
<p>"She and I were together," he said, the confession hanging in the air like a heavy mist. "In the Underlayer. For four months."</p>
<p>"I am aware," Celestina said, her voice chillingly indifferent.</p>
<p>"Then you already knew the answer to whether it is a liability," Zane snapped, the frustration finally breaking through the surface. "You didn’t ask because you needed the information."</p>
<p>"You asked because you wanted to see if I would tell you the truth."</p>
<p>A long, suffocating pause followed. Then, a soft, terrifyingly calm sound: the ghost of a breath that might have been a laugh.</p>
<p>"Yes," she admitted.</p>
<p>"It is not a liability," Zane repeated, more quietly this time, more certain. "She and I will do the work."</p>
<p>"The personal component makes the coordination faster, not slower."</p>
<p>"We know how each other operates. We don’t need to guess; we already know."</p>
<p>"Then go to her," Celestina commanded, the moment of psychological interrogation over. "She is currently at the eastern coastal approach, moving toward the position she identified as the optimal entry point for the foundational work."</p>
<p>"You will intercept her in approximately four hours if you move now. Do not waste the window, Zane."</p>
<p>"The reincarnator who defeated Kregg," Zane said, his voice dropping into a low, intense register that halted the rhythm of the conversation.</p>
<p>The silence that followed was absolute.</p>
