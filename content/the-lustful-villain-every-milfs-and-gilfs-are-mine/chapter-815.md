<p>"He would not have wanted you to do that," Iris said.</p>
<p>Her voice was a hollowed out thing, stripped of its usual vibrance, possessing the flat, brittle quality of someone who had wept until the tears had turned to salt and the crying itself had become a physical exhaustion. "He never liked formal things... He always thought they were a waste of time..."</p>
<p>"Then I did it wrong," Rex replied.</p>
<p>There was no apology in his voice, only the clinical admission of a miscalculation.</p>
<p>Something shifted in the landscape of her face. It wasn’t a smile, nor was it a sob; it was the infinitesimal adjustment of a person who had been drowning in the depths of their own grief and had suddenly felt a single, unexpected current of air from the world above.</p>
<p>"He liked..." she started, then faltered, the words catching in her throat like jagged glass.</p>
<p>Rex did not press her. He simply waited, a silent, unmoving presence beside the wooden box.</p>
<p>"He liked the wind on the western cliffs," she finally whispered, her eyes glazing over as she stared into the distance. "He used to go there in the early mornings, just to stand there and let the gale tear at him."</p>
<p>"He said it was the only place on the island where the wind felt like it was actually doing something, rather than just passing through a hollow space."</p>
<p>"He was a wind affinity user... I always thought that was why he sought it out."</p>
<p>"A logical preference," Rex said.</p>
<p>She fell silent, the only sound the distant, mournful rustle of the garden leaves.</p>
<p>"Tremor killed him," she said.</p>
<p>She didn’t scream it. She didn’t wail.</p>
<p>She said it with the terrifying, surgical precision of an assassin, with the specific tendency of her designation to name a target, to identify the cause, and to label the fact before allowing the emotion to catch up and tear her apart.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"I watched the final sequence from the eastern building line," she continued, her voice gaining a frantic, rhythmic momentum as she began to dissect the memory. "The plaza was laid out perfectly before me..."</p>
<p>"I saw the exact moment Tremor deployed the earthquake protocol." Her hands pressed so hard against the coffin’s lid that her fingernails creaked against the wood. "Veylor was caught in the second wave."</p>
<p>"The ground instability at the southern approach vector... the way the substrate just... disintegrated." She stopped, her breath hitching. "And the rest of it... it’s just too fucked up to even tell..."</p>
<p>Rex remained silent, providing the space for her to navigate the wreckage of her memory.</p>
<p>"He was not in Tremor’s direct path," she said, her voice regaining its analytical edge as she pushed through the pain. "He wasn’t a primary target..."</p>
<p>"He wasn’t one of the high-value contacts the entity was hunting..."</p>
<p>"He was just... in the wrong place..."</p>
<p>"He was caught in the crossfire when the physics of the engagement resolved in a direction that didn’t account for his existence."</p>
<p>She spoke with a brutal, analytical precision, the assassin’s way of processing agony by turning it into data. Rex recognized the framework immediately.</p>
<p>It was the exact same logic he had offered Mireya only twenty minutes prior. It was the cold, unyielding truth of the forty-year archive: the universe was not cruel; it was merely indifferent.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>She turned her head, her eyes locking onto his with the sharp, perceptive gaze of a predator. This was the ’assassin designation’s read,’ a look that bypassed social niceties and emotional performances, searching for the core of the matter.</p>
<p>"You stopped it," she said. It wasn’t a question.</p>
<p>"The engagement’s final phase," Rex confirmed. "Yes."</p>
<p>"Tremor was going to release the meteor," she said, her voice rising with the tension of the memory. "I saw Valentina’s and Morwenna’s positions as the mass began to assemble."</p>
<p>"I ran the trajectory in my head, Rex... I saw the impact point." She looked back down at the coffin, her expression darkening. "If that thing had landed where it was aimed, the ground would have been a crater of pulverized bone and meat."</p>
<p>"There would have been significantly more coffins in this garden today."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"And you caught it," she whispered, her eyes wide with a mixture of awe and a lingering, primal terror. "You caught a falling star, and you threw it back."</p>
<p>"You forced Tremor to the ground." She searched his face, looking for the limit of his humanity. "I do not know how you did that."</p>
<p>"The capability you demonstrated... it was completely outside the model I had for your designation."</p>
<p>"My model of my own designation has been updating," Rex said, his gaze steady and unblinking. "The Sable Island assessment produced results that were still being processed when the engagement began."</p>
<p>Iris stared at him for a long moment, her eyes performing that specific, terrifying ’read’ the way an assassin dissects a target, not looking for a soul, but for the structural weaknesses in a lie. She was looking through the skin, through the muscle, searching for the tremor of a conscience that wasn’t there.</p>
<p>"You were not supposed to be able to do that," she said.</p>
<p>There was no suspicion in her voice, no accusation of cheating or hidden power. It was something far more profound: the hollowed-out realization of someone who had prepared her heart for a total catastrophe, only to find that the universe had granted her a mercy she hadn’t asked for.</p>
<p>"No," Rex replied.</p>
<p>"And yet," she whispered, the word hanging in the air like a ghost.</p>
<p>"And yet," Rex echoed.</p>
<p>The silence returned, heavier than before, pressing down on the garden like the weight of the very earth that had swallowed Veylor.</p>
<p>"I am angry at Tremor," she said suddenly.</p>
<p>She spoke the words with a brutal, unadorned clarity, stripping away the softness of mourning to reveal the jagged edges of rage. "It isn’t the quiet, weeping kind of angry... but it’s the other kind."</p>
<p>"The kind that knows exactly what it wants but can’t reach it because the thing it wants is nothing more than a shattered stone mask, lost somewhere in the northern rubble."</p>
<p>Rex held her gaze, his eyes as steady as the mountains.</p>
<p>"That is a reasonable thing to be," he said.</p>
<p>"Is it?" she challenged, her eyes flashing with a momentary, dying ember of fire.</p>
<p>"He made a tactical decision to deploy a high-output earthquake protocol within an enclosed geological structure without fully accounting for the secondary seismic physics," Rex said, his voice providing a cold, structural framework for her chaos. "The decision was his."</p>
<p>"The carnage, the broken bones, the pulverized earth, and the lives lost to the secondary waves were the consequence of that decision."</p>
<p>"To be angry at the decision is not an irrational response; it is a logical one."</p>
<p>She turned her gaze back to him, her expression hardening into something sharp and lethal.</p>
<p>"You killed him," she said. It wasn’t a jab; it was a statement of fact, as indisputable as the sun rising in the east. "Tremor..."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>She didn’t blink. "And you are standing here."</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said.</p>
<p>"Why?" she demanded.</p>
<p>The question was a blade, thrust at his chest to see if he would bleed.</p>
<p>Rex looked down at the coffin, processing the query. There were many honest answers, but only one that would satisfy the specific architecture of her grief.</p>
<p>He had to choose the one that offered truth without the insult of pity.</p>
<p>"Because he died in a fight he chose to be in," Rex said, his voice resonant and calm. "And the grief that results from a chosen death is the most legitimate kind."</p>
<p>"And a legitimate grief deserves acknowledgment from the person who brought it into being."</p>
<p>Iris watched him, her "assassin’s read" narrowing. She was looking for the performance, the feigned sympathy, the social mask that people wore to make themselves feel better after a killing.</p>
<p>She was looking for the lie.</p>
<p>"You are not sorry," she said, her voice dropping to a chilling, flat register.</p>
<p>"No," Rex said.</p>
<p>He didn’t flinch. He didn’t offer a hollow apology to soothe her.</p>
<p>She looked back at the coffin, a small, bitter nod of her head following his honesty.</p>
<p>"He wouldn’t want you to pretend," she murmured. "He couldn’t stand it when people pretended about things."</p>
<p>"He hated the theater of it." She pressed her palm flat against the lid, her touch almost reverent. "He fought because he believed the fight mattered..."</p>
<p>"In a world of shifting sands and dying gods, that was the only thing he was ever certain of."</p>
<p>Rex looked at the wood, at the grave, and at the woman who had lost everything to a moment of physics.</p>
<p>"He was a good fighter," Rex said.</p>
<p>It was the only thing he could say about Veylor that was absolute, a truth that required no qualification, no emotion, and no lie.</p>
