<p>Rex found Iris in the household’s eastern courtyard two mornings after the training hall’s second session, a session that had left the stone floor stained with copper-scented blood and the air ringing with the wet sounds of broken ribs.</p>
<p>She was seated where she always sat, a shadow among shadows, sharpening a set of throwing knives with the specific, unhurried focus of someone who used lethal precision as a form of meditation.</p>
<p>The morning air was crisp, but the courtyard felt heavy, charged with the residual tension that always seemed to follow Rex like a storm cloud. He did not approach her with the careful, skittish energy most people brought to an assassin, that instinctive, primal recoil people felt when they sensed a predator in their midst.</p>
<p>Even after weeks of him becoming an undeniable fixture of the household, people still treated her as though she might slip a blade between their ribs for breathing too loudly.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t give a fuck about their fear. He walked toward her with the effortless, unbothered stride of a man who had already conquered everything he saw, and without waiting for an invitation, he dropped onto the low stone wall directly across from her. His presence was immediate, warm, and dangerously solid.</p>
<p>"Those are Valdric steel," he said abruptly, breaking the silence.</p>
<p>His voice was low, carrying a rough edge that commanded attention without needing to raise its volume. He nodded toward the glinting blades in her lap.</p>
<p>"The balance is different from the standard Aethelgard forge pattern... Lighter in the hilt, more aggressive in the tip."</p>
<p>Iris paused mid-stroke. She looked up, her pale eyes narrowing as she assessed him.</p>
<p>There was no fear in her expression, only a sharp, analytical curiosity.</p>
<p>"Most people cannot tell the difference," she said, her voice as cool as the steel she held. "Most people just see something that kills."</p>
<p>"Most people have never had to rely on the minute details of a weapon’s weight to decide whether they live or die," Rex countered.</p>
<p>He leaned forward slightly, his muscular frame casting a broad shadow over her work. "I have... I’ve spent enough time studying weapon balance for reasons that had nothing to do with appreciation and everything to do with survival."</p>
<p>"It shows, apparently, in ways I do not always intend."</p>
<p>Iris set one knife down on the stone with a soft clink and picked up the next. She tested the edge against her thumb with the casual, terrifying expertise of someone who had tested her own mortality ten thousand times.</p>
<p>"You intend most things, Rex." Her gaze locked onto his, piercing and unflinching. "I have noticed that about you."</p>
<p>"Even the things that look accidental—the way you position yourself in a room, the way you choose your words, and the way you seem to ’stumble’ into exactly where you need to be—all have a purpose."</p>
<p>’Nothing about you is truly random."</p>
<p>Rex felt a spark of genuine interest. Most people saw his brutality and assumed he was a blunt instrument, a hammer designed to crush.</p>
<p>But Iris saw the architect behind the violence. She saw the calculation beneath the carnage.</p>
<p>’She’s dangerous,’ Rex thought, a slow smirk spreading across his face. ’Not because she can kill me, but because she actually sees me.’</p>
<p>"Is that what you think?" Rex asked, his voice dropping an octave. "That I’m playing some grand game?"</p>
<p>"I think you’re a man who moves through the world like you’re rewriting it as you go," Iris replied. "Every action has a consequence. Every ’accident’ leaves a trail..."</p>
<p>"You don’t just walk into a room; you claim it."</p>
<p>Rex let out a short, rough laugh, one that sounded more like a growl. "Maybe."</p>
<p>"But even a strategist needs to breathe." He shifted his weight, his thighs straining against his trousers as he relaxed into the moment. "Not everything is a move on a board, Iris."</p>
<p>"Sometimes I simply enjoy sitting across from someone whose company I actually want, without there being a larger reason attached to it."</p>
<p>"No schemes, no manipulation. Just two people who understand what it means to carry steel."</p>
<p>Iris didn’t look away. For a long moment, the only sound between them was the rhythmic shick shick of stone against metal as she returned to her work.</p>
<p>The tension between them wasn’t the nervous energy of prey and predator; it was something far more volatile: the recognition of two apex killers finding a rare moment of stillness in a world built on slaughter.</p>
<p>Iris looked at him for a long moment, her pale eyes fixed on his with a scrutiny that would have made a lesser man flinch. She was testing the sentence the way she tested the edge of a Valdric blade, running a mental finger along the words, checking for the microscopic imperfections of dishonesty she had learned to expect from almost every human being she encountered.</p>
<p>Most people who claimed to want ’simple company’ were usually just fishing for information, planting seeds of false intimacy to harvest later.</p>
<p>But she found nothing. No hidden hook, no poisoned edge. Just the blunt, terrifying honesty of a predator.</p>
<p>"You are better at this than you should be," she said finally, her voice carrying a rare note of genuine observation. "Most people who try to be charming end up sounding like they are performing."</p>
<p>"There is a performance in their posture, a rehearsed quality to their smile. You do not sound like you are performing anything."</p>
<p>Rex leaned back, the sunlight catching the hard angles of his jaw and the corded muscle of his throat.</p>
<p>"I am not," he said simply. "Performing takes effort, Iris."</p>
<p>"It’s exhausting to maintain a mask when you could be using that energy for something that actually matters."</p>
<p>"I would rather spend my effort on the kill, on the strategy, on the things that keep us alive, and let the rest of the world be whatever it actually is."</p>
<p>"Raw and unfiltered," Iris murmured, almost to herself.</p>
<p>They talked for a while after that, conversations that would have seemed trivial to an outsider but held a profound weight between them. They spoke of weapon smithing, of the weather, of the mundane details of household logistics.</p>
<p>It was the specific, effortless cadence that develops between two people who have already acknowledged the leviathans swimming beneath their surface. They both knew the difficult things: the ghost of Veylor, the fresh wounds of grief, and the unspoken promises of work and violence. Rex had made for her were there, but they didn’t need to drag them into the light every time they sat together.</p>
<p>For the first time in months, Iris felt something foreign stirring beneath her ribs: lightness. She found herself laughing a genuine, unguarded sound at a particularly dry observation Rex made about the incompetence of the new recruits.</p>
<p>The laugh surprised even her; it was the involuntary reaction of someone whose guard had been lowered so gradually she hadn’t even noticed the breach.</p>
<p>’What am I doing?’ she wondered briefly, a flicker of self-awareness crossing her mind. ’Letting a monster make me feel human?’</p>
<p>But she pushed the thought away. In this house, feeling human was a luxury she couldn’t afford, yet with Rex, it felt less like a weakness and more like a different kind of weapon.</p>
<p>Later that afternoon, the atmosphere shifted from the quiet intimacy of the courtyard to the raw, bone-deep exhaustion of the training hall. The session had been brutal, a high-intensity drill that had left several junior guards nursing cracked ribs and bruised sternums.</p>
<p>The air in the hall was thick with the metallic tang of sweat and the faint, copper smell of blood from a split lip.</p>
<p>The hall had emptied out, leaving only a handful of the elite unit to stretch and recover. Sela, one of the Twelve, dropped onto the wooden bench beside Iris with the loose-limbed, boneless exhaustion of someone who had just spent two hours pushing her body to the point of failure.</p>
<p>Sela didn’t start with training reports or tactical debriefs. Instead, she tilted her head toward the center of the hall, where Rex was standing.</p>
<p>"He is unfairly good-looking for someone who is also unfairly good at everything else," Sela said, her voice carrying a conspiratorial lilt.</p>
<p>She watched Rex as he corrected one of the junior guards’ stance. He was moving with that same patient, focused attention he gave everything, whether it was a conversation with Iris or teaching a recruit how not to break his own wrist during a parry.</p>
<p>"It should not be allowed, Iris. Truly... A person gets to be devastatingly capable or devastatingly handsome."</p>
<p>"To have both? It feels like the world is cheating."</p>
<p>Rilena, sitting close enough to overhear while she wrapped her bruised knuckles in fresh linen, snorted loudly without bothering to look up. "You say that every single week, Sela... Give it a rest."</p>
<p>"I say it every week because it remains true every week," Sela countered, completely unbothered.</p>
<p>She leaned her chin on her hand, her eyes tracking the way the sunlight caught the sweat on Rex’s shoulders as he moved. "Look at him... Just look!"</p>
<p>"He has been standing in that patch of sunlight for four minutes correcting Doran’s footwork, and I have gotten absolutely nothing done in that time except watch him do it."</p>
<p>"My brain has simply shut down and surrendered."</p>
<p>Iris didn’t join in the teasing, but she didn’t look away either. She watched Rex, her fingers unconsciously tracing the hilt of the knife at her belt, feeling the strange, dangerous pull of the man who could shatter a bone as easily as he could make her laugh.</p>
