<p>The impact was not a collision of matter but a collision of existence.</p>
<p>As the shards tore into him, Rex’s mind became a battlefield where biology fought against mathematics. His Full Potential Adaptation didn’t just observe; it attacked.</p>
<p>The moment each fragment touched his aura, the adaptation latched onto its unique frequency. It ran a frantic, hyperaccelerated analysis on every fragment’s mechanism as they screamed toward him.</p>
<p>The analysis for each completed in microseconds the exact time required to decode the complexity of the temporal wound, and with each completion, the adaptation’s coverage expanded, swallowing the fragment’s implementation class into its own arsenal.</p>
<p>But the cost of that analysis was paid in flesh and blood.</p>
<p>Rex was bleeding. But it wasn’t the clean spray of a blade’s cut.</p>
<p>As the fragments’ temporal authority pierced his personal timeline during those microsecond analysis windows, his very cells became confused.</p>
<p>His biological tissue became momentarily uncertain about which moment it was inhabiting. One cell believed it was ten years old; the neighbor believed it had not yet been born.</p>
<p>This cellular-level dissonance manifested as a horrific, wet tearing sound—the sound of his body literally unraveling at the seams.</p>
<p>His muscles convulsed as they tried to exist in multiple temporal states at once, rupturing vessels that shouldn’t have been there, spilling blood that seemed to drip upward before falling.</p>
<p>The agony was a white-hot supernova behind his eyes. ’Fuck... it feels like... my bones are turning to liquid and stone at the same time!’</p>
<p>[CRITICAL CELLULAR DISRUPTION DETECTED]</p>
<p>[ACTIVATING: INFINITE REGENERATION]</p>
<p>The response was immediate and violent. His Infinite Regeneration roared to life, a cascade of healing energy that slammed into the temporal wounds.</p>
<p>The sound was the sickening wet squelch of knitting flesh, the grinding crack of bones snapping back into alignment, and the hiss of steam as his body fought to reassert its singular timeline.</p>
<p>The regeneration didn’t just heal him; it forced his cells back into submission, violently re-anchoring his biology to the present.</p>
<p>Rex stood in the center of the carnage. The fractured domain groaned around him, amber gold fragments of broken divinity drifting through the air like deadly snowfall.</p>
<p>He stood panting, sweat mixing with the blood streaming down his neck, his chest heaving as his systems stabilized.</p>
<p>The adaptation’s coverage had done its work. He could feel the new understanding.</p>
<p>His power now extended to every mechanism Kaeleth had deployed. He had decoded the god’s language.</p>
<p>He had learned how to speak ’Time.’</p>
<p>And then, he laughed.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a human laugh. It wasn’t the controlled, amused chuckle of the man who navigated high society or commanded respect in the Nightwing household.</p>
<p>This was something primal, something darker. It was the involuntary, jagged sound of a predator who had walked directly into a trap, only to realize he had used the trap to sharpen his own claws.</p>
<p>It was the laugh of a man who had seen the abyss, measured it, and found it wanting.</p>
<p>"Is that it?" Rex roared, his voice carrying through the fractured silence, dripping with blood and triumph. "You threw everything you had at me, Kaeleth!"</p>
<p>"You tried to erase me from the calendar, and all you did was teach me how to read your handwriting!"</p>
<p>His eyes burned with a terrifying new light—the light of someone who had just seized the keys to the kingdom. The tension in the air was no longer just fear; it was the charged stillness before a lightning strike.</p>
<p>From the throne of shattered light at the center of the chaos, a figure began to move.</p>
<p>Kaeleth rose from his throne.</p>
<p>As Kaeleth rose, the reality around him stopped pretending. Up until this moment, Rex had been fighting a diminished shadow, a compressed, drained version of a deity whose power had been hemorrhaging through every exchange of authority and countermeasure.</p>
<p>But now, the God of Time ceased his restraint.</p>
<p>The domain exploded into full expression. The amber gold light intensified until it burned with the ferocity of a dying star, and the temporal notation symbols, the mathematical language of causality, began to scroll through the air at maximum density.</p>
<p>They weren’t just symbols anymore; they were razor-sharp glyphs of absolute law, spinning in complex, terrifying geometries that made Rex’s teeth ache from the sheer pressure of their presence. This was the God in his full, unvarnished majesty: the environment itself made sentient and hostile.</p>
<p>Yet, as Rex stared up through blood-matted hair and vision blurred by adrenaline, he saw something that shouldn’t have existed.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was sweating.</p>
<p>In the divine register, this wasn’t a biological secretion. Kaeleth had no sweat glands; he was a concept given form.</p>
<p>But Rex’s Divine Perception caught the truth. The sweat was a fundamental corruption in the divine expression.</p>
<p>The pristine amber glow was becoming fractionally less clean, tainted by a greasy, uncertain quality. The temporal symbols, once fluid as flowing water, were stuttering, their movements becoming jagged and hesitant.</p>
<p>It was the divine equivalent of a tremor, the unmistakable sign of a system exceeding its comfortable operating parameters.</p>
<p>The God of Time was struggling.</p>
<p>"You are still here," Kaeleth said.</p>
<p>His voice didn’t just carry through the air; it vibrated through Rex’s marrow, heavy with the weight of millennia.</p>
<p>Rex wiped a streak of blood from his chin, feeling the wet heat of it against his lips. "Yes."</p>
<p>"The domain level working," Kaeleth continued, his gaze piercing through Rex like a blade, "has contained every entity that has entered this space for two thousand years."</p>
<p>"None have survived the unraveling."</p>
<p>"I know," Rex countered, his voice steady despite the way his ribs creaked with every breath. "You didn’t just want to kill me, Kaeleth."</p>
<p>"You wanted to trap me in the debris."</p>
<p>"You expected the fragments to complete the containment after the working’s structure failed..."</p>
<p>"The fragments weren’t just weapons, but... they were the fallback mechanism."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s eyes narrowed, the golden light around him flaring dangerously. "Yes."</p>
<p>"But your math was off," Rex said, a predator’s grin splitting his bloodied face. "The fallback mechanism required the fragments to reach me before the adaptation completed its analysis."</p>
<p>"You needed the fragments to hit before I understood them..."</p>
<p>"But the analysis completed fast enough that by the time the shards arrived, I already had coverage."</p>
<p>"I didn’t just survive your trap... I learned it."</p>
<p>Silence fell over the fractured domain. It was a heavy, suffocating silence, the kind of silence that occurs when a cosmic intelligence processes an outcome that was never written in its prophecies.</p>
<p>Kaeleth was recalibrating, his divine mind racing to account for the variable that had just broken his perfect game.</p>
<p>"You... are an anomaly," Kaeleth finally said.</p>
<p>The accusation was quiet, almost reverent.</p>
<p>"Yes," Rex said. "I’ve been told."</p>
<p>"This adaptation capability..." Kaeleth’s voice grew colder, more analytical. "It is not standard for any system class in the mortal world’s reincarnator taxonomy."</p>
<p>"In two thousand years of observation, I have catalogued every permutation of power, every evolution of the soul."</p>
<p>"This specific combination of this adaptive cruelty has never appeared."</p>
<p>"Good," Rex said. "I’d hate to be predictable."</p>
<p>"You were helped," Kaeleth pressed, the amber light around him pulsing like a heartbeat. "You have a patron."</p>
<p>"Someone who provided resources and capabilities that the standard system structure simply cannot produce."</p>
<p>Rex’s inner monologue growled. ’He’s close.’</p>
<p>’But he doesn’t understand the difference between a gift and a tool.’</p>
<p>"The patron provided guidance," Rex said aloud, stepping forward despite the protesting scream of his healing muscles. "But the capabilities? Those were the result of choices I made."</p>
<p>"I took the resources the system gave me, and I forged them into something sharper."</p>
<p>"The system provided those resources because I built the structures that made the system reward me... I earned this."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s ambient expression shifted again. The gold light flickered, the symbols rearranging themselves as he revised his assessment.</p>
<p>He was no longer looking at a victim; he was looking at an equal threat.</p>
<p>"You are not going to tell me who the patron is," Kaeleth stated. It wasn’t a question; it was a cold recognition of Rex’s defiance.</p>
<p>Rex straightened his spine, blood dripping from his fingertips onto the fractured floor of reality. The tension between them was so thick it felt like physical pressure, a coiled spring ready to snap and shatter the universe.</p>
<p>"I’m not going to tell you who they are," Rex said, his voice dropping to a dangerous, intimate register. "But I am going to tell you something about the patron."</p>
<p>"Because the information is relevant to what happens next, and you deserve to know exactly what kind of war you’ve just started."</p>
