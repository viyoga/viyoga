<p>Rex remained pinned over the broken god for several long seconds, his chest heaving in ragged, wet gasps. Blood, both his own crimson lifeblood and the strange, shimmering ichor of his fracturing continuity, leaked from the wounds across his ribs and shoulders.</p>
<p>The air around him still hummed with the nauseating static of his own unraveling existence, each heartbeat feeling like a desperate attempt to stitch his soul back to his bones.</p>
<p>But beneath the agony, beneath the bone-deep exhaustion that threatened to collapse his lungs, there was something else: a terrifying, intoxicating surge of triumph.</p>
<p>He looked down at Kaeleth. The god’s face, once a monument of divine authority and unassailable certainty, was now a ruin.</p>
<p>The amber gold glow had faded to a sickly, guttering ember, casting long, grotesque shadows across the blood-slicked floor.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s eyes, which had once held the cold fire of eternity, now held only something far more pathetic: the hollow, glassy stare of a creature that had poured its entire essence into a void and received nothing in return.</p>
<p>A sound began to bubble up from Rex’s throat. At first, it was a wet cough, but then it transformed into something darker. A laugh.</p>
<p>It wasn’t a laugh of joy; it was a jagged, hysterical sound that tore through the silence of the dying domain. It was the laugh of a man who had walked through hell, seen the face of God, and punched it until it bled.</p>
<p>"Look at you," Rex rasped, his voice dripping with venomous amusement.</p>
<p>He tightened his grip on Kaeleth’s throat, forcing the god to meet his bloodshot eyes. "Look at what’s left of you!"</p>
<p>"Where’s that divine wrath now?!"</p>
<p>"Where’s that absolute certainty?!"</p>
<p>Rex’s laughter grew louder, more manic, echoing off the crumbling walls of the collapsing reality. "You thought you were the exception, didn’t you?"</p>
<p>"You thought the universe had rules that applied to everyone except you... But you’re just another fuckin’ variable I had to solve."</p>
<p>He leaned closer, his face inches from Kaeleth’s deteriorating features, his grin widening into something feral and predatory.</p>
<p>"Do you hear that, Kaeleth? That’s the sound of your entire worldview shattering." Rex’s eyes burned with a new, dangerous light—a spark of something that wasn’t just survival but pure, unadulterated dominance. "You’re not special and you’re not eternal either..."</p>
<p>"You’re just another corpse waiting to happen."</p>
<p>Rex stood up slowly, not releasing his grip until Kaeleth’s head lolled uselessly to the side. He stood tall despite his injuries, a blood-soaked monument to defiance.</p>
<p>He felt the power of his own adaptation humming in his veins, a coiled spring of potential that seemed to demand more.</p>
<p>"Send them," Rex whispered, loud enough for the dying god to hear, his voice carrying a promise of absolute violence. "Send your armies."</p>
<p>"Send your pantheons."</p>
<p>"Send every fucking god in whatever heaven you think you own."</p>
<p>"I don’t care how many of them there are."</p>
<p>He threw his head back and laughed again, a sound that promised nothing but carnage. "I’ll break them all."</p>
<p>"I’ll tear their thrones down and piss on the ashes."</p>
<p>"I am the constant that you couldn’t erase, and I’m just getting started."</p>
<p>The laughter died down as Rex turned his gaze toward the horizon of the dissolving domain. The fight was over, but the war—the war against everything that dared to stand in his way—had only just begun.</p>
<p>Kaeleth lay beneath him, a broken husk of failed divinity, and Rex felt nothing but the cold, exhilarating hunger to keep breaking things.</p>
<p>A wet, gurgling sound erupted from Kaeleth’s throat, a sound of pure, unadulterated desperation. His fingers twitched against the blood-soaked stone, claws scraping uselessly before suddenly igniting with a weak, sickly amber light.</p>
<p>With a shriek of agony that sounded more like a wounded animal than a deity, Kaeleth forced the last embers of his essence into a desperate magical discharge.</p>
<p>"Enough... GET OFF ME!" Kaeleth screamed, unleashing a point-blank burst of temporal distortion.</p>
<p>The air around Rex warped, the fabric of space rippling outward in a shockwave designed to age flesh to dust and shatter bone into powder.</p>
<p>FWOOOM SHATTER.</p>
<p>The shockwave hit Rex full force. But there was no impact. No pain. No disintegration. Rex didn’t even flinch.</p>
<p>His Enhanced Physical Attributes and his adaptive nature had already processed the frequency of Kaeleth’s divinity. To Rex, the devastating temporal blast felt like nothing more than a lukewarm breeze.</p>
<p>He stood in the center of the distortion, his clothes singed but his skin untouched, watching Kaeleth with an expression of bored contempt.</p>
<p>Rex’s eyes narrowed. The immunity wasn’t just a shield; it was a humiliation.</p>
<p>"That’s it?" Rex asked, his voice eerily calm amidst the swirling magical debris. "That’s all the ’divine wrath’ you’ve got left?"</p>
<p>"You’re pathetic."</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s eyes widened in terror. He tried again, summoning jagged shards of crystallized time to impale Rex, but Rex simply stepped forward, the shards passing through his aura as if he were a ghost.</p>
<p>Each failed attack from the god seemed to drain more of his life force, leaving him weaker and more exposed.</p>
<p>"Let’s see how you handle something a little more... personal," Rex whispered.</p>
<p>Rex moved faster than Kaeleth’s failing eyes could track.</p>
<p>CRACK.</p>
<p>He grabbed Kaeleth’s wrist, and with a slow, deliberate twist, he snapped the bones in the god’s forearm. The sound was like dry kindling breaking underfoot.</p>
<p>Kaeleth screamed, a high-pitched, keening sound that echoed off the dying walls.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t stop there. He wanted to dismantle the concept of divinity itself.</p>
<p>He drove his thumb deep into the wound where Kaeleth’s sternum had shattered earlier, grinding against the exposed bone fragments. "Does it hurt, Kaeleth?"</p>
<p>"Does it feel mortal?" Rex asked, tilting his head as he watched the god’s face contort in agony. "Does it feel like being nothing?"</p>
<p>Kaeleth tried to summon a shield, a weak barrier of amber light, but Rex simply reached through it. He grabbed the god’s jaw, his fingers digging into the soft tissue beneath the bone, and forced Kaeleth’s mouth open.</p>
<p>"Open wide," Rex mocked.</p>
<p>With a brutal shove of his palm, he forced a concentrated burst of Earthen Authority directly into Kaeleth’s throat. The magical pressure didn’t explode outward; it imploded inward, shredding the delicate tissues of the esophagus.</p>
<p>Kaeleth’s scream was cut short by a wet, choking gurgle as blood began to pour from his lips.</p>
<p>Rex let go, watching Kaeleth convulse on the floor. Then, he decided to play with the god’s connection to the domain.</p>
<p>Rex knelt beside him, placing his palm flat against the cracked ground. "You loved this domain so much, didn’t you?"</p>
<p>"You thought you owned it." Rex’s eyes glowed with a cruel, earthy light. "Let’s see how it feels when your precious domain rejects you."</p>
<p>Rex channeled his authority not to destroy but to rearrange. The stone beneath Kaeleth began to writhe like living flesh.</p>
<p>Sharp, needle-like protrusions of granite erupted from the floor, piercing through Kaeleth’s thighs and shoulders, pinning him to the earth like an insect in a display case.</p>
<p>"AAAAAGH GLRK—" Kaeleth’s cries were reduced to wet wheezes as the stone spikes tore through muscle and divine sinew.</p>
<p>Rex stood over him, looking down at the carnage. He picked up a shard of broken amber crystal from the floor, a remnant of Kaeleth’s power, and held it up to the fading light.</p>
<p>"You’re not a god anymore, Kaeleth."</p>
<p>"You’re not even a memory." Rex leaned down, pressing the sharp crystal against Kaeleth’s cheek, drawing a thin line of golden blood. "You’re just a lesson... And I’m the teacher."</p>
<p>Rex’s laughter returned, low and guttural, as he watched the light in Kaeleth’s eyes begin to flicker out, one agonizing second at a time.</p>
