<p>"Rilena..." she pointed toward a young woman whose knuckles were still scarred from recent drills, "...held six seconds against him on that first morning."</p>
<p>"Six seconds of desperate survival."</p>
<p>"Today, she can hold nearly a full minute of sustained pressure against a master combatant."</p>
<p>"She learned that survival isn’t about avoiding the blow; it’s about enduring the breaking point."</p>
<p>Sela shifted uncomfortably, but Aurelia’s gaze remained relentless. "Sela’s groundwork, which she once dismissed as a secondary skill, a mere formality, has been forged into a weapon."</p>
<p>"It is now the single most dangerous element of her fighting style: a lethal dance that leaves opponents bleeding on the stone before they even realize they’ve lost their footing."</p>
<p>Aurelia’s eyes moved to the veterans. "Doran no longer telegraphs his weight shift."</p>
<p>"Three separate opponents exploited that flaw against him before this month began, and he tasted defeat three times because of it."</p>
<p>"None have managed to exploit it since."</p>
<p>"He learned to move like a shadow because Rex refused to let him move like a man."</p>
<p>She paused, letting the weight of those specific improvements sink in. She looked around at every face in the courtyard, meeting the eyes of the junior guards, the seasoned officers, and the household staff who had witnessed the transformation.</p>
<p>"This household is stronger tonight than it has been in a generation," she declared, her voice ringing with an undeniable conviction that left no room for argument. "I do not say this as flattery."</p>
<p>"I do not say this to appease a guest... I say it because it is measurably, demonstrably true."</p>
<p>"Every person standing in this courtyard has felt the truth of it in their own body in the ache of their muscles, in the scars on their skin, and in the newfound clarity of their killing intent."</p>
<p>"We have been forged in fire, and we have come out as steel."</p>
<p>Aurelia did not break eye contact. Her gaze remained locked on Rex’s, unblinking and unnervingly direct.</p>
<p>The vulnerability of the moment was stripped bare; there was no room for diplomatic platitudes or the usual courtly deference.</p>
<p>"We asked you for help," she said, her voice dropping to a register that resonated in the very bones of those listening. "And you gave us considerably more than help."</p>
<p>"You gave this household something it did not know it was missing a spine made of something harder than tradition." A pause followed, one where the weight of the last thirty days—the blood spilt in training, the raw exhaustion, the shattered illusions—seemed to hang in the air like smoke. "I do not know how to thank a man properly for such a transformation, so I will say it plainly, before every soul who matters to me."</p>
<p>"Thank you, Rex Rexilion."</p>
<p>"This household is better because you walked into it, and we will not forget what that cost you the time, the patience, and the brutal honesty you did not owe us."</p>
<p>A heartbeat of silence passed before the courtyard erupted. This wasn’t the rehearsed cheer of a sycophantic court; it was a thunderous, guttural chorus.</p>
<p>Hundreds of cups rose in unison, the clatter of silver and pewter against wood creating a warm, overwhelming wave of genuine gratitude.</p>
<p>Rex accepted the moment with his practiced, warm humility, a mask he had worn so often over the last month that the lines between the performance and his own genuine satisfaction had begun to blur into something indistinguishable from the real thing.</p>
<p>Rex eventually drifted away from the heat and noise of the celebration, seeking the periphery where the golden lantern light bled into the velvet darkness of the estate’s gardens.</p>
<p>He needed the cool air to ground him. He needed to escape the suffocating intensity of their gratitude.</p>
<p>"You have thoroughly ruined this family for anyone less capable."</p>
<p>The voice was soft, laced with a particular brand of fond, private amusement. Elaris appeared beside him like a ghost woven from moonlight.</p>
<p>She held her own cup loosely, her eyes fixed on the distant figures dancing and drinking in the courtyard. There was a sharp intelligence in her expression, one that saw through every layer of Rex’s carefully constructed persona.</p>
<p>"Do you understand the implications of what you’ve done?" she continued, tilting her head toward the celebration. "Leonidas is going to return from his extended mission only to find his entire household quietly convinced that the acting director has set a standard he himself may struggle to match."</p>
<p>"You haven’t just trained them, Rex."</p>
<p>"You’ve raised their expectations of what it means to be alive, to be dangerous."</p>
<p>"That was not my intention," Rex replied smoothly, though his inner voice, the cold, calculating strategist, noted the tactical advantage of such a shift.</p>
<p>"I know it was not your intention," Elaris said, turning to face him. Her eyes glinted with something dangerously warm. "It rarely is with you."</p>
<p>"You move through life like a storm; you don’t intend to destroy the trees, you simply move forward, and the trees break because they cannot withstand your path."</p>
<p>"And yet, somehow, the outcome arrives in the same shape regardless."</p>
<p>She stepped closer, invading his personal space with the casual confidence of someone who had been thoroughly claimed by him.</p>
<p>The teasing affection in her gaze was real, but beneath it lay the unspoken history of the last month—the sweat-slicked nights, the raw vulnerability of their encounters, and the twisted intimacy born of his relentless possession.</p>
<p>"I am going to miss having you underfoot," she admitted, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper that sent a low hum through the space between them. "This household has needed someone like you in it for longer than I think anyone here would ever have the courage to admit."</p>
<p>Rex looked down at her, his lips quirking into something almost genuine. "You could simply keep finding reasons for me to visit."</p>
<p>Elaris let out a low, throaty laugh that vibrated against the quiet of the garden. She reached out, her fingers brushing against his arm with deliberate slowness before she pulled away.</p>
<p>"Oh, Rex," she said, her eyes darkening with a promise that had nothing to do with training schedules, military doctrine, or the needs of the Nightwing household. "I fully intend to."</p>
<p>Elaris reached up, her fingers lingering as she adjusted the collar of his shirt. It was a small, intimate gesture entirely unnecessary, but it served as a bridge, a way to ground herself in his presence while she studied his face.</p>
<p>Rex didn’t pull away; he offered her a rare, measured patience, a quiet stillness he reserved for her above almost anyone else in the Nightwing household.</p>
<p>In this moment, the commander and the strategist vanished, leaving only two people caught in the gravity of each other.</p>
<p>"Later," she whispered, the words meant only for his ears, carrying a weight that made the air between them feel thick. "When the celebration has properly wound down, when the wine has dulled their senses and the shadows grow long enough that nobody cares who slips away from the courtyard."</p>
<p>Rex’s gaze darkened, a predator recognizing a challenge.</p>
<p>"I will find you," he promised, his voice low and absolute.</p>
<p>Elaris smiled a delighted, private expression that stripped away decades of her carefully maintained authority, making her look briefly, disarmingly young.</p>
<p>Without another word, she turned and drifted back toward the warm glow of the gathering, moving with a practiced grace to ensure her absence didn’t raise any eyebrows.</p>
<p>Rex remained in the half light, letting the cool evening air settle the adrenaline of the evening’s performance. He wasn’t alone for long.</p>
<p>Twenty minutes later, the soft rustle of fabric announced Iris’s approach. She had clearly been tracking him from a distance, noting his retreat to the garden’s edge with the quiet observation of someone who had learned exactly how to read his patterns.</p>
<p>She didn’t speak immediately. Instead, she simply came to stand beside him.</p>
<p>Over the last month, she had learned the art of his silence and how to occupy the space next to him without demanding anything from it.</p>
<p>Their silence wasn’t awkward; it was companionable, a shared exhale after a long day of performance. Together, they watched the household below, a sea of lantern light and shifting shadows.</p>
<p>"Rilena told me today that she wants to continue training under your correction schedule even after you depart," Iris said finally, her voice cutting through the distant sounds of laughter. "She has already written half of it down from memory."</p>
<p>"She’s treating it like scripture." A pause. "I do not think she realizes how much of what you taught her cannot actually be captured on parchment."</p>
<p>"Some of it can," Rex countered, his mind automatically shifting back to the mechanics of violence. "The footwork, the timing drills, and the economy of motion transfer well enough to paper."</p>
<p>"But the rest?" He looked out into the darkness. "The rest she will have to learn through bone and sinew."</p>
<p>"She’ll learn it through repetition, through the sting of a failed parry, and through enough mistakes that the correction becomes written in her muscle memory rather than her notes."</p>
<p>"She will manage," Iris said softly. "She possesses that particular brand of stubbornness that refuses to break until it has learned exactly how to bend."</p>
