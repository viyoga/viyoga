<p>Veylor stood at the bleeding edge of the candlelight. He was translucent, his form faintly luminous like moonlight trapped in water, wearing the same simple, travel-worn field clothes he had been clad in the last time Iris had seen him breathing.</p>
<p>He looked impossibly fragile, yet he possessed a terrifying solidity that defied the laws of nature. The moment his pale and luminous eyes found hers, his entire expression shattered. His face broke open with a raw, naked disbelief so profound it acted like a sledgehammer against Iris’s carefully constructed emotional defenses.</p>
<p>"Iris," he said.</p>
<p>The sound of his voice transmitted a physical shockwave to her. It didn’t sound like a normal human voice; it was layered, echoing as though it were arriving from a vast distance while simultaneously vibrating inside her own skull.</p>
<p>It was the sound of a memory trying to become flesh again.</p>
<p>"I am here," Iris gasped.</p>
<p>Her voice was a wreck, a broken thing that betrayed every lie she had told herself over the last thirty-six days. The careful, rigid control she had maintained, the armor she had forged from grief and stubbornness, dissolved instantly.</p>
<p>Her hands trembled violently in Rex’s grip, and she felt a sob clawing at the back of her throat.</p>
<p>Veylor took a tentative step forward. As he moved, the light around him didn’t just bend; it rippled, distorting the air as if he were walking through deep water.</p>
<p>He wasn’t truly bound by the room’s physical space; he was choosing to manifest within it, forcing his ghostly essence into a shape that the world could recognize.</p>
<p>"I did not think I would see you again," Veylor whispered, his gaze raking over her face with desperate hunger. "I remember... I remember the fall, Iris."</p>
<p>"I remember the wind screaming past my ears and the ground rushing up so fast, so impossibly fast..."</p>
<p>"I remember the impact, that horrible, wet crack of bone, the taste of copper in my mouth as my lungs collapsed..." His voice wavered, his translucent fingers twitching as if feeling phantom pain. "And then there was nothing..."</p>
<p>"Just a vast, quiet grey place... No pain, no sound, no time."</p>
<p>"Just an endless, hollow emptiness. And now..." His eyes welled with something that looked like liquid silver. "...now I am here."</p>
<p>"I am standing before you, and I do not understand... I do not understand how the dead can speak."</p>
<p>Iris felt her heart breaking all over again, with each beat feeling like a fresh wound. She wanted to scream at him, to pull him into her arms and hold him until the universe corrected its mistake, but she knew the cost.</p>
<p>She knew the brutal mechanics of what Rex was doing.</p>
<p>"It is not going to last, Veylor," she said, her voice cracking on his name.</p>
<p>She forced herself to meet his luminous gaze, fighting the urge to collapse. "This isn’t a resurrection... This is a working soul thing... A temporary bridge built across the abyss."</p>
<p>She swallowed hard, the metallic taste of terror in her mouth. "One conversation..."</p>
<p>"That is all this ritual allows, and that is the price of breaking the veil."</p>
<p>"I knew the cost when I asked Rex to do this... I knew I would have to say goodbye again, immediately after saying hello."</p>
<p>"But I decided I wanted this anyway... I needed to hear your voice more than I needed to stay sane."</p>
<p>Veylor’s translucent face shifted, the luminous features contorting through something that looked, even in the eerie, silvered light of the ritual, unmistakably like raw, unvarnished grief. The spectral glow of his skin seemed to pulse in rhythm with his anguish.</p>
<p>"One conversation," he repeated, the words hollow and echoing. "After everything we were... After everything we promised."</p>
<p>"Just one conversation."</p>
<p>"I know," Iris choked out, the words feeling like shards of glass in her throat. "I am sorry, Veylor..."</p>
<p>"I am so sorry it couldn’t be more... I couldn’t save you... I watched Tremor... I watched that monster crush the life out of you while I stood there paralyzed, and I am sorry that I couldn’t give you more than this stolen moment..."</p>
<p>"Do not apologize," Veylor snapped.</p>
<p>His voice cracked violently, the specific, devastating fracture of someone who had been holding a fragile dam together only to feel it shatter completely. "Don’t you dare apologize to me for being here at all, Iris!"</p>
<p>He took a step closer, his form flickering as if the effort to maintain his presence was draining his very essence. "I have thought about you every single day since the fall..."</p>
<p>"Since that moment on the battlefield when the sky turned red and the earth shook under Tremor’s boots..."</p>
<p>"In whatever that grey place actually was—if ’thinking’ is even the right word for the consciousness that exists in that void—I thought about you."</p>
<p>His ghostly eyes burned with a desperate intensity. "I thought about the western cliffs..."</p>
<p>"I thought about the way the wind sounds through the pines... and I thought about you standing in my doorway the morning before the expedition, your hair messy, telling me to come back in one piece."</p>
<p>"And I didn’t, Iris... I didn’t come back this time from Tremor..." Veylor’s face looked in pain. "I failed the only promise that actually mattered."</p>
<p>Tears were streaming down Iris’s face now, hot and relentless. She made no attempt to wipe them away; she simply let them fall, each droplet feeling like a physical manifestation of her thirty-six days of torment.</p>
<p>Her grip on Rex’s hands tightened to the point of agony, her fingernails digging into his skin. Rex remained unnaturally still, his jaw clenched so hard the muscles stood out like cords.</p>
<p>He was a statue of concentration, his entire being focused on anchoring this impossible bridge between life and death. Iris could feel the strain coming off him in waves; the magical feedback of holding a dead soul against the pull of the grave was clearly taking a brutal toll on his own spiritual reserves.</p>
<p>"You did not break a promise," Iris said, her voice gaining a fierce, desperate strength. "You died doing something you chose to do..."</p>
<p>"You died protecting people who couldn’t protect themselves..."</p>
<p>"You died fighting a monster so others could live... I have made peace with that part, Veylor." Iris smiled. "I have fought through the guilt and the rage to find that peace..."</p>
<p>"I want you to know that before anything else: I am not angry at you for dying... I was never angry at you for that."</p>
<p>Veylor’s form flickered dangerously, his edges blurring as the candle flame sputtered. A look of profound confusion crossed his luminous features, a question that seemed too heavy for even a ghost to carry.</p>
<p>"Then what," Veylor asked, his voice dropping to a haunting whisper that seemed to vibrate through Iris’s very bones. "If you have made peace with my death... what is the conversation you needed?"</p>
<p>"What could possibly be worth breaking the laws of heaven and hell for, if not that?"</p>
<p>Iris took a breath, a ragged, unsteady thing that rattled in her chest like broken glass. It was the breath of someone standing on the edge of a precipice, about to speak words she had rehearsed in the dead of night until they bled into her subconscious, only to find that the reality of the moment made them nearly impossible to articulate.</p>
<p>The air in the room felt charged with a static fury, the magical tether between Rex, Iris, and Veylor pulling taut like a bowstring ready to snap.</p>
<p>"I need to let you go," she said.</p>
<p>The words were quiet, but they cut through the supernatural silence of the room with devastating precision. "I need to do it properly, Veylor..."</p>
<p>"Not just in the sense that you are already gone, not in the physical sense but in the sense that I need to release you from the memory of me."</p>
<p>Veylor flinched as if she had struck him. His translucent form wavered violently, his edges blurring into the shadows.</p>
<p>"These last thirty-six days..." Iris continued, her voice gaining a desperate, painful momentum. "I have been carrying you around like something unfinished..."</p>
<p>"Like a conversation that was severed mid-sentence by a blade... I have been hoarding your memory like a sacred relic, but it has become a cage."</p>
<p>"I cannot keep living as though we are still together when there is no you here to hold, no you here to argue with, and no you here to simply be..." Iris dropped a single tear. "It is not fair to me, Veylor."</p>
<p>"And God help me, it is not fair to you too... I refuse to let you exist as nothing more than a wound that refuses to close."</p>
<p>"I refuse to let you be defined by the way you died."</p>
<p>Veylor stared at her, his luminous eyes fixed on hers with an intensity that felt like it could burn through her skin. His face went unnaturally still, the silver light fading from his features to something colder and more hollow.</p>
<p>"You are... ending it...?" he said.</p>
<p>His voice was stripped of its earlier desperation, leaving behind something raw and terrifyingly empty. "You summoned me from the abyss; you tore through the veil of reality with Rex’s blood and magic, just to... to say goodbye...?"</p>
<p>"You used our one chance to speak to deliver a eulogy?"</p>
<p>"Yes!" Iris cried out, her voice finally breaking properly, the sound tearing from her throat in a sob she could no longer contain. "Because I cannot breathe without saying it!"</p>
<p>"I loved you, Veylor! Do you hear me? I loved you with everything I was, and I will love you until my own heart stops beating."</p>
<p>"That truth isn’t going anywhere... It isn’t going to stop being true just because I am saying goodbye."</p>
