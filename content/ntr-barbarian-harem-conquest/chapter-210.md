<p>A low murmur moved around the table: shock, calculation, reluctant acceptance. No one argued further. In an empire ruled by women who trusted no men, the empress’s word remained the final law.</p>

<p>Kane glanced once at Misha. Her eyes were still forward, but the smallest breath of tension had left her shoulders.</p>

<p>She had given him the elevation he needed.</p>

<p>He had given her mother the truth and the respect of not simply taking what could have been taken by force. Between them, the first real thread of unity had been drawn.</p>

<p>Slovia rose.</p>

<p>The motion was fluid, unhurried. The ministers rose with her out of long habit.</p>

<p>&quot;This council is adjourned,&quot; the empress said.</p>

<p>&quot;Preparations for joint drills along the southern approaches will begin within the week. The barbarian’s people will be given temporary credentials and access to the outer training yards. Further details will be issued through the usual channels.&quot;</p>

<p>Slovia walked into the war room without looking back.</p>

<p>The chamber was quieter than the great council hall, lit by a single high window and a row of low-burning lamps.</p>

<p>Maps covered the long central table, parchment weighted with polished stones, inked lines marking passes, rivers, and the distant borders where the beast clans still refused to speak with one voice.</p>

<p>The empress moved to the head of the table and sat, the violet of her robes settling around her like a second skin. Her long dark hair, still bound from the morning council, framed a face that gave nothing away.</p>

<p>Kane watched Misha for a moment in the corridor outside.</p>

<p>&quot;Go on,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;I will wait.&quot;</p>

<p>Misha trembled once, small, almost invisible. Then she nodded and stepped forward, following her mother into the room.</p>

<p>The door remained open behind her.</p>

<p>Kane stayed in the hallway, broad shoulders filling the frame, red hair catching the cooler light of the corridor.</p>

<p>He did not enter.</p>

<p>This conversation was not his to claim.</p>

<p>Slovia didn’t look up at first. Her eyes stayed on the maps, one elegant finger tracing the northern approaches where the mountain serpent had once nested.</p>

<p>Only when the sound of Misha’s steps reached the table did the empress lift her gaze.</p>

<p>The lean girl who stood before her carried the same eyes she had been born with, though something in them looked clearer now, less shadowed by the constant threat of a failing heart.</p>

<p>White light from the high window touched her face and made those eyes seem almost pale for a moment.</p>

<p>Slovia’s expression didn’t change.</p>

<p>She had ruled too long to startle at the sight of her own blood.</p>

<p>&quot;If you want me to apologize for stealing your husband,&quot; the empress said, voice even, &quot;I won’t.&quot;</p>

<p>Misha moved forward. She did not stop at a respectful distance.</p>

<p>She walked until she stood beside the high-backed chair, close enough that the faint scent of the morning’s council, ink, cold stone, and the residual trace of Kane still clung to both of them.</p>

<p>Slovia’s breath shifted, almost imperceptibly.</p>

<p>The girl who had once followed her through these halls on thin, determined legs now stood level with her, no longer a fragile obligation but a woman who had survived the fight and returned with a barbarian’s claim on her name.</p>

<p>Misha sat.</p>

<p>The motion was quiet.</p>

<p>She settled into the chair at her mother’s right, the same place she had occupied as a child during the long strategy sessions she had never been strong enough to attend.</p>

<p>Her hands rested in her lap for a moment. Then she looked at Slovia.</p>

<p>&quot;Mother.&quot;</p>

<p>Slovia turned her head.</p>

<p>The assessing eyes that had measured kings and queens and the red-haired man who had broken her spell the night before now rested fully on her daughter.</p>

<p>Misha’s voice stayed steady.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m happy where I am now. My heart was healed with the help of Griselda and Kane. And he promised to keep me by his side.&quot;</p>

<p>For a long moment the only sound in the war room was the hiss of the lamps.</p>

<p>Slovia’s mouth curved, small, almost reluctant, the first true smile she had offered anyone since the council ended.</p>

<p>&quot;Is that so?&quot; she said.</p>

<p>&quot;I hope he lives to fulfill it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I will make sure of it.&quot;</p>

<p>Misha reached out and touched her mother’s hand.</p>

<p>The contact was light, careful, the same way she had once touched the empress’s sleeve when she was small and still believed that staying close could keep the world from taking either of them.</p>

<p>Her fingers rested over the back of Slovia’s hand, feeling the fine bones and the residual warmth of a woman who had carried an empire alone for decades.</p>

<p>&quot;Thank you,&quot; Misha said.</p>

<p>&quot;For not giving up. If you had put me down when I was weak and young, I wouldn’t have survived this long. I wouldn’t have felt any of this.&quot;</p>

<p>Slovia did not pull her hand away.</p>

<p>The maps lay forgotten between them.</p>

<p>Outside the open door, Kane remained a silent presence in the corridor, but neither woman looked toward him.</p>

<p>The space between mother and daughter had become its own closed room.</p>

<p>Slovia’s gaze dropped to their joined hands.</p>

<p>The memory rose without permission, the night the healers had first placed the newborn in her arms, the tiny chest that rose and fell with such fragile determination that the senior physician had already begun speaking of contingencies.</p>

<p>She had refused the contingencies. She had kept the child. She had buried herself in the work of ruling so that the empire would still stand even if the small heart eventually failed.</p>

<p>And when the political debts came due, she had used that same child as currency, sending her north with a barbarian because the alternative had been worse for the realm.</p>

<p>Slovia had expected the girl to die.</p>

<p>The girl had not died.</p>

<p>&quot;You were always stubborn,&quot; Slovia said at last. The words were quieter than any she had spoken in the council chamber.</p>

<p>&quot;Even when your body could barely carry you from one end of the corridor to the other, you still followed me. I used to hear those small footsteps behind every closed door. I told myself it was inconvenient. I told myself a weak heir was a liability. The truth was simpler. I did not know how to be both empress and mother without one of them breaking.&quot;</p>

<p>Misha’s thumb moved once across the back of her mother’s hand.</p>

<p>&quot;You kept me alive long enough for someone else to finish the work. That is nothing.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It is not enough either.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It was enough for me.&quot;</p>

<p>Silence settled again, thicker this time, but no longer sharp. Slovia’s free hand rose and brushed a strand of hair from Misha’s cheek, the same automatic gesture she had used when the girl was small and feverish.</p>

<p>The motion surprised them both. Slovia let her hand fall, but she did not retreat.</p>

<p>&quot;...The barbarian,&quot; she said after a moment.</p>
