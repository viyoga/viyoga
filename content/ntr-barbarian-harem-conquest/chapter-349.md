<p>The pressure under the keel eased by degrees.</p>

<p>Kane watched the dark length turn in the deeper water outside the cut and hold there, one shield-sized eye regarding the boat for a long, flat moment before it sank.</p>

<p>Thora’s bracelet stuttered again and fell quiet against her ankle.</p>

<p>No one on the deck cheered.</p>

<p>The silence after the chase was heavier than the impacts.</p>

<p>&quot;Ashore,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;Guest right. Hands where they can see them. No sudden moves until someone with a voice taller than this mast tells us the rules.&quot;</p>

<p>The boat slid into the shadow of the cliffs. The water under the hull changed color, clouded grey over stone instead of the deep green the leviathan preferred.</p>

<p>Canvas came down in controlled heaps.</p>

<p>Lines went over to a rough quay that looked as if it had been cut for vessels three times their size and then left to weather.</p>

<p>The hull bumped wood and rock.</p>

<p>Elven crew made fast with the quick, practical motions of people who wanted solid ground under their boots more than they wanted dignity.</p>

<p>Two Giants stood watch on the quay itself.</p>

<p>They were not the full height of the corpse on the outer sand, but they still made the boat look like a toy left at a doorstep.</p>

<p>Broad through the chest and shoulders, skin the colour of old stone and sea-weather, soot worked into the lines of their faces and the creases of their hands.</p>

<p>One held a spear that could have served as a yard on a smaller ship.</p>

<p>The other had empty hands and a stare that had already finished counting every head on the deck.</p>

<p>The empty-handed one spoke first, voice low and tired, yet it boomed.</p>

<p>&quot;You came out of the deep one’s water still breathing.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane stepped onto the quay first, Mjoldr held loose at his side, free hand open and visible.</p>

<p>The household followed in the order the lists had named: Misha, then Griselda with the containment case, Kessa with her ears still half-flat from the salt, Firlia, the elven commanders, and Thora last, moving carefully, refusing any arm that offered itself.</p>

<p>&quot;We did,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;It decided the shallows were not worth the argument.&quot;</p>

<p>The spear-Giant’s mouth twitched.</p>

<p>Not quite a smile.</p>

<p>&quot;Humans escaped the Hydra’s wrath. That is a first.&quot;</p>

<p>The words rolled out slowly, as if tasting them for truth.</p>

<p>&quot;We have watched that length take fishing boats and the occasional fool who thought a spear and a prayer were enough. It does not usually let go once it has the keel.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa snorted under her breath.</p>

<p>&quot;We didn’t ask it politely.&quot;</p>

<p>The empty-handed Giant looked at her, at the stripes, the gauntlets, the way she stood like something that preferred forests to decks, and then back at Kane.</p>

<p>&quot;Beast-kin on a human boat. Winged one carrying a box that smells wrong. Elves under a barbarian’s word.&quot;</p>

<p>The Giant’s gaze settled on Thora for a moment, then returned.</p>

<p>&quot;You are not a fishing party that got lucky. Why have you come, small things, while the interior is still burning and the deep one is awake?&quot;</p>

<p>Kane didn’t dress it up.</p>

<p>&quot;Coalition business. Southern approaches. A treaty, if your authority will still hear one. We left an elven gate that learned what yellow eyes and stones mean, and we arrived to smoke that is the wrong colour and a familiar that treats coastal runners like meals. We are not here for your stone or your stores. We are here because the sky is going to crack whether your neutrality likes it or not, and the southern hole stays open if you stay out of the line.&quot;</p>

<p>Misha stepped up beside him, pale-wood token already in hand, voice even.</p>

<p>&quot;We carry the Elven Queen’s seal and written authority to speak for a provisional front. We do not pretend the conversation is simple. We only ask that it happen on stone instead of water.&quot;</p>

<p>The spear-Giant shifted the weapon’s butt against the quay.</p>

<p>&quot;Treaty talk while the pits are still open and the patient ones are still teaching our own rock to hate us.&quot;</p>

<p>A dry sound that might have been a laugh.</p>

<p>&quot;You have timing, I will grant you that.&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda’s wings shifted once.</p>

<p>The containment case sat against her side like a second heart.</p>

<p>&quot;The wrong smoke,&quot; she said.</p>

<p>&quot;How long?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Weeks.&quot;</p>

<p>The empty-handed Giant’s face darkened under the soot.</p>

<p>&quot;It started in the old places under the central ridge, holes we stopped using because the elders said some doors should stay shut. Something patient began opening them from the other side. Not a full war-host. Not at first. Tools. Turned beasts.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;How much of the empire has been affected?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Half. Then our own people coming back from the interior with eyes that were not theirs. We have been cutting and burning and losing ground by degrees while the rest of the world counted days to a sky that has already started cracking on our stone.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane felt the system’s earlier flag settle into place behind his eyes.</p>

<p>Active taint.</p>

<p>Higher concentration than the Sylvander belt.</p>

<p>The fifty-day clock had never been as comfortable as the number suggested.</p>

<p>&quot;The body on the outer sand,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;One of ours. Went down to the water when the Hydra rose, thinking a spear and a name would be enough to warn it off the harbour cut. It was not.&quot;</p>

<p>The spear-Giant’s knuckles whitened on the shaft.</p>

<p>&quot;We left the body where it fell. The deep one does not always respect the dead, but the shallows sometimes do. Superstition. Or courtesy. Hard to tell which, these days.&quot;</p>

<p>Thora’s voice came rough from behind Kane’s shoulder.</p>

<p>&quot;You are still standing watch on a quay while the interior burns. That is not surrender.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It is not victory either.&quot;</p>

<p>The empty-handed Giant looked past them at the boat, at the scraped hull and the salt-stained faces.</p>

<p>&quot;We are fighting a losing war between a demonic force and a god’s pet that suddenly got angry at its followers.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane understood it immediately.</p>

<p>&quot;Even the Hydra was affected?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;...Indeed. Ever since the day one of our own came bringing a stone from the forest.&quot;</p>
