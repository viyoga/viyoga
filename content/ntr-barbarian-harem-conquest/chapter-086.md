<p>Two days after sending Ravan back with his daring diplomatic message, the mountain pass was fully fortified.</p>

<p>Repurposed siege engines sat bolted into the stone, aiming directly down the choke point.</p>

<p>Seraphine decided her sixty elite warriors were staying put.</p>

<p>She claimed the choice was for ongoing supervision of fortification.</p>

<p>However, Kane noticed how she positioned herself whenever they shared the same space.</p>

<p>She was definitely not done assessing him yet.</p>

<p>Leaving Rutheus to watch the northern approach, a one-armed veteran with thirty years of campaign experience was exactly the right person to watch a vulnerable road; Kane rode back to Sylvandar.</p>

<p>Thora, Grieselda, Kessa, and six Bloodfang warriors flanked his horse as they made the journey home.</p>

<p>Stepping through the front gates, the manor felt noticeably different.</p>

<p>Brak actually kept the place in order, which was genuinely surprising.</p>

<p>Caelindra’s study was organized with frightening precision.</p>

<p>Firlia’s intelligence documents were filed in a complicated system that definitely wasn’t there before she arrived.</p>

<p>Thessaly had the kitchen staff producing aromas that made the Bloodfang warriors practically drool onto the courtyard cobblestones.</p>

<p>Jogging out to meet them, Brak practically vibrated with the pent-up energy of a boy who spent four days cooking instead of fighting.</p>

<p>&quot;Chief!&quot; Brak grinned, looking at the blood still crusted on Kane’s boots.</p>

<p>&quot;How many did we kill?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Three hundred and twelve,&quot; Kane answered casually, tossing his reins to a stable hand.</p>

<p>Brak nodded with happy satisfaction.</p>

<p>&quot;Good numbers. I conjecture we could have gotten four hundred if I was there to hold the line.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Keep practicing your shield bashes, and you might get the chance next time,&quot; Kane laughed, clapping the boy on the shoulder.</p>

<p>Evening settled into a comfortable rhythm Kane hadn’t felt since before the tournament began.</p>

<p>It wasn’t exactly true relaxation; he learned a long time ago never to drop his guard fully, but rather a much lower operational register.</p>

<p>The quality of a household finding its natural footing after a long period of crushing pressure.</p>

<p>Everyone gathered at the main table.</p>

<p>Thora, Sira, Kessa, Grieselda, Brak, Caelindra, Firlia, Lirael, and Lyssel all shared the space.</p>

<p>Thessaly brought out plates of meats and seasoned vegetables that clearly served as a bold statement about what proper cooking actually looked like.</p>

<p>Conversation flowed easily.</p>

<p>Nobody discussed strategy or troop movements.</p>

<p>&quot;My father’s face was like a painting,&quot; Lyssel laughed softly, taking a sip of wine.</p>

<p>&quot;When he heard about the channel fight, I could see him instantly recalibrating his entire political existence. He suddenly realized he backed a very winner, and he is scrambling to figure out how to exploit it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Speaking of repositioning,&quot; Firlia added, smiling over her cup.</p>

<p>&quot;Aldrin was just reassigned to the Sylvandar gate guard. A very quiet administrative decision that moved him much closer to my location without anyone needing to officially acknowledge why.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane watched Caelindra carefully cut her meat, saying nothing to Brak.</p>

<p>Brak stared intensely at his own plate, saying nothing to Caelindra.</p>

<p>They were both performing this awkward silence very specifically.</p>

<p>’I conjecture those two are going to be a problem eventually,’ Kane smiled to himself, taking another bite of perfectly cooked venison.</p>

<p>’I guess Caelindra is off the menu now.’</p>

<p>After dinner, Kane retreated to the quiet study.</p>

<p>Pouring a cup of elven wine, he spread the captured Morvak intelligence reports across the polished wood.</p>

<p>Ravan was a consummate professional.</p>

<p>He would have delivered the message within forty-eight hours of his release.</p>

<p>Chancellor Morvak was currently sitting in his capital, processing the humiliating reality that his eight-year grand strategy failed in a single, embarrassing engagement.</p>

<p>’He retaliates,’ Ravan had warned.</p>

<p>Kane tapped his finger against the parchment.</p>

<p>’Not escalates. Retaliates. That’s a specific word. A man who loses an entire invasion force without taking a single inch of ground usually takes that failure personally. I need to be ready for an unconventional response.’</p>

<p>Filing the documents away, he closed the leather portfolio.</p>

<p>Finding Thora on the upper balcony, he leaned against the stone railing beside her.</p>

<p>Below them, Sylvandar’s evening lights flickered to life.</p>

<p>Faded tournament banners still hung from the silver spires, lingering weeks after the Celestial Aurora concluded.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s quiet tonight,&quot; Thora noted softly, resting her head on his shoulder.</p>

<p>&quot;For now,&quot; Kane agreed.</p>

<p>&quot;When are you going to let yourself actually enjoy the quiet for once?&quot; she asked, tilting her face up to look at him.</p>

<p>Staring out at the managed elven woods beyond the city walls, he let out a long breath.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m trying. It feels strange not having a sword pointed at my neck.&quot;</p>

<p>Taking his hand, she laced her fingers through his.</p>

<p>They stood there for a long time, not planning traps or anticipating troop movements.</p>

<p>Just existing in the shared space.</p>

<p>[System Notification]</p>

<p>[Note: Ravan delivered the message fourteen hours ago.]</p>

<p>[Chancellor Morvak read it this morning.]</p>

<p>[His first response was to close his office and see no one for three hours.]</p>

<p>[His second response has not yet been determined.]</p>

<p>[Enjoy the quiet.]</p>

<p>[It has approximately thirty-six hours left.]</p>

<p>Boom!</p>

<p>The fuck?</p>

<p>The attack arrived during the third hour of the night.</p>

<p>Morvak didn’t strike at the fortified pass or a legitimate military target.</p>

<p>He aimed straight for Sylvandar.</p>

<p>Specifically, he targeted the quiet manor in the old district where a red-haired warlord who embarrassed his empire was currently sleeping.</p>

<p>This retaliation was not eight hundred arrogant cavalrymen.</p>

<p>It was forty silent professionals.</p>

<p>They wore no insignias and carried no crimson armor.</p>

<p>Dressed in midnight fabrics and wielding poisoned blades, they were the tools an empire deployed when they wanted a problem solved without anyone taking credit.</p>

<p>They were paid purely for outcomes, not honorable engagements.</p>

<p>Grieselda noticed them first.</p>

<p>Awake long before the first grappling hook caught the stone wall, the intricate mark on her chest pulsed with a burning rhythm.</p>

<p>It only did that when hostile intent closed in.</p>

<p>Making no sound at all, she rose from her chair, gliding gracefully down the hall to Kane’s bedroom door.</p>

<p>Pulling the door open, she found him already standing.</p>

<p>He felt her approaching before her hand even touched the handle.</p>

<p>&quot;How many?&quot; Kane asked smoothly.</p>

<p>It was not a question of if, just a matter of logistics.</p>

<p>She held up four pale fingers.</p>

<p>Closed her fist.</p>

<p>Then held up four fingers again.</p>

<p>Forty.</p>

<p>&quot;Positions,&quot; he shouted, stretching his neck.</p>
