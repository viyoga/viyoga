<p>Elves and household fighters scrambled aside as the elephant crashed into the wreckage of its own making, tusks gouging stone, legs kicking once in a final reflex before the yellow light in its ruined eyes went out.</p>

<p>The corrupted nearest the body climbed over it without mourning.</p>

<p>The gap in the gate, at least, stopped getting wider.</p>

<p>Kane dropped from the carcass, breathing hard.</p>

<p>The system flicked a brief combat summary at the edge of his vision: load tolerated, no structural failure, strain within limits.</p>

<p>He ignored it.</p>

<p>Thora arrived at his shoulder, blood freckled across one cheek, compact axe dark.</p>

<p>&quot;The flood is still coming.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I know.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s gaze lifted past the broken gate, past the yellow-eyed tide, past the smoking piles of the first wave, into the tree line where the elephant had come from.</p>

<p>Something stood there.</p>

<p>The shape in the tree line resolved into a woman.</p>

<p>Kane knew her at once: the scarred lip, the weight of authority that had held a painted ring from loosening on him when he still had an empty hand and an offer instead of a swing.</p>

<p>She stood between the trunks the way the heat-haze presence had stood, except this time the body was solid.</p>

<p>Her eyes were completely white.</p>

<p>No iris. No pupil.</p>

<p>Just flat, milk-bright surfaces fixed on him across the slaughter yard and the broken gate.</p>

<p>Possession.</p>

<p>Something older sitting inside a borrowed frame and looking out.</p>

<p>Kane raised Mjoldr.</p>

<p>The woman fainted before the axe could leave his hand.</p>

<p>She folded without a cry, as if whatever had been holding her upright had simply let go.</p>

<p>At the same instant, every corrupted tribesman inside the gate and on the approach went down in a single, ugly wave.</p>

<p>Bodies dropped mid-climb, mid-bite, mid-scream.</p>

<p>The yellow light in their eyes snuffed out together.</p>

<p>Chest motion stopped.</p>

<p>The sudden absence of the roar left a hole in the air that made the remaining elven shouts sound too loud.</p>

<p>&quot;What?&quot; an elven sergeant got out, bow still drawn on nothing.</p>

<p>&quot;Did they die?&quot; another voice called from the wall.</p>

<p>&quot;All of them—did they just—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Hold your lines,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>He was already moving.</p>

<p>&quot;No one leaves the arch. No one touches a body until we know whether this is finished or only paused.&quot;</p>

<p>He jumped down from the elephant’s carcass into the yard, stepped over the newly fallen, and walked toward the tree line where the scarred woman had collapsed.</p>

<p>Thora fell in at his shoulder without being asked.</p>

<p>Griselda took height again.</p>

<p>The rest of the household held the breach as the elves stared at a battlefield that had stopped fighting back.</p>

<p>The woman was still breathing when Kane reached her.</p>

<p>Barely.</p>

<p>Her neck had already been torn open: deep, ragged, the kind of bite that should have ended her long before she walked out of the forest with white eyes.</p>

<p>Blood soaked the dirt under her head.</p>

<p>The white film over her eyes was fading in patches, ordinary color bleeding back in broken pieces.</p>

<p>When her gaze found his face, there was a person in it again for a moment, not a passenger.</p>

<p>&quot;Who?&quot; Kane asked.</p>

<p>Her mouth worked.</p>

<p>The word came out wet and thin.</p>

<p>&quot;De—devils...&quot;</p>

<p>The breath after it rattled.</p>

<p>Her eyes fixed on a point past his shoulder and stopped tracking.</p>

<p>The chest under his hand rose once more and did not rise again.</p>

<p>Kane stayed crouched there longer than the tactical situation deserved.</p>

<p>A small, useless guilt moved through him.</p>

<p>If he had stayed in the belt a few more hours, if he had pushed the scarred woman harder instead of walking away with an offer and a mark on a tree, some of the band might still be breathing on the correct side of a gate.</p>

<p>He set the thought aside the same way he set aside extraction locks and pod questions.</p>

<p>He was not in a position to undo the morning.</p>

<p>He was in a position to stop the next one from being worse.</p>

<p>&quot;Thora. Pull our people into a secure cordon around the bodies. No one loots. No one burns until Griselda and the city warders say the essence is inert.&quot;</p>

<p>He raised his voice for the elven captain nearest the gap.</p>

<p>&quot;You will send a fast rider to Menual under my seal. Message is simple: no new arrivals from the forest belts are taken inside without full eye-and-vein checks. Any yellow film, any black tracking under the skin, any charm of the bone-circle pattern, quarantine and hold for my household’s word. If they refuse the check, they do not enter. Repeat it back.&quot;</p>

<p>The captain repeated it, already signaling for a runner.</p>

<p>Misha had come down from the second ring. Her barrier work had left a fine tremor in her fingers that she ignored.</p>

<p>&quot;The Queen needs this before the rumors grow legs,&quot; she said.</p>

<p>&quot;White eyes. Mass drop. A possessed speaker naming devils on our doorstep. If the World Tree is the first target they have always wanted, then this was not a random emptying of the belt. It was a probe that spent a whole people to see how fast we close a gate.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane nodded once.</p>

<p>&quot;Then she hears it from us, not from a panicked ward report. Firlia, with me when I go up. Everyone else holds the yard and starts the count of the living.&quot;</p>

<p>Sira dropped from her tree, bow still in hand, orange hair full of leaf litter and fury.</p>

<p>&quot;The boat?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Delayed,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;We do not sail to the Giants while Sylvander’s outer approach is a corpse pile and the forest is still answering to something that can wear a woman’s face. We clean this. We brief the Queen. Then we decide how much of the clock we still own.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa wiped black-tinged blood from her gauntlets onto a fallen hide, ears still flat.</p>

<p>&quot;Fun while it lasted. Next time the forest sends a party, I want the thing that was driving them, not just the driven.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;So do I,&quot; Kane said.</p>
