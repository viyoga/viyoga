<p>Kane’s eyes narrowed.</p>

<p>The figure on the stone didn’t move.</p>

<p>It sat with a posture that still remembered authority: spine long, hands resting on thighs that had once held a people through worse winters than this pit.</p>

<p>The face, what the light allowed, wasn’t the clean decay of the bone drifts around them.</p>

<p>It was held.</p>

<p>Preserved in the wrong way.</p>

<p>Eyes closed, or almost closed.</p>

<p>Mouth a flat line.</p>

<p>The same man the lower halls still named when they spoke of doors shut from the inside.</p>

<p>The same Elder who had walked into the dark with a stone and a sentence about ending the lesson.</p>

<p>Supposed to be dead.</p>

<p>Sitting above the thing that should have finished him.</p>

<p>One of the elves whispered, barely air, &quot;He is not breathing.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;He does not need to,&quot; the hammer-Giant said, and there was horror in it that had nothing to do with battle.</p>

<p>&quot;Not if the stone is doing what Givan’s stones did. Not if the pit learned him the way it learned the ones we threw.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane didn’t step closer yet.</p>

<p>He held the red light steady and let the system’s edge paint what it could: dense taint at the seated figure, warmer core beneath, no immediate lunge-signature, a pressure that felt less like a body and more like a door that had decided to wear a face.</p>

<p>&quot;Elder Ragnar,&quot; he said, not as a greeting, as a fact checked against the dark.</p>

<p>&quot;The one who stayed behind.&quot;</p>

<p>The quieter Giant swallowed hard enough to be heard.</p>

<p>&quot;If that is still him, land-walker, then the door never shut. It only learned how to wait in a chair.&quot;</p>

<p>The warm stone pulsed once, slow, like a heart that had forgotten it was supposed to stop.</p>

<p>Around them the bone drifts stayed still.</p>

<p>The statues kept their empty watch.</p>

<p>The five-hundred-metre throat above held the rope and the thin circle of sky.</p>

<p>And fifty metres ahead, on a seat of patient wrong heat, the previous Elder of the island sat without breath, without blink, and without any promise that the years had made him safe to approach.</p>

<p>Kane adjusted his grip on Mjoldr.</p>

<p>&quot;No one speaks to it,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>He looked at the seated Elder for a long moment.</p>

<p>Then he threw Mjoldr.</p>

<p>Not a careful toss.</p>

<p>The full commitment of arm and shoulder and the weight he used when he meant a weapon to arrive as a verdict, or the performance of one.</p>

<p>The axe left his hand in a hard red arc. The Giants gasped as one. The elves flinched. The blade spun through the thick air, effortless and exact, so close to Elder Ragnar’s still face that the red light painted the closed lids and the flat mouth and the lines sixty-five years should have softened.</p>

<p>It didn’t touch him.</p>

<p>Mjoldr curved as if the dark itself had handed it back, returned on the same force, and slapped into Kane’s palm with a sound that was too loud in a place that had been saving its noise.</p>

<p>Through the entire flight and return, the Giant on the stone didn’t move once.</p>

<p>Not a twitch of a finger.</p>

<p>Not the smallest acknowledgment that death had passed a finger’s width from the bridge of his nose.</p>

<p>Kane settled the axe and didn’t look away from the seated shape.</p>

<p>&quot;How long has it been since he’s dead?&quot;</p>

<p>The quieter escort’s voice came out thin.</p>

<p>&quot;Sixty-five years. Root-Binder walked into the primary with the warmest of Givan’s stones and a sentence about shutting the door. We held the mourning. We stopped using his chair in the lower hall. Sixty-five years.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane nodded once.</p>

<p>The body said otherwise.</p>

<p>Unlike the drifts around them: bone to bone, soft parts long gone, years pressed flat, Elder Ragnar sat in a preservation that had nothing to do with care and everything to do with the stone beneath him.</p>

<p>No collapse of cheek.</p>

<p>The hands on the thighs still looked like hands that could close on a hammer.</p>

<p>Worms moved in the damp at the base of the rise; they did not climb his boots.</p>

<p>They didn’t touch his eyes.</p>

<p>The pit had kept him the way a door keeps a hinge.</p>

<p>&quot;That is not sixty-five years of ordinary dead,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;That is a seat. The stone is doing the holding. We do not test the face again. We take the stone, and we leave while the rope still answers.&quot;</p>

<p>One of the elves swallowed.</p>

<p>&quot;And if the stone does not want to leave?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then we learn that before we put our hands on anything else.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane started forward.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay in the light. No one speaks to him. Spear and hammer, watch the drifts. If a bone moves before we touch the core, you call it.&quot;</p>

<p>They approached.</p>

<p>The red circle of Mjoldr’s light shrank the distance in careful steps. The air grew heavier with each one. The Giants’ breathing turned rough; the elves’ faces had gone the colour of old parchment.</p>

<p>Twenty metres.</p>

<p>Ten.</p>

<p>The warm stone filled the last of the light: brimming, patient, the same family of wrong heat Griselda had named on a Sylvander courtyard.</p>

<p>It sat under Ragnar’s weight as if the Elder were only another layer of seal.</p>

<p>Kane stopped at the edge of the rise.</p>

<p>&quot;I take it alone,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;You hold the circle. If I say cut, you cut the rope of this idea, and you run for the throat. Clear?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;...Clear,&quot; the hammer-Giant managed.</p>

<p>Kane stepped onto the rise.</p>

<p>The heat came up through his boots in a slow, continuous wave.</p>

<p>Up close, Elder Ragnar smelled of stone and something sweetly metallic that did not belong to any living blood.</p>

<p>The closed eyes didn’t flicker.</p>

<p>Kane kept Mjoldr in his right hand and reached with his left, not for the Elder, for the edge of the stone where it met the carved seat, where a gap might let a man lift what a Giant had thrown, and a pit had kept.</p>

<p>His fingers found the rim.</p>

<p>The stone was warmer than flesh.</p>

<p>It pulsed once against his palm, slow, like a heart that had forgotten it was supposed to stop.</p>

<p>Kane set his weight, Hybrid Lock a quiet door, and began to lift.</p>

<p>For a moment it came.</p>

<p>The warm core shifted.</p>

<p>Dust and old grit spilled from under Ragnar’s still thighs.</p>

<p>The Giants held their breath as if breath could steady the dark. The elves’ knuckles were white on spear and rope-sense.</p>

<p>Kane’s shoulder burned with the angle and the wrong weight, not heavy like rock, heavy like attention.</p>

<p>Then the stone broke.</p>

<p>’Huh?’</p>

<p>Elder’s eyes snapped open.</p>
