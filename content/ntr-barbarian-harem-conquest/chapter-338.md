<p>The outer gate was gone.</p>

<p>Yellow-eyed tribes poured through the broken timbers in a continuous flood, climbing over their own dead, biting anything that still moved with ordinary fear.</p>

<p>&quot;HOLD THEM OFF!&quot;</p>

<p>Elven line fighters met them in the arch and the first yard with spears and short blades, working in tight pairs, calling targets by the color of the eyes alone.</p>

<p>Civilians were already running deeper into the districts: parents dragging children, merchants abandoning carts, wardens shouting routes toward the second ring and the high shelters.</p>

<p>The sound of the city changing shape under panic rolled out ahead of the fighting.</p>

<p>Thora moved through the breach like she had been born in it.</p>

<p>The compact axe rose and fell in clean arcs.</p>

<p>She didn’t chase.</p>

<p>She held a stretch of broken stone wide enough for three elves to stand behind her, and anything yellow-eyed that entered that stretch died.</p>

<p>A painted man lunged with broken nails.</p>

<p>She took his arm at the elbow and his head on the return.</p>

<p>Another tried to climb past her on a fallen beam.</p>

<p>She kicked the beam out and finished him on the ground without looking down for long.</p>

<p>&quot;Left side is thickening!&quot; an elven sergeant yelled.</p>

<p>&quot;I see it,&quot; Thora answered, already shifting.</p>

<p>&quot;Keep your people behind my shoulder and stop trying to be brave in front of me.&quot;</p>

<p>Further along the inner face of the ruined gate, Misha had pulled three city mages into a working knot. Her voice cut through the noise with the same precision she used on ledgers.</p>

<p>&quot;On my count. Barrier along the arch line, low and wide, not a dome. We do not have the reserves for a dome. One—two—now.&quot;</p>

<p>Mana sheeted across the broken threshold in a hard translucent band.</p>

<p>The next surge of corrupted bodies hit it and staggered.</p>

<p>Some burned where the working touched open wounds.</p>

<p>The press stacked against the barrier instead of pouring freely into the yard, buying the ground fighters seconds that mattered.</p>

<p>&quot;Hold it,&quot; Misha said.</p>

<p>&quot;If it starts to crack, you tell me before it fails, not after. Firlia—get the rear lanes clear and keep the Queen’s runners moving. No one blocks the stairs with baggage.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia’s answering shout was already half-lost in the noise as she ran.</p>

<p>Above the nearest inner court, Sira had put herself in a tree that overlooked the breach.</p>

<p>Orange hair tied back hard, new bow up, she picked targets with spiteful accuracy: eyes, open mouths, the occasional climber trying to crest a side wall.</p>

<p>Shaft after shaft dropped bodies that had been about to flank the elven knots.</p>

<p>She put one heavy arrow into the elephant’s shoulder.</p>

<p>The shaft stuck and hung.</p>

<p>The animal didn’t slow.</p>

<p>&quot;Of course,&quot; Sira muttered, already nocking again.</p>

<p>&quot;Of course the giant nightmare has skin like a fortress. Fine. I will just kill everything around it until someone with an axe does something useful.&quot;</p>

<p>On the left wall walk, Kessa had stopped pretending this was only defense.</p>

<p>She dropped into a cluster of corrupted tribesmen with both gauntlets live, grabbed the first by the skull, and smashed him into the second hard enough that both went down in a broken heap.</p>

<p>A third reached for her.</p>

<p>She caught his arm, twisted, and used him as a flail against the ones behind.</p>

<p>Tiger-striped and grinning, she moved through the press like the violence had been waiting for her specifically.</p>

<p>&quot;Hehe... this is fun.&quot;</p>

<p>She drove a gauntleted fist through a yellow-eyed woman’s chest and flung the body off the walk.</p>

<p>&quot;Come on then. Climb faster. I am getting bored of the slow ones.&quot;</p>

<p>A horn answered her from deeper in the city: high, structured, the formal call for full knight deployment.</p>

<p>Sirens followed, rolling from tower to tower, waking every district that had not already heard the gate break.</p>

<p>Elven knights in heavier plate began arriving in ranked groups, shields locked, pushing into the yard to relieve the first exhausted line fighters.</p>

<p>Kane stood in the middle of the breach with a headless corpse still sliding off Mjoldr’s edge.</p>

<p>He had cut a path.</p>

<p>It was not enough.</p>

<p>For every yellow-eyed body that fell, two more shoved through the wreckage.</p>

<p>And behind them all, the elephant kept working the broken gate like a living ram: tusks hooking timber, bulk shoving the gap wider, bloody plates turning arrows into decoration.</p>

<p>Each time it surged, another section of the arch crumbled and another dozen corrupted poured inward.</p>

<p>Thora’s voice reached him over the slaughter.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane! That animal is doing more damage than the entire first wave! If it keeps opening the wall, we lose the second ring before the knights finish forming!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I know.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane planted a boot on the dead tribesman, tore Mjoldr free, and looked at the elephant properly.</p>

<p>Arrows and ordinary mana flares were a waste.</p>

<p>A frontal cut would bounce.</p>

<p>He needed weight, angle, and a place the corruption had not bothered to armor.</p>

<p>&quot;Griselda!&quot; he shouted upward.</p>

<p>&quot;When I move, you put everything you have into its eyes! Not the body—the eyes! Blind it for three seconds!&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda’s wings snapped wide on the high perch.</p>

<p>&quot;On your mark!&quot;</p>

<p>Kane ran straight at the elephant.</p>

<p>The animal saw him and screamed, high and wrong, swinging a tusk in a horizontal arc meant to take a man in half.</p>

<p>Kane slid under the sweep close enough to feel the wind of it, came up along the beast’s left foreleg, and did not swing for the hide.</p>

<p>He swung for the back of the knee joint where the plating folded.</p>

<p>Mjoldr’s spike drove in with full commitment and the system’s unrestricted strength behind it.</p>

<p>The joint gave with a structural crack.</p>

<p>The elephant staggered hard, screaming again, weight collapsing toward the injured side.</p>

<p>&quot;Now!&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda’s cast hit both eyes in a searing double flare.</p>

<p>The animal reared in pure reactive agony, half-blind, knee failing, head lifting high enough to expose the softer underside of the throat where the plates did not fully close.</p>

<p>Kane was already climbing.</p>

<p>He used the lodged spike as a step, hauled himself up the shoulder with his good hand and the axe haft, and brought Mjoldr around in a two-handed descending cut aimed at the throat gap.</p>

<p>The edge went in deep.</p>

<p>Black-tinged arterial spray erupted across the broken gate stones.</p>

<p>The elephant’s scream turned into a bubbling roar.</p>

<p>Kane ripped the axe free and cut again in the same line, deeper, until the head listed and the whole impossible bulk started to go down.</p>

<p>&quot;Clear the fall line!&quot; he yelled.</p>
