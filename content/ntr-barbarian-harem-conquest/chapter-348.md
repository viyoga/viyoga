<p>Kane’s eyes met the leviathan’s.</p>

<p>The skin around the great eye folded back in a deliberate motion.</p>

<p>A black vertical slit opened in a field of deep red, ancient and flat, and for a fraction of a second the thing under the water looked at him the way a craftsman looks at a tool that has wandered into the wrong workshop.</p>

<p>Then the head plunged.</p>

<p>The ridge of scales vanished as the pressure under the hull changed.</p>

<p>&quot;FUCK—FUCK—&quot;</p>

<p>Pain wasn’t the right word.</p>

<p>Recognition was.</p>

<p>The same cold arithmetic that had hit him under the serpent’s gaze and in Grathak’s arena: this was not a random sea beast.</p>

<p>This was a domain tool, and the domain had already decided the boat was inside it.</p>

<p>&quot;Move—move!&quot;</p>

<p>Thora stared at him from the stay, bracelet still ticking against her ankle.</p>

<p>&quot;I have never seen you so—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m not scared,&quot; Kane cut in.</p>

<p>&quot;I am counting how many ways this ends with us in the water. Helmsman—full canvas, any line that still has wind!&quot;</p>

<p>The boat didn’t answer the helm.</p>

<p>Canvas strained.</p>

<p>The tiller fought.</p>

<p>Elven crew threw their weight into braces and sheets.</p>

<p>Kessa slammed both gauntlets onto a stay and hauled with a snarl, tiger strength pouring into wood that refused to advance.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s... not moving.&quot;</p>

<p>The hull shuddered in place.</p>

<p>Five hundred meters of dark water still lay between them and the island’s cut.</p>

<p>The forty-foot corpse on the sand didn’t move.</p>

<p>Kane ran to the bow.</p>

<p>Below the stem, something held them, not a current, not a snag.</p>

<p>A length of the leviathan’s body had come up under the keel and simply stopped being water.</p>

<p>Mass against mass.</p>

<p>The boat was a toy on a muscle that didn’t care about sails.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s under us!&quot; he shouted.</p>

<p>&quot;Full length along the keel—don’t give it a clean lift!&quot;</p>

<p>The muscle moved, making the boat jerk backward.</p>

<p>Distance to the island opened in a sick slide.</p>

<p>Everyone on deck stumbled.</p>

<p>Firlia went to one knee and held a crate by pure stubbornness.</p>

<p>One of the elven warriors recovered first, ripped a spear from the rack, and drove it down over the rail into the dark shape under the surface.</p>

<p>The point hit scaled hide and skipped as if it had struck wet stone.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s strong,&quot; the elf got out, already reaching for another shaft.</p>

<p>&quot;Spears will not hurt it,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>’Rage is useless in open water. I cannot stand on what I need to kill.’</p>

<p>Misha’s face was white under the salt spray, but her voice stayed level.</p>

<p>&quot;What do you suggest?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Leave.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;How?&quot;</p>

<p>Before he could answer, the water ahead of the bow rose.</p>

<p>Not the full leviathan.</p>

<p>A lesser head, or a projection of the same will, broke the surface in a column of scale and muscle two and a third meters high.</p>

<p>Dragon-like in the structure of the face, no wings, no forelimbs, body continuing down into the dark in a single thick rope of strength.</p>

<p>It looked down at the deck.</p>

<p>At the household.</p>

<p>At the elves.</p>

<p>Breath caught across the boat in a shared, involuntary silence.</p>

<p>Thora’s jaw locked.</p>

<p>Misha’s hand found the rail hard enough to hurt.</p>

<p>Kessa’s ears were flat, and her lips were peeled back from her teeth in a snarl that had no target she could reach.</p>

<p>The head’s eyes found Kane.</p>

<p>Its mouth opened.</p>

<p>Light in the color of deep water under storm began to gather along the palate and tongue.</p>

<p>A beam or a cone or a simple erasure aimed at the place where the small wooden thing and the red-haired man still insisted on existing.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t swing at it.</p>

<p>He did three things in the order the system and the boat would allow.</p>

<p>&quot;Griselda—flare, now, in its eyes, not the body! Misha—signal the cliffs, any fire, any flag, guest right or help, I do not care which! Helmsman, when the weight under the keel slips, you run for the cut, and you do not look back!&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda, still fighting the wrong air above the mast line, folded her wings once and dumped a searing double cast straight into the risen head’s eyes.</p>

<p>The working in its mouth stuttered.</p>

<p>Its head reared, vision burned white, and the muscle under the boat reacted on pure reflex: tightening, then shifting to protect the blinded end.</p>

<p>The keel came free for a heartbeat.</p>

<p>&quot;NOW!&quot;</p>

<p>Canvas took the wind.</p>

<p>The boat surged forward as if released from a hand.</p>

<p>The risen head slammed sideways, still half-blind, and the main bulk under the surface rolled to follow.</p>

<p>Kane was already at the rail with Mjoldr reversed.</p>

<p>He didn’t try to kill what could not be killed from a deck.</p>

<p>He slammed the axe spike into the only part of the lesser head still in range, the softer hinge where jaw met neck column, and put a single, ugly, structural wound into the joint.</p>

<p>Black-tinged fluid sprayed.</p>

<p>The head screamed in a register that made the bracelet on Thora’s ankle shriek in answer and then pulled under hard enough to rock the hull again.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t celebrate!&quot; Kane snarled.</p>

<p>&quot;It will come back along the stern. Kessa, watch the wake! Firlia, if anything comes over the rail, you cut the line, and you let the cargo go!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I am not losing the medical wraps!&quot; Firlia shot back, already re-lashing with bleeding fingers.</p>

<p>&quot;You will lose the wraps, or you will lose the people who need them. Choose fast.&quot;</p>

<p>The island’s cut was closing: four hundred meters, three-fifty.</p>

<p>On the cliffs, the watching Giants had finally moved.</p>

<p>One of them was descending a path that had not been visible from the water, spear in hand, soot still on its face.</p>

<p>The tired voice from before didn’t call down.</p>

<p>It only came, as if the sea’s hunger had finally crossed some line the stone was willing to answer.</p>

<p>Thora dragged herself along the stay until she was level with Kane, ribs screaming, bracelet frantic.</p>

<p>&quot;You said you were not scared.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I am not. I didn’t want to start a losing battle.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Was that just a sea monster?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane looked around to see the Leviathan not moving anymore under the water.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s a familiar. Of the gods.&quot;</p>
