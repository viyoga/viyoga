<p>One of the vines moved first.</p>

<p>It climbed the trunk in a slow, deliberate curl, passed the empty medical kit, and settled around Firlia’s throat with the care of something that had practiced the gesture.</p>

<p>The coil tightened.</p>

<p>&quot;Gah—&quot;</p>

<p>Her breath cut off.</p>

<p>The administrative calm shattered into pure animal panic for half a second before she forced it down again, jaw locked, eyes still on Kane.</p>

<p>Kessa moved first.</p>

<p>There was no warning growl.</p>

<p>Her eyes went dark, pupils blown wide, and she launched across the broken ground with a snarl that belonged in the deep forest of Berakamon.</p>

<p>Two vines shot from opposite sides of the trunk to meet her.</p>

<p>She slashed the first with the cracked edge of her remaining gauntlet, opened a long black seam that wept sap thick as tar, then bit the second clean through.</p>

<p>Bitter fluid filled her mouth.</p>

<p>She spat it out and kept coming.</p>

<p>The tree answered with its branches.</p>

<p>A heavy limb swung out of the canopy like a club.</p>

<p>It caught Kessa across the ribs and shoulders and flung her backward through the air.</p>

<p>BAM!</p>

<p>She hit the wet stone hard, slid, and fetched up against a slab of broken masonry.</p>

<p>&quot;FUCK!&quot;</p>

<p>The shout tore out of her raw.</p>

<p>She was already trying to push up when the vine around Firlia’s throat loosened of its own accord.</p>

<p>Firlia dragged in a desperate, ragged breath.</p>

<p>Colour flooded back into her face in an ugly rush of red.</p>

<p>The tree waited. The remaining vines flexed once, almost politely, as if giving Kane time to understand the rules.</p>

<p>Then it touched her.</p>

<p>A thinner tendril slid across the upper curve of her breast, slow, mapping the shape under the torn fabric of her wrap.</p>

<p>Another followed the line of her ribs downward, found the soft junction of her thighs, and pressed with deliberate, exploratory pressure against the heat between them.</p>

<p>Firlia’s body jerked once against the binding.</p>

<p>A small, involuntary sound escaped her before she locked her teeth again.</p>

<p>The tendril began to push inward—</p>

<p>The hammer left Kane’s hand like a thrown spear.</p>

<p>Mass roared up the engraved head in a single violent spike.</p>

<p>[Mass multiplier: 10× – Maximum]</p>

<p>[Warning: Host skeletal stress critical]</p>

<p>[Impact projected: Structural]</p>

<p>The hammer hit the main supporting branch that held Firlia’s weight.</p>

<p>Wood that should have been denser than iron exploded in a spray of black sap and splinters.</p>

<p>The entire upper fork of the tree shuddered.</p>

<p>Vines recoiled in a thrashing wave.</p>

<p>Firlia dropped half a body-length before the remaining coils caught her again, tighter, angry.</p>

<p>Kane was already running.</p>

<p>He closed the distance in a flat sprint, no technique, no measured approach: just the raw forward drive of a man who had watched one too many things put hands on what he had sworn to keep breathing.</p>

<p>A vine whipped for his ankles.</p>

<p>He stamped through it.</p>

<p>Another came for his throat.</p>

<p>He caught it in his left hand, ignored the way the barbs opened his palm, and ripped it out of the trunk by sheer strength.</p>

<p>Black fluid sprayed across his chest and the open rake along his ribs.</p>

<p>&quot;Kessa—left side, keep it busy!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Working on it—&quot;</p>

<p>She was back on her feet, limping, striped face streaked with sap and blood.</p>

<p>She threw herself at the lower coils that still held the three empty-eyed elves, tearing with teeth and hands, buying seconds.</p>

<p>The Giant children had dragged the wounded warrior further back and now stood between him and the tree with the stubborn stillness of people who had decided they would die before they ran again.</p>

<p>Kane reached the trunk.</p>

<p>A thick vine lashed around his waist and tried to haul him upward into the canopy.</p>

<p>He let it lift him just far enough to get both hands on the section that still pinned Firlia’s arms, then drove his knee into the wood hard enough to crack it.</p>

<p>The coil loosened.</p>

<p>Firlia’s left arm came free.</p>

<p>She immediately reached for the tendril still pressed between her thighs and ripped it away with a sound of pure, focused disgust.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I know. Hold still.&quot;</p>

<p>He braced one foot against the trunk, tore the hammer free of the ruined branch it had lodged in, and swung again—short, brutal arcs aimed at every point of connection he could reach.</p>

<p>Mass stayed high only for the instants of impact.</p>

<p>The tree screamed in a register that was more pressure than sound.</p>

<p>Vines whipped across his back and shoulders, opening new lines of blood.</p>

<p>One wrapped his thigh and squeezed until the muscle went numb.</p>

<p>He ignored it and kept cutting.</p>

<p>Firlia’s right arm came free.</p>

<p>Then her legs.</p>

<p>The last coil around her waist tightened in spite, digging into soft flesh hard enough to bruise before Kane put the hammer through its base and the whole length went slack.</p>

<p>She fell.</p>

<p>He caught her with his free arm, staggered under the sudden weight, and went to one knee in the black-sap mud at the foot of the trunk.</p>

<p>Firlia’s green hair stuck to his chest and throat.</p>

<p>She was shaking: adrenaline, pressure from the vines, the particular humiliation of having been handled like inventory, but her eyes were clear and furious.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re hurt...,&quot; she got out.</p>

<p>&quot;Lower limb. Left. There are still wraps—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Later.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane stood with her against him and started backing away from the trunk.</p>

<p>Kessa fell in on his other side, one arm hooked under Firlia’s for extra support, still spitting sap.</p>

<p>Behind them, the tree changed.</p>

<p>The remaining branches folded inward with a wet, deliberate sound. Bark split along vertical seams.</p>

<p>Something larger than the trunk itself began to push up from the root system, shoulder plates of denser wood, a suggestion of a head forming out of the canopy, vines thickening into limbs that ended in too many joints.</p>

<p>The three empty-eyed elves were drawn upward into the transforming mass and vanished without a cry. The red energy Kane had seen earlier climbed the new shape in visible veins.</p>

<p>Kessa’s ears went completely flat.</p>

<p>&quot;That is not a tree anymore.&quot;</p>
