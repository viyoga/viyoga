<p>Kane looked at Ragnar.</p>

<p>Rain fell in the colour of a wound, sheeting off both of them.</p>

<p>The quay was a running red mirror.</p>

<p>Thunder stood on the bruise-sky and didn’t move on.</p>

<p>&quot;What happened in the pit?&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s voice came out rough through the blood in his mouth.</p>

<p>&quot;Why didn’t you make a move for sixty-five years?&quot;</p>

<p>The sky answered with another hard crack. The rain, which had been water tinted by the light, poured thicker now: red enough that where it met the flooded harbour it spread like a sea of blood under the colourless cliffs.</p>

<p>Ragnar’s remaining eye held him.</p>

<p>The dark socket still wept black-gold.</p>

<p>When he spoke, the deep voice was quieter than the storm and clearer than it had any right to be.</p>

<p>&quot;What do you think a ruler is?&quot;</p>

<p>Kane didn’t decorate it.</p>

<p>&quot;Someone who saves others.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar’s mouth moved.</p>

<p>Not the feeder’s scream-shape.</p>

<p>Something that might once have been a smile on a living Elder’s face.</p>

<p>&quot;Someone willing to take others’ burden.&quot;</p>

<p>He shifted his weight in the flood.</p>

<p>The stump of his arm hung useless.</p>

<p>&quot;The pit had nothing to do with it. Not at the start.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane went still in the flood, water to his waist, Mjoldr still in hand.</p>

<p>&quot;What?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I created the pit.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar’s gaze didn’t leave his.</p>

<p>&quot;I found the stone’s corruption beginning, carried in from the beast lands, the same family you have been chasing across empires. Warm stones. Circle marks. A patience that does not belong to ordinary death. I had the feeders cut. I had the primary sunk. I told the halls we were throwing enemies and traitors into a dark that would hold them.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane listened carefully.</p>

<p>Ragnar continued.</p>

<p>&quot;That was true. It was not the whole truth. I made the mouths to keep the corruption off our posts and out of our children’s eyes.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;But—?&quot;</p>

<p>Kane already knew.</p>

<p>He asked anyway.</p>

<p>&quot;But the corruption got me first.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar’s single blinking eye dropped to the red water, then lifted again.</p>

<p>&quot;I went down with the warmest of the carriers to shut the door from the inside. The door shut on me instead. I couldn’t let it go free. Could not walk back up and paint the lower halls with what I had become. Could not let everyone die. Especially not my granddaughter.&quot;</p>

<p>His remaining eye locked past Kane’s shoulder, up the cut, to where the Stone-Mother stood in the red rain with her hammer and her warders, too far to hear, close enough to be seen.</p>

<p>The heavier tiredness on her face was, for a moment, only a woman’s fear for a shore that would not stop drowning.</p>

<p>&quot;She doesn’t know?&quot; Kane asked.</p>

<p>&quot;She doesn’t.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar’s voice roughened.</p>

<p>&quot;She was a child when I walked into the primary. She grew into the halls believing Root-Binder died holding a line. That is the only version that doesn’t put the pit’s grammar in her blood by story alone. Let her keep it.&quot;</p>

<p>He stood.</p>

<p>The motion cost him.</p>

<p>Black-gold ran from under his jaw where Kane’s spike had gone deep.</p>

<p>One eye burned.</p>

<p>One was dark.</p>

<p>Around them, on the flooded slabs and the lower path, the last scraps of the bone-column still twitched, yellow light guttering, not yet finished.</p>

<p>&quot;Time’s up, barbarian.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar raised his remaining hand.</p>

<p>Somewhere under the red water, the engraved hammer stirred, then tore free of the flood and slapped into his palm as if the years had never taken it.</p>

<p>&quot;The sky turning red was just the beginning. What answers that light does not sit in a chair. It does not wear an Elder’s face. You have broken a hinge. The heart still drinks. When you go to the primary, you will not find another me. You will find the reason I stayed under for sixty-five years.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane didn’t lift Mjoldr.</p>

<p>&quot;You could still climb. Tell her. Let the halls decide.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And give the pit a voice in the lower rooms?&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar shook his head once.</p>

<p>&quot;No. I have one useful thing left.&quot;</p>

<p>He turned on the drowned quay and walked toward the remaining undead.</p>

<p>Not toward Kane.</p>

<p>Toward the yellow sockets still crawling in the red rain.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer rose and fell: tidy, final, the same refusal he had shown when bone-clusters came too close to his attention on the tide-line.</p>

<p>Each strike ended a light.</p>

<p>Each body that had worn a familiar mark went still for the last time.</p>

<p>&quot;Forgive... me, comrades. Let me rest you in peace.&quot;</p>

<p>He didn’t use their names.</p>

<p>He only put the pit’s last climbing scraps down so they would not follow him or turn back toward the children’s ledges while the living were watching the sky.</p>

<p>When the quay was quiet of yellow, Ragnar looked once more at Kane.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t tell her I made the mouths,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;That is enough truth for a morning like this.&quot;</p>

<p>Then he walked into the sea.</p>

<p>The deeper green beyond the bollards, where the Hydra had withdrawn when the sky went wrong.</p>

<p>Red rain sheeted off his shoulders.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer hung in his one hand.</p>

<p>He didn’t run, and he did not hesitate. He waded until the water took his chest, his shoulders, the ruin of his arm, and the yellow of his remaining eye was a single point in a sea that had turned the colour of a wound.</p>

<p>The Hydra came up to meet him.</p>

<p>Not in rage.</p>

<p>In the same flat, domain hunger it had shown the bone-column, only this time the meal walked into the jaw on purpose.</p>

<p>Coils rose.</p>

<p>A head the size of a cart opened.</p>

<p>Ragnar didn’t raise the hammer against it.</p>

<p>He only stood in the red water and let the mouth close.</p>

<p>The sound was brief under the thunder.</p>

<p>Black-gold and ordinary dark mixed in the foam.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer slipped free and sank before anyone on the cut could mark where.</p>

<p>The Hydra’s head shook once, the way it had shaken his hand, and then drove under again, away from the shore, away from the bruise-sky, a god’s dog that had been fed a door and found the meal heavier than bone.</p>

<p>On the cut, the Stone-Mother’s voice broke across the rain.</p>

<p>A name she hadn’t used in the open halls for a long time, raw and unfinished, as if some part of her had felt the hinge finally leave the frame.</p>

<p>Kane stood in the flooding quay with Mjoldr hanging in a burned hand.</p>

<p>The sky had started bleeding.</p>
