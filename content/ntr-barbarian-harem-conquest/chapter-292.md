<p>Beast warriors who had stared with open contempt or hungry calculation now inclined their heads as he passed.</p>

<p>Some greeted him outright—&quot;Barbarian,&quot; spoken without the earlier sneer, sometimes even with a short nod of acknowledgment.</p>

<p>A pair of southern-aligned fighters stepped aside on a narrow bridge and waited until he had crossed before continuing.</p>

<p>The scent of the deep forest still lingered faintly in the pre-dawn air from the previous evening’s manifestation, but the atmosphere around Kane himself had shifted.</p>

<p>He was no longer simply an interesting outsider. He was the man who had stood through three Measures and forced the Guardian’s fragment to give ground.</p>

<p>That fact traveled faster than any formal announcement.</p>

<p>He found Marisa on a low stone terrace that overlooked the eastern approaches of the capital.</p>

<p>She sat in a simple chair near the rail, dark wrap drawn close against the morning chill, long black hair loose. She didn’t look surprised when his footsteps approached. She had clearly expected him.</p>

<p>Kane stopped a few paces away and studied her profile for a moment.</p>

<p>Am I already moving inside the tracks of her visions even when I think I am choosing?</p>

<p>The thought sat uncomfortably.</p>

<p>Every major decision since the road, arriving alone, the style of the first two Measures, the way he had forced the manifestation to acknowledge him, could be read as his own will.</p>

<p>It could also be read as the variable she had selected simply performing the role the visions required.</p>

<p>He sat on the stone rail beside her chair.</p>

<p>The city below was still mostly dark, pinpricks of torchlight moving as early runners and patrols began their circuits.</p>

<p>&quot;You didn’t sleep,&quot; Marisa said.</p>

<p>It wasn’t a question.</p>

<p>&quot;Can’t.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Finally afraid?&quot;</p>

<p>Kane turned his head and looked at her fully.</p>

<p>The political mask was present but thinner in the grey light.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m thinking about the fallout.&quot;</p>

<p>Marisa waited.</p>

<p>The silence stretched long enough for a distant horn to sound from one of the outer posts.</p>

<p>When Kane spoke again, his voice was quieter, stripped of the public edge he had carried in the arenas.</p>

<p>&quot;You say leaving him alive spoils every future that still has packs left to fight. I say killing him the day after he opened formal talks risks turning half the banners that just arrived into enemies. Both can be true at the same time. What I can’t decide is how much of your certainty is the visions and how much is simply the cleanest way to get the throne without your brother’s bulk still sitting on it.&quot;</p>

<p>Marisa’s expression did not change, but something colder moved behind her eyes.</p>

<p>&quot;You think I am lying to you.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I think you want him dead more than you want any coalition. The visions are convenient for that. They may also be accurate. I have no clean way to separate the two.&quot;</p>

<p>She looked out over the dark city for a long moment before answering.</p>

<p>&quot;If I only wanted the throne, I could have found a quieter knife years ago. I didn’t need a barbarian who carries an axe that bites divine-adjacent flesh. I needed a variable the visions could not fully predict, someone who could stand in front of the Guardian’s power and make the packs recalculate. You did that. The board is open because of it. The next piece is still the same piece I named on the road: my brother must not be the one holding the center when the sky breaks.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane rested his good hand on Mjoldr’s haft.</p>

<p>&quot;And if the deeper packs only stay in line because they still fear him? If removing him shatters the very strength we need for the seventy days?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then we discover whether the strength you showed yesterday is enough to hold them without his shadow.&quot;</p>

<p>Marisa’s voice stayed even.</p>

<p>&quot;That is the risk either way. Leave him alive, and the visions say the packs turn on each other the moment real pressure arrives. Kill him, and some will resist the transition. There is no path without blood. There is only the path that still has an empire left at the end of it.&quot;</p>

<p>A pair of early-duty warriors passed on the terrace below and openly inclined their heads toward Kane.</p>

<p>Marisa watched the small exchange with a faint, unreadable curve of her mouth.</p>

<p>&quot;You see it already. They greet you. They make space. That didn’t exist three days ago. It exists now because you forced a manifestation to withdraw under the pacts. That legitimacy is real. It is also fragile. My brother can still decide the talks are a useful delay and that the barbarian who wounded the forest’s power needs to be removed once the immediate spectacle is over. Entertaining him is not the same as binding him.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane was quiet for a time, watching the sky lighten by slow degrees.</p>

<p>&quot;I will sit through the first session. I will listen to how solid the support actually is when the pure-bloods start arguing, and the deeper banners start naming prices. After that we talk again about method and timing. I am not refusing the kill. I am refusing to hand you a corpse that costs us the army we came here to build.&quot;</p>

<p>Marisa turned her head and studied him.</p>

<p>&quot;You are still trying to keep every claim you have already made and add an entire beast throne without paying the full price in blood. That is the romanticism speaking again.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Call it whatever you want. I didn’t come here only to swap one tyrant for another and call it progress. I came here to put enough strength in one place that the sky breaking does not simply erase everything already tied to me. If that requires your brother’s death, then it requires it. If it requires keeping him breathing long enough to lock the deeper packs into a real commitment, then I will weigh that too.&quot;</p>

<p>Marisa’s amber eyes held his for a long, measuring stretch.</p>

<p>The first true light of morning touched the highest edges of the palace roofs behind them.</p>

<p>Somewhere in the lower courts horns sounded again, signaling the formal opening of the day’s gatherings.</p>

<p>&quot;You can try,&quot; she said at last.</p>

<p>&quot;Then we speak privately and without the others listening. I will not wait through weeks of talk while the window narrows. You have the support you demanded. I want the kill I named when I first put my body on the table. That bargain does not dissolve simply because the Measures went in your favor.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane stood.</p>

<p>The ache in his body was present but no longer limiting.</p>

<p>&quot;I’ll see you in the hall,&quot; he said.</p>
