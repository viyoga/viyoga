<p>Kane didn’t wait for the Giants to finish waking.</p>

<p>Stone-Mother was still on one knee, one hand braced on the stone, black water running from her mouth in thick strings.</p>

<p>Her eyes were clear.</p>

<p>The bone band in her hair sat crooked.</p>

<p>Around her, some of the white-eyed had folded and gone still for good.</p>

<p>Others dragged air like they had forgotten how lungs worked. The hall smelled of iron and blood when a superior stopped existing.</p>

<p>She looked up when his shadow crossed her.</p>

<p>&quot;Save your people,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;I will save mine. We meet when both lists are shorter.&quot;</p>

<p>Stone-Mother’s mouth twitched once: not a smile, not agreement, just the recognition of a man who had stopped explaining himself.</p>

<p>She nodded.</p>

<p>Then she turned toward the nearest Giant who could still stand and started giving orders in the low, hard language of the ridge.</p>

<p>No talk of the hammer, the flood, or the man who had broken the loop. The work had already moved on.</p>

<p>Kane left her to it.</p>

<p>The blood on the floor was running now, a steady pull toward the far wall. He followed it. The lower opening had not been there when he first stepped through the mirror.</p>

<p>It was there now: a dark throat in the stone, wide enough for a Giant to walk without stooping, exhaling air that was colder than the hall and older than the banquet tables above.</p>

<p>The taint from the succubus’s dying information sat at the edge of his attention like a half-remembered map.</p>

<p>Some of the women had been carried deeper before the blood hall sealed.</p>

<p>Some had been pulled into the outer waters when the Hydra fled the red sky. The strongest signal would tell him which problem to solve first.</p>

<p>He went through alone.</p>

<p>The passage sloped down, then sideways, then down again.</p>

<p>The walls were ribbed in a way that had nothing to do with tools.</p>

<p>Every few steps the engraved hammer’s head brushed stone and came away with a thin film of black dust that the primary seemed to shed like skin.</p>

<p>Kane kept the mass modulation low.</p>

<p>Rage was ash.</p>

<p>Life force sat at the critical edge the system had named and did not pretend otherwise.</p>

<p>The bites on his neck and the open rake across his ribs spoke every time he breathed.</p>

<p>He ignored them the way he ignored the ache low in his gut where the feeding link had been.</p>

<p>The link was gone. The women were not.</p>

<p>[Taint gradient: moderate → Rising along vector]</p>

<p>[Familiar signatures: fragmented – Three clusters]</p>

<p>[Strongest cluster: bearing 034 – Surface exit available]</p>

<p>The system’s voice was thinner than it had been before the mirror. He took the bearing and kept walking.</p>

<p>The passage ended in a crack rather than a door.</p>

<p>Kane pushed through into open air and the smell of wet stone and dead floodwater.</p>

<p>The island night was the same bruise-red it had been when the sky first turned. The water had dropped in places, leaving slick ledges and the broken shapes of houses that had not been built to survive a sea that climbed sideways.</p>

<p>Bodies lay where the surge had put them. Some were Giant. Some were not. None of them were the ones he was looking for.</p>

<p>He stood on the ledge long enough to let the system finish its sweep.</p>

<p>[Cluster A: weak, intermittent – East-northeast]</p>

<p>[Cluster B: moderate, moving – North along ridge]</p>

<p>[Cluster C: strong, contested – Northwest, ravine structure]</p>

<p>[Recommendation: Cluster C – Highest probability of active survival]</p>

<p>Northwest.</p>

<p>Kane adjusted his grip on the hammer and started down the wet stone toward the ravine the system had marked.</p>

<p>The path was not a path.</p>

<p>It was the memory of one, half-collapsed, littered with the things the water had not wanted to keep.</p>

<p>He moved without stealth.</p>

<p>Anything still hunting on this side of the island would already know something had killed a superior in the primary; silence would not change the arithmetic.</p>

<p>Halfway to the ravine floor, he found the first clear sign that was not wreckage.</p>

<p>Tracks.</p>

<p>Not the broad, dragging marks of the tentacle-wolves.</p>

<p>Smaller, clawed, deliberate, and recent enough that the wet stone had not yet filled them.</p>

<p>Beside the tracks, half-buried in silt, lay a gauntlet.</p>

<p>Kane picked it up.</p>

<p>Tiger-striped leather, reinforced at the knuckles, the left one split along a seam he had watched Kessa argue with a cobbler about in Sylvander.</p>

<p>The inside still held the heat of a hand that had been wearing it less than an hour ago.</p>

<p>Blood on the cuff—some of it hers, some of it not.</p>

<p>[Additional traces: Two smaller heat signatures – non-combatant scale]</p>

<p>She was alive. She was carrying or protecting something. She was moving toward the same ravine the system had flagged as contested.</p>

<p>Kane turned the gauntlet once in his hand, then tucked it into his belt and followed the tracks.</p>

<p>The ridge path that led down into the ravine didn’t exist on any map the Giants had shown him.</p>

<p>It had been made by the flood and by whatever had been using the fold in the stone as a holding ground.</p>

<p>The walls closed in. The red sky narrowed to a strip. At the bottom, water stood in a black pool that should have been empty, held back by a crude barrier of broken masonry and fresh claw marks.</p>

<p>Sounds carried up the cut: the impact of bodies, a short feline snarl, the high, wrong cry of something that had learned to wear a wolf’s shape and then forgotten the rules.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t call out.</p>

<p>He went down the last of the path with the hammer in both hands, and the gauntlet’s residual heat still against his hip, and the obligation that had started as a sentence in a white room and become a direction in a blood hall tightened into the simple, ugly fact of a striped woman still breathing on the other side of the next corner.</p>

<p>The tracks ended at the barrier.</p>
