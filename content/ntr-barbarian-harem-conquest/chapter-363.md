<p>The landing was mostly dark.</p>

<p>It opened under them like a held breath that had never been released, the floor sloping away into a distance the eye could not finish.</p>

<p>Broken pillars leaned at wrong angles, some still holding fragments of a ceiling that had given up, others only stubs of carved stone.</p>

<p>Along the walls, half-swallowed by damp and time, stood ranks of figures cut in the old Giant style: warriors in formal stance, hammers and spears frozen at the shoulder, faces worn smooth by the pit’s slow weather.</p>

<p>Between the pillars and the statues, the floor wasn’t empty.</p>

<p>Bodies.</p>

<p>They lay in drifts and lines, almost bone to bone, the parts long since claimed by whatever the dark fed.</p>

<p>Some still wore scraps of wrap or harness.</p>

<p>Most did not.</p>

<p>Years had pressed them flat and pale.</p>

<p>The air that rose past the rope smelled of old mineral and older endings.</p>

<p>Kane’s boots found solid stone.</p>

<p>The three elves landed in careful sequence behind him, then the two Giant escorts, the last of them grunting as the handholds finally ran out and the drop became a short free step to the floor.</p>

<p>Kane looked along the nearest drift of remains.</p>

<p>&quot;You threw your enemies here?&quot;</p>

<p>One of the Giant fighters nodded once.</p>

<p>His voice stayed low, as if volume itself might wake something the years had only put to sleep.</p>

<p>&quot;Prisoners. Traitors. The ones the halls wouldn’t keep under roof. It’s been years since anyone descended from above with a purpose. We sealed the mouths we could and told ourselves the dark would hold what we put in it. We never really considered this would bring death back up on us.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Well,&quot; Kane said, &quot;you should’ve.&quot;</p>

<p>He raised Mjoldr.</p>

<p>A red light woke along the axe’s edge, not a flare for battle, a steady working glow that pushed the dark back in a wide, hard circle.</p>

<p>Pillars jumped into relief.</p>

<p>Statues’ empty eyes caught the colour.</p>

<p>The nearest bones shone the dirty white of things that had been waiting without language for it.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay close. No one wanders. No one answers a voice that isn’t one of us. Priority is the stone. We take what we came for, and we leave while the rope still answers. Everything else is secondary.&quot;</p>

<p>They moved.</p>

<p>The red light walked with them, a moving cell of visibility in a space that refused to admit edges.</p>

<p>Kane’s eyes kept returning to the bodies, checking the angles of limbs, the stillness of skulls, the places where a hand might decide to close after years of being open.</p>

<p>Nothing rose.</p>

<p>The only sounds were their own steps, the complaint of Giant joints in air that was already thickening, and the distant, irregular drip of water that never quite found a pool.</p>

<p>One of the elves spoke through a tight jaw.</p>

<p>&quot;How many did you put down here?&quot;</p>

<p>The quieter Giant answered without looking away from the dark beyond Kane’s light.</p>

<p>&quot;Enough that we stopped counting. Enough that the lower halls slept better for a generation. Root-Binder, Elder Ragnar, when he still used the name, said the pit would keep them. Said a closed door was a closed door.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And then he walked into the primary with a stone and the same sentence,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;And then he did.&quot;</p>

<p>Ten more minutes of careful ground.</p>

<p>The air changed.</p>

<p>It compressed, pressure against the chest and the back of the throat, a weight that made each breath a negotiation.</p>

<p>The Giants felt it first and hardest.</p>

<p>Their strides shortened.</p>

<p>One of them put a hand to the wall and left it there for a moment, as if the stone might loan him space the air would not.</p>

<p>&quot;This is wrong,&quot; the hammer-Giant muttered.</p>

<p>&quot;Feeder air is foul. It is not supposed to press like a hand on the ribs.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It is supposed to do whatever the tap taught it,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>He didn’t slow.</p>

<p>The system’s passive tags climbed in blunt increments at the edge of his sight, taint gradient rising, no immediate overwrite spike, something dense ahead.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay on the light. If you cannot breathe, you signal, and you go up. You don’t prove courage by fainting in the dark.&quot;</p>

<p>The spear-Giant’s voice came out rougher.</p>

<p>&quot;We can walk. We have walked worse for less reason.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then walk. And keep your mouths shut if the dark starts using familiar names.&quot;</p>

<p>They went on.</p>

<p>The red circle of Mjoldr’s light found broken tools, a snapped spear-haft large enough to serve as a yard on a human ship, a child’s charm of the old bone-circle pattern half-buried in grit: out of place, wrong scale, the same grammar as the belt and the Sylvander gate.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t touch it.</p>

<p>Firlia would have bagged it for the list.</p>

<p>Griselda would have refused to let it near the case without a ward.</p>

<p>Here there was only the note of it and the decision to leave it lying.</p>

<p>Then the air tightened another degree, and Kane raised his hand.</p>

<p>Fifty metres ahead, at the limit of the red light’s honest reach, something sat on a low rise of stone.</p>

<p>Not a random drift of the dead.</p>

<p>A shape that had been placed, or had placed itself, above a point of light that was not Kane’s.</p>

<p>The stone under that shape brimmed with energy.</p>

<p>Warmth rolled off it in a continuous wave, the same family of wrong heat Griselda had named on a courtyard table in Sylvander, the same grammar the belt charms and Givan’s carriers had carried in their quieter forms.</p>

<p>It pulsed without rhythm. It waited without hurry.</p>

<p>And above it, seated as if the warm stone were a chair and the years a minor inconvenience, was a Giant.</p>

<p>The escorts stopped hard enough that one of the elves nearly walked into a hammer-haft.</p>

<p>&quot;Is that—&quot;</p>

<p>The hammer-Giant’s eyes had gone wide in the red light. His mouth worked once before sound came.</p>

<p>&quot;Who is it?&quot;</p>

<p>The quieter Giant’s voice broke on the first syllable and had to start again.</p>

<p>&quot;El... Elder Ragnar...&quot;</p>
