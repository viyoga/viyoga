<p>The rain thickened with pressure.</p>

<p>Once the Hydra took Ragnar under and the black-gold mixed into the red harbour, the sea climbed as if someone had tilted the world.</p>

<p>A wall of water the colour of a wound built beyond the cut, higher than the quay, higher than the first switchbacks, a tsunami with no quake under it except the sky’s bruise and the hinge that had finally left the frame.</p>

<p>Kane’s system flared hard at the edge of his sight.</p>

<p>[Alert: Layer instability spike]</p>

<p>[Devil Arrival Estimate: recalculating]</p>

<p>[40 days — ???]</p>

<p>[Correction: 28 days...]</p>

<p>[Correction: 20 days...]</p>

<p>[Warning: Local catastrophic event in progress]</p>

<p>[Host survival probability under current vector: Declining]</p>

<p>&quot;Move!&quot; he roared toward the cut.</p>

<p>&quot;Higher ground—now—leave the lip!&quot;</p>

<p>The Stone-Mother was already shouting the same shape of order.</p>

<p>Warders abandoned the secondary mouth.</p>

<p>Giants ran for the upper paths.</p>

<p>The household scattered into the only direction that still had stone above the red line.</p>

<p>They didn’t outrun it.</p>

<p>The first wave took the quay and the lower switchbacks in a single dirty surge.</p>

<p>Kane saw Kessa on the path above him, ears flat, gauntlets still on, striped tail lashing as she turned to snarl at the water as if it were a beast she could break.</p>

<p>&quot;Kessa—!&quot;</p>

<p>The wave hit her side-on.</p>

<p>&quot;Agh—&quot;</p>

<p>Tiger strength and stubbornness held for a fraction of a second; then the red mass simply removed her from the stone.</p>

<p>Her shout cut off in spray.</p>

<p>Kane’s throat tore on her name again and got only thunder back.</p>

<p>’No... no... no...’</p>

<p>He ran faster.</p>

<p>Firlia was at the next ledge, medical kit abandoned, green hair plastered dark, eyes wide and somehow still clear.</p>

<p>She saw him coming.</p>

<p>She saw the wall behind him.</p>

<p>Her mouth moved into something that was not fear: small, steady, almost a smile, the logistics officer who had finally written herself into the column and refused to blot the line.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane—&quot;</p>

<p>Water took her before he reached her hand.</p>

<p>The smile was the last clean thing he saw.</p>

<p>Then the surge folded over the ledge and Firlia was gone into the red.</p>

<p>&quot;No—!&quot;</p>

<p>Misha was farther up, white hair a pale streak against the cliff, brief still clutched to her chest as if paper could outrank a sea.</p>

<p>She looked down the path once, counted, the way she always counted, and her face did the rare thing it rarely did in public.</p>

<p>A single tear cut through the red rain on her cheek.</p>

<p>Then the water climbed the stone under her boots and floored her without drama, without a scream, only the brief’s pages blooming and vanishing in the flood.</p>

<p>Kane felt fear.</p>

<p>Not the arithmetic of Rage and Hybrid Lock and countdown clocks.</p>

<p>’Fuck.’</p>

<p>The plain, human kind, the kind the system could not tag, climbing his spine as the island’s lower half became ocean and the people who had made the run worth surviving were erased from the ledges one by one.</p>

<p>He found Thora on a narrow cut above the worst of the first surge, bracelet dead against her ankle, compact axe still in one hand, ribs forgotten.</p>

<p>She saw his face and understood before he spoke.</p>

<p>&quot;Hold on to me,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;I am not—&quot;</p>

<p>He pulled her in anyway, arms locked around her, back to the cliff, the red wall rising for the second strike.</p>

<p>The wave hit.</p>

<p>Stone went away.</p>

<p>Direction went away.</p>

<p>They were in it together: cold, violent, full of broken branches and the debris of a harbour that had stopped being a harbour.</p>

<p>Something sharp punched into Kane’s side.</p>

<p>&quot;GAH—&quot;</p>

<p>A broken limb of wood, quay-timber or tree, driven by the surge.</p>

<p>His grip loosened against his will.</p>

<p>Thora’s hand found his face in the dark water.</p>

<p>Fingers on his jaw, on the blood, on the man who had refused to let her into the primary and was now failing to hold her in a sea that did not care about assignments.</p>

<p>&quot;I love you,&quot; she said.</p>

<p>Not a shout.</p>

<p>Close enough that the water could not quite steal it.</p>

<p>Then the surge turned and tore her out of the circle of his arms.</p>

<p>He reached.</p>

<p>His fingers closed on nothing.</p>

<p>The red closed over the place she had been.</p>

<p>Kane found bottom, or something like it, for a single standing moment, chest-deep in a world that was only flood and thunder and the absence of every name he had been trying to keep on the board.</p>

<p>Rage opened.</p>

<p>[Controlled Berserker Output: 100%]</p>

<p>[Warning: Overload threshold]</p>

<p>[120%...]</p>

<p>[Tissue stress critical]</p>

<p>[Host—]</p>

<p>The numbers climbed. The red in his vision was no longer only the sky.</p>

<p>He took one step toward where Thora had been, toward where Kessa and Firlia and Misha had been, as if will could rewrite a tsunami.</p>

<p>The third wall of water took him clean off the stone. The burn in his palm and the hole in his side and the system’s alarms stacking into a single white noise.</p>

<p>The bruise-sky vanished. The island vanished.</p>

<p>Mjoldr’s weight vanished from his hand as if the axe had never been bound.</p>

<p>Everything went dark.</p>

<p>He woke up to white.</p>

<p>Not the white of snow or bone or emptied sockets: the flat, artificial white of a room that had never seen weather.</p>

<p>A door without a bar.</p>

<p>Light that didn’t come from a sun or a bruise-cloud.</p>

<p>A single bed.</p>

<p>Kane sat up, and the motion was wrong.</p>

<p>No brace of muscle from a body that had carried Rage and Hybrid Lock and a Giant’s kick.</p>

<p>No red hair falling into his eyes.</p>

<p>He put a hand to his head and felt short black hair, soft, ordinary.</p>

<p>His hands were clean.</p>

<p>No calluses in the places Mjoldr had built them.</p>

<p>A mirror on the far wall, small and cheap, showed a stranger who was not a stranger: pale from indoor light, dark-haired, the face of a man who sat in chairs and looked at screens, not at pits.</p>

<p>Streamer.</p>

<p>The word arrived without mercy.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m... back?&quot;</p>

<p>On the bed beside the dent where he had been, Thora slept.</p>

<p>Naked under a thin sheet, dark hair loose across a pillow that didn’t smell of stone or rain, scarred shoulder soft in sleep, breathing the even rhythm of someone who had not just been torn out of a tsunami’s arms.</p>

<p>No bracelet on her ankle.</p>

<p>Kane stared.</p>

<p>The white room didn’t explain itself.</p>

<p>No system window opened at the edge of his sight.</p>

<p>Only the quiet of climate control and the faint electronic hum of a building that had never heard of Berakamon or a Stone-Mother or a hinge that walked into the sea.</p>

<p>He found his voice, and it came out thinner than the barbarian’s, scraped by a throat that had not been screaming names into red rain.</p>

<p>&quot;Huh?&quot;</p>
