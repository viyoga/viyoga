<p>The water outside the cut bulged.</p>

<p>A ridge of scales broke the surface, longer than the quay, older than the posts on the high ridge, and the black vertical slit of an eye opened in a field of deep red.</p>

<p>The Hydra regarded the shore the way it had regarded the boat: without hurry, without mercy, with the flat interest of a domain tool that had found something on its table that didn’t belong to the sea.</p>

<p>Kane held Mjoldr’s red pulse high.</p>

<p>&quot;Come on, then,&quot; he called toward the river of yellow-eyed bone pouring down the path.</p>

<p>&quot;Dinner bell’s still ringing.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar stepped onto the wet slabs.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer hung easy in a hand that hadn’t needed blood for sixty-five years.</p>

<p>Behind him the dead spread in a broken fan, sockets fixed on the red light, on Kane, on the heat of living bodies at the tide-line.</p>

<p>&quot;You bring a mouth to a mouth, barbarian,&quot; Ragnar said.</p>

<p>&quot;The pit doesn’t fear the sea.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It doesn’t have to fear it.&quot; Kane shifted his stance.</p>

<p>&quot;It only has to fit.&quot;</p>

<p>He moved.</p>

<p>Not at Ragnar first.</p>

<p>At the angle of the quay.</p>

<p>He cut left along the edge, red edge trailing, and the nearest of the bone-column turned with him like iron filings after a magnet.</p>

<p>A half-wrapped torso lunged.</p>

<p>Mjoldr took it apart at the spine.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t stop to finish the pieces.</p>

<p>He kept the light moving, toward the deeper green, toward the place where the Hydra’s bulk had begun to rise in earnest.</p>

<p>The first of the dead reached the tide-line.</p>

<p>The Hydra’s head, one of them, lesser but still the size of a Giant’s cart, struck.</p>

<p>Water and stone exploded.</p>

<p>The jaw closed on a cluster of yellow-lit bone and lifted, crushing, shaking, flinging scraps back onto the quay in a wet arc.</p>

<p>The domain’s attention sharpened.</p>

<p>More of the dead poured forward, still fixed on Kane’s red banner, and the sea answered with a second strike, then a third, coils rolling, water sheeting off scales, the black-slit eye tracking anything that still moved with the pit’s light in it.</p>

<p>&quot;Herd them!&quot; Kane shouted up toward the Stone-Mother’s line on the cut.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t hold the path—push the column to the water! Stay off the red!&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa’s voice carried from higher ground, half snarl, half laugh:</p>

<p>&quot;He is feeding the sea god our problem—!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then help him feed it!&quot; the Stone-Mother’s answer cracked down.</p>

<p>&quot;Hammers on the flanks! Nothing with gold in the sockets walks back toward the children’s ledges!&quot;</p>

<p>The quay became a killing funnel.</p>

<p>Kane ran the edge with Mjoldr as the lure and the blade both.</p>

<p>Dead that lunged for him died or were shoved.</p>

<p>Dead that followed the light too far found the Hydra’s reach.</p>

<p>A warrior-shaped ruin with a familiar set to the remaining shoulder got a hand on Kane’s belt.</p>

<p>He put the spike through its skull and kicked it into a coil that was already turning.</p>

<p>Yellow light flared and went out under the pressure of something that had never needed the pit’s grammar to be hungry.</p>

<p>Ragnar didn’t charge into the water.</p>

<p>He walked the slabs parallel to Kane’s run, hammer ready, yellow eyes tracking the barbarian instead of the sea.</p>

<p>When a cluster of bone got too close to the Elder, he broke them himself, not in panic, in the tidy refusal of a door that would not share its attention.</p>

<p>&quot;You use a god’s dog for a broom,&quot; Ragnar called across the spray.</p>

<p>&quot;The pit will remember the insult.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;The pit can file a complaint.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane cut another climber off his heels and spun the axe so the red light flared brighter.</p>

<p>&quot;I am busy.&quot;</p>

<p>A lesser Hydra head struck so close that the displaced water knocked Kane to one knee.</p>

<p>He came up coughing salt, Mjoldr still in hand, and saw the opening: a dense knot of the dead packed at the quay’s outer corner, light thick among them, the main mass still pouring from the path.</p>

<p>He threw the axe, not to kill, to plant.</p>

<p>Mjoldr spun out over the knot and hung for a heartbeat above the waterline, red pulse screaming against the yellow, and the dead surged after it in a single mindless tide.</p>

<p>The Hydra took the tide.</p>

<p>Coils and jaws and the sheer mass of a domain familiar that had been agitated since the sky went wrong closed on the packed dead.</p>

<p>Bone snapped like dry wood.</p>

<p>Yellow light drowned in green depth and red eye-shine.</p>

<p>The quay shook.</p>

<p>Warders on the upper cut shouted.</p>

<p>Someone, Giant or elf, screamed in pure visceral reaction as the sea god fed.</p>

<p>Kane called Mjoldr back.</p>

<p>The axe tore free of a collapsing ribcage and slapped into his palm.</p>

<p>He turned to find Ragnar already closing the distance the dead had left open.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer came in a horizontal arc that would have broken a lesser man in half.</p>

<p>Kane caught it on Mjoldr’s cheek, slid, and felt the impact drive him back toward the tide-line.</p>

<p>Rage opened another notch, not a full burn, enough to keep his feet.</p>

<p>&quot;You broke my seat,&quot; Ragnar said, driving forward.</p>

<p>&quot;You kicked me in the chest.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane shoved off the bind and cut for the knee.</p>

<p>&quot;We are even on manners. We are not even on the island.&quot;</p>

<p>The hammer changed line mid-swing, the same trick from the feeder landing.</p>

<p>Kane was ready this time.</p>

<p>He stepped in, let the haft scrape his shoulder, and put the spike toward the yellow of an eye.</p>

<p>Ragnar turned his head.</p>

<p>The spike scored the temple again and drew the same black-gold fluid.</p>

<p>The Elder didn’t slow.</p>

<p>He swung short and brutal and forced Kane toward the water as the Hydra’s bulk rolled past the quay’s edge, one great eye regarding the two figures still standing among the ruin of the bone-column.</p>

<p>Most of the dead were gone.</p>

<p>Scraps still twitched on the slabs.</p>

<p>A few half-shapes tried to crawl toward the path and met the Stone-Mother’s hammers on the cut.</p>

<p>The river that had poured from the secondary mouth had been fed into a mouth that didn’t spit back.</p>

<p>What remained in the centre of the quay was simple and wrong: the Hydra’s risen head and coils in the deep green, Ragnar with his engraved hammer and yellow eyes, and Kane with Mjoldr’s red edge, three points of attention on a strip of Giant stone.</p>
