<p>Dust clouds signaled approaching riders along the paved highway long before Brak dragged a terrified Menual captain before the vanguard.</p>

<p>Tossing the man onto the road like a sack of grain, Brak crossed his burly arms and snorted.</p>

<p>&quot;Found this one waving a white flag near the milestone, Lord Kane,&quot; Brak rumbled, kicking the envoy.</p>

<p>&quot;Said he had a message for the man who broke the river. I figured you might want to hear him squeak before we toss him into the surf.&quot;</p>

<p>Wearing unblemished southern silk, the envoy struggled to keep his knees from visibly shaking.</p>

<p>He held up a wax-sealed parchment toward the towering gladiator, unable to maintain eye contact with the man who had just slaughtered an entire battalion of war elephants.</p>

<p>&quot;Lord Kane!&quot; the envoy stammered.</p>

<p>&quot;His Imperial Majesty proposes a cessation of hostilities. I bring a formal invitation to a truce banquet inside the Marble Canopy to negotiate the surrender of the delta.&quot;</p>

<p>Thora leaned against her axe handle, laughing openly at the trembling man.</p>

<p>&quot;A banquet? Chief, they just realized they lost their fleet before lunch, so they want to feed you a poisoned pigeon to save their own skins. Tell him we prefer our meals cooked over a campfire.&quot;</p>

<p>Stepping up beside her, Seraphine narrowed her silver eyes at the messenger.</p>

<p>&quot;Palace truces always involve hidden crossbows and spiked wine. I spent twenty years navigating imperial courts, gladiators, and they never invite a conqueror to dinner unless they plan to bury him under the floorboards. Tell your Emperor we will negotiate when we break his gates.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane didn’t immediately dismiss the messenger.</p>

<p>Crossing his arms, he stared past the sweating captain toward the white-domed towers gleaming in the distance.</p>

<p>’Should I attend a dinner hosted by a cornered rat?’ Kane pondered, his mind spinning rapidly through the tactical board.</p>

<p>’He knows I wiped his armada. A truce just gives him a convenient stall tactic to position his royal guard behind the curtains while pretending to wave an olive branch. Skipping it means a drawn-out siege, which costs time and drains our stamina.’</p>

<p>’Breaking walls takes effort, and my rowers are already tired from the tsunami ride. If I walk right into his snare, I can crush his spirit in front of his entire court, audit the treasury I plan to steal, and spare my men a meaningless meat grinder at the outer walls.’</p>

<p>Unfolding his arms, Kane took a slow step forward.</p>

<p>The envoy flinched, shrinking back as the gladiator’s shadow fell over him.</p>

<p>&quot;Tell your Emperor I accept,&quot; Kane rumbled, snatching the parchment from the man’s trembling fingers.</p>

<p>&quot;Have the wine poured by sundown. If the food is cold, I will start breaking furniture.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Y-yes, my Lord! Right away!&quot;</p>

<p>The envoy scrambled to his feet, bowing awkwardly before rushing back toward his horse.</p>

<p>Once the rider became a speck on the horizon, Seraphine and Thora immediately stepped forward.</p>

<p>Both women checked their weapon straps with lethal intent, fully expecting to form his bodyguard detail.</p>

<p>Kane held up a hand, stopping them in their tracks.</p>

<p>&quot;You two are staying with the column,&quot; Kane instructed smoothly.</p>

<p>&quot;I am taking the Princess.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Kane, you cannot walk into a trap without a vanguard!&quot; Seraphine argued, her brow furrowing with concern.</p>

<p>&quot;They will try to assassinate you the moment you sit down. I know how these southern nobles operate. They smile to your face and stab you in the kidneys when you reach for a bread roll.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;She is right, Chief,&quot; Thora added.</p>

<p>&quot;Let us come along and carve up their royal guard. We can turn their fancy banquet into a proper Bloodfang tavern brawl. It builds character.&quot;</p>

<p>Before Kane could formulate a response, the command tent flap pushed open.</p>

<p>Misha stepped out into the midday sun, her golden eyes glowing calmly.</p>

<p>&quot;He does not need a sword,&quot; Misha stated, her dual-tone voice echoing gently across the highway.</p>

<p>&quot;He needs a deterrent.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I reckon our little oracle understands the board perfectly,&quot; Kane explained, his voice laced with confident amusement.</p>

<p>&quot;The Emperor thinks I’m just a mercenary warlord trying to extort him for coin. If he drops a chandelier on my head while I stand next to Milfheim royalty, the Northern Empress will burn his southern capital to ash.&quot;</p>

<p>Turning his gaze toward Seraphine, Kane winked at the paladin.</p>

<p>&quot;Misha is the ultimate political shield. He cannot touch us without starting a global war, and he knows it. You two secure the perimeter and keep the men ready. When I give the signal, you breach the gates.&quot;</p>

<p>Thora sighed, leaning her head against his shoulder.</p>

<p>&quot;Fine. But if they serve roasted boar, you’d better save me a plate.&quot;</p>

<p>Sundown painted the capital in vivid shades of orange by the time Kane reached the towering gates.</p>

<p>He didn’t bother changing into formal or polishing his gear to impress the aristocracy.</p>

<p>Wearing his battle-scarred leather harness, he kept Mjoldr strapped securely to his back while his boots remained crusted with dried river salt and dried blood.</p>

<p>Walking gracefully at his side, Misha looked like a descending angel in a clean white Milfheim gown Grieselda had salvaged from the flagship’s cargo.</p>

<p>Holding her chin high, the luminous rings of gold in her eyes radiated quiet authority.</p>

<p>As they walked up the grand staircase toward the brass doors, Kane glanced at the intricate carvings lining the walls.</p>

<p>&quot;Nice stonework,&quot; Kane commented.</p>

<p>&quot;I figure it took a century to build this place.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Built on the backs of conquered tributaries, husband,&quot; Misha replied.</p>

<p>&quot;The Menual empire takes from the weak to build monuments for the vain. It is a fragile ecosystem.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Isn’t that what your mother is doing too?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;That’s right.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I can be a hypocrite.&quot;</p>

<p>Tall brass doors swung open, revealing an opulent hall illuminated by dozens of crystal chandeliers.</p>

<p>Southern citizens and armored guards lined the entrance, parting nervously as the Bloodfang warlord strode into their sanctuary.</p>

<p>Perfumed nobles hid behind painted fans and jeweled goblets, whispering insults to one another as they analyzed the newcomers.</p>

<p>&quot;Look at the state of him,&quot; a powdered duke muttered to his wife, loud enough to carry across the marble floor.</p>

<p>&quot;The unwashed barbarian dares to track river water into our halls. Does he even know how to use a fork?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;A savage from the borderlands,&quot; a countess sneered.</p>

<p>&quot;His Majesty should have him whipped for lacking proper attire.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane ignored the sneers, letting his foot echo loudly against the polished floor.</p>

<p>Scanning the room, he mapped the exits, the guards stationed near the tapestries, and the elevated throne waiting at the far end of the banquet hall.</p>

<p>Leaning down slightly, Kane whispered to Misha without breaking his stride.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay close to my left side, little oracle. If anyone offers you a drink, politely decline.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I do not intend to eat their food, husband,&quot; Misha replied, her eyes tracking the hidden crossbowmen stationed in the upper balconies.</p>

<p>&quot;They reek of fear.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane let out a low rumble of laughter, thoroughly enjoying her deadpan assessment.</p>

<p>&quot;I guess you are right. Let us go meet this Emperor.&quot;</p>
