<p>Putting the war map away for the very first time all day felt just like taking off a restrictive pair of boots.</p>

<p>Without any formal organization or given orders, everyone naturally drifted into the main living space.</p>

<p>It was the undeniable gravity of people who spent months surviving together, automatically seeking the same room at the end of a long day.</p>

<p>Chewing loudly, Brak worked his way through a roasted bird, occasionally tossing a clean bone toward the rug.</p>

<p>Sitting cross-legged on the floor, Kessa caught the scraps effortlessly while carefully sharpening a hunting knife.</p>

<p>She definitely didn’t need to sharpen it, considering she possessed literal claws, but the repetitive motion seemed to soothe her feral instincts.</p>

<p>Rutheus already poured his third cup of wine.</p>

<p>The old man grumbled constantly about elven vintages lacking a proper bite, yet he never stopped drinking them.</p>

<p>Lirael occupied a plush chair slightly apart from the rest of the group.</p>

<p>She was still actively calibrating where a former enemy sellsword fit into a lethal family that formed entirely without her.</p>

<p>Finally closing her ever-present portfolio, Caelindra stared blankly at the crackling fireplace.</p>

<p>The secretary looked thoroughly exhausted by the fast-paced reality of war planning.</p>

<p>Firlia sat at a small side desk nearby, swiftly writing a letter that she folded shut before anyone could read over her shoulder.</p>

<p>Watching the quiet chaos unfold, Kane leaned comfortably against the wooden wall.</p>

<p>’Look at this ridiculous group,’ he mused fondly.</p>

<p>’All this happened because a grumpy merchant sold me a sealed fallen angel for ten gold coins, and I walked into a forest camp looking for a fight.’</p>

<p>Thora materialized right beside his elbow.</p>

<p>She always appeared there, possessing the comforting gravity of the person who was there before anyone else even existed in his world.</p>

<p>&quot;Tomorrow we march toward Milfheim,&quot; Thora murmured, keeping her voice low.</p>

<p>&quot;Yes,&quot; Kane agreed, offering a slight nod.</p>

<p>&quot;And you’re going to negotiate directly with the Empress,&quot; she pressed, arching a curious eyebrow.</p>

<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>

<p>Pausing for a long moment, Thora studied his relaxed profile.</p>

<p>&quot;She’s going to be...&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Probably,&quot; Kane laughed softly, knowing exactly what she meant without her needing to finish the sentence.</p>

<p>&quot;You haven’t even met the woman yet,&quot; Thora pointed out, shooting him a skeptical sideways glance.</p>

<p>&quot;Milfheim is an empire ruled exclusively by highly powerful, mature women,&quot; Kane explained, his lips curling into a smirk.</p>

<p>&quot;I know the place. I’ve already met four of her knights. I conjecture I have a very accurate general picture of what to expect when those doors open.&quot;</p>

<p>’If the name of the empire wasn’t a big enough clue, the developers definitely had a specific type in mind,’ he thought, highly amused by the impending diplomatic meeting.</p>

<p>Silence stretched comfortably between them before Thora spoke again.</p>

<p>&quot;Will you come back?&quot; she stated calmly, leaning closer.</p>

<p>&quot;Always,&quot; Kane promised, meaning every single word.</p>

<p>Leaning her head against his shoulder, she just stood there.</p>

<p>There was no theatrical performance, no petty claim of ownership, and zero jealousy.</p>

<p>She just offered the weight of a partner who had been present for every single moment of his rise.</p>

<p>Flames slowly burned down to embers in the stone hearth.</p>

<p>People gradually drifted toward their individual rooms, leaving the main hall quiet and empty.</p>

<p>Kane remained standing by the window long after the others retired.</p>

<p>Sitting in her favorite high-backed chair, Grieselda had not moved an inch.</p>

<p>She felt far more present now, and while the intricate mark still rested on her collarbone, its oppressive energy felt much quieter.</p>

<p>[The World Tree seed.]</p>

<p>Turning his head, Kane met her golden eyes.</p>

<p>[You actually chose it.]</p>

<p>&quot;Yes,&quot; Kane answered aloud.</p>

<p>[You are planning to plant it right at the mountain pass.]</p>

<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>

<p>Looking past him, she stared at the streets outside the glass, and her hands moved again to frame her silent words carefully.</p>

<p>[It will change that pass permanently. Whatever grows from a true World Tree cutting... it doesn’t just unhappen once the fighting ends.]</p>

<p>&quot;I know,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>[You’re altering the physical and magical landscape of a continent just to win one single battle.]</p>

<p>&quot;I’m altering the landscape of a continent to win everything that comes after this battle,&quot; Kane corrected her, his eyes hardening.</p>

<p>’If Menual wants to play a long game, I will just rewrite the board.’</p>

<p>She studied his face for a long time, her expression unreadable.</p>

<p>[Eight hundred and forty-seven years.]</p>

<p>Grieselda sighed softly, her hands moving in tragic grace.</p>

<p>[I waited sealed in the void for eight hundred and forty-seven years. And somehow, you are exactly as much trouble as I expected you to be.]</p>

<p>&quot;Is that a complaint?&quot; Kane challenged, crossing his muscular arms.</p>

<p>Turning her gaze back to the moonlit window, she shook her head once.</p>

<p>That faint, almost-smile that first appeared at the Sylvandar fountain surfaced once again, and it was a rare expression that Kane quickly decided was one of his favorite things in this world.</p>

<p>&quot;Get some sleep,&quot; Kane advised gently, pushing off the wall.</p>

<p>Sunlight barely touched the manor roof before a visitor arrived the next morning.</p>

<p>Opening the front gate himself, Kane found Lyssel standing in the morning air.</p>

<p>She looked tired, her honey-blonde hair pinned up in a rush, completely abandoning the carefully managed aristocratic style she wore to the royal banquet.</p>

<p>’She looks so much fucking better when she isn’t trying to perform for the court,’ Kane thought, leaning comfortably against the gatepost.</p>

<p>Meeting his eyes, she lifted her chin stubbornly.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m not staying long.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Alright,&quot; Kane replied, waiting for her pitch.</p>

<p>&quot;My father’s political coalition is officially dissolved,&quot; Lyssel announced, her voice tight but remarkably steady.</p>

<p>&quot;Three of the five allied houses pulled their support the very second the queen made her post-tournament announcement regarding your new status, so my father is currently adjusting his position.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And what does that have to do with me?&quot; Kane asked, genuinely amused by the collapsing political web.</p>

<p>&quot;He wants to secure a new alliance before the other lords swallow our territory,&quot; she answered proudly, refusing to look away or show weakness.</p>

<p>&quot;My father wishes to talk to you.&quot;</p>
