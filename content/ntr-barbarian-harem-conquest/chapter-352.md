<p>Thora pushed up from the platform an inch and stopped when the ribs reminded her why she was sitting.</p>

<p>&quot;Then take someone.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane checked Mjoldr’s weight once and left the rest of the gear.</p>

<p>&quot;Lower tier only. No smoke, no ridge path. If I am not back before the second night-horn, you don’t search. You hold this door, and you wait for the dawn escort. That is the rule.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa’s ears flattened.</p>

<p>&quot;I can smell the village now. I should—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You should stay on the threshold and make sure no one walks a wrong-smelling box out of this room while I am gone.&quot;</p>

<p>He looked at Griselda.</p>

<p>&quot;Fragment stays under your hands.&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda didn’t argue.</p>

<p>&quot;It stays.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia, still at the shelf, only said, &quot;Second horn. We heard you.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane opened the door on the mist and the waterfall’s constant thunder and stepped out into a village that was trying very hard to look like it still had ordinary nights.</p>

<p>The lower tier was quieter than the scale suggested.</p>

<p>Doorways that should have held voices were shuttered.</p>

<p>Able-bodied Giants on the paths were fewer than the ledges and cave-mouths implied.</p>

<p>What moved at this hour moved with purpose: work gangs hauling stretcher-sized loads uphill under covered cloth, not market goods.</p>

<p>Kane kept to the guest paths, hands visible, Mjoldr loose at his side, and counted without staring.</p>

<p>A side cave mouth glowed with low lamp light.</p>

<p>Clean wraps went in.</p>

<p>Stained ones came out.</p>

<p>He only noted how often the exchange happened and how little talk accompanied it.</p>

<p>Near a water channel, two Giant children, human-adult height, still called children here, sat on a step repeating phrases they had clearly overheard too many times.</p>

<p>&quot;It... It had different eyes.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;The deep one is listening to the ridge.&quot;</p>

<p>No adult corrected them.</p>

<p>The fear had already become furniture.</p>

<p>Further along, under a storage ledge, a sealed crate sat with a crude circle-charm burned into the lid.</p>

<p>The pattern matched the bone-circles from the forest belt close enough that Kane’s system flicked a passive recognition at the edge of his sight.</p>

<p>A Giant guard sat on the crate itself.</p>

<p>When Kane’s path brought him within polite distance, the guard’s hand went to the wood first, not the spear at his knee.</p>

<p>Containment over confrontation.</p>

<p>The priority was clear.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t stop at the crate.</p>

<p>He walked on until the path narrowed between two working fires where a pair of lower-tier fighters were sharing a skin and the kind of talk that assumed a land-walker would not follow the names.</p>

<p>&quot;—Root-Binder walked in after the stone,&quot; one said, voice rough with smoke and not enough sleep.</p>

<p>&quot;Said he could shut the door from the inside. Preferred line or not, he was still the Elder. Stone-Watcher only took the sight lines because someone had to watch the pits breathe.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Half the ledges still call him a stopgap,&quot; the other answered.</p>

<p>&quot;The other half want the outsiders on a short rope. Disposable scouts. Get them killed mapping a secondary hole before the Hydra’s mood and that box in the guest cottage start answering each other.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;There’s a smaller voice,&quot; the first said, quieter.</p>

<p>&quot;Seal the taps with whatever works. Even land-walker methods. I’m in that voice. I lost a brother to eyes that came back wrong. I don’t care whose hands hold the lid.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s step scuffed once on purpose, enough to be heard, not enough to look like stalking.</p>

<p>Both Giants turned.</p>

<p>He kept his free hand open.</p>

<p>&quot;Guest path,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;Looking for the long way back to the waterfall cottage.&quot;</p>

<p>The nearer fighter studied him, then the axe, then the fact that he was alone.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re the one who came out of the deep one’s water.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;We were lucky with the shallows.&quot;</p>

<p>A dry sound.</p>

<p>&quot;Luck. Sure.&quot;</p>

<p>The Giant nodded up the path, away from the smoke.</p>

<p>&quot;That way loops to the lower hall stairs and then down. Don’t follow the covered loads. They’re not for guests.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;One question,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;Guest curiosity. The previous Elder, Root-Binder, went into a pit after a forest stone arrived. Is that the open story, or the quiet one?&quot;</p>

<p>The two exchanged a look.</p>

<p>The second fighter answered in a flat voice.</p>

<p>&quot;Open enough that children repeat it. He went in. He didn’t come out. Stone-Watcher watches because the alternative is pretending the pits aren’t breathing. That’s all a quay-walker gets at this hour.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane inclined his head.</p>

<p>&quot;Appreciated.&quot;</p>

<p>He was three turns further along when the restraint party crossed the path ahead: four Giants, a fifth between them in heavy bindings, joints moving in a rhythm that was almost right and then not.</p>

<p>He didn’t follow them.</p>

<p>The second night-horn hadn’t sounded.</p>

<p>He turned back toward the waterfall before it could.</p>

<p>The cottage door took his weight without complaint.</p>

<p>Mist and spray clung to his shoulders.</p>

<p>Inside, the oversized room had settled into the particular quiet of people who had spent a sea crossing and a climb and were done pretending they were not tired.</p>

<p>Kessa sat at the threshold in a half-doze, upright out of stubbornness, ears twitching at the latch.</p>

<p>Thora was out on the platform, axe still in reach, breathing the careful rhythm of ribs that had won an argument with pride for one night.</p>

<p>Misha had fallen asleep over the finished brief, white hair spilled across the pale-wood token.</p>

<p>Griselda remained near the containment case, eyes closed, ward-line undisturbed.</p>

<p>Only Firlia was fully awake.</p>

<p>Green hair down, the work braid undone, damp strands loose around her face and neck.</p>

<p>Her hands were still moving along the shelf she had already ordered twice: wraps, water skins, the sealed rations, the small medical kit adjusted a finger-width for reasons only she could see.</p>

<p>The logistics mind that did not stop until the room was correct.</p>

<p>She looked up when the door closed.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re back before the horn.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane set Mjoldr against the wall.</p>

<p>He could still smell the sick-line cave and the restraint party on his coat if he let himself.</p>

<p>&quot;Lower tier only,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;As promised.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia’s hands finally went still on the edge of the shelf.</p>

<p>Kane noticed her trembling hands.</p>

<p>&quot;Come outside for a minute.&quot;</p>
