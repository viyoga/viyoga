<p>The three of them held the quay in a triangle that didn’t move.</p>

<p>Kane’s breath came out rough.</p>

<p>Blood ran from a line across his ribs where the engraved hammer had caught him on the last exchange, deep enough to matter, not deep enough to put him down.</p>

<p>Mjoldr’s red edge still burned in his grip.</p>

<p>Ragnar stood between him and the path, yellow eyes steady, hammer ready, the last of the bone-scraps twitching and dying under the Stone-Mother’s hammers on the cut above.</p>

<p>Beyond the tide-line, the Hydra’s head held the depth, black-slit pupil wide enough to swallow a man whole, regarding both the pit’s door and the land-walker who had set the table.</p>

<p>No one charged.</p>

<p>&quot;You cannot outlast a mouth,&quot; Ragnar said.</p>

<p>The deep voice carried over the slap of water on stone.</p>

<p>&quot;The pit taught me patience. The sea has only hunger. Hunger finishes. Patience does not.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane shifted his weight off the bleeding side and didn’t raise the axe for another blow.</p>

<p>&quot;Then we stop playing at patience.&quot;</p>

<p>He didn’t throw Mjoldr. He reached to his belt with his off-hand and pulled the warm fragment free, the piece of core taken from Ragnar’s seat, still pulsing with the same patient wrong heat, the same family as the Sylvander stone and the carriers Givan had brought.</p>

<p>In the open air of the quay it brightened, yellow-white at the centre, and the Hydra’s eye twitched toward it as if a second dinner bell had rung under the first.</p>

<p>Ragnar’s head turned a fraction.</p>

<p>&quot;You would wave a stolen heart at a god’s dog.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I would finish the introduction.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane held the fragment out at arm’s length, not toward Ragnar, toward the water, toward the black-slit pupil, toward the place where the domain’s attention was already dividing.</p>

<p>&quot;You sat on this for sixty-five years. The sea has been agitated since we crossed its cut. I am asking which mouth is hungrier.&quot;</p>

<p>Up on the cut, the Stone-Mother’s voice cracked down:</p>

<p>&quot;Land-walker—if you feed that thing the core, you may not get a shore left to stand on!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I am not feeding it the core,&quot; Kane called back, without looking away from Ragnar.</p>

<p>&quot;I am letting it smell the kitchen.&quot;</p>

<p>He stepped sideways along the tide-line, fragment extended, Mjoldr still in the other hand.</p>

<p>The Hydra’s head tracked the warm pulse.</p>

<p>Water sheeted off scales.</p>

<p>A coil thicker than a Giant’s waist rolled closer under the surface, displacing the grey-green in a long, slow bulge.</p>

<p>Ragnar moved to cut Kane off from the path, and in doing so put himself one stride nearer the edge than he had been.</p>

<p>Kane saw the geometry close.</p>

<p>He didn’t swing at the Elder’s skull.</p>

<p>He threw the fragment, not into the sea, onto the wet slab between Ragnar’s forward boot and the tide-line, where the wrong heat flared against the stone and the spray.</p>

<p>The Hydra struck at the flare.</p>

<p>Ragnar struck at Kane.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer came in short and brutal, aimed at the bleeding ribs.</p>

<p>Kane caught part of it on Mjoldr and part of it on his own guard.</p>

<p>The impact drove him to one knee and opened the wound wider.</p>

<p>Blood ran hot over his hip.</p>

<p>Rage flared to hold him upright.</p>

<p>Hybrid Lock stayed closed against the overwrite pressure that rolled off Ragnar in a spike of yellow.</p>

<p>And the Hydra’s jaw closed on the slab where the fragment lay, and on the hand that had reached, too late, to claim it back.</p>

<p>Ragnar’s roar was the first sound he had made that was not speech or scream-pressure.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer fell from fingers that were no longer there.</p>

<p>Black-gold fluid and sea-water mixed on the stone.</p>

<p>The Hydra shook its head once, the way a dog shakes a rat, and the Elder’s forearm ended in a ruin the pit’s preservation could not immediately rewrite.</p>

<p>The fragment tumbled from the jaw, skittered across the quay, and fetched up against Kane’s boot, still pulsing.</p>

<p>Kane forced himself up.</p>

<p>Blood ran freely now from the hammer-wound.</p>

<p>Mjoldr felt heavier.</p>

<p>He didn’t pick up the fragment yet. He stood, breathing hard, and looked past Ragnar’s hunched, yellow-eyed pain into the Hydra’s open eye.</p>

<p>The black slit held him.</p>

<p>For a moment there was no pit, no coalition, no southern line, only a domain familiar’s regard and a man who had used its hunger as a tool and was still standing on its shore, bleeding.</p>

<p>The Hydra’s pupil narrowed.</p>

<p>Something passed between them that was not language: recognition of the red edge, of the door in the blood, of the debt already paid in bone and the debt still open in the deeper ridge.</p>

<p>Then the great head drew back.</p>

<p>Water poured off the scales.</p>

<p>The coil slid into the green depth, not in retreat, in the settled motion of a mouth that had eaten enough for one tide.</p>

<p>Ragnar went to one knee on the quay, ruined arm clutched to his chest, yellow light flickering in his sockets like a lamp in a draft.</p>

<p>The engraved hammer lay in a pool of black-gold and salt.</p>

<p>He looked at Kane with something that might have been hatred and might have been the first true surprise in sixty-five years.</p>

<p>&quot;You... feed a god... with my hand.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I paid a debt with your hand.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s voice came out rough.</p>

<p>He bent, carefully, and closed his fingers on the fragment.</p>

<p>The warm wrongness sat against his palm again, weaker, as if the Hydra’s touch had taken something from it.</p>

<p>&quot;The pit can keep the rest of you for the primary. The secondary’s broom is done.&quot;</p>

<p>On the cut above, Kessa’s snarl carried clearly:</p>

<p>&quot;He is still moving—do we end him?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No,&quot; the Stone-Mother answered, and there was iron in it.</p>

<p>&quot;He is Root-Binder’s door. If he crawls back to the feeder, we watch the lip. If he crawls toward the children’s ledges, we end him. The land-walker has spent the dead. We do not spend the morning chasing a one-handed corpse into a hole we have not closed.&quot;</p>

<p>Ragnar’s yellow eyes flicked from Kane to the dark of the upper paths, then to the water where the Hydra had sunk.</p>

<p>He began to drag himself, slow and terrible, toward the stone that led away from the sea, toward the ridge, toward the mouths that still answered the pit’s grammar.</p>

<p>Warders tracked him with hammers ready.</p>

<p>No one stepped into his path.</p>

<p>The Elder who had watched food leave the table was leaving the table himself, one-handed, unarmed, and still not finished.</p>

<p>Kane stayed where he was until the shape was a shadow on the switchbacks.</p>

<p>Then the fragment in his hand changed.</p>

<p>The patient yellow-white pulse stuttered.</p>

<p>A harder light woke at the centre, red, the same family as Mjoldr’s edge, not the pit’s gold, and climbed.</p>
