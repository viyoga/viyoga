<p>&quot;So do I,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>The yard didn’t give them time to turn the wish into a plan.</p>

<p>Elven captains were already shouting for cordons.</p>

<p>Bodies lay in drifts across the broken approach: painted, torn, yellow light gone out of their eyes as if someone had snuffed a hundred candles with one breath.</p>

<p>The elephant’s carcass still blocked part of the gate wreckage.</p>

<p>Smoke from the earlier mana flares hung low and sour.</p>

<p>Civilians who had fled the nearer streets were being pushed farther in by wardens with open hands and tight voices, district by district, away from the smell and the sight.</p>

<p>Kane raised Mjoldr just enough to catch attention.</p>

<p>&quot;Cordon first. No one walks the outer stones without a partner. No one burns anything until Griselda and the city warders say the residual is inert. If a body twitches, you call it before you swing.&quot;</p>

<p>An elven sergeant repeated the order down the line.</p>

<p>Rope and spear-posts started going up in rough rings around the densest piles.</p>

<p>Thora moved into the work without being asked, compact axe still dark, expression reduced to pure sequence: count, separate, mark.</p>

<p>Misha had already found a flat stretch of unbroken wall and was turning the morning into paper: names of the living, gaps in the watch rotations, which lanes still needed clearing before the panic invented its own disasters.</p>

<p>Sira stood with the new bow half-raised, orange hair full of leaf litter, eyes too bright.</p>

<p>&quot;They all dropped at once. That is not a victory. That is a hand letting go of puppets. I want to know where the hand went.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;So does everyone with a working brain,&quot; Kessa said.</p>

<p>She flexed her gauntlets; dried black-tinged blood flaked off the knuckles.</p>

<p>&quot;I say we take a hunting line into the belt before the trail goes cold. Whatever wore that woman’s face is still out there.&quot;</p>

<p>Thora didn’t look up from the body she was turning for the warders’ mark.</p>

<p>&quot;We don’t chase a presence that can drop a hundred corrupted at once into trees we do not control while the gate is still a hole. We secure. Then we brief. Then we argue about hunts.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Practical as ever,&quot; Kessa muttered, but she stayed inside the cordon.</p>

<p>Griselda landed on a clear stretch of wall walk, wings folding tight.</p>

<p>A pair of city warders in pale scarves climbed up to meet her.</p>

<p>Their conversation was low and technical.</p>

<p>Essence bleed, inert thresholds, which piles could be moved and which had to wait.</p>

<p>When Griselda finally spoke loud enough for Kane to hear, her voice carried the flat certainty he trusted more than hope.</p>

<p>&quot;Essence is decaying. Not clean. Not active enough to turn a fresh wound on contact. We can begin ordered burning on the outer third within the hour if the wind holds. Inner piles wait for a second pass.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Do it,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;Firlia—status on the civilian lanes.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia appeared from the direction of the second ring, green hair stuck to her neck, a slate already half-filled.</p>

<p>&quot;The nearest three districts are moving under warden escort. No confirmed yellow eyes inside the walls. Two false reports already: paint and shock, not corruption. I have runners on the Menual road with your seal as ordered. Outer posts are under instruction to hold any belt arrival for eye-and-vein check.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Good.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane glanced once toward the tree line.</p>

<p>&quot;Misha. Pattern.&quot;</p>

<p>Misha didn’t need the invitation expanded. She spoke while still writing.</p>

<p>&quot;Cross-check is consistent. The runner’s account and the scarred woman’s last word fit the same chain. Teachers move through the bands. Charms are issued, anti-attention craft, not true protection. Warm stones appear where maps show none.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;No traces?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Bands are taught to burn traces. Then the turn: animals first, people second, coordinated enough to hit a gate as a single intention. When the possessing presence withdraws, the turned drop together. This was not a random frenzy. It was a probe that spent a people to measure our close-down speed.&quot;</p>

<p>Sira’s jaw worked.</p>

<p>&quot;And the World Tree is the thing they actually want.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;If the old accounts are right,&quot; Misha said, &quot;yes. Corruption of the tree is an opening move, not a trophy at the end. Today was the hand knocking on the outer wood to see how fast we answer.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane walked the inner edge of the cordon while the first authorized burns were lit on the outer third.</p>

<p>Flame took the inert bodies with a smell that made several younger elves turn away.</p>

<p>The forest beyond the approach watched in ordinary green silence, birds starting to call again too soon, undergrowth ordinary, no white eyes, no heat-haze between the trunks.</p>

<p>The speed of that ordinary return sat wrong in his gut.</p>

<p>A battlefield this size should have left the belt raw for longer.</p>

<p>Instead, the trees looked almost eager to pretend nothing had stepped out of them.</p>

<p>He stopped at the approximate place where the scarred woman had stood.</p>

<p>&quot;System. Any signature in a forward cone.&quot;</p>

<p>[Scan: Low-grade decay only]</p>

<p>[No active possession markers]</p>

<p>[Forest noise floor: Returning to baseline faster than projected]</p>

<p>&quot;Too fast,&quot; Kane said under his breath.</p>

<p>[Agreed. Anomaly logged.]</p>

<p>Thora joined him, wiping her axe edge on a scrap of already-burned hide.</p>

<p>&quot;You are thinking about going back in.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I am thinking about the fact that whatever drove this could stand in the open, look at me through a dying woman’s eyes, and leave the entire wave on the ground like dropped tools. A hunt into that without a better fix is how you feed it more bodies.&quot;</p>

<p>He nodded toward the burns.</p>

<p>&quot;We finish the count. We get the Queen the sequence before the rumors finish inventing a second gate. Then we decide whether the boat still has a morning to leave on.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa called from the wall, impatient.</p>

<p>&quot;Chief. The warders want a second pair of eyes on a pile that is smoking wrong.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Griselda has it,&quot; Kane answered.</p>

<p>&quot;You stay on the cordon. If anything sits up, you end it, and then you shout.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Boring,&quot; Kessa said, but her ears stayed forward, and her gauntlets stayed ready.</p>

<p>Sira dropped from a low wall block to walk a few paces with him, bow still in hand.</p>

<p>&quot;When this is paper and ash, I am not sitting in a rear chair while you sail toward Giants and leave the forest’s hand uncut.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You may not get a choice,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;Someone with a spine has to hold the protocols if the belt knocks again while the main strength is on water. We will have that argument after the Queen, not in a yard full of burning dead.&quot;</p>
