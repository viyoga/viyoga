<p>&quot;I noticed.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane kept moving, Firlia’s weight solid and real against his side, the hammer heavy in his blood-slicked hand.</p>

<p>The Giant children and the wounded elf were already climbing the slope toward higher ground. The thing that had been a tree took a first experimental step after them, roots tearing free of the stone with a sound like the island itself complaining.</p>

<p>Firlia’s voice was hoarse but steady against his neck.</p>

<p>&quot;If we live through the next ten minutes, I am updating every threat assessment I have ever written about this place.&quot;</p>

<p>They ran the opposite direction.</p>

<p>The thing that had been a tree took a second step, roots tearing wider furrows in the stone, and the sound of it followed them up the slope like something clearing its throat.</p>

<p>Kane could have turned and fought.</p>

<p>The hammer still answered.</p>

<p>Rage was there, but mass modulation was not.</p>

<p>He could’ve put the engraved head through whatever passed for a heart inside that moving wood and ended the chase in the open.</p>

<p>He did not.</p>

<p>Not with the Giant children still climbing ahead.</p>

<p>Not with the wounded elf barely upright.</p>

<p>Not with Firlia shaking against his side, green hair plastered to his chest, body still marked by the coils that had held her open under the red sky.</p>

<p>Her weight was real and warm and too light.</p>

<p>Every few steps, her legs tried to take more of it and failed.</p>

<p>She noticed the way he ran: too careful, body angled to take any strike that came from behind, distance held even while he carried her.</p>

<p>Her hand rose, trembling, and traced the line of his jaw.</p>

<p>Dried blood, the edge of a bruise, the tension that had nowhere to go.</p>

<p>She kissed his cheek once, brief and deliberate.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t be scared.&quot;</p>

<p>The words landed harder than the vine that had tried for his throat.</p>

<p>Kane’s world narrowed for a single stride.</p>

<p>Scared.</p>

<p>He was the one who had walked back into a drowning island with a stolen hammer and a list of names.</p>

<p>He had killed a superior in a hall of blood. He had women who had followed him across empires and a body that still answered when he asked it for violence.</p>

<p>Fear was a luxury he had stopped budgeting for.</p>

<p>His grip on her tightened anyway.</p>

<p>The guilt- the old, quiet knowledge that he had pulled them into his orbit with protection that looked a lot like claiming- could wait.</p>

<p>Surviving the next stretch of ground could not.</p>

<p>&quot;Hold on,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>The ground itself turned against them.</p>

<p>Vines that had been lying slack across the wet stone suddenly lifted and connected, knitting into a low, sweeping net that linked the nearer trees in a single motion.</p>

<p>The net came at ankle height, fast, trying to take their legs out and drag them back toward the larger shape still climbing the slope.</p>

<p>Kane jumped.</p>

<p>Kessa twisted and cleared it by a hand’s width.</p>

<p>The smaller Giant child did not.</p>

<p>&quot;Eek—!&quot;</p>

<p>The vine caught the girl’s ankle and yanked.</p>

<p>She hit the stone hard.</p>

<p>The boy shouted something and lunged for her wrist.</p>

<p>Kessa stopped dead.</p>

<p>&quot;Tch.&quot;</p>

<p>She turned back down the slope at a dead run, ears flat, and put her whole body between the child and the net.</p>

<p>One slash of the cracked gauntlet opened the vine holding the girl.</p>

<p>The child scrambled free. Then the tree answered. Fresh coils shot from the undergrowth and wrapped both of Kessa’s wrists, yanking her arms wide.</p>

<p>Two thicker vines drove up between her legs, hooked behind her knees, and lifted.</p>

<p>She hung three feet off the ground, thighs forced open, striped body stretched in a hard X against the red sky.</p>

<p>The vines cinched tighter.</p>

<p>One tendril slid up the inside of her thigh with deliberate, exploratory pressure, tasting the heat there through the torn remaining cloth of her wrap.</p>

<p>&quot;You... degenerate...&quot; Kessa whispered, voice shaking with fury rather than fear.</p>

<p>&quot;I will burn every root you have.&quot;</p>

<p>The tendril pressed higher.</p>

<p>Kane set Firlia down in the angle of a broken ledge, hard enough that she felt the urgency, gentle enough that nothing new tore.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Kane—&quot;</p>

<p>He was already gone.</p>

<p>Multiple vines shot up from the net to meet him.</p>

<p>He didn’t slow.</p>

<p>The first he took on the hammer’s haft and snapped with a short twist of mass. The second whipped for his face; he let it wrap his forearm, used the grip as leverage, and yanked the entire length out of the ground in a spray of black sap and dirt.</p>

<p>The third caught his waist.</p>

<p>He drove the hammer’s spike straight down into the point where it joined the net and felt the whole lattice shudder.</p>

<p>&quot;Kessa!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m—nngh—fine—just kill the fucking thing—&quot;</p>

<p>The vine between her legs had found skin.</p>

<p>It stroked once, slow, mocking, before Kane reached her.</p>

<p>He put the hammer through the base of both coils holding her wrists. Mass flared for the single impact.</p>

<p>[Mass multiplier: 4.2× – impact window]</p>

<p>Wood and whatever passed for tendon inside the vines exploded.</p>

<p>Kessa dropped.</p>

<p>Kane caught her around the waist before her knees hit stone, felt the tremor running through her, the heat where the tendril had pressed, the pure murder in the way her claws flexed against his shoulder.</p>

<p>&quot;Can you run?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I can kill.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Run first.&quot;</p>

<p>He shoved her up the slope toward Firlia and the children, then turned to face the thing that was still coming.</p>

<p>The transformed tree had finished pulling its roots free.</p>

<p>It stood on two trunk-like legs, canopy folded into a rough approximation of shoulders and head, vines hanging from its arms like the sleeves of a coat made of nerves.</p>

<p>Red energy crawled the length of its body in visible veins.</p>

<p>It looked at Kane the way the succubus had looked at him in the blood hall, assessment, appetite, the faint irritation of a tool that had been interrupted.</p>

<p>Kane rolled his shoulders once.</p>

<p>The open rake across his ribs pulled.</p>

<p>The bites on his neck pulled harder.</p>

<p>He didn’t care.</p>

<p>&quot;Come on, then.&quot;</p>

<p>It came.</p>
