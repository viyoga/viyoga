<p>&quot;How are you, Misha?&quot; the woman asked, finally pulling her attention away from the knights still pinned helplessly to the floorboards.</p>

<p>&quot;I am fine, Saintess,&quot; Misha answered, taking a sip of her spiced tea.</p>

<p>&quot;And your heart?&quot; The silver veils shifted slightly as the guest tilted her head, her tone carrying a genuine note of concern.</p>

<p>&quot;The curse placed upon it?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It has been healing,&quot; Misha replied, a rare softness entering her dual-tone voice.</p>

<p>&quot;Slowly, but steadily.&quot;</p>

<p>Turning her gaze back across the mahogany table, the Saintess studied Kane with renewed interest.</p>

<p>&quot;You did this? You eased the oracle’s burden?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;She’s my wife,&quot; Kane stated simply, leaning back in his chair.</p>

<p>&quot;You didn’t rape her?&quot;</p>

<p>The question came out blunt, devoid of courtly manners or polite phrasing.</p>

<p>Kane let out a laugh, finding the absurdity of the question amusing.</p>

<p>&quot;Why would I do that?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Barbarians usually do,&quot; she pointed out calmly, her voice echoing gently in the large room. &quot;Conquerors of your particular reputation tend to claim their spoils without asking for consent. It is what the history books record.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m different,&quot; Kane corrected, swirling the remaining tea in his cup.</p>

<p>&quot;A forced bond breaks the second you turn your back on it. I prefer loyalty that actually holds up in a fight. Plus, I enjoy women who actually want to be in my bed.&quot;</p>

<p>Footsteps echoed from the main corridor just then.</p>

<p>The grand doors swung open wider, admitting Thora, Sira, Firlia, and Lyssel into the hall.</p>

<p>They had just finished organizing the southern recruits and sorting the treasury ledgers for the evening.</p>

<p>Seeing the paladins still kissing the wood, Thora smirked, nudging Sira’s shoulder before they all walked forward to claim empty seats around the long table.</p>

<p>Looking around at the capable women flanking the warlord, the Saintess hummed a quiet note of amusement.</p>

<p>&quot;You have many wives,&quot; she noted, her eyes crinkling beneath the translucent silver fabric.</p>

<p>&quot;I take care of them,&quot; Kane replied, refusing to justify his unconventional council to a temple representative.</p>

<p>&quot;They run this empire just as much as I do. Anybody who touches them they lose their head. Now, about that overgrown snake sleeping on your mountain.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Right to business,&quot; the Saintess agreed, her demeanor shifting into something much more clinical.</p>

<p>&quot;Then you need to know how it hunts. The Serpent does not just live in the snow. It generates a localized freezing domain. Once you step into its territory, the air itself will try to stop your heart.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I can buy potions for the cold,&quot; Kane noted, already doing the mental math on system shop prices.</p>

<p>&quot;Potions only delay the frostbite,&quot; she corrected, tapping a slender finger against her teacup.</p>

<p>&quot;Its scales blend perfectly with the mist. The beast erases its own presence completely. You will not see it until its fangs are already inside your throat.&quot;</p>

<p>’Just like the game,’ Kane remembered, drumming his fingers on the table.</p>

<p>’Invisible boss mechanics always annoyed me. You cannot hit what you cannot track. Brute strength is useless if you are swinging at empty air.’</p>

<p>&quot;How do I fight something I cannot see?&quot; Kane asked, leaning in closer.</p>

<p>Reaching into her robes, the Saintess pulled out a small brass bell tied to a frayed crimson rope.</p>

<p>She slid it across the mahogany table until it stopped right in front of him.</p>

<p>&quot;This is a temple relic,&quot; she explained softly.</p>

<p>&quot;Ring it once when the fog rolls in. The chime disrupts divine camouflage for exactly ten seconds.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane picked up the brass object, inspecting the intricate runes carved into the metal. It felt warm against his palm, carrying a faint hum of ancient magic.</p>

<p>&quot;Ten seconds,&quot; Kane repeated, testing the weight of the bell.</p>

<p>&quot;That is all the opening you will get,&quot; the Saintess warned.</p>

<p>&quot;The relic requires an hour to recharge between uses. If you miss your strike, the Serpent will swallow you whole.&quot;</p>

<p>’Ten seconds is plenty if I time the berserker aura right,’ Kane calculated, his combat instincts running through various scenarios.</p>

<p>’I just need to bait it close enough to guarantee a fatal blow. Make it think it has the upper hand.’</p>

<p>Thora leaned forward, eyeing the bell with intense curiosity.</p>

<p>&quot;Why are you helping him? Your temple worships the mountain deities. Giving away a relic to kill their familiar feels like treason to your own gods.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;My gods abandoned us a long time ago,&quot; the Saintess confessed, a bitter edge slipping into her bright voice.</p>

<p>&quot;That serpent has eaten three of my initiates this winter alone. It is not a divine guardian anymore. It is a starving plague. If Kane wants to remove it, I will gladly offer him the tools to do so.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia retrieved a fresh piece of parchment from her satchel, dipping her quill into the nearby inkhorn.</p>

<p>&quot;What else does the creature do? Does it have ranged attacks we should prepare for?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;It spits a freezing venom,&quot; the veiled woman answered, turning toward the elven clerk.</p>

<p>&quot;If the venom touches your skin, it crystallizes your blood instantly. Armor offers zero protection against it. A shield will just freeze and shatter in your hands.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;So don’t get hit,&quot; Kane summarized, tossing the bell lightly in his hand before slipping it into his pouch.</p>

<p>&quot;Sounds straightforward enough.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Nothing about this is straightforward,&quot; the Saintess sighed, standing up from her chair and brushing out her robes.</p>

<p>&quot;You are walking into a suicide mission. But since you spared my idiotic guards, I will offer one last piece of advice. Do not bring an army. The Serpent senses vibrations in the snow. A marching battalion will wake it up before you even reach the halfway point. Go alone, or take a very small, elite team.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I planned on keeping the raiding party light anyway,&quot; Kane agreed, standing up to match her height.</p>

<p>Glancing down at the five paladins still struggling to breathe under the lingering pressure of his aura, Kane finally snapped his fingers.</p>

<p>The crushing weight vanished instantly.</p>

<p>Gasping loudly, the knights collapsed against the floorboards, coughing and clutching their chests in desperate relief.</p>

<p>&quot;Gather your men,&quot; Kane suggested, offering a polite, almost mocking smile.</p>

<p>&quot;We have guest quarters prepared for you in the east wing. Rest up before your journey back north.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Thank you, Kane,&quot; the Saintess bowed her head slightly, offering him a genuine sign of respect.</p>

<p>&quot;May fortune favor your blade on the mountain.&quot;</p>
