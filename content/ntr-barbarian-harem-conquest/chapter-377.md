<p>Leon hit the ground hard.</p>

<p>His lip split on impact.</p>

<p>Blood, finally something the forest allowed him to feel, ran over his chin.</p>

<p>He pushed up on an elbow and tasted it.</p>

<p>&quot;Was I always this weak?&quot;</p>

<p>Kane stood over him, red hair catching the smoke-hole light, and looked at his own blood-smeared palm with open disgust.</p>

<p>&quot;What a disappointment I was. Stuck in a room all day, jerking off and streaming. Now I finally get somewhere I like, real place, real strength, women who understand a hammer, and my conscience walks in naked trying to trip me.&quot;</p>

<p>He drew his foot back.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay down. The tribe is watching the tent whether you hear them or not.&quot;</p>

<p>The kick came for Leon’s face.</p>

<p>He rolled.</p>

<p>The foot grazed his ear instead of breaking his nose.</p>

<p>On the way past, he hooked the knife at Kane’s belt, the small one, not a warhammer, the tool for cutting rope and meat, and ripped it free.</p>

<p>Kane’s eyes widened a fraction.</p>

<p>Leon came up inside the next grab and smashed the pommel into the barbarian’s temple, hard enough to make the red-haired head snap sideways.</p>

<p>Kane froze, one hand going to the spot.</p>

<p>&quot;You...&quot;</p>

<p>Leon stood.</p>

<p>Spat blood on the dirt.</p>

<p>The streamer’s body was shaking.</p>

<p>His mind was not.</p>

<p>&quot;What? You get a system, and suddenly you feel like the boss? I was you, idiot. Just experienced. I already played the raid you bragged about. I already watched Thora say she loved me while a red sea tore her out of my hands. You haven’t earned the right to call me a disappointment.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane lunged first.</p>

<p>Strength versus timing.</p>

<p>The barbarian body was denser, faster in a straight line, built for the layer.</p>

<p>Leon had none of that and all of the memory of every fight that body would later win by refusing to stay down.</p>

<p>He didn’t meet force with force.</p>

<p>He gave ground into the tent pole, let Kane’s shoulder hit hide instead of ribs, and cut, not deep, a line across the forearm that made the next punch clumsy.</p>

<p>Kane snarled and grabbed for the knife.</p>

<p>Leon let him have it and drove a thumb toward the eye.</p>

<p>Kane turned his head and took the jab on the cheekbone, then headbutted.</p>

<p>Leon staggered.</p>

<p>Kane’s fist found his stomach and folded him.</p>

<p>&quot;You fight like a man who learned from screens,&quot; Kane panted.</p>

<p>&quot;You fight like a man who hasn’t learned from consequences.&quot;</p>

<p>Leon drove upward from the crouch and put his shoulder into Kane’s knee the way he remembered a Giant’s mass teaching him: wrong angle for a clean break, right angle for a stumble.</p>

<p>Kane went down to one knee.</p>

<p>Leon climbed his back, forearm across the throat, weight on the spine, and locked on while the stronger body thrashed.</p>

<p>They hit the dirt together.</p>

<p>Kane rolled.</p>

<p>Leon went with the roll and came out on top, knees pinning the barbarian’s arms, knife recovered and held flat to the underside of Kane’s jaw.</p>

<p>Both of them breathing hard.</p>

<p>Blood on both mouths.</p>

<p>The tent was full of the smell of sweat and the first kill of a campaign.</p>

<p>&quot;Yield,&quot; Leon said.</p>

<p>Kane’s eyes burned up at him: rage, curiosity, the beginning of something that might later become Hybrid Lock, and a refusal to stay dead.</p>

<p>&quot;Or what? You cut your own throat in advance?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Or you listen for ten breaths before Thora and Sira walk through that flap and you treat them like a clear reward.&quot;</p>

<p>Footsteps.</p>

<p>Voices.</p>

<p>Leon looked up.</p>

<p>Thora stood in the open hide flap, younger, hard-eyed, one hand on Sira’s shoulder as if she had already decided the orange-haired girl was her responsibility and the new chief was a problem to be measured.</p>

<p>Sira’s mouth was open on a joke that had not landed.</p>

<p>They weren’t smiling the way the system’s affection flags would later teach them to smile.</p>

<p>They were assessing.</p>

<p>Choosing, or being forced to choose by a tribe that understood succession as a body on the dirt and a man in the tent.</p>

<p>Leon’s grip on Kane loosened without his permission.</p>

<p>He had thought, back then, after he forced himself, that they would like it when he came.</p>

<p>That protection was a fair trade.</p>

<p>That the glow of a bond window meant consent had been processed.</p>

<p>Looking at them now, before the first night, before the first forced binary of stay with the strong one or stay with the dying tribe, he saw the gap.</p>

<p>The system had made them enjoy it.</p>

<p>They hadn’t chosen the way a person chooses in a white room with a door.</p>

<p>He had stood in front of them with a bloody fist and a promise of safety and called the result a harem.</p>

<p>Every woman after, Misha with the weak heart, Firlia with the lists, Kessa with the heat, Marisa with the visions- had been handed the same shape of bargain.</p>

<p>I can protect you.</p>

<p>Therefore you are mine.</p>

<p>None of them had been asked what they would have been without the threat he arrived inside.</p>

<p>The sky above the smoke-hole turned red.</p>

<p>Not sunset.</p>

<p>The same wrong dome that had hung over the Giant quay when the fragment climbed, and the Hydra fled, and Ragnar walked into a mouth on purpose.</p>

<p>Kane, pinned, twisted his head enough to see the light change on the hide.</p>

<p>&quot;Fuck. What is that?&quot;</p>

<p>Leon stared up through the smoke-hole at a sky the layer should not have been able to render yet.</p>

<p>&quot;So that’s what it is.&quot;</p>

<p>His voice came out quiet.</p>

<p>’Devil’s Mirror. One of the eight. The one that doesn’t break your bones; it shows you the emptiness. Your worst moments. The places you called a win while the board was empty underneath.&quot;</p>

<p>Thora’s hand tightened on Sira’s shoulder.</p>

<p>She was looking at Leon now, at the soft naked double of the man on the ground, and her expression was the one she wore when a fight had more than one threat in it.</p>

<p>Leon met her eyes across the tent.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m sorry.&quot;</p>

<p>Thunder broke directly overhead.</p>
