<p>Kane dropped into the ravine without a sound.</p>

<p>The barrier of broken masonry rose ahead of him, claw marks raking fresh white lines across the dark stone.</p>

<p>Water pressed against it from the far side, black and patient, held only by the weight of whatever had piled the rubble.</p>

<p>Beyond the crude wall, the fight was already underway.</p>

<p>Kessa stood with her back to a collapsed ledge, striped body marked by bites and smears of black fluid.</p>

<p>Her ears lay flat against her skull.</p>

<p>Two Giant children, barely more than half her height, huddled behind her legs, one clutching a broken length of bone, the other simply staring with the wide, stunned eyes of someone who had already seen too much water and too many teeth.</p>

<p>Three tentacle-wolves circled.</p>

<p>Their bodies were wrong in the usual way: too many joints, hide that flexed like wet rope, mouths that opened sideways.</p>

<p>A fourth shape crouched higher on the opposite slope: half-manifested, shoulders too broad, spine ridged with something that still smoked where the air touched it. Lesser devil-form.</p>

<p>It watched the wolves work, waiting for an opening.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t speak.</p>

<p>He vaulted the barrier, hammer already rising.</p>

<p>The first tentacle-wolf turned too late.</p>

<p>The engraved head took it across the neck and continued through. Black fluid sprayed. The body folded without a cry. The second lunged.</p>

<p>Kane stepped inside the reach of the tentacles, drove the hammer low into the hinge of a joint that should not have existed, then reversed and crushed the skull on the rebound.</p>

<p>The third tried to go for the children.</p>

<p>Kessa met it with both fists and a vicious snarl.</p>

<p>Kane finished it with a downward blow that pinned the thrashing mass to the wet stone until it stopped moving.</p>

<p>The devil-form dropped from the slope.</p>

<p>It came in fast, claws raking for Kane’s throat.</p>

<p>He let the mass of the hammer answer.</p>

<p>One short, ugly arc.</p>

<p>The head connected with the thing’s sternum and kept going.</p>

<p>Bone and whatever passed for cartilage gave.</p>

<p>The creature hit the barrier hard enough to crack masonry.</p>

<p>Kane followed, hammer rising and falling three more times until the smoking shape no longer tried to stand.</p>

<p>Silence settled over the ravine floor, broken only by the soft slap of water against stone and the ragged breathing of the two children.</p>

<p>Kessa straightened.</p>

<p>She limped once, caught herself, then walked straight to him.</p>

<p>Her nostrils flared.</p>

<p>She leaned in close, scenting the blood and the taint still clinging to his skin and the open wounds across his ribs.</p>

<p>Whatever she found, it was not the same drain-scent the succubus had left.</p>

<p>She exhaled once through her nose, sharp, then shoved him hard in the chest with the heel of one cracked gauntlet.</p>

<p>&quot;Took you long enough.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane caught her wrist before the shove could put real distance between them.</p>

<p>He didn’t pull her closer.</p>

<p>He simply held the contact.</p>

<p>She stepped in anyway.</p>

<p>Forehead pressed hard against his for a single breath.</p>

<p>The heat of her skin, the faint tremor still running through the muscles of her shoulders, the iron-and-salt smell of a fight that had lasted longer than it should have: all of it registered and was filed without comment.</p>

<p>&quot;You’re late...,&quot; she whispered.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m back.&quot;</p>

<p>Then she pulled back.</p>

<p>The children stayed pressed against the ledge.</p>

<p>The smaller one, a girl whose hair was matted with silt, stared at the hammer. The boy kept his eyes on Kessa’s back as if the striped woman were the only solid thing left in the world.</p>

<p>Kane’s voice stayed low.</p>

<p>&quot;Status.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Alive. Moving. Not collected.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa flexed the fingers of her left hand.</p>

<p>Blood welled along a fresh split in the leather.</p>

<p>&quot;They tried the ledger trick first. Named me. Offered ’protection’ if I walked into the water with the others. I broke the mouth that spoke the offer. After that, they stopped talking and started hunting.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;The others.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Misha.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa’s ears twitched once, forward then back.</p>

<p>&quot;Caught her scent once, strong, before the ridge path above us gave way. Direction was further inland, higher. Not the same ravine. After that the flood rearranged everything. I couldn’t follow without leaving these two.&quot;</p>

<p>She jerked her chin toward the children.</p>

<p>&quot;Found them half-drowned under a collapsed section of the outer wall. The water was still rising. The tentacle things were already circling. I pulled them clear. They stay with me. Non-negotiable.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane looked at the pair.</p>

<p>Giant blood, clear in the bone structure and the size of the hands, but young enough that the white eyes of the primary had not yet taken them.</p>

<p>Or perhaps the primary had never reached this deep.</p>

<p>Either way, they were breathing, and Kessa had decided that mattered.</p>

<p>He gave a single nod.</p>

<p>&quot;We move higher. The water’s still held, but the barrier won’t last if more of those things decide to push.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa’s gaze tracked the black pool and the crude masonry. &quot;How many did you drop in the hall?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;One superior. Enough of the white-eyed that the rest started remembering how to breathe on their own. Stone-Mother is still standing. She has the rest of her people.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Good.&quot;</p>

<p>The word came out flat.</p>

<p>&quot;I never liked the way they looked at us when the banquet tables were full. Like we were already measured and portioned.&quot;</p>

<p>She turned to the children and spoke in the low, hard language of the ridge.</p>

<p>The boy answered in the same tongue, voice thin but steady. The girl only nodded. Kessa straightened and started climbing the opposite slope, favoring the right leg without complaint.</p>

<p>Kane followed, hammer across his shoulder, the recovered gauntlet still tucked against his belt.</p>

<p>The path out of the ravine was little more than a series of broken ledges and places where the flood had scoured the stone smooth.</p>

<p>Twice they had to lift the smaller child across gaps where the rock had simply vanished.</p>

<p>Kane took the weight without comment. Kessa watched him do it, ears forward, then looked away.</p>

<p>Halfway up, the system flickered at the edge of his attention.</p>
