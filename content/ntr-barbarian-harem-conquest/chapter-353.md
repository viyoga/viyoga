<p>Kane noticed the tremor in her fingers before she could hide it against the shelf.</p>

<p>&quot;Come outside for a minute.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia hesitated only long enough to glance at the sleeping shapes: Misha over the brief, Thora on the platform, Kessa at the threshold, Griselda near the case, then followed him into the mist and the waterfall’s constant voice.</p>

<p>The moonlight on the island was sharper than it had any right to be.</p>

<p>It turned the spray silver and put hard edges on the Giant ledges above them.</p>

<p>Firlia stood with her shoulders squared the way she squared supply columns, expression solemn, green hair still down around her face.</p>

<p>The competence was still there.</p>

<p>Under it, something had been held too tight for too many hours.</p>

<p>Kane leaned his back against the cottage’s outer stone, arms loose, and watched her instead of the ridge.</p>

<p>&quot;What is it?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Hmm?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Why are you so on guard ever since we entered this island?&quot;</p>

<p>She let out a long breath.</p>

<p>The strict line of her shoulders didn’t fully ease.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s... it’s my first time travelling out of the Elven empire.&quot;</p>

<p>A small, crooked smile.</p>

<p>&quot;It’s scary.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Scary as in how?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Because here... no one knows me. If I were to die... I guess no one would even know I exist.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane looked at her for a long moment.</p>

<p>The logistics mind that had kept wraps and runners and sealed rations moving while everyone else bled or argued or slept.</p>

<p>The woman who had walked the climb without asking to be carried.</p>

<p>&quot;Why are you putting yourself down?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m not. It’s just facts.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Well, your facts ignore a few variables.&quot;</p>

<p>Her eyes shot up.</p>

<p>&quot;Such as?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Such as us. You think Thora, Misha, Kessa—or even Sira, who’s somewhere far away on a wall- wouldn’t rush down here the moment you encountered real danger?&quot;</p>

<p>Her eyes widened a fraction.</p>

<p>Kane moved closer.</p>

<p>The moonlight caught the damp strands of green at her neck.</p>

<p>&quot;You think I would let anything get to you in the first place? Giants? Sea Hydra? The last thing any of them would manage is taking one of you. They would be long dead before they touched you at all.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia’s mouth opened, closed.</p>

<p>The solemn mask cracked into something more complicated.</p>

<p>&quot;That is a very dramatic way to say you noticed my hands shaking.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I notice when the person who keeps the lists starts vibrating like a poorly lashed crate.&quot;</p>

<p>He tilted his head.</p>

<p>&quot;Also, if you die on this island, Misha will invent a new form of administrative punishment for the rest of us, Thora will try to fight a cliff, and Kessa will start a blood feud with an entire people over a shelf arrangement. So yes. Variables.&quot;</p>

<p>A short, startled sound escaped her, almost a laugh, almost a protest.</p>

<p>&quot;You are using the household as a threat.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I am using the household as evidence. You are not a blank line on a foreign ledger. You are the reason the medical wraps were dry, and the boat didn’t leave half its brain on the quay. Start counting yourself in the same column as the rest of us, or I will have Misha add a formal entry.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia’s cheeks coloured under the moonlight.</p>

<p>She looked away toward the waterfall, then back, shoulders still tight but no longer rigid with the particular loneliness of being useful and unseen.</p>

<p>&quot;I kept thinking,&quot; she said, quieter, &quot;that if something came over the rail or out of the mist, everyone would know what to do with Thora’s bracelet and Kessa’s claws and Griselda’s wings. And I would still be the one holding the inventory when the inventory stopped mattering.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then stop holding only the inventory.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s voice stayed even.</p>

<p>Not a performance.</p>

<p>The same blunt clarity he used on battlefields and in beds when the truth was simpler than the fear around it.</p>

<p>&quot;You are allowed to want something that is not a correct shelf.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia’s breath hitched.</p>

<p>The green hair moved as she shook her head once, not in refusal, in the last resistance of a woman who had built her safety out of lists.</p>

<p>&quot;If I ask,&quot; she said, &quot;it will not be efficient. It will not be scheduled. It will not look like the person who briefs harbour masters.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Good.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Kane—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Firlia.&quot;</p>

<p>He closed the last step between them.</p>

<p>&quot;You have been awake in every room we have used since Sylvander. You watched me carry the others up a Giant path and said nothing. You are standing in moonlight on an island that is half on fire, telling me no one would know you existed. I am done letting that sentence stand. Ask. Or don’t ask. But don’t call yourself a blank line in front of me again.&quot;</p>

<p>Her hands, steady now for a different reason, rose and settled against his chest.</p>

<p>The tremor had become something else.</p>

<p>Heat under the competence.</p>

<p>Need under the solemn mouth.</p>

<p>&quot;I don’t want to be careful,&quot; she said.</p>

<p>&quot;Not tonight. I want—&quot;</p>

<p>She stopped, then forced the rest out with the same precision she used on supply counts.</p>

<p>&quot;I want you to take me apart the way you take problems apart. Properly. Without leaving a line unfinished.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s mouth curved.</p>

<p>&quot;That is the least efficient request you have ever made.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I know.&quot;</p>

<p>Her fingers curled in his shirt.</p>

<p>&quot;I am making it anyway.&quot;</p>

<p>He kissed her.</p>

<p>This was deliberate and thorough, an answer to a woman who had spent days being the correct column and had finally put her own name in it.</p>

<p>Firlia made a small sound against his mouth and pressed closer, green hair spilling over his hands as he cupped the back of her neck.</p>

<p>When he broke the kiss, her eyes were dark and clear.</p>

<p>&quot;Inside,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;The cottage has walls. The shelf can survive without you for an hour.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;If Kessa wakes—&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Kessa is half-asleep on a threshold and will pretend she heard nothing if she knows what is good for the peace. Move.&quot;</p>

<p>Firlia let him guide her back through the door.</p>

<p>The oversized room held its quiet.</p>

<p>He didn’t take her to the boat-length platform where Thora slept.</p>

<p>He took her to the deeper shadow near the hearth, where the Giant-scale furniture made a private angle, and the waterfall’s noise covered more than spray.</p>

<p>Her clothes came away under his hands.</p>
