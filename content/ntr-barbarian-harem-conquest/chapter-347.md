<p>Griselda’s report was still hanging in the salt air when Kane raised his hand.</p>

<p>The elven helmsman saw the signal and brought the boat off the wind.</p>

<p>Canvas cracked once.</p>

<p>The hull settled into a slower, uneasy glide.</p>

<p>Faces turned toward him along the rail: commanders, crew, household, waiting for the next order in a silence that had nothing to do with discipline.</p>

<p>&quot;Something’s wrong,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>Thora stood up from the crate too fast.</p>

<p>The careful breathing she had been rationing broke on a sharp intake.</p>

<p>The bracelet on her ankle: plain metal, the first gift he had given her when they were in Greta’s village, had begun a rapid beep that didn’t belong to any ordinary alarm.</p>

<p>She looked down at it, then at him.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane...&quot;</p>

<p>He nodded once.</p>

<p>&quot;Hold the rail. Do not try the mast.&quot;</p>

<p>He went up himself, boots finding the ratlines, Mjoldr left sheathed at his hip for the climb.</p>

<p>From the crosstrees the island was clearly close: cliffs, the thin harbor cut, the wrong-colored smoke inland.</p>

<p>And under the surface between boat and stone, something moved.</p>

<p>Not current.</p>

<p>A long, dark displacement that made the water’s skin crawl in a line too straight for weather.</p>

<p>&quot;Griselda.&quot;</p>

<p>She was already opening her wings.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t go far,&quot; Kane called down.</p>

<p>He dropped back to the deck and drew Mjoldr in the same motion.</p>

<p>&quot;Eyes only. You feel the air change, you come back. No heroics over open water.&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda took height in three hard beats.</p>

<p>Salt spray silvered her feathers.</p>

<p>From the deck, Kessa watched with her ears flat and her nose useless, hands tight on the rail.</p>

<p>Misha had already pulled the elven commanders into a tight knot near the helm.</p>

<p>&quot;If he calls for speed, you give it without asking why,&quot; Misha said.</p>

<p>&quot;If he calls for hard about, you do not argue the trim. Firlia—lash anything that can become a projectile. Thora, sit before you fall.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I am standing,&quot; Thora said through her teeth.</p>

<p>The bracelet kept beeping against her skin like a second pulse.</p>

<p>Above them, Griselda leveled into a safe arc, trying to map the approach without committing to the island’s air.</p>

<p>The closer she came to the cliff line, the more the atmosphere thickened: wrong pressure, a resistance that had nothing to do with wind shear.</p>

<p>Her wings stumbled once, feathers fighting her for a fraction of a second longer than they should have.</p>

<p>She corrected, jaw tight.</p>

<p>Griselda could still use them.</p>

<p>She could still keep the flight under control.</p>

<p>But every stroke made the next one harder.</p>

<p>She looked down.</p>

<p>On the dark sand of the shore, half in the wash, a Giant lay unmoving.</p>

<p>Forty feet of mass and limb, face turned toward the water, no rise of breath that she could see from height.</p>

<p>A body.</p>

<p>The wrong smoke inland had company on the beach.</p>

<p>Griselda banked to return.</p>

<p>Something in the water caught her shadow and decided it was prey.</p>

<p>&quot;KANE—MOVE!&quot;</p>

<p>Her voice cracked across the gap like a thrown blade.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t ask for detail.</p>

<p>&quot;Sail fast! Hard for the open line—away from the cut! Now!&quot;</p>

<p>The elven helmsman threw his weight into the tiller.</p>

<p>Canvas filled with a sound like a sail tearing and not quite tearing.</p>

<p>The boat surged.</p>

<p>Kessa cursed as the deck pitched.</p>

<p>Firlia drove a lashing around a crate with both hands.</p>

<p>Thora locked her free arm through a stay and held the compact axe against her ribs as if the weapon could argue with the sea.</p>

<p>The hull shook.</p>

<p>Something thrice the length of the boat moved under them: slow at first, then matching, a dark pressure that made the planks complain and the ballast complain harder.</p>

<p>Kane felt the recognition hit at the same time the system tried to surface a combat tag he did not need.</p>

<p>Mountain serpent had been a god’s familiar on land.</p>

<p>This was the water’s version.</p>

<p>Poseidon’s leviathan, or whatever this layer had named the same design: long enough to treat a coastal runner like a toy, thick enough that a single coil could break a keel, eyes somewhere in the deep that had already decided the flying shape and the wooden shape were the same meal.</p>

<p>&quot;Griselda—height and distance!&quot; Kane shouted.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t try the water!&quot;</p>

<p>She was already climbing, wings fighting the wrong air, yellow eyes locked on the dark line under the surface as it tracked the boat’s turn.</p>

<p>&quot;It is under you! Full length—I cannot see the end of it!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then stop looking and stay above the mast line!&quot;</p>

<p>The boat heeled hard as the helmsman took them off the island’s lee and into a run that put the wind on the quarter.</p>

<p>Spray came over the rail in sheets.</p>

<p>Kessa’s claws scored the wood where she held on.</p>

<p>One of the elven commanders lost his footing and caught himself on a line before the sea could claim him.</p>

<p>&quot;What is it?&quot; Misha demanded, braced beside Kane, hair plastered to her face by salt.</p>

<p>&quot;Leviathan.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane’s knuckles were white on Mjoldr’s haft.</p>

<p>&quot;We do not fight it on a hull this size. We outrun the first pass, and we pray the second one comes slow.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;And if it does not?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then we learn how fast a Giant island can launch a boat of its own while we are still in one piece.&quot;</p>

<p>The boat lifted a fraction and slammed back down hard enough to jar teeth.</p>

<p>Thora made a sound that was pure anger at her own ribs.</p>

<p>The bracelet’s beep had gone frantic, then steady again in a pattern that meant proximity without contact—yet.</p>

<p>&quot;Firlia!&quot; Kane snapped.</p>

<p>&quot;Anything not lashed becomes a club when this thing bumps us again. Kessa, eyes on the surface breaks. If you see a ridge of water that should not be there, you call it before you try to smell it.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I cannot smell anything but salt and fear-sweat!&quot; Kessa snarled, but her gaze was already sweeping the chop.</p>

<p>&quot;There—port side, long line, moving with us!&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Helmsman, starboard three points. Don’t give it a straight belly.&quot;</p>

<p>The boat answered.</p>

<p>Canvas strained.</p>

<p>Griselda’s shadow crossed the deck once as she fought for altitude the wrong air did not want to give her.</p>

<p>Below, the dark length adjusted, patient as a god’s tool, and the island’s cliffs watched the small wooden thing run for deeper water with a forty-foot corpse on the sand and smoke still rising from a war that had started without them.</p>

<p>&quot;Hold on!&quot; the helmsman yelled.</p>

<p>The leviathan rose enough to break the surface in a long, slick ridge: scales the color of deep water under cloud, a fin like a sail, an eye the size of a shield that turned toward the boat with flat, ancient interest.</p>

<p>Kane met that gaze for a fraction of a second and felt the same cold recognition he had felt under the sight of serpent.</p>
