<p>Kane ignored the flicker at the edge of his sight.</p>

<p>The system could wait. The path underfoot was wet stone and loose shale, and the two children were already struggling with the next rise.</p>

<p>He adjusted the smaller girl’s grip on his free hand and kept climbing.</p>

<p>Kessa moved beside him, closer than the trail required.</p>

<p>Her eyes kept cutting sideways: first to the open rake along his ribs, then to his face, then away again as if the glance itself might draw attention.</p>

<p>&quot;Are you okay?&quot;</p>

<p>The question came low, almost under the sound of their boots.</p>

<p>&quot;I’m fine.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You don’t look fine.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Yeah.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane shifted the hammer higher on his shoulder so it stopped knocking against the wound.</p>

<p>&quot;I thought I lost you all.&quot;</p>

<p>The Giant children stopped a few paces ahead and looked back, then deliberately turned their faces toward the higher ridge.</p>

<p>The boy made a small, formal gesture with one hand: something that meant space, or privacy, or simply that we are not listening. The girl copied it a second later.</p>

<p>They stood with their backs to the adults and waited.</p>

<p>Kessa caught Kane’s wrist.</p>

<p>She brought it close to her face and breathed in, slow and thorough, the way she had checked him for the drain-scent in the ravine.</p>

<p>Her ears angled forward.</p>

<p>The cracked leather of her remaining gauntlet creaked when her fingers tightened.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;What?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Something happened.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m fine, Kessa. Better than fine.&quot;</p>

<p>She held the wrist a full moment longer.</p>

<p>Then she turned his forearm, leaned in, and bit: light, precise, just enough pressure to leave a clean crescent of teeth in the skin above the pulse.</p>

<p>Kane blinked.</p>

<p>&quot;What was that?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;My mark of approval.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa released him and wiped a fleck of his blood from the corner of her mouth with the back of her hand.</p>

<p>&quot;Any sadness you have will fly away. Beast empire logic.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane almost smiled.</p>

<p>The expression pulled at the dried blood on his face and didn’t quite finish.</p>

<p>He leaned down and pressed his mouth to her forehead once, briefly, then straightened.</p>

<p>&quot;Where did you catch the scent?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Somewhere near.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa’s ears flicked toward the inland dark.</p>

<p>&quot;Strong for a few breaths, then the ridge path above us came down, and the water rearranged the whole cut. I thought Thora and Misha had been taken by something stronger, something that didn’t bother with wolves and ledgers. But Firlia...&quot;</p>

<p>She paused, tasting the air again.</p>

<p>&quot;Firlia’s markers are still here. Medical wraps. The way she ties off a kit strap. She has to be close.&quot;</p>

<p>They walked.</p>

<p>The trail levelled into a stretch of broken ground where the flood had left a scatter of limbs and splintered timber.</p>

<p>An elven warrior lay half in a shallow pool, chest rising in shallow, uneven pulls.</p>

<p>One arm was bent wrong.</p>

<p>His eyes tracked Kane when the shadow fell across him, but he didn’t try to rise.</p>

<p>Kane crouched.</p>

<p>&quot;Are you alright?&quot;</p>

<p>The elf’s mouth moved.</p>

<p>A small nod.</p>

<p>Nothing more.</p>

<p>&quot;Where are the others?&quot;</p>

<p>The pointed finger shook.</p>

<p>It aimed past Kane’s shoulder toward a stand of growth that should not have existed on this side of the island: a single tree, trunk thicker than any Giant’s waist, bark the colour of old bruises, branches that moved when there was no wind.</p>

<p>Vines hung from the lower limbs in slow, deliberate loops.</p>

<p>The Giant children came without being asked. The boy took the elf under the arms; the girl supported the bad leg.</p>

<p>Between them, they lifted the warrior clear of the water and settled him against a drier slab of stone.</p>

<p>The elf’s eyes stayed on the tree.</p>

<p>Kessa’s posture changed.</p>

<p>The casual roll of her shoulders vanished. Her weight settled onto the balls of her feet.</p>

<p>Ears flat, tail still, every stripe along her arms and neck seemed to tighten.</p>

<p>She didn’t draw a weapon.</p>

<p>She had none left that were not broken, but her hands curled into the shape of claws anyway.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I see it.&quot;</p>

<p>They closed the last distance together.</p>

<p>The tree was worse up close. The vines were not vines. They flexed with a slow, muscular intelligence, tightening and loosening around the shapes they held.</p>

<p>Lower down, three elven warriors hung in the coils: alive, breathing, eyes open and empty of anything useful.</p>

<p>Higher, at the place where the trunk divided, Firlia was bound.</p>

<p>Green hair plastered dark against her face and throat.</p>

<p>Arms forced behind her back.</p>

<p>The ropes, or whatever the tree used for ropes, cinched tight under her breasts and around her thighs, holding her upright and exposed to the red sky.</p>

<p>Her medical kit hung open and empty from one of the lower limbs, straps cut.</p>

<p>A line of blood ran from the corner of her mouth where she had bitten through her own lip rather than make a sound that might call something worse.</p>

<p>She saw them.</p>

<p>For a second the administrative calm cracked.</p>

<p>Relief, raw and unguarded, moved across her face.</p>

<p>Then she locked it down again and gave the smallest possible shake of her head: careful, not free, something still watching.</p>

<p>Kessa’s voice came out very quiet.</p>

<p>&quot;If that tree tries to take her deeper, I am going to set the whole ridge on fire, and we can argue about tactics later.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane shifted the hammer into both hands.</p>

<p>The engraved head caught the bruise-red light.</p>

<p>&quot;We cut her down first,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;Then we decide what burns.&quot;</p>

<p>The vines nearest the ground began to move with more purpose, tasting the air in their direction.</p>

<p>The Giant children set the wounded elf down further back and stood shoulder to shoulder, waiting.</p>

<p>Firlia’s eyes stayed on Kane’s, steady, counting the distance and the angles the way she had always counted supply lines.</p>

<p>His eyes focused on the vines.</p>

<p>It was bound to her.</p>

<p>Firlia’s movement made it move.</p>

<p>She smiled, as if accepting that this was always meant to happen, and she was glad to be alive till now.</p>

<p>Kane’s eyes narrowed as red energy spread throughout the forest.</p>
