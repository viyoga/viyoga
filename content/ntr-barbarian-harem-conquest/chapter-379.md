<p>Skin, chair, the soft streamer hands, the room’s recycled air, all of it breaking into the same clean squares the loading screens had always used, only this time he was inside the grid as it pulled him apart.</p>

<p>He felt the piece that had been left behind receive him: not a full spawn, a violent seat into a body already mid-catastrophe.</p>

<p>Roar of currents.</p>

<p>Pressure of water on ribs that remembered a broken branch.</p>

<p>Cold that the white room had never allowed.</p>

<p>Kane snapped his eyes open.</p>

<p>His shoulder screamed.</p>

<p>The sky above him was pitch red: no sun, no ordinary cloud, only the bruise-dome gone total, a low ceiling of wrong light that made the flooded land look like the bottom of a wound.</p>

<p>He was half in water, half on broken stone.</p>

<p>When he turned his head, the Giant’s island as he had known it was gone under a red-black sea.</p>

<p>No living figures at eye level or warders on a lip.</p>

<p>Only roofs and ridge-lines poking through the flood like the spines of drowned beasts.</p>

<p>Kane raised his right hand out of habit.</p>

<p>Nothing answered.</p>

<p>No weight of Mjoldr settling into the palm.</p>

<p>The binding was a severed nerve.</p>

<p>’Was it swept away?’</p>

<p>He pushed up.</p>

<p>The body was the barbarian’s again: height, denseness, the old calluses, but the shirt on his back was torn to rags and heavy with red water.</p>

<p>He stripped it off, threw the sodden cloth into the flood, and stood bare-chested in the ruin of the lower village, breathing air that tasted of salt, iron, and the sweet-rot of something the pits had finally been allowed to exhale into the open.</p>

<p>He was standing in what was left of the broken houses near the lake, near the cottage where Firlia had ordered a shelf, and Kessa had called the basin a pond with ambitions.</p>

<p>The waterfall was a muddy cataract dumping into a sea that had no right to be this high. The guest paths were channels. The banquet hall’s outer cut was a black mouth under the waterline.</p>

<p>&quot;Everyone...&quot;</p>

<p>The word came out raw.</p>

<p>He walked inland as far as the flood would let him, then climbed when the stone rose.</p>

<p>The first thing that met him past the waterline was bodies.</p>

<p>Giants.</p>

<p>Severed torsos jammed between shattered ledges.</p>

<p>A warrior pinned upright by a snapped branch through the chest, eyes open and ordinary-dead, killed by the surge or the collapse before the corruption could finish the lesson.</p>

<p>Another face-down in a pool of red-black runoff, hammer still in a hand that would not let go.</p>

<p>The lower halls’ pride laid out without ceremony for a sky that did not care about debt or coalition.</p>

<p>Kane moved between them without speaking.</p>

<p>The obligation he had named in a chair had no use for speeches over corpses.</p>

<p>He scanned for smaller frames: striped fur, white hair, green, orange, dark wings, and found none.</p>

<p>Only Giant dead and the scrape of something that wasn’t water moving in the trees above the high-water mark.</p>

<p>Wolves.</p>

<p>Or what had been wolves.</p>

<p>Lean forest shapes, too many joints, and from each spine a cluster of wet tentacles had forced their way out, tasting the red air.</p>

<p>Eyes filmed with the same family of wrong light he had seen in Ragnar’s sockets and the feeder’s bone-drifts.</p>

<p>Possessed.</p>

<p>Already feeding on the island’s leftover meat.</p>

<p>One of them lifted its head.</p>

<p>&quot;Kane.&quot;</p>

<p>The voice was human.</p>

<p>Female.</p>

<p>Thin with distance or with something wearing a throat that was not its own.</p>

<p>His head snapped toward the sound.</p>

<p>The ridge path that had led toward the primary shaft before the world tilted.</p>

<p>&quot;Misha—?&quot;</p>

<p>No answer in her cadence.</p>

<p>Only the name, repeated once more from a different angle, as if the forest were testing which direction would make him run.</p>

<p>The nearest wolf-thing jumped.</p>

<p>No time to mourn the missing axe.</p>

<p>Kane stepped inside the leap, got an arm around the neck, and twisted until something gave with a wet crack.</p>

<p>The tentacles slapped his back and shoulders.</p>

<p>He tore free and threw the body into the flood below the ledge.</p>

<p>Black fluid mixed with red water.</p>

<p>Three more came out of the brush.</p>

<p>&quot;Of course,&quot; he muttered.</p>

<p>&quot;Of course there’s no quiet minute.&quot;</p>

<p>He backed toward higher stone, hands open, eyes on the tentacles more than the teeth.</p>

<p>One feinted low.</p>

<p>He kicked it in the chest hard enough to send it sliding.</p>

<p>Another latched onto his forearm with a mouth that had too many needles.</p>

<p>He smashed its head against a broken pillar until the jaws loosened.</p>

<p>The third circled, tentacles tasting the air toward the blood on his shoulder and the older wound in his side that the branch and the quay had left.</p>

<p>A wave, or the sea still settling, slapped the rock at his feet and left something heavy in the foam.</p>

<p>Iron.</p>

<p>Engraved.</p>

<p>Kane’s eyes dropped.</p>

<p>Ragnar’s hammer.</p>

<p>The same Giant-scale weapon that had met Mjoldr on the feeder landing and on the quay, head still marked with the old working-lines of the lower halls, haft dark with water and worse.</p>

<p>It had come up out of the flood as if the island, or the hinge that had walked into the Hydra, had decided the land-walker was not allowed to stand empty-handed in the ruin he had helped open.</p>

<p>Kane closed his fingers on the haft.</p>

<p>The weight was wrong for a human frame: too long, too heavy in the head, but Rage was a door he still knew how to open.</p>

<p>He lifted the engraved hammer and felt the balance settle into a usable brutality.</p>

<p>The circling wolf-thing lunged.</p>

<p>His hand moved on instinct, smashing the wolf on its head.</p>

<p>The weapon pulsed once, then its weight increased with force.</p>

<p>Bone and tentacle and wrong light came apart.</p>
