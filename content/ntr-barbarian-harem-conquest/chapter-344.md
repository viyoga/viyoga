<p>Kane didn’t look at Thora for more than a heartbeat.</p>

<p>The ape was still on its feet: ruined knee, opened shoulder, skull streaming around the place her axe had been, and the yellow eyes fixed on him alone.</p>

<p>He set his stance and opened the thing he had kept throttled since Grathak.</p>

<p>[Controlled Berserker Output: 55%]</p>

<p>[Backlash: Elevated]</p>

<p>[Warning: Tissue stress will compound if load is sustained]</p>

<p>He didn’t dismiss the window this time.</p>

<p>The cost sat in the numbers, honest and ugly.</p>

<p>Fifty-five percent, higher than the elephant, lower than the kill that had ended an emperor.</p>

<p>Enough.</p>

<p>The ape came.</p>

<p>Kane met it at the bad knee first.</p>

<p>Mjoldr’s spike drove through the already-compromised joint with the extra weight of the Rage behind it.</p>

<p>Bone and corrupted plating gave in with a wet, structural crack.</p>

<p>The animal’s leg folded.</p>

<p>It screamed and raked for his throat.</p>

<p>He was already moving to the second engagement, using the collapse to put the edge into the throat seam he had opened earlier.</p>

<p>Black arterial spray hit the roots.</p>

<p>The ape still didn’t die.</p>

<p>It caught his forearm in both hands and tried to pull him into a bite.</p>

<p>Kane tore free, reopened a line across his own ribs, and set for the third cut.</p>

<p>&quot;Stay down,&quot; he said without looking at Thora.</p>

<p>&quot;I am finishing this.&quot;</p>

<p>The skull was the last piece.</p>

<p>He stepped inside the animal’s failing reach, brought Mjoldr down in a full descending arc aimed at the base of the head where Thora’s axe had already weakened the cord, and put everything the fifty-five percent would give him into the strike.</p>

<p>The edge went through.</p>

<p>Slash.</p>

<p>The yellow light in the eyes snuffed.</p>

<p>The bulk of the mini-boss finally understood it was dead and fell sideways into the roots with a weight that shook the clearing.</p>

<p>Kane stood over it, breathing hard, blood running from the reopened lines on his forearm and the scrape along his ribs.</p>

<p>The system flashed amber at the edge of his vision: tissue stress, backlash climbing, recovery window confirmed. He let the Controlled Output drop and felt the cost settle into his body like lead.</p>

<p>[Controlled Rage: Offline]</p>

<p>[Amber flags: active]</p>

<p>[Recommendation: limited load until recovery window closes]</p>

<p>&quot;Recommendation noted,&quot; he muttered, and turned.</p>

<p>Thora was trying to stand.</p>

<p>She had made it as far as one knee.</p>

<p>Her free hand was pressed to her midsection.</p>

<p>The compact axe lay a few paces away in the leaf litter.</p>

<p>When she pushed for the second leg, the breath left her in a sharp, involuntary sound, and she went back down to the root she had landed against.</p>

<p>&quot;Don’t,&quot; she got out.</p>

<p>&quot;I can—&quot;</p>

<p>Kane crossed the space, set Mjoldr aside long enough to get his arms under her, and lifted.</p>

<p>Thora’s weight settled against his chest.</p>

<p>For a moment her body tensed, the old reflex to refuse help on anything physical, and then, for the first time he could name, she let the tension go.</p>

<p>She didn’t fight the carry.</p>

<p>Her forehead dropped against his shoulder once, brief and involuntary, and she said nothing while he got his balance and his axe back into his free hand.</p>

<p>&quot;Ribs,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;Bruised. Maybe cracked. I have had worse.&quot;</p>

<p>Her voice was tight.</p>

<p>&quot;Put me down when the ground is honest. Not before.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;When the ground is honest.&quot;</p>

<p>He didn’t put her down yet.</p>

<p>Kane walked her as far as the base of the mini-boss tree and set her carefully against a root that would hold her upright.</p>

<p>Only then did he look properly at what the fight had been sitting on top of.</p>

<p>Between the major roots, in a hollow that the ordinary forest floor should not have allowed, a stone shrine had been built into the dark.</p>

<p>Small animal bones arranged in patterns.</p>

<p>Bone-circle charms laid in deliberate rings.</p>

<p>Dried blood worked into the grooves of the stone.</p>

<p>At the center, something pulsed with a wrong heat that had nothing to do with sunlight or residual combat.</p>

<p>Kane didn’t touch it with bare skin.</p>

<p>He pulled a cloth from his pack, wrapped the fragment once, then again, and only then lifted it free of the shrine bed.</p>

<p>The heat still came through the fabric against his fingers, persistent, familiar in a way that made the system surface without being asked.</p>

<p>[Object class: Unknown]</p>

<p>[Signature: Partially consistent with Berakamon post-Grathak projection]</p>

<p>[Recommendation: Do not handle without Griselda assessment]</p>

<p>[Secondary note: Devil-adjacent craft. Containment advised.]</p>

<p>He held the wrapped weight and, for a single breath, was alone with it.</p>

<p>The heat against his fingers was not the pod.</p>

<p>It wasn’t the extraction lock.</p>

<p>It was only a fragment of the same war that had already spent a people on a gate and was now sitting under a tree that had once been a game’s gate-check boss.</p>

<p>The door back to the real world stayed shut behind his eyes the way it always did: present, unopenable, set aside because the body in front of him and the woman on the root still needed him in this layer, not the other one.</p>

<p>He put the thought away with the cloth.</p>

<p>&quot;Thora. Can you walk if I take most of the weight?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I can walk if you stop asking.&quot;</p>

<p>She got her feet under her with his help, jaw locked, and tested a step.</p>

<p>The breath she took was careful.</p>

<p>&quot;Dusk is coming. If we are not back before full dark, Misha will invent a new kind of angry.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then we move.&quot;</p>

<p>They reached Sylvander’s outer works at dusk.</p>

<p>Thora refused the carry for the last stretch.</p>

<p>She walked through the gate under her own power: slow, upright, one hand still pressed to her ribs, while Kane matched her pace with the wrapped fragment in his pack and Mjoldr dark on his shoulder.</p>

<p>The household was already at the arch.</p>

<p>Misha’s eyes went first to the blood on both of them, then to the way Thora was breathing.</p>

<p>Kessa’s ears went flat.</p>

<p>Griselda’s gaze locked on the shape in Kane’s pack before it ever rose to his face.</p>

<p>&quot;Are you alright, Thora?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I’m... fine.&quot;</p>

<p>Kessa sniffed the air near Thora and grimaced.</p>

<p>&quot;You smell like cracked ribs and stubbornness.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I smell like a woman who is going to sit down before she falls,&quot; Thora said, and did exactly that on the nearest solid crate.</p>

<p>&quot;Someone find a clean cloth.&quot;</p>

<p>Griselda held out both hands for the pack.</p>

<p>Kane gave her the wrapped fragment without ceremony.</p>
