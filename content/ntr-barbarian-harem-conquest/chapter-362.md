<p>The cut held.</p>

<p>What could be burned was burned.</p>

<p>What still twitched was ended.</p>

<p>Hammer-teams walked the lower-tier paths twice until even Kessa’s nose found only smoke, iron, and the ordinary stink of a fight already finished.</p>

<p>Thora’s bracelet slowed to a sullen tick: deeper warning still present, no longer screaming at the lip.</p>

<p>Kane left the household with the Stone-Mother’s escort captain and the elven commanders, then crossed the stained stone alone.</p>

<p>She stood at the edge of the cleared line, hammer butt resting on the rock, bone band in her hair dark with spray that was not only waterfall.</p>

<p>Up close, the heavier tiredness was still there.</p>

<p>Under it, a fine sheen of sweat at her temples that the morning’s cold should have discouraged.</p>

<p>&quot;Is the Elder alright?&quot;</p>

<p>The question surprised her.</p>

<p>Her eyes flicked once toward the high cuts, toward the posts where Stone-Watcher had not come down, then back to Kane’s face.</p>

<p>&quot;Yes.&quot;</p>

<p>A single nod.</p>

<p>&quot;You did well, slaying him quicker. Givan. The longer a turned body walks, the more it teaches the stone around it. You cut the lesson short.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane watched the sweat, not the words.</p>

<p>Hybrid Lock sat quiet under his own skin.</p>

<p>The system’s passive edge had nothing clean to hang an accusation on, only a gradient, a maybe, the same patient pressure that lived in the ridge.</p>

<p>If the Elder’s body was fighting an overwrite with whatever mana a Giant of his line carried, saying so on this ledge would turn a provisional ally into a hall full of hammers pointed the wrong way.</p>

<p>He didn’t spend it.</p>

<p>&quot;I will dive in,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>The Stone-Mother’s mouth tightened.</p>

<p>&quot;Secondary mouth. As agreed. My escort, your method, box shut until the warders say otherwise.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Secondary mouth,&quot; Kane agreed.</p>

<p>&quot;My household stays on the lower tier and the hall doors. I am not feeding it more of them until I know what the smaller wound costs.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;You will take some of your own.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Elven commanders. Three. Enough to hold a rope and a signal. Not enough to empty the defence if the lip learns how to climb again while I am under.&quot;</p>

<p>She studied him for a long moment, the way he had asked after her husband before he asked after the shaft.</p>

<p>&quot;East secondary is a five-hundred-metre throat,&quot; she said.</p>

<p>&quot;Straight in places. Not in others. Root-Binder’s primary is deeper and meaner; this one is the feeder that taught the cut to spit Givan’s smile. You will smell the warm stones before you see them. You will hear things that use familiar voices. If you turn back at the first wrong word, I will not call you a coward.&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;I will turn back when the job is done or when the rope is the only thing left that still answers,&quot; Kane said.</p>

<p>&quot;Not before.&quot;</p>

<p>They stood at the ridge’s secondary mouth under a sky that had gone thin and colourless.</p>

<p>Kane.</p>

<p>Three elven warriors in travel harness and pale faces.</p>

<p>Two of the Stone-Mother’s paid fighters, hammer and spear, eyes that had already watched friends end for gold in the iris.</p>

<p>The household was a weight on the lower paths and a set of orders. Here there was only the hole.</p>

<p>The pit wasn’t a clean well.</p>

<p>It opened like a throat that had been worried by teeth.</p>

<p>Five hundred metres of dark, the first drop sheer enough that a dropped stone took a long time to speak back.</p>

<p>The walls wept a thin mineral damp.</p>

<p>Old Giant handholds, cut for fingers twice the width of Kane’s, descended in irregular ranks and then simply stopped, as if the diggers had decided the rest of the depth did not deserve courtesy.</p>

<p>A cold draft rose from below, not clean mountain air.</p>

<p>Something that had sat on warm stone and closed doors and the memory of a man who thought he could shut a pit from the inside.</p>

<p>One of the elves: lean, jaw tight, the same commander who had held Misha’s choke-line, cleared his throat.</p>

<p>&quot;Depth estimate holds. Five hundred to the first ledge the warders marked. After that, the maps become guesses.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane settled Mjoldr’s strap across his chest and checked the rope anchors with his own hands.</p>

<p>The system’s passive scan painted the gradient in blunt tags at the edge of his sight: elevated taint, rising with depth, no immediate divine-adjacent spike, fragment resonance at the surface ward still stable.</p>

<p>&quot;Signals,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;Three tugs: stop. Two: coming up. Continuous: something has the rope that is not us. You don’t climb down after me on continuous. You cut if the anchor starts to go, and you run for the Stone-Mother’s line. Clear?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Clear,&quot; the second elf said.</p>

<p>Her voice was steady. Her knuckles on the spear were not.</p>

<p>One of the Giant escorts spat over the lip and listened to the long absence of a splash.</p>

<p>&quot;It doesn’t always answer with water,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;Sometimes it answers with breath. Sometimes with a name. My brother went into a different feeder last month because he heard our mother’s voice. We ended up walking back out. Do not answer anything that knows what to call you.&quot;</p>

<p>The other Giant, quieter, adjusted his hammer-strap.</p>

<p>&quot;Root-Binder said the same kind of thing before the primary. Said the dark was only dark. Said a door was only a door. The stone learned his voice well enough to borrow it for the next three who went looking.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane looked into the five-hundred-metre throat until the dark stopped being a colour and became a pressure against the eyes.</p>

<p>&quot;No one answers the pit,&quot; he said.</p>

<p>&quot;We are here to find the stones and the tap grammar and cut what can be cut. We are not here to negotiate with whatever learned Givan’s smile.&quot;</p>

<p>The first elf swallowed.</p>

<p>&quot;And if it has already learned yours?&quot;</p>

<p>&quot;Then I don’t make it easier by speaking when I should be cutting.&quot;</p>

<p>Kane hooked onto the line, tested the brake, and felt the island’s cold draft climb his sleeves.</p>

<p>&quot;Stone-Mother’s warders hold the mouth. You three hold the rope and the signal. You two—&quot; he nodded at the Giants—&quot;you know the handholds better than we do. You tell me when the wall stops being honest. I go first.&quot;</p>

<p>Thora’s bracelet was a distant fact on a lower path.</p>

<p>He could almost feel the argument she was not having with the air.</p>

<p>Kessa’s nose would have been working this draft into a map.</p>

<p>Griselda would have read the wrongness in the pressure against her wings.</p>

<p>He had left them there on purpose.</p>

<p>The pit didn’t get to collect the whole family on the first descent.</p>
